ROMI: is your marketing paying off, and how to calculate it

"Is advertising paying off?" deserves an answer from a formula, not a feeling. ROMI (return on marketing investment) shows how much profit each unit of money spent on marketing brought back. Here is how to calculate ROMI, how it differs from ROI and ROAS and which costs to include.

The formula

ROMI = (profit from marketing − marketing costs) ÷ marketing costs × 100%

The result is more accurate if "profit" means gross profit (revenue minus cost of goods) rather than revenue. Calculating from revenue alone inflates ROMI artificially.

An example (illustrative numbers)

  • Marketing costs: ads $700 + agency $300 = $1,000.
  • Sales from ads: $6,000.
  • A 30% margin → gross profit $1,800.

ROMI = (1,800 − 1,000) ÷ 1,000 × 100% = 80%. Every dollar spent returned 80 cents of net profit.

ROMI vs ROI vs ROAS

MetricWhat it measuresFormula
ROMIReturn on marketing spend(profit − marketing) ÷ marketing
ROIReturn on any investment(profit − investment) ÷ investment
ROASRevenue per unit of ad spendRevenue ÷ ad spend

In the example, ROAS = 6,000 ÷ 700 ≈ 8.6. ROAS looks high but ignores margin and service fees — which is why ROMI is more reliable for decisions.

Which costs to include

  • The ad budget.
  • Specialist or agency fees.
  • Content production: video, design.
  • Tools: CRM and services (the marketing share).

What makes it tricky

  • Long sales cycles — the lead comes this month, payment next month. Match periods correctly.
  • Repeat purchases — customers buy several times; account for them with LTV.
  • Attribution — which sale came from which ad; you need a CRM and UTM tags.

What a good ROMI looks like

A positive ROMI means marketing pays off. But comparing channels matters more than any single number: move budget towards the channel with the higher ROMI. ROMI belongs in your monthly advertising report.

ROMI by channel: a sample table

An example (illustrative numbers) — one month's results:

ChannelSpendGross profitROMI
Instagram ads$1,000$1,80080%
Posts in Telegram channels$500$60020%
Google search$800$2,000150%

The table suggests moving part of the budget to Google. But every channel has limited capacity: raise the budget in stages and watch that ROMI doesn't fall.

4 ways to raise ROMI

  1. Lower the cost per lead — improve creatives and audiences; see Cost per lead (CPL).
  2. Raise lead-to-sale conversion — fast replies and a sales script.
  3. Increase the average order — add-ons, bundles or a higher tier.
  4. Repeat sales — turn one-off customers into long-term ones.

Ways 2 and 4 are often the cheapest: ROMI rises without spending more on ads.

Common mistakes

  • Using revenue instead of gross profit.
  • Forgetting agency, content and tool costs.
  • Calculating ROMI too early when the sales cycle is long.
  • Comparing channels over different periods.
  • Ignoring repeat purchases.

Questions and answers

If ROMI is negative, should you stop the ads immediately?

Not immediately. First check that the period is right, repeat purchases are counted and you're not still in a test phase. If ROMI stays negative for several months even after optimisation, move the budget to another channel.

How often should you calculate ROMI?

Every month. If the sales cycle is long — in real estate or B2B, for example — quarterly figures are more accurate.

How should you judge brand spending?

Brand advertising doesn't bring quick sales, so judging it by ROMI alone is wrong. Look at indirect signals: searches for your brand name, direct enquiries and a falling cost per lead in other channels.

Can you calculate ROMI without a CRM?

Roughly — with a spreadsheet and by asking every customer "Where did you hear about us?". But accuracy will be lower: a CRM links every sale to its source. Choosing one is covered in CRM for small business.

How do you include repeat purchases in ROMI?

If customers come back, use the average total profit per customer rather than just the profit from the first purchase. An example (illustrative numbers): the first purchase brings $30 profit, but over a year a customer buys three times on average — $90 per customer. Counted this way, a campaign that looked unprofitable on the first purchase can turn out to be profitable.

Conclusion

ROMI lets you manage marketing as an investment rather than a cost. Calculate it from gross profit, include every marketing cost and compare channels. The key input is cost per lead — see our CPL guide.
