Live selling: selling via live streams on Instagram and TikTok

A live stream lets you show products in real time, answer questions instantly and take orders on the spot. For viewers, it's like walking into a shop: they see the product in someone's hands and talk to the seller. Many brands have made live selling a regular format. But simply "going live" doesn't work — you need a script, a team and an ordering system. Here's how to sell through live streams.

Who it suits best

  • Clothing and footwear — sizes, fabric, trying items on.
  • Cosmetics — application, results, ingredients.
  • Homeware and appliances — showing them in action.
  • Food and sweets — the making process.
  • Expert services — Q&A streams followed by consultation bookings.

A stream script (45–60 minutes)

TimeWhat happens
0–5 minutesGreeting, what the stream is about, why to stay till the end (the stream-only offer)
5–35 minutes5–8 products, 3–4 minutes each: show, price, questions
35–45 minutesQ&A, showing the most-requested products again
45–50 minutesThe offer's deadline, how to order, goodbye

For each product: what it is, who it's for, the price and how to order. Short and clear.

Team roles

  • Host — shows the products and talks.
  • Moderator — reads comments, passes questions to the host and logs orders.
  • Salesperson — processes orders in DMs and Telegram after the stream.

One person can't do it all — orders will get lost.

Taking orders

  • A simple code: "Product number + size", in the comments or by DM.
  • Reply to every buyer within an hour of the stream — lead response time.
  • A Telegram bot or link for fast ordering — Telegram Business features.
  • Log every order in the CRM with the source "Live".

Promoting the stream

Gathering an audience is half the job:

  • 3–5 days ahead, a story and a post with the date, time and stream-only offer;
  • reminders on the morning of the stream and an hour before;
  • an announcement in your Telegram channel;
  • if needed, an ad promoting the announcement.

The stream offer must be real: a gift or bonus only for viewers. Think discounts through — the discount trap.

The technical minimum

  • A stable internet connection and a fully charged phone (or a power supply).
  • A tripod, good lighting and a clean background.
  • A microphone — sound matters more than picture — sound in video ads.
  • Products laid out in order in advance.

After the stream

  • Save the recording and cut it into short Reels — one shoot, 10 ad creatives.
  • DM everyone who asked questions in the comments.
  • Count the results: viewers, orders, sales total.

Common mistakes

  • No announcement, so no viewers.
  • One person both presenting and logging orders.
  • A long intro — people leave within 2 minutes.
  • Not stating prices — "send us a DM".
  • Slow replies to orders after the stream.

Questions and answers

How often should we go live?

Once a week, on the same day and time, so it becomes a habit.

What if few people watch?

That's normal at the start. Even 10–20 viewers can place orders. Consistency and promotion grow the audience.

Should we state prices on the stream?

Yes. A stream without prices slows sales; prices must be in soum — advertising law in Uzbekistan.

Conclusion

Live selling takes a script, clear team roles, a simple ordering system, promotion in advance and fast replies afterwards. Regular streams give a brand trust and a steady sales channel. In a free audit, Level Marketing will show you the sales opportunities on your Instagram page.
