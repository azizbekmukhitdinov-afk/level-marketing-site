7 marketing mistakes new entrepreneurs make

At the start of a business, marketing mistakes are especially costly: money is short and time is shorter. Here are the 7 most common mistakes new entrepreneurs make, and how to fix them.

1. A product "for everyone"

"Everyone needs our product" means your ads speak to no one in particular. Choose one core segment: who they are, where they are and what problem they have. See our guide to target audiences.

2. Ads without a strategy

Randomly boosting posts is the fastest way to waste a budget. Before launching, define the goal, the offer and the customer journey: ad → profile or page → lead → sale. More on the sales funnel.

3. Decisions without numbers

If you don't know how many leads came in, how many became sales and what each customer cost, you can't tell which ads work. Start with a simple spreadsheet or a CRM, and mark sources with UTM tags.

4. Blindly copying competitors

The same offer, the same photos, the same copy — price becomes the only difference, and a price war begins. Find what makes you different: your unique selling proposition.

5. Expecting instant results

Switching ads off after 2–3 days because "it doesn't work" is a common mistake. Every campaign needs a test period: try several creatives and audiences, then keep the best. Here's how: A/B testing ad creatives.

6. Weak lead handling

The ad brought a lead, but the reply comes hours later or the manager doesn't know what to say. The money spent on ads then walks away with the lost customers. The fix: a response time standard and a sales script.

7. No budget plan

The money runs out in a month — or a channel that works never gets scaled. Make a monthly plan: how much for tests, how much for proven channels, how much for content — see planning a marketing budget.

A 90-day marketing plan

PeriodWhat to do
Month 1Main segment, offer and USP; profile or website; a spreadsheet or CRM for leads
Month 2Test 3 creatives × 2 audiences; a response standard and a script
Month 3Scale the channels that worked; collect reviews; start repeat sales

At the end of each month, record the same numbers: spend, leads, sales and cost per customer. That way growth or problems show up immediately.

Bonus: 3 more small but costly mistakes

  • Not building a customer list. Every buyer's contact is the cheapest channel for the next sale.
  • Relying on a single channel. If the account gets blocked or the algorithm changes, the flow of leads stops.
  • Hiding the price. "Price in DM" makes many people leave without writing.

Check yourself: 8 questions

  1. Can you describe your main customer in one sentence?
  2. Can you say in one sentence why people should buy from you?
  3. Do you track leads by source?
  4. Do you know your average response time to a lead?
  5. Do your managers have a written script?
  6. Is there a planned monthly marketing budget?
  7. Do you know what one customer costs you?
  8. Do you get back in touch with past customers?

Every "no" is a ready-made task for next month.

Questions and answers

Should you do marketing yourself or hire a specialist?

At the start it helps to understand the basics yourself — otherwise you can't judge anyone else's work. Once the numbers and processes are clear, hand tasks to a specialist; how to choose one is covered in How to choose a paid ads specialist.

How big should a starting budget be?

There's no universal amount. A simple guide: for a test, budget for about 20 leads — multiply your expected cost per lead by 20.

When can you expect results?

The first leads from ads arrive within days, but a stable system usually takes 2–3 months to form. Organic channels — content, SEO, a Telegram channel — take longer.

Which channel should you start with?

The one where your customers are. For many B2C businesses that's Instagram and Telegram; for products people search for, it's Google and maps. Get one channel working as a system, then add a second.

What if competitors sell for less?

Don't compete on price — find your difference: service, warranty, speed or specialisation. How to do it is covered in Competitor analysis.

Which numbers should you check every week?

Five key ones: ad spend, number of leads, cost per lead, number of sales and cost per customer. Record them in one spreadsheet week by week — then you'll notice a channel slipping without waiting for the end of the month. The report structure is covered in What an ad report should include.

Conclusion

All of these mistakes can be fixed — often without raising the ad budget. To test an idea properly from day one, read how to validate a business idea. If you need help putting your marketing on a systematic footing, write to the Level Marketing team.
