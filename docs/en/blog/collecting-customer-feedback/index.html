How to collect customer feedback and what to do with it

Customers are ready to tell you why they buy from you and why they leave — you just have to ask. Feedback is free market research: it gives you words for your ads, ideas for improving the product and public reviews. Here is when and how to ask, what NPS is and how to use the answers.

When to ask

  • Right after a purchase or service — while the impression is fresh.
  • After the product has been used — for example, 7–14 days later.
  • After a complaint is resolved — to see how well the problem was handled.
  • When a customer stops buying — knowing why they left is especially valuable.

Which channels to use

A short Telegram message or bot, a call from a manager, Google Forms, a QR code at the till and a face-to-face conversation with B2B clients. Pick the channel that suits your customers, and keep the survey to 2–3 minutes.

NPS: loyalty in one question

The question: "On a scale of 0 to 10, how likely are you to recommend us to a friend or colleague?"

ScoreGroup
9–10Promoters — they recommend you
7–8Passives — satisfied but easy to lose
0–6Detractors — unhappy and may spread negative word of mouth

NPS = % promoters − % detractors. An example (illustrative numbers): out of 100 responses, 50 are promoters, 30 passives and 20 detractors → NPS = 50 − 20 = 30. The follow-up question matters more than the number: "Why did you give that score?"

A question list

  1. Why did you choose us?
  2. What held you back before buying?
  3. Who or what did you compare us with?
  4. What did you like most?
  5. What should we improve?
  6. Where did you hear about us?
  7. How would you describe us to a friend?

How to use the answers

What the answers containWhere to use it
The words customers useAd copy and headlines, your USP
Fears before buyingYour FAQ and sales script
Competitors they mentionCompetitor analysis
PraiseAsk for a public review — see online reputation
ComplaintsFixes to the product and service
SourcesSplitting the budget between channels

Rules

  • Don't ask leading questions: not "Did you enjoy our service?" but "What would you say about our service?"
  • Thank people for every answer.
  • Tell customers what you changed — they'll be more willing to answer next time.
  • Keep all answers in one spreadsheet or your CRM.

A sample Telegram survey

"Hello, [name]! Thanks for your order. To help us improve, three short questions: 1) On a scale from 0 to 10, how likely are you to recommend us to a friend? 2) Why did you give that score? 3) If we could improve one thing, what would it be?"

Always say thank you after a reply. If the score is low, have a manager get in touch the same day and try to fix the problem.

Talking to a customer who left

Call a customer who stopped buying — not to sell, but to understand. Four questions are enough: why did you stop, what are you using now, what would need to change for you to come back, and what did you like about us? Record the reasons in groups: price, quality, service, changed needs or moved away. Within a few months you'll see which reason comes up most.

A monthly review routine

  1. Gather all the answers in one spreadsheet.
  2. Tag each one with a topic: price, quality, delivery, staff.
  3. Count the answers by topic and pick the three most frequent.
  4. Assign an owner and a deadline to each.
  5. Tell customers what you changed — in a post or a message.

Common mistakes

  • A long survey — customers give up halfway.
  • Asking at the wrong moment: a month after the service, or in the middle of a complaint.
  • Collecting feedback and changing nothing.
  • Asking only happy customers.
  • Arguing with negative feedback.

Turning feedback into marketing: 3 examples (illustrative)

What customers sayWhat you can do
"Delivery was really fast"Make delivery time the main advantage in your ads
"I didn't understand the return terms"Simplify the terms and add them to the FAQ
"More expensive, but better quality"A post explaining what the price is made of

Fill in a table like this every month, and feedback won't get stuck in a "reviewed" folder.

Questions and answers

Should you give a gift for answering a survey?

A small thank-you for an internal survey — say, a bonus on the next purchase — is fine. But don't reward reviews on public platforms: it's against their rules.

How many answers are enough?

To understand reasons, even 10–20 in-depth interviews reveal recurring patterns. For NPS, the change from month to month matters more than any single number.

Is an anonymous survey better?

Anonymous answers are often more candid, but you can't contact the customer to solve their problem. Combine the two: a named main survey with an optional anonymous "any other thoughts" section.

How do you get feedback from B2B clients?

A short quarterly meeting with the decision-maker, or a 15-minute call when a project ends. In B2B, one conversation often tells you more than ten questionnaires.

How should you take negative feedback?

Don't take it personally: criticism is free advice. Say thank you, clarify the details and say what you'll change. Once the problem is solved, let the customer know the result.

Conclusion

Collect feedback continuously, not once a year: it's the cheapest source of insight for your ads, sales and product. Videos from happy customers also make great ads — read our UGC content article. For how feedback connects to repeat sales, see the LTV article.
