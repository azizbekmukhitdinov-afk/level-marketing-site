What is artificial intelligence? A plain guide for business

"Artificial intelligence" is everywhere today: in the news, in ads, in phone apps. But for a business owner, the key question is simple: what is it, and what does it have to do with my business? Here's AI explained without technical jargon — its types, its real value for business and its limits.

Artificial intelligence: a simple definition

Artificial intelligence (AI) is a computer's ability to do tasks that normally need human intelligence: understanding text, answering questions, recognising images, suggesting decisions.

Uzbekistan's legislation has a definition too. Under Law No. ZRU-1115 of 21 January 2026, which amended the Law "On Informatisation", artificial intelligence is a set of technological solutions that imitate human knowledge and skills, including independent learning and searching for solutions (lex.uz).

How it works: an example

AI learns not from rules but from examples. Think about it: to explain "cat" to a child, you don't write rules — you show them hundreds of cats. AI works the same way: after "seeing" millions of texts, images and examples, it finds patterns.

That's why AI:

  • works well with things it has seen a lot of — English text, for instance;
  • makes mistakes with things it has seen little of — rare Uzbek expressions, for instance;
  • may give a confident but wrong answer instead of saying "I don't know".

The real value for business

AI's main benefit is time. It speeds up work that takes a lot of time but doesn't need complex decisions:

  • text drafts: posts, replies, descriptions;
  • organising information: reviews, questions, tables;
  • technical tasks: subtitles, draft translations, formatting;
  • ideas: content, promotions, name options.

Limits: what AI can't do

  • It doesn't know your business. Customers, numbers, competitors — without your input, AI guesses.
  • It gets facts wrong. Always check laws, prices and statistics against the primary source.
  • It takes no responsibility. You're accountable for your ads, contracts and prices.
  • It doesn't create trust. Customers trust real people, real products and real results.

The law says the same: decisions affecting a person's rights and freedoms can't rely solely on AI outputs (ZRU-1115). What not to hand over to AI — what not to delegate to AI.

Uzbek and AI

Uzbek support has improved in recent years: in November 2025, for example, it was announced that Google Gemini officially supports Uzbek (Kun.uz). But quality is still below English and Russian, so a native speaker should always check Uzbek text.

Where to start: 3 steps

  1. Pick one task — the one that takes the most time and carries the least risk.
  2. Test for a week — compare how long it takes with and without AI.
  3. Write the rules — what never goes in, who checks, where it's used. Training your team — train your team on AI.

Common mistakes

  • Treating AI as an all-knowing expert.
  • Using output without checking it.
  • Entering customer data.
  • Trying to automate everything at once.
  • Or the opposite — never trying it because "it's not for us".

Questions and answers

Are artificial intelligence and ChatGPT the same thing?

No. ChatGPT is one AI-based tool. Artificial intelligence is the broader concept.

Will AI take jobs?

AI mostly changes tasks, speeding up the manual part. Employees who can work with AI become more valuable.

Does a small business need AI?

Yes, especially a small team that's short on time. But the goal isn't "using AI" — it's speeding up a specific task.

Must AI-generated content be labelled?

The final text of Law ZRU-1115 contains no mandatory labelling requirement for AI content. But advertising must still be truthful and not misleading. More in AI and the law in Uzbekistan.

Conclusion

Artificial intelligence is technology that learns from examples and speeds up human work. It helps with text, images, video and analysis, but it doesn't know your business, makes factual mistakes and takes no responsibility. Start small, measure the result and write down the rules.
