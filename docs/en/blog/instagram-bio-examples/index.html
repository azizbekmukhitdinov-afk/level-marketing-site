Someone who saw your Reel or ad and tapped through to your profile reads the bio first — and decides within seconds whether to follow, message you or leave. Many businesses have generic words in their bio like "Quality and trust ✨", which say nothing. An Instagram bio fits up to 150 characters. Here's how to fill it, plus 12 ready-made examples.

A bio should answer 4 questions

  1. What? — the product or service.
  2. For whom, or where? — city, audience.
  3. Why you? — one concrete difference: a guarantee, a turnaround time, experience.
  4. What's next? — how to order or get in touch.

The formula: What + where + difference + action.

The name field is for search

Your profile name (the "Name" field, not the username) counts in Instagram search. Add a keyword there, not just your brand name:

  • not "Zamon Mebel" → "Zamon Mebel | Custom kitchens Tashkent"
  • not "Dr. Aliyev" → "Dr. Aliyev | Dentist Chilanzar"

(names are illustrative examples).

The link

The bio link is the bridge from profile to lead. Options:

  • a Telegram bot or salesperson — a Telegram bot for leads;
  • a website or landing page — a landing page that converts;
  • if you need several links, Instagram lets you add up to 5 links to your profile.

Add a UTM tag to each link so you know where leads come from — UTM tags.

After the bio: highlights and first posts

The bio is the door. Next, people look at your highlights and first 9 posts: prices, work, reviews, delivery. The full structure — an Instagram profile that sells.

Common mistakes

  • Generic words: "quality", "trust", "the best".
  • No address or city.
  • No link, or a broken one.
  • Too many emojis and hashtags — hard to read.
  • A confusing mix of two languages.
  • No call to action.

Questions and answers

Which language should the bio be in?

Your main audience's language. For a bilingual audience, two short, clear lines can work — ad language: Uzbek or Russian.

How often should I change the bio?

When your offer changes: a promotion, a new branch, a season. The basic structure stays.

Do I need hashtags?

One brand hashtag is enough. Lots of hashtags clutter the bio.

Should I include a phone number?

Business profiles have contact buttons — it's more convenient to put the number there.

Conclusion

A good bio says what, where, why you and what's next — in 150 characters. Put a keyword in the name field and a UTM tag on the link. After the bio, highlights and first posts carry on the work. In a free audit, Level Marketing will show you where your profile is losing customers.
