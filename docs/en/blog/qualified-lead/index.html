Qualified leads: what MQL and SQL mean and how to qualify leads

"Lots of leads, but poor quality" is the most common argument between marketing and sales. The marketer says "I brought 100 enquiries"; the salesperson says "only 5 were real customers". The problem is usually that nobody agreed in advance what a "qualified lead" is. What a lead is — what is a lead. Here we look at how to define lead quality and qualify leads.

Lead stages

StageWhoSign
LeadAnyone who left contact detailsName and phone
MQL (marketing-qualified)Meets marketing's criteriaTarget area, need, budget
SQL (sales-qualified)Confirmed by a salespersonA conversation happened; clear need and timeline
CustomerHas paidFirst payment

Each stage has its own conversion rate, so you can see where leads are lost — what is a sales funnel.

Qualification criteria

A classic set of questions (approaches like BANT):

  • Need: do they really need the product?
  • Budget: does it fit your price range?
  • Decision: who makes the decision — family purchase decisions?
  • Timing: when do they need it?
  • Area: are they within your service area?

Pick 3–4 criteria for your business and write them down.

Quality starts with the lead form

  • Add 1–2 qualifying questions: "When do you need it?", "What's your budget?" — lead form questions.
  • Don't make the form too easy, or you'll get more cheap but poor leads — the cheap leads trap.
  • Show prices or terms before the enquiry, so poor fits filter themselves out.

CRM statuses

Each lead gets a status: "new", "contacted", "qualified (SQL)", "unqualified — reason", "meeting", "paid". The reason matters: "didn't answer", "no budget", "outside our area", "mistaken enquiry" — leads don't answer the phone.

A marketing–sales agreement

A simple service-level agreement:

  • marketing delivers X MQLs a month against agreed criteria;
  • sales replies to every lead within 15–30 minutes and sets a status within 24 hours — lead response time;
  • a short weekly meeting covers reasons for unqualified leads and ad fixes — marketing and sales alignment.

Feeding quality back into ads

Send data on qualified leads and sales back to the ad platforms (Meta Conversions API, Google conversions), so the system learns to find the right people rather than the cheapest — ad leads vs CRM mismatch.

Questions and answers

What share of leads should be qualified?

There's no universal benchmark. Measure your own baseline and work to improve it.

Who's responsible for unqualified leads?

Often both: the ads may have reached the wrong audience, or the salesperson may have replied late. The reasons in the CRM will show which.

Which matters more: cost per lead or cost per qualified lead?

The cost per qualified lead and per customer — customer acquisition cost.

Conclusion

Qualified leads need criteria agreed in advance, a qualifying form, CRM statuses with reasons and an SLA between marketing and sales. Then the "leads are poor" argument turns into numbers. In a free audit, Level Marketing will show you where your leads are lost.
