Replying to "How much?": 5 steps that turn a chat into a sale

A large share of messages in DMs and on Telegram consist of one word: "Price?" Many businesses reply in one of two ways: they either just send a number and the customer goes quiet, or they hide the price — "send us a DM", "give us a call" — and the customer goes elsewhere. Either way, the money paid for the ad is lost. Here are five steps that turn "how much?" into a conversation and a sale.

Why "price?" is an opportunity

Someone who writes "how much?" is already interested: they saw the ad, visited your profile and took the time to write. This isn't a cold lead. But they leave quickly if the reply is late, unclear, or the price looks high without context.

Two common mistakes:

  • Just a number. "1,200,000 soum." — and the conversation is over. The customer doesn't know what's included or why it costs that much, so they message someone else to compare.
  • Hiding the price. "Call us to find out the price." Many people don't want to call — they wrote because writing is convenient.

5 steps

1. Reply fast

Rule one is speed. The customer may have messaged several businesses at once, and the first to reply has the edge. More in lead response time.

2. Don't hide the price — give it with context

State the price straight away, but not as a bare number:

Hello! This sofa is 4,900,000 soum. The price includes delivery across Tashkent and a 2-year warranty. (illustrative example)

If the price depends on size or configuration, give a range:

Kitchen prices depend on size and materials: usually 12 to 25 million soum. May I ask a couple of questions for an exact quote? (illustrative example)

3. Ask one clarifying question

After the price, one question — not several. It draws the customer into the conversation and helps you make a fitting offer:

  • "Which room are you shopping for?"
  • "When do you need it?"
  • "Which colour did you like best?"

Keep it simple: a 5-second answer.

4. Add value

Once the customer replies, show value that matches their need: a photo of similar work, a customer review, a short video. At this point the price shifts from feeling "expensive" to feeling "understandable".

If ready-made content exists, the salesperson sends it. On videos that answer objections — content from customer objections.

5. Offer a next step

Every conversation should end with a concrete next step:

  • "Could you visit the showroom tomorrow at 11?"
  • "When shall we send someone to measure?"
  • "Shall I place the order?"

Not an open question ("Any other questions?") but a concrete choice.

When they say "too expensive"

The most common response to a price is "too expensive" — or silence. Here:

  • don't argue: "No, it isn't" doesn't work;
  • ask what they're comparing with: "What are you comparing it to?" — often it's a product of different quality;
  • offer a cheaper option, if there is one;
  • show the difference — with proof, not words.

If the customer goes quiet, follow up once, then stop. More in "I'll think about it" follow-up.

Automation: when auto-replies help

Meta Business Suite lets you set automated replies to frequent questions — see Meta Business Suite automated responses. Auto-replies help outside working hours and in the first minutes: the customer gets a price range instantly. But an auto-reply doesn't replace a conversation — it's only the first step. A real salesperson must carry on.

Profile and ads: fewer price questions

If "price?" messages pile up and your team can't keep up, show the price earlier: in ads, highlights, posts. Questions drop, and the remaining conversations are better quality. When to show the price in ads — should you show the price in your ads.

Common mistakes

  • "Price in DM" — the customer is already in your DMs.
  • "Call us" — forcing someone who wrote to make a call.
  • Just a number, with no question or next step.
  • Five questions in one message.
  • Replying hours later.
  • Writing "price in DM" under a comment and then sending nothing.

Questions and answers

If someone asks the price in a comment, should I answer publicly?

If the price is standard, yes — it helps other readers too. If it varies, give a range and say "we've sent details by DM" — and actually send them.

If I post the price, won't competitors see it?

A competitor can get your price with one message anyway. Hiding it only loses customers.

How can I check the quality of my team's replies?

Read a few conversations each week, or message as a "mystery shopper". Measuring salespeople's conversion — sales rep conversion.

Don't templates annoy customers?

If sent word for word without a name, yes. A template is a structure; the text gets adapted to each customer.

Conclusion

"How much?" is an interested customer's first step. Reply fast, give the price with context, ask one question, add value and offer a concrete next step.
