"Advertising agency Tashkent", "marketing agency", "targeted ads price" — these searches usually come from business owners tired of running marketing themselves or wanting to grow faster. But "agency" covers very different services: some only set up targeted ads, some produce content, others handle marketing end to end. Here's what an advertising agency does, how the work is organised, how pricing is formed and how to prepare for working together.

What the process looks like

A well-run partnership usually moves in stages. At Level Marketing, for example, the process has six steps:

  1. Brief and point A — we map your business, current sales, audience and goals.
  2. Strategy — channels, offer, budget and a monthly plan (actual vs plan).
  3. Content — our videographers and designers produce Reels, ad creatives and posts.
  4. Launch — campaigns go live and cost per lead is tracked every day.
  5. Optimisation — A/B test results tell us where to move the budget.
  6. Report and point B — every month we present results in your office along with next month's plan.

How pricing is formed

An agency's fees depend on several factors:

  • the set of services: targeted ads only, or ads + content + video;
  • volume: number of channels, videos and posts per month;
  • ad budget size — separate from the agency fee and paid directly to the platforms;
  • niche complexity: medicine, finance, real estate carry extra requirements.

Payment models you'll find on the market:

ModelHow it worksWhen it suits
Fixed monthly feeOne amount for agreed servicesWhen volume is stable
Percentage of budgetA set share of the ad budgetLarge, variable budgets
HybridA fixed part + a part tied to results or budgetWhen goals are clear and measurable

Whatever the model, the contract should spell out what's included and what isn't, what reporting looks like and who holds the access. Ad accounts and assets should be in your name — giving an agency access to your Meta ad account. Agreeing KPIs — KPIs in an agency contract.

What to prepare for an agency

To get results faster:

  • numbers: monthly sales, average order, margin, where customers come from;
  • about customers: who buys, what they ask, why they say no;
  • materials: photos, videos, reviews, results of past advertising;
  • access: business portfolio, Instagram, website analytics;
  • a point person: someone who stays in touch with the agency and can make decisions;
  • the sales team: ready to reply to leads quickly.

How to judge results

  • Month one — preparation and testing; expect the system to start working.
  • Months two and three — optimisation; cost per lead and per sale stabilise. More in what to expect from marketing in 90 days.
  • Every month — a plan-vs-actual table: spend, leads, sales, cost per sale. The meeting format — the monthly marketing meeting.

Judge by sales, not cost per lead — which means working together with your CRM and sales team.

Questions and answers

Isn't an agency too expensive for a small business?

It depends on the task and budget. Start with a free audit — you'll understand what the agency proposes and whether it fits your numbers.

Does an agency guarantee results?

An honest agency won't guarantee a specific number (say, "100 customers") — results depend on the market, the offer and sales. But process, reporting and response speed can be guaranteed.

How long is the contract?

Usually several months — a marketing system takes 2–3 months to form. Discuss the terms in advance.

If I switch agencies, will I lose my ad history?

Not if the ad accounts are in your portfolio. That's why it pays to set things up correctly from the start.

Conclusion
