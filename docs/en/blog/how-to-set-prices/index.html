How to set prices for a product or service: pricing methods

Many owners set prices in one of two ways: "cost plus 30%" or "a bit cheaper than the competition". Both are easy, and both can leave money on the table. Price is the strongest profit lever there is: it changes profit even when sales volume stays the same. Here's how to set prices properly.

Three main methods

MethodHowAdvantageRisk
Cost-plusCosts + a percentageSimple, no lossIgnores what customers will pay
Competitor-basedAround the market priceFast, in line with the marketPrice wars, no differentiation
Value-basedThe value of the result to the customerHigh marginYou have to prove the value

In practice, all three are used together: cost is the floor, competitors are the context, value is the ceiling.

Step 1: calculate your true cost

Many people count only raw materials. Full cost includes:

  • the product or raw materials;
  • wages (production and sales);
  • rent, utilities, equipment;
  • advertising and customer acquisition cost — CAC;
  • delivery, packaging, commissions;
  • taxes.

Calculating per unit — unit economics.

Step 2: check the margin

An illustrative example: a product costs 100,000 soum and sells for 150,000 — a margin of 50,000 (33%). If acquiring one customer costs 40,000, net profit from the first purchase is only 10,000. So the price must also carry the ad spend — or the customer must buy again — repeat sales and LTV.

Step 3: define the value to the customer

Ask: what does your product give the customer, and what is that worth?

  • It saves time — what's the customer's time worth?
  • It reduces risk — what would a mistake cost?
  • It generates income — how much?

The more concrete the value, the higher the price can be. How to show it — unique selling proposition.

Price psychology

  • Packages (3 options): most people pick the middle — put the option you most want to sell there.
  • Anchoring: next to a premium option, the main one looks more affordable.
  • Instalments: turn a large sum into a monthly payment, following the legal requirements — installment advertising rules.
  • Precise numbers: an unrounded price can look calculated and fair.

Add value instead of discounting

Discounts eat margin fast. Instead of cutting the price, add free delivery, a guarantee, a bonus service or a faster turnaround — the discount trap.

Testing your price

Price isn't a guess; it's an experiment:

  • test a new price on new customers;
  • run different packages in different ad campaigns;
  • track conversion and margin together: a lower price may bring more sales but less profit.

When it's time to raise prices — how to raise prices.

The law

In Uzbekistan, prices in advertising must be shown in soum, and comparative claims such as "the lowest prices" must be provable — advertising law in Uzbekistan.

Common mistakes

  • Counting only raw material costs.
  • Leaving advertising out of the price.
  • "Cheaper than the competition" as a strategy.
  • A single price, with no choice.
  • Not changing prices for years while inflation eats the margin.

Questions and answers

Should I show prices in ads?

It depends on the product and audience — should you show the price in your ads.

Should a new business start with low prices?

A very low price is hard to raise later. It's better to use a standard price plus a time-limited entry offer — entry offer.

What margin is normal?

There's no universal benchmark; it depends on the industry, volume and costs. What matters is that the margin can carry advertising and growth.

Conclusion

The right price sits where cost (the floor), competitors (the context) and customer value (the ceiling) meet. Calculate full cost including advertising, work with packages and added value, and test your price.
