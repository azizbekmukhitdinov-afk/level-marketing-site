Uzbek search: Latin, Cyrillic and Russian-language queries

In Uzbekistan, the same service is searched in at least three ways: "oshxona mebeli" (Uzbek in Latin), "ошхона мебели" (Uzbek in Cyrillic) and "кухонная мебель" (Russian). There's a fourth variant too — with no apostrophe or a different one: "o'quv markazi", "oquv markazi", "o‘quv markazi". Many businesses write their website and maps listing in only one variant and lose the other searches. Here's how search in Uzbek works and how to prepare for it.

Three scripts, one market

Uzbek's official alphabet is Latin-based. But in practice:

  • Latin script — younger people, official documents, many new websites;
  • Cyrillic script — the older generation, many older websites and some media;
  • Russian — Tashkent and big cities, business and technical topics.

The same person may switch depending on the situation: Latin on a phone, Cyrillic on a computer with a Russian keyboard layout. So the assumption "my customers search in Latin" is often wrong.

The apostrophe problem

In Latin script, the letters o‘ and g‘ use an apostrophe — and there are several variants:

VariantWhere it appears
o‘, g‘ (the correct character)Official texts, quality websites
o', g' (a plain apostrophe)Searches typed on phones, social media
o` (with a backtick)Some keyboard layouts
o, g (no apostrophe)Quickly typed searches

Search engines often treat these variants as close, but not always fully. The practical takeaway: write your main site copy with the correct characters, but in key places — headings, the listing description, FAQs — naturally use the variants people actually type.

How to check demand

Check before assuming:

  1. Yandex Wordstat — query volumes for the Uzbekistan region, especially useful for Cyrillic and Russian queries.
  2. Google Keyword Planner — inside Google Ads, for Latin and Russian queries.
  3. Google autocomplete — the suggestions shown as you type reveal what people write.
  4. Your site's statistics — Google Search Console and Yandex Webmaster show the queries people found you with.
  5. Your sales team — the words customers use on the phone.

Preparing your website

Language versions

Uzbek (Latin) and Russian versions should be separate pages with separate URLs. Don't mix two languages on one page. Each page should carry hreflang tags so search engines know which version to show to whom.

Cyrillic

A separate Cyrillic version isn't always needed — it's a lot of work. But these help:

  • naturally using the Cyrillic query variant in the FAQ block of key service pages;
  • in your maps listing — in the services list and description;
  • if your audience is mostly older — seriously consider a Cyrillic version.

Headings and URLs

URLs in Latin letters, without apostrophes: /oshxona-mebeli/. Correct spelling in headings, natural variants in descriptions.

Maps listings

On Google and Yandex Maps, the listing often matters more than the website: people search for "... near me". In the listing:

  • the name in its official spelling;
  • the right category;
  • description and services in Uzbek and Russian;
  • replies to reviews in the customer's language.

Content: which language to write articles in

If your site has a blog, write in at least two languages: Uzbek usually has less competition, Russian more search volume. A useful Uzbek article often climbs faster because there's little quality competing content.

Common mistakes

  • Website and listing in one script only.
  • Mixing two languages on one page.
  • Assuming "everyone searches in Russian" without checking demand.
  • Cyrillic characters or apostrophes in URLs.
  • Machine translation — if the Uzbek copy isn't natural, both people and search engines notice.

Questions and answers

Does the website need a Cyrillic version?

Not always. If Wordstat shows significant Cyrillic queries or your audience is older, yes. Otherwise, Cyrillic variants in FAQs and your listing are enough.

Do Google and Yandex understand Uzbek equally well?

Both support Uzbek, but results may differ. Track your site's positions in both.

Which character is correct for the apostrophe?

The official correct form is ‘ (o‘, g‘). Use it on your site, but remember that people type a plain '.

When will I see results?

SEO usually takes months. For faster results, combine it with search ads — Yandex Direct, Google Ads.

Conclusion

Search in Uzbekistan means three scripts and several apostrophe variants. Check demand with Wordstat and Keyword Planner, prepare your site and listing in at least Uzbek and Russian, and use Cyrillic variants naturally in key places.
