“I’ll think about it”: a 14-day follow-up plan

"Thanks, I'll think about it" is one of the phrases sales teams hear most. For many salespeople it ends the conversation: they wait for the customer to come back. Usually the customer doesn't — not because they decided not to buy, but because they buy elsewhere or simply forget. Yet a customer who says "I'll think about it" is interested: they didn't say no. Here's how to make follow-up with these customers systematic.

What "I'll think about it" really means

There's usually another reason behind the phrase:

Real reasonHow it showsWhat's needed
PriceWent quiet after hearing the priceShow value, a cheaper option, instalments
Trust"I'll look at other options"Reviews, customer videos, a guarantee
Someone else decides"I'll check with my spouse"Material for the second person — see family purchase decisions
Timing"Not now, later"A specific date, a reminder
Didn't understandHad lots of questionsA short explanation, a meeting
No interestA polite noStop

So step one is finding the reason.

Step 1: ask for the reason at the end of the conversation

When the customer says "I'll think about it", ask one polite question:

Of course, take your time. What matters most for your decision — price, timing or something else? Maybe I can send you some information?

The answer shapes the follow-up plan. The second important thing is to agree on the next contact:

May I message you on Thursday?

If they say yes, it's no longer pushy — it's an agreed contact.

Step 2: record it in the CRM

If "I'll think about it" customers live only in a salesperson's memory, they get lost. In the CRM:

  • status: "considering";
  • the reason (from the table);
  • the next contact date, as a task;
  • what was promised: "send reviews", "will talk to spouse".

Step 3: a 14-day plan

An illustrative plan for a mid-priced purchase (furniture, a course, a clinic service):

DayContactWhat you give
0End of conversationFind the reason, agree the next contact
1Telegram/DM messageThe promised material: prices, photos, a review
3MessageValue that fits the reason: a similar customer's video or answers to questions
7Call or message"Have you decided? Any questions left?"
14Final messageA gentle close: "If you need it later, just write"

For expensive purchases (property, cars), the plan is longer — 30–60 days. For cheap ones, shorter — 3–5 days.

Add value at every touch

The most common mistake is sending the same "Have you decided?" again and again. It's irritating. Each contact should bring something new:

  • useful material: "3 questions to ask when choosing a sofa" (a short video);
  • social proof: the story of a customer in a similar situation — customer testimonial video;
  • an answer: a detailed reply to a question they asked;
  • news: a new colour arrived, the measurer is in your district tomorrow.

Ready-made content that answers objections — content from customer objections.

When to stop

Follow-up isn't harassment. Stopping rules:

  • if the customer clearly says "not needed", stop at once and thank them;
  • no reply to 2–3 messages — send a final one and stop;
  • if they say they bought elsewhere, ask why (valuable information) and stop.

Stopped customers are marked "gone cold". You can contact them months later with a new offer — that's a different process.

Supporting it with ads

Besides salespeople's messages, "considering" customers may see you on Instagram. Retargeting ads — reviews, customer videos, guarantees — build trust while they decide. More in how retargeting works.

Measuring results

Check once a month:

  • how many customers entered "considering";
  • how many of them bought after follow-up;
  • which reasons come up most;
  • which touch (which day, which material) produced the most sales.

Sales from "considering" customers after follow-up often add a noticeable extra result — without spending a single extra soum on ads. Measuring salespeople's results — sales rep conversion.

Common mistakes

  • Not asking for the reason.
  • Not agreeing on the next contact, so the later call feels "unexpected".
  • The same "Have you decided?" every time.
  • No task in the CRM — the salesperson forgets.
  • Never stopping: ten unanswered messages damage the brand.

Questions and answers

Isn't follow-up pushy?

Not if it's agreed, brings value and has a stopping rule. Many customers are grateful for the reminder.

Which channel is better: a call or a message?

Whichever channel the customer used. At a key stage (day 7), a call may work better.

Should I offer a discount?

Only if the reason is price and it's planned in advance. A discount for every "I'll think about it" trains customers to wait. More in the discount trap.

Can it be automated?

Reminders and sending materials — yes, through a CRM or a bot. But key contacts should come from a real salesperson.

Conclusion

"I'll think about it" isn't a no — it's a decision in progress. Find the reason, agree the next contact, record it in the CRM, add value at every touch and stop in time. It's one of the cheapest ways to grow sales without growing the ad budget.
