Instagram partnership ads: running creator content as ads

A blogger made a great Reel about your product, it worked for 2–3 days and then sank in the feed. Instagram partnership ads let you relaunch that video from your own ad account, to an audience you choose and with your own budget. This article covers what the format is, how to get the creator's permission, how to set it up in Ads Manager and where businesses usually go wrong.

What partnership ads are

A partnership ad is an ad run from your ad account that appears not just under your brand, but together with a creator or partner account. Both accounts show in the ad header, with a "Paid partnership" label on top. Meta's developer documentation describes the format as ads you run with creators, brands and other businesses (Meta for Developers).

The difference from an ordinary paid post by a blogger is simple: you stay in control.

Post on the blogger's profilePartnership ad
Who sees itThe blogger's followersThe audience you pick in Ads Manager
How long it runsUntil the feed moves onAs long as your budget and dates allow
ButtonUsually none"Send message", lead form, website
ReportingScreenshots from the bloggerIn Ads Manager, down to leads
Who paysYou pay the bloggerYou pay the blogger + Meta for delivery

The format used to be called branded content ads before Meta renamed it partnership ads. Interface labels change from time to time, but the logic stays the same.

Why it matters for businesses in Uzbekistan

Many businesses in Tashkent work with bloggers but judge results only by views. We cover choosing a blogger and measuring results separately: Influencer advertising. Partnership ads are the next step: the money you paid the blogger doesn't end with a single post.

The format is especially useful in three cases:

  • The blogger's post performed well — lots of comments, questions in DMs. Running that content as an ad is cheaper than shooting a new creative.
  • Your brand is still new — people don't know you yet, but they trust the blogger. Their name in the ad header adds a familiar face.
  • Retargeting — showing the blogger's video to people who visited your site or messaged you but didn't buy. How it works: Retargeting.

A Meta report published on October 7, 2026 cites 20 studies of advertisers in Europe, the Middle East and Africa: in campaigns where at least 30% of creatives were partnership ads, the median cost per incremental purchase was 45% lower (PPC Land). That's Meta's own data from other markets, so treat it as a reason to test, not a guarantee.

What you need for Instagram partnership ads

Check four things before you start:

  1. Professional accounts on both sides. Both the blogger and your brand need a professional (business or creator) Instagram account — the basic requirement in Meta's documentation.
  2. Instagram connected to your ad account. The brand account is linked to a Facebook Page and Ads Manager.
  3. A post with the "Paid partnership" label. The blogger tags you as a partner when publishing. It's both a Meta requirement and an honest way to show the post is an ad.
  4. Permission. The blogger allows you to use their content in ads. Without it, the ad won't deliver.

There are two kinds of permission (Meta for Developers):

Permission typeHow it worksBest for
Account levelThe blogger approves your brand once, then you can promote their posts without separate permissionLong-term partnerships, ambassadors
Post levelThe blogger sends you an ad code for one postOne-off integrations

A handy detail: if both accounts belong to the same business (say, the company account and the founder's personal account) and an employee has ad access to both, no separate partnership permission is needed. That's convenient when you use the founder's personal brand in company ads.

How to set up partnership ads: 6 steps

Step 1. Put ad usage rights in the contract

Before paying, agree with the blogger: will the post be used in ads, for how long (for example, 30 or 90 days), in which formats, and is there an extra fee. Many bloggers price an organic post and ad usage separately. A verbal agreement tends to end in a dispute.

Step 2. The blogger publishes with the "Paid partnership" label

When posting a feed post or Reel, the blogger selects your account as a partner under "Partnership label & ads". The same section has a toggle that allows ads. Uzbek law also requires advertising to be clearly identified — see Advertising law in Uzbekistan.

Step 3. Get an ad code or account-level permission

For post-level permission, the blogger turns on "Get partnership ad code" in the post settings and sends you the code. For account-level permission, you send a request from Ads Manager and the blogger approves it. According to Meta's documentation, an ad published without permission stays pending until the creator approves.

Step 4. Build the campaign in Ads Manager

Pick the objective that matches your business goal: messages, lead form or website traffic. At the ad level, turn on partnership ads, choose the blogger's account and add the existing post with the ad code. Meta's documentation lists messaging and lead generation among the supported capabilities, so a blogger's video can bring leads directly. Choosing between messages and a lead form: Lead form or messages.

Step 5. Compare against a regular creative

Put two ads in one ad set: a regular video from your brand and a partnership ad with the blogger. Same audience, same budget. After 5–7 days, compare cost per lead. How to run the test properly: A/B testing ad creatives.

Step 6. Measure all the way to leads

Views and likes matter to the blogger; what matters to you is cost per lead and sales. Partnership ads report in Ads Manager just like any other ad, so compare them with your other creatives in one table.

Illustrative example. A furniture store paid a blogger for a Reel. Organically, the post brought a handful of questions in 2 days. The store runs the same video for three weeks as a partnership ad to retargeting and lookalike audiences and compares its cost per lead weekly with brand-account videos. Whichever is cheaper gets more of the budget.

What creator content works well as an ad

Not every creator post makes a good ad. When choosing, look for:

  • The product or the problem in the first 3 seconds. Long greetings and "hi everyone, today I…" get skipped in ads. More on strong openings: Reels hooks.
  • The product actually in use. Not just held up, but used: the blogger sits on the sofa, wears the item, goes through a clinic consultation.
  • No price or promo dates baked into the video. Otherwise you can't run it once the promo ends.
  • Organic reaction. Questions in the comments and saves show the audience was interested.
  • Vertical format and subtitles. Many people watch with the sound off.

Creator content sits close to customer videos (UGC): both give the "a regular person is talking" effect. More on UGC: UGC content.

5 common mistakes

  1. Not agreeing on rights up front. Asking "can we use it in ads?" after the post is live — the blogger may refuse or quote a new price.
  2. Choosing bloggers by follower count alone. In ads, it's their content that works, not their audience. A small but lively blogger can produce a strong creative.
  3. Putting the whole budget on one creator video. Creatives fatigue quickly. Have several videos and mix them with regular creatives.
  4. Measuring only reach. Cheap impressions aren't leads. Track cost per lead and sales in your CRM.
  5. Removing or hiding the ad label. That breaks both Meta's rules and the advertising law. Also, a person's name and image can't be used in advertising without their consent — so get a written agreement.

Checklist before launching a partnership ad

  • The contract covers ad usage rights, duration and price
  • Both the blogger and the brand have a professional Instagram account
  • The post is published with the "Paid partnership" label
  • You have an ad code or approved account-level permission
  • The campaign objective matches the business goal (messages, lead form, website)
  • A test is set up against a regular creative
  • Leads land in the CRM with the source marked
  • The video shows the product in the first 3 seconds and has subtitles

Questions and answers

Do I need to pay the blogger extra for a partnership ad? Meta doesn't charge the blogger anything — you pay Meta for delivery. But the blogger may price ad usage of their content separately. Put it in the contract.

What happens if the blogger revokes permission? The blogger can turn off the ad code, and then no new ads can be created with it. That's why terms and duration should be agreed in advance.

Can I promote an old post? Yes, if the blogger grants permission for that post. According to Meta's documentation, a code can be created for archived content too. Just make sure it doesn't show an old price or an expired promotion.

Are partnership ads only on Instagram? No, the format works on Facebook as well. In 2026 Meta announced it was extending the format to new placements, but for businesses in Uzbekistan the main places are still the Instagram feed, Reels and Stories.

Does a small business need this format? If you already pay bloggers — yes, it's the simplest way to get more out of that money. If you don't work with bloggers yet, start with one or two integrations.

Conclusion

Instagram partnership ads turn a creator's post from a one-off mention into a working creative: you choose the audience, budget and button, and see results in Ads Manager all the way to leads. The key is to agree on rights up front, publish with the "Paid partnership" label and compare the creator's video honestly against your regular creatives.
