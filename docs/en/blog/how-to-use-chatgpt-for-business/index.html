How to use ChatGPT for business: a starter guide for owners

Everyone has heard of ChatGPT, many have tried it once or twice — and closed it thinking "interesting, but useless for business". Usually the problem isn't the tool but the prompt: a one-line "write me an ad" gets a generic answer that fits nobody. Here's how to use ChatGPT in business, how to structure a prompt and where to stop.

What ChatGPT can and can't do

ChatGPT is an AI assistant that works with text. It answers questions, writes, shortens, translates, suggests ideas and analyses tables. But it:

  • doesn't know your business, customers or numbers — until you tell it;
  • can be confidently wrong about facts;
  • may give outdated information about Uzbekistan's laws and market prices.

So ChatGPT is a fast-writing assistant, not an expert. What AI is in general — what is artificial intelligence.

The 5 parts of a good prompt

Answer quality depends on prompt quality. A good prompt has five parts:

PartWhat to writeExample (illustrative: a furniture store)
RoleWho should answer"You're the marketer for a furniture store in Tashkent"
ContextBusiness, customer, situation"Custom kitchens, 12–25 million soum, customers are young families"
TaskWhat exactly you need"Write 5 Instagram post ideas"
FormatWhat it should look like"A table: headline, main idea, call to action"
ConstraintsWhat to avoid"No discounts or 'best', in Uzbek, Latin script"

The first answer won't be perfect — that's normal. Keep the conversation going: "expand idea 2", "make it shorter", "add a customer objection".

Working in Uzbek

ChatGPT understands and writes Uzbek, but makes more mistakes than in English or Russian: a translated tone, wrong words, confusion with o‘ and g‘. Practical tips:

  • say clearly in the prompt "in Uzbek, Latin script, simple everyday language";
  • give an example of good Uzbek copy and ask for "this style";
  • have a native speaker proofread every Uzbek text.

What never goes in

This is the most important rule. Never enter into ChatGPT:

  • customers' names, phone numbers, addresses or chats;
  • passwords, card details, access keys;
  • complete contracts and financial statements;
  • employees' personal data.

In Uzbekistan, Law No. ZRU-1115 of 21 January 2026 introduced a specific fine for unlawfully processing personal data using AI (lex.uz). If you need analysis, anonymise the data: no names or numbers. More in AI and the law in Uzbekistan.

Checking the output: 4 questions

Before using a ChatGPT answer:

  1. Are the facts right? Check every number, law and price against the primary source.
  2. Is it about us? Does it include your offer and your customer, not generic statements?
  3. Is the language natural? Does it read like a translation?
  4. Is it legal? No "the best", dollar prices or unprovable numbers in ads? More in advertising law in Uzbekistan.

Common mistakes

  • A one-line prompt and publishing the first answer.
  • Trusting ChatGPT's laws and statistics.
  • Uploading the customer base for analysis.
  • Writing every post with the same prompt — the page becomes monotonous.
  • Putting AI copy into ads without reading it.

Questions and answers

Is ChatGPT free?

There's a free version; paid plans offer more. Prices and terms change often — check the official site.

Does ChatGPT work in Uzbekistan?

Yes, many people use it. Check access and payment terms on the official site.

Will ChatGPT replace marketers?

No. It provides drafts and speed; strategy, testing and analysing results by sales are human work. More in AI in marketing for small business.

Which task should I start with?

The one that takes the most time and carries the least risk: reply templates or post ideas, for example.

Conclusion

The secret to ChatGPT is a good prompt: role, context, task, format and constraints. It speeds up drafts, ideas and templates, but facts, language and customer data must stay under human control.
