From consultation to sale: halve the cost of a customer without adding ad spend

In many businesses a sale happens in two steps, not one: first a consultation, visit, trial lesson or test drive, then the purchase. Most marketing aims at the first step — getting people to the consultation. The second step often gets little attention: "they came, they talked, they said they'd think about it". Yet this is exactly where you can halve the cost of a customer without adding a single so‘m to ads. This article shows how to raise conversion from consultation to sale.

Why this is the cheapest lever

The cost of one customer works out like this:

Cost per customer = cost of one consultation ÷ consultation-to-purchase conversion

If a consultation costs 100,000 so‘m and every 6th person buys, a customer costs 600,000 so‘m. If every 3rd buys, a customer costs 300,000 so‘m. The ads didn't change, the budget didn't change — the customer got twice as cheap. (Illustrative example.)

Why people leave after a consultation

  • The next step isn't clear. The consultation ends with "great, have a think".
  • Price and plan are vague. The person doesn't know exactly what they'll pay and what they'll get.
  • The decision-maker isn't there. They need to consult a spouse, parents or a boss, and have nothing to show them.
  • Fears go unanswered. "Will it hurt?", "What if it doesn't work?", "Can I pay in instalments?" — unspoken, but very real.
  • No follow-up. A week goes by and they go somewhere else.

Structuring the consultation: 6 steps

1. Prepare

Before the consultation, read the lead details: what the person asked, which ad they came from, what was promised. Asking everything again from scratch erodes trust.

2. Diagnose

Questions first: what's the problem, for how long, what have they tried, what result do they expect. Once people have described their situation, they're more receptive to the offer.

3. A concrete plan

Not "you need [service]", but a step-by-step plan: what will be done, how long it takes, what result to expect. In a clinic — a treatment plan; in an education centre — the course and level; in real estate — 2–3 suitable options.

4. Price and options

State the price openly. Where possible, give 2–3 options: standard, extended, minimal. Options turn "should I buy?" into "which one?". If instalments are available, say so straight away while following advertising rules — installment advertising rules.

5. Answer the fears

Write down the 3–5 most common objections in advance and prepare an answer, proof or example for each — sales script.

6. Book the next step on the spot

End the consultation with a date and time: the first session, first lesson, contract signing, a second viewing. If the person wants to think, agree when you'll call.

Something to take home

The person will go home and talk it over with family or a partner. Give them something to show:

  • a written plan or quote;
  • the price and options;
  • answers to common questions;
  • the specialist's or manager's contact details.

You can send it on Telegram as a PDF or image — it will travel through the family chat.

What to measure

  • the share of booked consultations that actually show up;
  • consultation-to-purchase conversion — separately for each specialist and manager;
  • reasons for declining;
  • average time from consultation to purchase.

Conversion often differs several-fold between specialists. Record the consultations of whoever gets the best results and use them as a model for the rest.

Across industries

  • Clinic: consultation → treatment plan → first session — marketing a clinic.
  • Education centre: trial lesson → level test → group enrolment — marketing an education centre.
  • Real estate: office visit → property viewing → reservation.
  • Car dealership: test drive → financing calculation → contract.

Common mistakes

  • Judging marketing only by the number of consultations.
  • Ending consultations without a price or a concrete plan.
  • Ignoring the decision-maker.
  • Not following up, or stopping at a single call.
  • Not comparing specialists' results.

Questions and answers

Should the consultation be free?

A free consultation brings more people — but more "just curious" people too. A paid one lowers attendance, but conversion to purchase is usually higher. Test both and compare them by cost per customer.

What if the specialist doesn't want to sell?

Their job isn't to sell but to explain the plan and price clearly. A manager can take over the sales part.

What about people who don't show up?

Send reminders (the day before and 2 hours before), and offer no-shows a new time. Measure the no-show rate separately too.

How soon will results show?

Once the structure and follow-up are in place, changes usually appear within a few weeks — they work immediately, without waiting on ads.

Conclusion

Consultation-to-purchase conversion is the cheapest lever for lowering customer cost without raising the ad budget. A concrete plan, an open price, options, answers to fears, a next step booked on the spot and follow-up within 24 hours — all of it happens inside the business.
