Video ad length: 6, 15, 30 or 60 seconds?

"Keep it short — nobody watches long videos" and "you can't explain anything in a short video" — both are said often, and both are partly true. Video ad length isn't a matter of taste; it depends on the video's job: grabbing attention, explaining or persuading. Here's what videos of different lengths are for and how to choose yours.

What Meta recommends

On its video ads page, Meta says ads can run from 5 seconds to 10 minutes, but it generally recommends a maximum of 15 seconds (Meta for Business). Meta's guides add another tip: show the key message or brand within the first 3 seconds.

That recommendation describes the average case. In practice, 15 seconds is a starting point, not a rule: short videos get watched to the end by more people, but not everything can be said in 15 seconds.

Short videos: when they work

Short videos (up to 15 seconds) work when:

  • the product explains itself — clothing, food, accessories: clear at a glance;
  • the offer is simple — a discount, a new product, a price;
  • the audience already knows you — reminders in retargeting;
  • it's an impulse purchase — no long thinking needed.

The main mistake with short videos is trying to fit everything in. Fifteen seconds, one idea. If you have three advantages, make three separate videos.

Long videos: when you need them

Longer videos (over 30 seconds) are needed when:

  • the product needs explaining — services, tech, education, medicine;
  • the purchase is expensive — property, furniture, cars: people want more information before deciding;
  • trust is required — when a customer or expert speaks, it's hard to build trust in 15 seconds.

In a long video, length isn't the problem — boredom is. Something should change every 3–5 seconds: the shot, the angle, on-screen text, a new idea. And the first 3 seconds are still the hook. Hooks — Reels hooks.

Different lengths from one shoot

You don't need a separate shoot for each length. On one shoot day, plan the material like this:

  1. The main long version (45–60 seconds) — the full story.
  2. A 15-second cut — the strongest hook and offer.
  3. 6–10 second cuts — each with one idea or one shot.

Measuring results

When comparing lengths, don't look at views alone. The metrics that matter:

  • share of 3-second views — is the hook working;
  • ThruPlay — in Meta, a view of at least 15 seconds (or nearly to the end if the video is shorter) (Meta Business Help);
  • average watch time — where people drop off;
  • cost per lead and per sale — what matters most in the end.

A common pattern: short videos bring cheap views and more reach, while long videos bring fewer but higher-quality leads. So judge by sales.

Testing length

  1. Two or three lengths from the same material, e.g. 15 and 45 seconds.
  2. Same audience, same offer, same budget.
  3. At least 7–10 days.
  4. Compare cost per lead and lead-to-sale rate.

Testing technique — A/B testing ad creatives.

Common mistakes

  • Trimming a 60-second video to 15 without re-editing — the start and end get cut, losing the hook and the call to action.
  • Cramming everything into a short video.
  • Opening a long video with a 5-second intro: logo, "hi", "today we'll talk about".
  • Using only short videos because "everyone says so" — trust suffers for expensive purchases.
  • Judging length by view count.

Questions and answers

What's the best length for Reels ads?

To start, up to 15 seconds — Meta's general recommendation. But for services and expensive products, test a 30–45 second version too.

Do people watch long videos?

If they're interesting and relevant to their problem, yes. What matters is holding interest every second, not the length.

How many lengths per ad?

At least two: a short one for cold audiences and a longer one for warm audiences and expensive purchases.

Does length differ for Stories and Reels?

People tap through Stories faster, so shorter videos with a quick start work better. Check the format for each placement.

Conclusion

The right video ad length is the one that fits the job. Short for attention and reminders, medium for the offer, long for explanation and trust.
