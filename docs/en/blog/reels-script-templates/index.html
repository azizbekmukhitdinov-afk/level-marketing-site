Reels scripts: 5 ready-made templates

Most Reels are filmed without a script — "I'll just say something" — and that's why nobody finishes them. A script makes a video short, clear and persuasive. Here is the structure of a Reel that sells, plus 5 templates you can adapt to any business.

The structure of a Reel that sells

  1. Hook (1–3 seconds) — grab attention. Types of hooks are in our hooks article.
  2. The main part — a benefit, a story or a process.
  3. Proof — a result, a number or a review.
  4. Call to action — "DM us 'price'", "Save this".

One video, one idea. For a 30–45 second video, roughly 60–120 words is enough.

5 templates

1. Problem → solution → result

"Has your sofa sagged within a year? (hook) The cause is a cheap frame and foam. We use birch and high-density foam (solution). Our customer Dilshod has been using his sofa for 5 years (result). DM us for a price in your size."

2. "3 mistakes"

"3 mistakes people make preparing for IELTS. First… Second… Third… Save this and send it to a friend."

3. Before / after + process

"This kitchen looked like this after 3 days. Day 1 — removal, day 2 — installation, day 3 — the result. Message us to price your project."

4. Myth vs fact

"Myth: teeth whitening damages enamel. Fact: a properly performed procedure under a dentist's supervision… Book a consultation."

5. A customer's question

"A customer asked: 'Can I pay in instalments?' Answer: yes, up to 12 months with no down payment. Terms are in the 'Prices' highlight."

Script-writing rules

  • Write the way you speak — conversational, short sentences.
  • On-screen text: repeat the main point in large type.
  • Don't open with a greeting — get straight to the point.
  • Ask for one specific action at the end.

Connecting templates to your plan

Use each template several times a month on different topics, following the content types in our content plan article. For filming technique, see shooting Reels on a phone.

3 more templates

6. Behind the scenes

"How one order gets made — in 30 seconds. Receiving… preparing… checking… packing. Every single order. To place one, send us a DM."

7. What the price is made of

"Why isn't our [service] cheap? Because the price includes [component 1], [component 2] and [component 3]. Cheaper options usually cut corners on exactly these. Message us for a detailed breakdown."

8. Comparison

"Two [products]: one cheaper, one more expensive. The difference is [detail]. Here's how it looks after a year… Let's pick the one that suits you."

Checking a script: 5 questions

  1. Does the first line work without sound — is there text on screen?
  2. Is there only one idea in the video?
  3. Is there proof: a result, a number, a review or the process?
  4. Is there one clear call to action?
  5. Can the text be read calmly in 45 seconds?

Common mistakes

  • A long intro: "Hi, my name is…, today I'm going to…".
  • Three or four ideas in one video.
  • Jargon the customer doesn't understand.
  • Several calls to action: "follow, message, call".
  • The video stops abruptly, with no ending.

Questions and answers

Should you memorise the script?

No. Memorise the key points and the first line, and say the rest in your own words. You can record a long text sentence by sentence or use a teleprompter.

How many times can you reuse a template?

Keep reusing a template that works with new topics. A video that performed well can be reshot with a new hook — often that works better than a completely new idea.

Who should write the script?

The expert knows the content; an SMM manager or copywriter shapes it. A convenient method: the expert sends the idea as a voice message, and an editor turns it into a script. For formats, see Types of video ads.

Do Stories and live streams need a script too?

A full text isn't necessary — 3–4 key points and an ending are enough. Reels are short, though, so even a script in the form of a simple list improves results noticeably.

Where should you keep your scripts?

In one shared spreadsheet or document: topic, template number, hook, text, status (idea, written, shot, published) and result. Then you can see at a glance which templates and hooks worked best, and plan the next month faster.

Conclusion

A script is the skeleton of a Reel: hook, value, proof and a call to action. Rotate the five templates, compare results and repeat the formats that work best.
