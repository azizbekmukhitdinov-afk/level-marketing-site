Hiring a salesperson: the job ad, interview and first month

The ads are working and leads are coming in — but there are no sales. Often the problem isn't the leads but the person handling them. A good salesperson can turn the same leads into several times more sales — sales rep conversion. That makes hiring a salesperson a marketing decision. Here's how to get it right.

First: who exactly do you need?

"We need a salesperson" is too broad. Define:

QuestionOptions
Which leads will they handle?Inbound (ads, calls) or outbound (finding prospects themselves)
How long is the sales cycle?One call or several meetings
Where will they work?Phone, Telegram, office, client visits
Who will they talk to?Ordinary buyers or company directors (B2B)

Someone who handles inbound leads fast and someone who manages a months-long B2B deal are completely different people.

The job ad

A good job ad attracts the right people and filters out the wrong ones:

  • A concrete task: "Handling 30–50 inbound leads a day via Telegram and phone" (illustrative example).
  • The pay system: base + commission, how it's calculated, with an approximate earnings range.
  • Conditions: schedule, location, CRM, training.
  • Who it doesn't suit: "If 40 calls a day sounds hard, this job isn't for you."

The interview: a practical test, not a chat

Salespeople are good at selling themselves, so everyone looks good in an interview. Run a practical test instead:

  1. A role-play: you're the client, they're the salesperson. Your real product, real objections: "It's expensive", "I'll think about it".
  2. A written test: replying to a customer on Telegram — speed and literacy.
  3. A call review: play them a real call — what would they improve?
  4. Questions: what was your monthly target at your last job, and how much did you hit? Which CRM did you use?

Handling objections — replying to "how much?" and the "I'll think about it" follow-up.

The pay system

  • Commission only — experienced people won't apply, and newcomers leave within two months.
  • Base only — little motivation to sell.
  • Base + commission + bonus — the most common balance: the base gives stability, while commission and a target bonus focus on results.

Important: calculate commission when payment is received, not when the deal is "agreed".

The first 30 days

Don't put a new salesperson on live leads on day one:

  • Week 1: the product, prices, competitors, the CRM and the script — sales script.
  • Week 2: listening to an experienced colleague's calls, then first calls under supervision.
  • Weeks 3–4: independent work with daily call reviews.
  • Day 30: a decision based on results — stay, more training, or part ways.

Control and measurement

  • Every lead in the CRM, with its status — CRM for small business.
  • Calls are recorded and reviewed weekly.
  • Key metrics: response time, lead-to-meeting and meeting-to-payment conversion — what is conversion rate.

Common mistakes

  • Hiring a friend or relative because they're "trustworthy".
  • Deciding on interview impressions alone.
  • Putting new hires on leads without training.
  • No CRM, so nobody knows who did what.
  • All leads going to one salesperson — if they leave, sales stop.

Questions and answers

Experienced or new?

Experienced people get results faster but bring old habits. Newcomers are cheaper and easier to train but need time. With a strong script and training, a newcomer can do very well.

How many salespeople do I need?

Look at lead volume: each salesperson must be able to answer every lead quickly and properly — lead response time.

If a salesperson leaves, do the clients leave too?

Not if all communication happens in the CRM and on company numbers.

Conclusion

Hiring a salesperson is a decision that directly affects your marketing results. A clear profile, an honest job ad, a practical test, balanced pay and a structured first month beat a random choice by a long way.
