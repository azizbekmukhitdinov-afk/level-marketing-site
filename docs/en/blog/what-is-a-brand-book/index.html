What is a brand book? A minimal brand book for small businesses

Every new designer places the logo differently, the SMM manager picks a new colour each month, the shop sign looks one way, Instagram another and the packaging a third — and nobody recognises the brand. A brand book solves this: a single document with the rules for using your brand. Here's what a brand book is and what minimal version is enough for a small business.

A simple definition

A brand book is a set of visual and verbal rules for a brand: how the logo is used, which colours and fonts, what kind of photos, what tone of voice. It exists so every new employee, designer or agency presents the brand the same way.

Brand vs logo — what is a brand. Visual identity basics — visual identity for small business.

Brand book vs guidelines

  • A brand book is a broad document: the brand idea, values, audience, tone of voice and visual rules.
  • Guidelines are mostly visual rules: logo, colours, fonts, templates.

For a small business, the distinction doesn't matter — just gather the essentials of both into one short document.

A minimal brand book for a small business: 10 pages

#SectionWhat goes in
1About the brandFor whom, what, what's different — 3–5 sentences
2LogoMain and alternative versions, minimum size, clear space
3Logo don'tsStretching, recolouring, shadows, busy backgrounds
4ColoursCodes for main and secondary colours (HEX, RGB, CMYK)
5FontsFor headings and body text, with Latin and Cyrillic support
6Photo styleWhat kind of photos: light, people, backgrounds — and what not
7Tone of voiceInformal or formal address, friendly or official, banned words
8Social mediaTemplates for posts, Stories, Reels covers
9OfflineBusiness cards, signage, packaging, uniforms
10FilesWhere all the files are kept

The most important parts

Logo rules

The logo should be vector, in several versions (colour, white, black). Specify a minimum size and clear space — otherwise the logo looks different everywhere. On AI logos — AI logo.

Colours

Two or three main colours are enough. Record each colour's code — not "blue" but an exact code. Remember that print and screen colours differ.

Fonts

An important detail for Uzbek: the font must display o‘, g‘ and the apostrophe correctly, and support Cyrillic for Russian. A beautiful Latin font often doesn't work in Cyrillic.

Tone of voice

How the brand speaks: a DM reply, an Instagram post and an ad should sound alike. Write it with examples: "we write like this / not like this".

Who prepares the brand book

  • A designer or agency — the logo and visual part.
  • You — the brand story, audience and tone: nobody knows them better than you.

When you need a brand book

  • when working with a new designer or agency;
  • when opening a branch or franchise — marketing a second branch;
  • during a rebrand — when to rebrand;
  • before ordering packaging and signage.

Common mistakes

  • An 80-page beautiful PDF nobody reads.
  • Colours without codes.
  • Fonts that don't support Cyrillic or Uzbek letters.
  • Files on one person's computer — if they leave, everything is lost.
  • A brand book that exists but nobody follows.

Questions and answers

Does a small business need a brand book?

Yes, but a short one: 5–10 pages. The main goal is for everyone to present the brand the same way.

How much does a brand book cost?

It depends on scope and who makes it: guidelines alone are cheaper; with a full brand strategy, more expensive.

Can I write the brand book myself?

The text part, yes. You'll need a designer for the logo and templates.

How often should it be updated?

The core rarely changes for years. Add templates as new formats appear.

Conclusion

A brand book is the set of rules for using your brand consistently: logo, colours, fonts, photo style, tone of voice and templates. Ten pages are enough for a small business — what matters is that everyone follows it and the files are in one place.
