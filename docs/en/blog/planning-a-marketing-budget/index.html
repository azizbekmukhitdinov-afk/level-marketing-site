How to plan a marketing budget: share of revenue vs goal-based

"How much should we spend on marketing?" is one of the questions business owners ask most. There's no universal number, but there are two sound ways to plan a budget. Here are both, along with the cost structure, test and scale periods and a sample monthly table.

Method 1: a share of revenue

The budget is set as a share of revenue or planned revenue. The exact percentage depends on your industry, margin and growth goals: a new business aiming to grow fast usually allocates a larger share than an established company.

  • Pro: simple and safe — the budget never exceeds income.
  • Con: it isn't tied to a goal, and a new business has no revenue yet.

Method 2: working back from the goal

Work backwards from the number of sales you need.

An example (illustrative numbers):

  1. The goal is 30 sales a month.
  2. Lead-to-sale conversion is 10% → you need 300 leads.
  3. Cost per lead (CPL) is $2 → ad budget = 300 × 2 = $600.
  4. Add specialist fees and content on top.

Then check: does the gross profit from 30 sales exceed all marketing costs? That's the ROMI calculation. If you don't know your CPL yet, you'll find it during the test period: see our CPL guide.

Test and scale periods

  • Test (first 1–2 months): a smaller budget, several audiences and creatives. The goal is to find combinations that work and your real cost per lead.
  • Scale: increase the budget on winning campaigns step by step. Sharp jumps can make results unstable.

Account for seasonality

Demand isn't flat through the year: education centres peak before the academic year, many shops before holidays. Plan the budget month by month and raise it ahead of the peak.

Rules for reallocating the budget

At the end of each month, compare plan against actual and move money by rule:

  1. Cut the budget in channels where cost per lead or per sale is above target.
  2. Add to channels where the cost is below target and there's room to grow.
  3. Always keep a small reserve for testing new ideas.
  4. Don't switch a channel off after one bad week — look at at least a month of results.

Hidden costs

What often gets left out of the plan:

  • payment fees and taxes: card fees, currency conversion, taxes added by the platform;
  • content reshoots and extra design;
  • subscriptions to services and tools;
  • staff time: replying to leads, taking part in shoots;
  • promotions and discounts — these are marketing costs too.

Leave them out and your ROMI will look better than it really is.

Common mistakes

  • No budget for content — ads keep running on old creatives.
  • Spending the whole monthly budget in the first week.
  • No reserve for tests.
  • Scaling a working channel too quickly.
  • Stopping marketing completely in off-season months.

Budget control: a weekly routine

Don't wait for the end of the month — check the budget for 15 minutes every week:

  • Spending pace: does weekly spend match the monthly plan, or will the money run out early?
  • Cost per lead by channel: which channel has gone over its target cost?
  • Lead quality: how does the sales team rate the leads — are there more "cold" ones?
  • An alert rule: if a channel's cost per lead stays well above target for two weeks in a row, review it.

One responsible person and one spreadsheet are enough for this check.

Questions and answers

How much should a small business spend on marketing?

There's no universal figure. Use the goal-based method and start with a small test: once you know your real cost per lead, the budget becomes clear.

Should you spend the whole budget at the start of the month?

No. Spread it evenly with a daily budget: ad systems optimise better when spending is steady. Keep a small reserve for unexpected opportunities at the end of the month.

Does an agency's or specialist's fee count as part of the budget?

Yes. It's part of your marketing costs, and it must be included when you calculate ROMI.

If money is tight, which channel should you keep?

The one with the lowest cost per sale, plus channels that capture existing demand — search and maps. Cut from the weakest channels first.

How do you plan an annual budget?

Month by month, taking seasonality into account. Review the plan against actuals every quarter — a plan drawn up at the start of the year never survives unchanged.

How do you know it's time to raise the budget?

When cost per lead has stayed below target for 2–3 weeks, the sales team can handle more leads and ROMI is positive. Raise the budget in stages and check the results after each step.

Conclusion

Combine both methods: calculate from the goal and sanity-check against a share of revenue. Update the plan every month based on your advertising report. If you'd like help calculating a goal-based budget, get in touch with Level Marketing.
