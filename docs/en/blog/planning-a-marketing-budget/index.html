How to plan a marketing budget: share of revenue vs goal-based

"How much should we spend on marketing?" is one of the questions business owners ask most. There's no universal number, but there are two sound ways to plan a budget. Here are both, along with the cost structure, test and scale periods and a sample monthly table.

Method 1: a share of revenue

The budget is set as a share of revenue or planned revenue. The exact percentage depends on your industry, margin and growth goals: a new business aiming to grow fast usually allocates a larger share than an established company.

  • Pro: simple and safe — the budget never exceeds income.
  • Con: it isn't tied to a goal, and a new business has no revenue yet.

Method 2: working back from the goal

Work backwards from the number of sales you need.

An example (illustrative numbers):

  1. The goal is 30 sales a month.
  2. Lead-to-sale conversion is 10% → you need 300 leads.
  3. Cost per lead (CPL) is $2 → ad budget = 300 × 2 = $600.
  4. Add specialist fees and content on top.

Then check: does the gross profit from 30 sales exceed all marketing costs? That's the ROMI calculation. If you don't know your CPL yet, you'll find it during the test period: see our CPL guide.

Test and scale periods

  • Test (first 1–2 months): a smaller budget, several audiences and creatives. The goal is to find combinations that work and your real cost per lead.
  • Scale: increase the budget on winning campaigns step by step. Sharp jumps can make results unstable.

Account for seasonality

Demand isn't flat through the year: education centres peak before the academic year, many shops before holidays. Plan the budget month by month and raise it ahead of the peak.

Conclusion

Combine both methods: calculate from the goal and sanity-check against a share of revenue. Update the plan every month based on your advertising report. If you'd like help calculating a goal-based budget, get in touch with Level Marketing.
