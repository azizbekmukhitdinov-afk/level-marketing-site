Competitive advantage: why customers choose you

"High quality, low prices, great service" — nearly every business says it, so customers stop hearing it. A competitive advantage is a difference that matters to customers and that competitors can't easily copy. Here's how to find it and show it.

Porter's three strategies

In his 1985 book Competitive Advantage, Michael Porter set out three main paths:

  1. Cost leadership — the lowest costs, and therefore low prices.
  2. Differentiation — unique value customers will pay extra for.
  3. Focus — serving a narrow segment (cheaply or uniquely).

For a small business, the most realistic path is usually focus plus differentiation: being the best not for everyone, but for one specific group.

8 sources for small businesses

  • Speed — "delivered in 2 hours", "installed today".
  • Specialisation — just one niche or problem.
  • Guarantees — of results, or your money back.
  • Location — the nearest or the most convenient.
  • Specialist expertise — a well-known person — personal brand: where to start.
  • Service — fast replies, convenient payment, aftercare — customer service standards.
  • Process — your own method or technology.
  • Customer relationships — your base, loyalty, referrals — loyalty programs.

A test for a real advantage

Check each option with three questions:

  1. Does it matter to customers? Ask them; don't guess — marketing research.
  2. Do competitors have it? If everyone does, it's not an advantage but a minimum requirement — competitor analysis.
  3. Can you prove it? With a number, a guarantee, reviews or a process.

If the answer to all three is yes, you've found your advantage.

Showing it in ads (illustrative example)

Weak: "Quality air conditioner installation." Strong: "We install your air conditioner on the day you order — if we're late, installation is free."

An advantage should be short, specific and proven — unique selling proposition. Be careful with comparisons: disparaging competitors is prohibited under advertising law — advertising law in Uzbekistan.

Keeping your advantage

  • Advantages get copied over time, so keep renewing yours.
  • Your team and processes must deliver on the promise.
  • Turn the advantage into your brand positioning — brand positioning.

Common mistakes

  • "Quality and price" — everyone says it, nobody believes it.
  • Boasting about things customers don't care about.
  • Competing on price alone — your margin disappears.
  • Promises you can't keep.

Questions and answers

Can price be an advantage?

Yes, if your costs really are lower. Otherwise a price war will wipe out your margin.

Should there be just one advantage?

In ads, one main advantage. The rest serve as supporting arguments.

What if I can't find any advantage?

You can create one, through a guarantee, speed or specialising in a narrow niche.

Conclusion

A competitive advantage is a difference that matters to customers, that competitors lack and that you can prove. To find it, study your customers and market, then show it in your ads briefly and specifically.
