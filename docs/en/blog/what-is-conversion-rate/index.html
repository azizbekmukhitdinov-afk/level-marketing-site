What is conversion rate? How to calculate and improve it

"Conversion is low", "we need to improve conversion" — marketers and agencies say it all the time. For business owners, though, the word often stays abstract. In fact, conversion is one of the most useful numbers in a business: it shows exactly where customers are being lost. Here's what conversion is, how to calculate it and how to raise it, with simple examples.

A simple definition and formula

Conversion is the share of people who move from one stage to the next.

Conversion = people who reached the next stage ÷ people at the previous stage × 100%

Illustrative example: 1,000 people clicked an Instagram ad and 50 submitted a lead. Conversion = 50 ÷ 1,000 × 100% = 5%.

Where conversion is measured

WhereFrom what to whatExample (illustrative)
AdsImpression → click150 clicks from 10,000 impressions = 1.5% (CTR)
Website / landing pageVisit → lead25 leads from 500 visits = 5%
DMsMessage → meeting or order20 orders from 80 messages = 25%

StoreVisitor → buyer50 receipts from 200 visitors = 25%

The customer journey is a chain of several conversions. Illustrative example: a furniture store.

There's a "loss" at every stage. Work where the loss is biggest. On funnels — what is a sales funnel.

Why it matters: one number, a big difference

In the example, lead-to-measurement conversion is 40%. If the sales team raises it to 50%, sales grow by 25% (about 11 instead of 9) without a bigger ad budget. Raising conversion is often cheaper than raising the budget. More in before increasing your ad budget.

How to raise conversion, stage by stage

In ads (impression → click)

  • a hook in the first 3 seconds — Reels hooks;
  • a clear offer and price guide — should you show the price in your ads;
  • creative testing — A/B testing ad creatives.

On the page (visit → lead)

  • the offer and a button above the fold;
  • trust: reviews, photos, guarantees;
  • a short form — a landing page that converts.

In sales (lead → sale)

  • response speed — lead response time;
  • a script and objection handling — sales script;
  • follow-up — "I'll think about it" follow-up.

Is a conversion rate "good" or "bad"?

There's no universal benchmark: conversion depends on niche, price, channel and lead type. For an expensive purchase, 5% lead-to-sale may be normal; for a cheap product, low. The fairest comparisons are:

  • with your own previous months;
  • within the same channel;
  • between salespeople — sales rep conversion.

Common mistakes

  • Watching only one conversion (such as CTR) and forgetting sales.
  • Mixing stages: "2% conversion" — from what to what?
  • Drawing conclusions from small numbers: 2 leads from 20 clicks isn't statistics yet.
  • Raising conversion with excessive discounts.
  • Calculating without a CRM, so leads and sales aren't linked.

Questions and answers

Are CTR and conversion the same?

CTR is one type of conversion: impression to click. But for a business, lead-to-sale matters most.

Where can I see conversion?

In the ad account (clicks, leads), in website analytics (visits, form submissions) and in the CRM (leads, sales).

Which conversion should I improve first?

The stage with the biggest loss. Usually it's the sales stage: response speed and follow-up.

How does conversion relate to cost per lead?

When page conversion rises, cost per lead falls. More in cost per lead.

Conclusion

Conversion is the share of people moving from one stage to the next. Measure each stage of the funnel, find the biggest loss and fix that first — often cheaper than increasing the ad budget.
