What is lead generation? Building a lead system for your business

Lead generation is a system that brings your business the contact details of interested people regularly and at a predictable cost. Many business owners think of it as "we ran ads and enquiries came in", but ads are only one part of the system. Here's what lead generation is made of, which channels work in Uzbekistan, how to measure lead cost and quality, and how to launch the system in 30 days.

What is lead generation: a simple definition

A lead is a potential customer who has left contact details: a name, a phone number or a chat. The concept is explained in detail in what is a lead. Lead generation means getting those leads as a steady flow.

How ordinary advertising differs from lead generation:

  • advertising puts you in front of people — they may remember you, visit your site or just scroll past;
  • lead generation aims for a specific result — contact details, followed by an enquiry your sales team can work with.

In a good lead generation system you know three answers: how many leads arrive each month, how much one lead costs and how many of them turn into purchases.

The lead generation system: 5 parts

The system is only as strong as its weakest part. Great ads plus a two-day delay in replying to enquiries means the system doesn't work.

PartQuestionExample
1. TrafficWhere do people come from?Instagram ads, Google search, a Telegram channel
2. OfferWhy would they leave contact details?A free measuring visit, a trial lesson, a price list
3. Contact pointWhere do they leave them?Lead form, DMs, Telegram bot, website form
4. HandlingWho replies, when and how?CRM, sales manager, script, response-time standard
5. AnalyticsWhich channel brought the sale?UTM tags, CRM report, cost per lead and per sale

Most of the money usually goes into part 1, while parts 2–5 get little attention. The conclusion then is "ads don't work", even though the problem lies elsewhere.

Start with one or two main channels. One channel set up properly brings more than five channels run at half strength at the same time.

The offer: why people leave contact details

People don't hand over their phone number for nothing — they need something in return. Offers come in three levels:

  1. A direct offer. "Order now", "Book a slot". Works for hot audiences and simple products.
  2. A small first step. A free consultation, a check-up, a trial lesson, a measuring visit. The main approach for expensive or complex services.
  3. A lead magnet. Useful material: a checklist, a price guide, a calculator, a how-to. For people who are interested but not ready yet. Formats and delivery are covered in the lead magnet.

The rule is simple: the more expensive the product and the longer the decision, the smaller and easier the first step should be. A property developer doesn't say "buy a flat right now" — they say "get the floor plans and prices".

The contact point: form, message, bot or website

There are four main ways to collect contact details, and each has its place.

  • Instant form (lead form). Inside Instagram or Facebook, no website needed. Fast and convenient, but you also get more accidental enquiries.
  • A message in DMs or Telegram. The person writes first and the conversation starts straight away. Reply speed is critical.
  • Telegram bot. Asks questions in sequence, collects data neatly and replies automatically.
  • Website or landing page. Gives more information and trust, more accurate analytics, but takes time to build.

Which brings more sales, forms or messages, is compared in lead form or Direct messages. The short answer: test both and judge by sales, not by the number of leads.

How much lead generation costs: the maths

The most quoted number in lead generation is cost per lead (CPL): ad spend divided by the number of leads. Making decisions on that number alone is risky.

Illustrative example. A Tashkent training centre tests two campaigns for a month, 5 million soums each.

MetricCampaign ACampaign B
Leads250125
Cost per lead20,000 soums40,000 soums
Qualified leads100 (40%)88 (70%)
Cost per qualified lead50,000 soums~57,000 soums
Sales1522
Cost per sale~333,000 soums~227,000 soums

Look only at cost per lead and A is twice as cheap. Look at cost per sale and B is clearly better. The figures are illustrative, but this situation is common in practice. The trap is covered in detail in the cheap leads trap.

So judge lead generation by three numbers: cost per lead, cost per qualified lead and cost per sale. The third matters most.

Controlling lead quality: 4 rules

  1. Write down what a "qualified lead" means. For example: picked up the phone, is in your service area, has a need and a budget. Marketing and sales must use the same definition.
  2. Every lead goes into the CRM. Lead form enquiries shouldn't stay stuck in the ad account. Ways to connect them automatically are in connect a Meta lead form to your CRM.
  3. Give every lead a status. "Qualified", "not a fit", "no answer", "sold" — and a reason for each "not a fit".
  4. Compare channels once a week. Which campaign and which creative brought leads that bought? If this data doesn't flow back into your ads, they keep multiplying cheap but useless enquiries.

Launching lead generation in 30 days

Week 1: foundations. Describe your customer and one main offer. Agree on what a qualified lead is. Set up a CRM or at least a shared spreadsheet, and set a response-time standard: how many minutes it takes to reply during working hours.

Week 2: launch. Pick one or two channels. Prepare 3–5 different creatives, set up the form or bot and add UTM tags. Start with a small budget.

Week 3: first review. Look at leads, qualified leads and first sales by creative and channel. Switch off what didn't work, keep the best.

Week 4: scale. Gradually raise budgets on the campaigns that work, add a follow-up sequence for leads who didn't buy and test a second channel.

After 30 days you'll have your first numbers: cost per lead, share of qualified leads and cost per sale. They become the basis for next month's plan and budget.

Can you buy leads?

Some services sell "ready-made leads" and charge per lead. This can work, but check three things: where and with what offer the lead was generated, whether it's also sold to other companies, and whether poor-quality leads get replaced. A list of strangers' numbers isn't leads: those people never showed interest in you.

In the long run, your own lead generation system is cheaper and more stable: the channels, creatives, audiences and customer base stay with you.

Common mistakes

  • Looking only at cost per lead. A cheap lead that doesn't buy is the most expensive one.
  • Not tracking reply time. Even the best ads are wasted by slow replies.
  • Every channel at once. The budget gets spread thin and no channel has enough data to learn.
  • Ads without an offer. "We're the best" isn't an offer. People need a reason to leave contact details.
  • No analytics. If you don't know which channel brought sales, next month you'll be spending by guesswork again.

Questions and answers

Is lead generation the same as targeted advertising?

No. Targeted ads are one of the channels in the traffic part. Lead generation also includes the offer, the contact point, handling enquiries and analytics. If targeting is set up well but enquiries aren't handled, lead generation doesn't work.

Does a small business need lead generation?

Yes, if it sells through enquiries or calls: services, education, furniture, renovation, clinics. For a small business the system can be simple: one channel, one offer, a CRM or spreadsheet and a reply standard.

How soon will I see results?

The first leads can arrive within days of launching ads. But stable numbers — cost per lead, quality and cost per sale — usually show after a month of work. For services with long decisions, sales come even later.

What should my cost per lead be?

There's no universal answer: it depends on niche, region, offer and channel. The better question is: how much can you spend on one sale? Work that out from your margin and customer lifetime value, then find the cost per lead that fits within that limit.

Conclusion

Lead generation isn't advertising — it's a system: traffic, offer, contact point, enquiry handling and analytics. It's only as strong as its weakest part. Start with one or two channels, give people a clear reason to leave contact details, get every lead into your CRM and judge results by cost per sale, not cost per lead.
