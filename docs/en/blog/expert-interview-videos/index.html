Expert interview videos: a format that builds trust

An interview is the most natural way to show expertise: you talk, someone asks questions and the viewer "listens in" on the conversation. And one interview gives you a month of content. Here is how to prepare and film an interview and how to turn it into 10 Reels.

Why interviews work

  • Trust. The expert speaks naturally rather than reading a script.
  • The Q&A format mirrors the viewer's own questions.
  • Lots of content. An hour of conversation becomes a YouTube video, dozens of Reels, posts and an article.

Preparation

  1. 10–15 questions — ones customers actually ask.
  2. An interviewer sits behind or beside the camera.
  3. Location: a quiet room, a clean background, window light or two lamps.
  4. Sound: lavalier mics for both people.
  5. Camera: horizontal for YouTube with a vertical crop for Reels, or two cameras.

Sample questions

FieldQuestions
DentistryHow long does an implant last? Does it hurt? What affects the price?
LawHow do I register a business? What should I check in a contract?
EducationHow many months to learn a language? Which level to start from?
MarketingWhat budget do I need for ads? How do I choose an ads specialist?

Filming rules

  • Ask the expert to start each answer by restating the question: "An implant usually lasts…". Then every answer works as a standalone video.
  • Keep answers short — one idea in 30–60 seconds.
  • Record every important answer twice.

What one interview produces

MaterialCount
Full video for YouTube1
Reels and Shorts8–12
Quote posts5–10
Telegram posts5–10
Website article1–2

For Reels, pick the best moments and add a hook to each — Reels script templates help. Post the long version on your YouTube channel.

Conclusion

An expert interview is the most efficient content factory: one day of filming gives a month of credible content. Take questions from customers, pay attention to sound and plan every answer as its own video.
