Who should star in your video ad: employee, influencer or actor

The script is ready, the offer is clear, the shoot day is booked — and one last question remains: who goes on camera? The answer is often random: "our sales manager is photogenic", "we know an influencer", "I'll do it myself". Yet the person in the video is the first thing viewers judge, in the first second. Do they inspire trust? Do viewers see themselves in them, or is it obvious they're an "ad actor"? Let's compare five kinds of hero.

The key question: who does the viewer trust?

When choosing a hero, answer one question: whose words will my customer believe?

  • A specialist — they know, it's their job.
  • Someone like themselves — they faced the same problem.
  • Someone familiar — "I already follow them".
  • The owner — they stand behind their business.

Each answer points to a different hero.

Employee: the trust of expertise

Employees are often the most underrated heroes. When a craftsman shows how furniture is assembled or a dentist gives one tip, it creates trust no actor can: they genuinely do this work.

The upside: cheap and fast — employees are always around, so a new video can be shot today. The risk: they may leave. So develop two or three "faces". More in team content.

Owner: the trust of accountability

When the owner steps on camera and says "this is my business, I'm personally responsible for quality", that's something large companies can't offer. For small and mid-sized businesses, it's a competitive advantage.

But it isn't for everyone: owners are short on time, and not everyone is comfortable on camera. More in founder on camera.

Customer: the trust of similarity

When a customer says "I came with exactly the same problem", viewers see themselves. It's one of the strongest formats, especially for expensive purchases with long decisions.

How to film it — customer testimonial video. Videos customers film themselves — UGC content.

Influencer: the trust of an audience

An influencer "rents out" their audience's trust. For a new brand, it's a fast route to awareness. But:

  • is their audience your customer? What matters is who the followers are, not how many;
  • the influencer's reputation becomes yours — check their past posts;
  • the ad must be labelled — that's a legal requirement (Article 18).

More in influencer advertising and brand ambassadors. Using influencer content in your own ads — Instagram partnership ads.

Actor or model: control, but less trust

Actors speak well, look good on camera and follow the script precisely. For clothing, cosmetics and accessories a model is often essential: someone has to wear the product.

But in service businesses, an actor is risky: viewers sense that "this person doesn't work here". If an actor plays a doctor or craftsman, it can be misleading. If you use an actor, cast them as the user, not the specialist.

How to choose: a simple algorithm

  1. Is your product a service or goods? Service — an employee or the owner. Goods — a customer, model or influencer.
  2. Is your brand known? If not, an influencer or the owner brings fast awareness. If so, customers and employees reinforce trust.
  3. What's the main objection? "I'm not sure about quality" — a specialist or customer. "Is this for me?" — a similar customer or a model.
  4. What's the budget? Small — employee, owner, customer. Larger — add influencers and professional actors.

The best results come from testing several heroes. Same offer, an employee in one video, a customer in another. Compare by sales — A/B testing ad creatives.

Consent and rights

Whoever appears, you need a written agreement on where and for how long the video is used. The law prohibits using an individual's image in advertising without their consent (Article 16, lex.uz). With influencers and actors, specify in the contract how long the video may be used in ads — it's often priced separately.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing a "good-looking" person rather than a "believable" one.
  • Casting an actor as a specialist.
  • Choosing an influencer by follower count.
  • Tying all your ads to one hero.
  • Not agreeing on usage terms — a year later the influencer asks for the video to be removed.

Questions and answers

What if the owner doesn't want to appear on camera?

That's fine. Employees, customers and the product itself are strong heroes too. The owner can take part by voice or in just one video.

How much do influencers and actors cost?

It depends on the market, the influencer's audience and usage terms. When asking for a quote, state the usage period and channels up front.

How often should heroes change?

Two or three regular heroes work well — the audience recognises them. Add new ones for testing.

Can children appear in ads?

The law has separate requirements for advertising involving minors. Check parental consent and legal requirements in advance.

Conclusion

The hero of your video ad is one of the most important creative decisions. Employees bring expertise, owners accountability, customers similarity, influencers audience trust, and actors control. Work out whom your customer trusts and test several options.
