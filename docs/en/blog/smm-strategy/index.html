SMM strategy: a social media plan in 7 steps

Plenty of pages post every day, yet sales don't grow. The reason is that there's no strategy: nobody knows why they're posting, for whom, or what result to expect. An SMM strategy is a plan that defines how social media serves your business goals. Here's how to build one in 7 steps.

1. The business goal

Your SMM goal comes from your business goal:

  • new leads and sales;
  • brand awareness (a new market, a new branch);
  • retaining existing customers;
  • hiring staff.

Put a number on it: "100 leads a month from Instagram within 3 months".

2. The audience

Who buys, what they fear and what they trust — target audience and customer persona. Customers' questions and objections are a ready-made content source — content from customer objections.

3. Platforms

You don't have to be everywhere:

  • Instagram — visual products, services, B2C.
  • Telegram — trust and repeat sales through a channel — create a Telegram channel.
  • YouTube — in-depth explanations, an expert brand.
  • LinkedIn — B2B and hiring.

Running 1–2 main platforms well beats running five badly.

4. Content pillars

Pick 3–5 recurring themes (illustrative example — a dental clinic):

  • useful tips (education);
  • the process and the team (trust);
  • results and reviews (proof);
  • questions and answers (objections);
  • offers (sales).

A detailed plan — Instagram content plan.

5. Formats and frequency

  • Reels reach new audiences — how the Instagram algorithm works.
  • Carousels earn saves and carry in-depth information.
  • Stories keep conversations and sales going with existing followers.

Choose a frequency you can sustain: 3 strong posts a week beat weak daily ones.

6. Linking with advertising

Organic content builds trust; advertising delivers it to the right people. Use posts that performed well in your ads, and make sure your profile is ready for people arriving from ads — what is SMM.

7. KPIs and a monthly review

  • reach and new followers — reach vs impressions;
  • engagement rate — what is engagement rate;
  • enquiries and leads from the profile;
  • sales (tracked in your CRM).

Every month: what worked, what didn't and what changes next month.

Common mistakes

  • Only "pretty" content with no path to a purchase.
  • Copying a competitor — no voice of your own.
  • Likes as the only KPI.
  • A strategy written once and forgotten.

Questions and answers

What period should a strategy cover?

Usually 3–6 months, with monthly adjustments.

Can I build an SMM strategy myself?

Yes, using these 7 steps. The hard part is consistent execution and analysis.

When will I see results?

With ads, within the first few weeks; organic growth usually takes a few months.
