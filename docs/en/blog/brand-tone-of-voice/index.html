On Instagram the brand jokes, in Direct the operator dryly writes "Price 2 mln", and the Telegram channel reads like a press release. To a customer, that is three different companies. A brand tone of voice fixes this: you describe once how the company speaks, and everyone writes by those rules. Here is how to put one together in 6 steps.

What tone of voice is and why it matters

Tone of voice is how a brand talks to customers: which words it uses, whether it addresses people formally or casually, whether it jokes, how long its sentences are. A logo and colours make a brand recognisable to the eye; tone of voice does the same in text and conversation.

Keep two ideas apart:

  • Voice is constant. It is the brand's character, for example "open, specific, never boastful". It doesn't change for years.
  • Tone shifts with the situation. Livelier in an ad, more serious when answering a negative review. The voice stays the same.

Many brands use this split. Mailchimp's public content style guide, for example, says the voice stays the same while the tone adapts to the situation.

What it gives a small business:

  • a new SMM manager or operator starts writing in the company's language within a week;
  • ads, posts and Direct replies don't contradict each other, so customers trust you more easily;
  • discussing a post turns from "I don't like it" into a clear "it doesn't follow the rules".

Tone of voice is one section of a brand book. The other sections are covered in what is a brand book.

How to define your brand tone of voice: 6 steps

1. Describe your customer in one sentence

Tone starts with the audience. Copy for a 45-year-old business owner and for a 19-year-old student can't be the same. One sentence: "Our customer is a Tashkent family aged 30–45, buying quality furniture for the first time, comparing prices, looking for a reliable place."

If your positioning isn't settled yet, start there; our article on brand positioning explains how.

2. Decide who the brand is to the customer

The fastest way is to imagine the brand as a person. Who are you to the customer?

  • Friend: warm, simple words, sometimes humour (café, clothing store).
  • Adviser: explains, compares, helps choose (furniture, appliances, training centre).
  • Expert: precise, calm, no extra emotion (clinic, legal services).
  • Craftsman: practical, short, "here's how" (renovation, car service).

Pick one role and describe it with three adjectives: "open, specific, patient". No more than three, because nobody remembers a list of seven.

3. Place your tone on 4 scales

Researchers at Nielsen Norman Group suggest rating the tone of a text on four dimensions. Each is a line between two extremes, and the question is where your brand sits:

ScaleOne endOther end
HumourFunnySerious
FormalityFormalCasual
RespectfulnessRespectfulIrreverent
EnthusiasmEnthusiasticMatter-of-fact

Give each scale a score from 1 to 5. A clinic, for example: humour 1 (serious), formality 3, respectfulness 5, enthusiasm 2. A clothing store: humour 4, formality 2, respectfulness 3, enthusiasm 4. Extreme scores on every scale rarely suit a business.

4. Formal or informal address, language and script

For a brand in Uzbekistan this is a separate decision, and it belongs in the document:

  • Formal or informal "you". In Uzbek it's "siz" or "sen", in Russian "вы" or "ты". For most businesses the formal form is the safe choice. The informal one works for youth brands but can put off older customers. Don't mix them within one channel.
  • Language. Which language goes where: for example, Instagram in Uzbek, Telegram in Uzbek and Russian, the website in three languages. For bilingual posts, decide which language comes first.
  • Script. Latin or Cyrillic for Uzbek. People search in both, as we explain in Uzbek search: Latin and Cyrillic. Write it plainly: "main script is Latin, with the correct o‘ and g‘ characters".
  • Russian loanwords in Uzbek copy. "Skidka", "zakaz", "dostavka", or "chegirma", "buyurtma", "yetkazib berish"? One choice everywhere.

5. Build a word list: we say / we don't say

This is the most useful part of the document. Two columns:

We sayWe don't say
"your order", "we deliver"slang and loanwords you've ruled out
"the price is ...", an actual figure"price in DM", "message us privately"
"sorry we replied late""unfortunately, due to technical reasons..."
"we'll help you choose""the best", "unbeatable", "No. 1"
plain words: "free measuring"clichés like "innovative solution", "premium quality"

Add words tied to advertising law to the banned list as well: unproven "the best" or "guaranteed results" claims are risky. If the brand has a slogan, it should sound like the same voice; see how to create a slogan.

6. Write "do" and "don't" examples

Rules alone don't teach anyone to write; people need examples. For each common situation, write one good and one bad version. Say a customer asks in Direct "How much is it?":

  • Don't: "2,450,000."
  • Do: "Hello! This sofa is 2,450,000 sum, and delivery within Tashkent is free. Would you like help choosing a colour?"

One voice, different tones: a situations table

The voice is always the same, but the tone changes with the situation. Add a table like this to the document:

SituationToneWhat to watch
Ads and ReelsLively, shortBenefit in the first line, no clichés
Price question in DirectOpen, specificDon't hide the price, offer the next step
Late replyApologetic, simpleNo excuses, help right away
Negative reviewSerious, respectfulNo jokes, acknowledge the problem, offer a fix
Useful Telegram postAdviserOne idea, with an example
Official notice (hours, prices)Matter-of-factDate, figure, clear wording

How to answer criticism is covered in detail in online reputation and reviews. The key point: even a brand that jokes doesn't answer a complaint with a joke.

Illustrative example: one page for a furniture store

Here is what a tone of voice page could look like for a furniture store in Tashkent (illustrative example):

For whom: families aged 30–45 furnishing a flat for the first time. Who we are: an adviser. Open, specific, patient. Scales: humour 2, formality 2, respectfulness 4, enthusiasm 3. Address: formal "siz" / "вы". Instagram in Uzbek, Telegram in Uzbek and Russian. Latin script. We say: order, free measuring, instalments, warranty period. We don't say: "message us privately", "the cheapest", "premium quality". Rules: always state the price; no sentence over 15 words; every reply ends with a question or a next step. Examples: price question, apology for a delay, negative review, each as "do / don't".

One page is enough. Nobody reads a long document, but a single page can sit on the operator's desk.

How to roll it out to the team

Writing the document is half the job. The other half is getting people to follow it:

  1. Give it to everyone. SMM manager, designer, Direct operator, sales staff, agency: everyone gets the same file.
  2. Rewrite saved replies. Update saved replies and automated messages in Direct to the new tone. Setup is covered in automated responses in Meta Business Suite.
  3. Check 10 messages a month. Take 10 random Direct chats, 5 posts and 3 review replies and compare them with the document.
  4. New situation, new example. When a question comes up that the document doesn't cover, write the answer and add it to the "do / don't" list.
  5. Review once a year. If the audience or the product has changed, update the scales.

Questions and answers

Is tone of voice only for big brands? No. In a small business, messages are written by 2–3 different people who often change. That is exactly why a one-page rulebook helps.

Is tone of voice the same as a brand book? No. Tone of voice is the verbal part of a brand book. A brand book also covers the logo, colours, fonts and photo style.

Do Instagram and Telegram need different tones? The voice is one. The tone can differ a little: shorter and livelier on Instagram, more detailed on Telegram. But the form of address and banned words are the same everywhere.

How do I know the tone is right? Watch how customers respond: do Direct conversations continue, do reviews mention polite replies, has negative feedback dropped? You can also test two versions of ad copy, changing only one thing.

Conclusion

A brand tone of voice is a one-page rulebook on how the company speaks: who the customer is, who the brand is, 4 scales, formal or informal address, language and script, "we say / we don't say" words, and "do / don't" examples. The voice is constant; the tone adapts to the situation. Share the document with the whole team and check messages once a month.
