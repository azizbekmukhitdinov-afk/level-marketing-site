10 ad creatives from one video shoot: an editing plan

Organising a new shoot for your ads every week is expensive and exhausting. But one well-planned shoot can give you 10 ad creatives — if the shots are thought through in advance and the edit is treated not as "one video" but as "a family of videos". This article walks through the plan step by step: the shot list, the creative matrix, the editing order and testing in Ads Manager.

Why one ad video is not enough

Launch ads with a single video and two problems show up quickly.

The first is fatigue. People see the same video again and again, clicks drop and each lead gets more expensive. Meta itself has separate creative fatigue recommendations in Ads Manager. Signs and a replacement routine are covered in our article on creative fatigue.

The second is that there is nothing to compare. If your only video flops, you don't know why: a weak hook, the wrong offer or the wrong format? With several variants, the answer shows up in the numbers.

One important point: 10 creatives are not 10 copies of the same video with different music or colour grading. Ad systems often treat near-identical videos as the same ad and show them to the same people. Useful variants differ in meaning: a different pain point, a different promise, a different first frame.

What makes creatives different: 4 axes

Plan every creative along four axes:

AxisWhat changesExample (furniture store)
Angle (idea)The reason we give the customerComfort, price, quality, delivery
HookThe first 1–3 secondsA question, a result, a number, an unexpected shot
FormatHow it looksTalking head, footage only, carousel, still image
LengthDuration6–10, 15–20, 30–45 seconds

The angle makes the biggest difference, then the hook. Format and length come on top. So plan the shoot starting from the angles.

Before the shoot: the shot list

Once the matrix is ready, turn it into a shot list. To make sure nothing is forgotten on the day, split it into groups:

GroupWhat to shootHow many variants
Talking shotsThe main script for each angle2–3 takes each
HooksDifferent versions of the first line2–3 per angle
ProductWide, medium and close-up shots2–3 angles each
ProcessHands, mechanism, assembly, delivery5–8 short shots
DetailsFabric, seams, fittings, colour samples5–10
PeopleA customer or salesperson using the product3–5
StillsWell-lit static shots5–10

A few practical rules:

  • Record hooks separately. Say the first line three different ways — that is enough to swap the opening in the edit. Hook ideas are in 10 Reels hooks.
  • Roll 3–5 seconds longer on every shot. The editor needs room to cut.
  • Shoot vertical, 9:16, and leave margins. For Reels, Meta recommends keeping text and logos out of the top 14%, the bottom 35% and 6% on each side (Meta Ads Guide). If the key subject sits in the centre, cropping to 4:5 and 1:1 later is easy.
  • Check the sound separately. A lavalier mic noticeably improves even an ordinary phone. Light and sound are covered in shooting Reels on a phone.

Each angle needs a short script — ready templates are in Reels script templates.

The editing plan: 4 steps

Edit in order, and 10 creatives won't turn into 10 separate projects.

Step 1. A "master" version for each angle

First build one complete video per angle: hook → problem or idea → product → offer → call to action. These are your master versions.

Step 2. Swap the hooks

Replace the first 2–3 seconds of a master with another hook and leave the rest untouched. One angle now gives two or three creatives, and you learn which opening works better.

Step 3. Short and long cuts

Cut a 6–10 second version from the master: one idea, one sequence of shots, the offer at the end. For the expert and problem angles, a longer 30–45 second version can also be useful.

Step 4. Formats and subtitles

Add subtitles to every video and keep text inside the safe zone. Build a carousel and a still image from the best frames. Which app is most convenient — see video editing apps.

Naming system. Name files the same way every time, for example sofa_problem_hook2_15s_9x16. Then the ad report tells you at a glance which angle and which hook worked.

How to test in Ads Manager

The most common mistake is dropping all 10 creatives into one ad set on day one. On a small budget the system gives almost all the money to one or two videos, and the rest barely get shown.

A simpler order:

  1. First wave: one video per angle (4–5 in total). The goal is to find which idea works.
  2. Second wave: hook variants and the short cut of the winning angle.
  3. Third wave: the carousel and the still image — for warm audiences and retargeting.
  4. Reserve: keep 1–2 creatives back to launch a new angle when results start to slip.

How to compare variants properly is covered in A/B testing ad creatives.

One fact about format: according to Meta's own tests, Reels videos with sound, in 9:16 and with key elements inside the safe zone delivered on average a 34.5% lower cost per result than static image ads (Meta: Reels ads). This is Meta's own data and your niche may differ, but it is a good reason to make video your main format.

5 common mistakes

  • Shooting without a plan. In the edit you find the hook was recorded only once and there are no product details at all.
  • Variants differ only in music or colour. Such "10 creatives" are really one creative.
  • Trying to say everything in every video. One creative, one idea. Price, quality and delivery go into different videos.
  • Text at the bottom of the frame. In Reels it gets covered by the button and the caption.
  • Launching everything on the same day. Release in waves and your creatives live longer — and by the next shoot you know exactly what to film.

Questions and answers

How long does one shoot take?

It depends on the number of shots. For a simple product and one location, half a day is often enough — provided the shot list and scripts are ready in advance. Most time is lost when shooting without a plan.

Can I shoot on a phone?

Yes. For ads, good light, clean sound and a clear idea matter more than an expensive camera. Professional gear is more useful for brand films and complex shots.

Do all 10 creatives need to run at once?

No. It is better to launch in waves: test the angles first, then the winner's variants. The remaining creatives wait in reserve and replace older ones as they fatigue.

Does this work for service businesses?

Yes. For a clinic, a training centre or a renovation company you film the process, the specialist and the result instead of a product. The angles change too: experience, price, convenient hours, outcome. If customers or their data appear on camera, get their consent first.

Conclusion

To get 10 ad creatives from one video shoot you need three things: an angle matrix, a shot list and a disciplined edit. Then your creatives differ in meaning, tests give clear answers and you don't have to start from scratch every month.
