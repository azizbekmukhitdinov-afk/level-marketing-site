Content copyright: who owns the photos and videos you paid for

You paid the photographer, the videographer delivered the clip, and your SMM agency produced posts for a year. Many business owners assume that paying makes the work theirs. The law works differently: content copyright first belongs to the person who created the work, and you receive only what the contract says. Below is what the law states, how five kinds of contractors differ, and which 8 clauses to put in the contract.

This article is not legal advice. For a large project or an existing dispute, show the contract to a lawyer.

Content copyright: what the law says

In Uzbekistan this area is governed by the Law on Copyright and Related Rights (No. ZRU-42). Four points matter to a business owner.

First, the right appears when the work is created. Once the photo is taken or the video is edited, it is a protected work. No registration or special mark is required.

Second, there are two kinds of rights. Personal non-property rights (Article 18) cover being recognised as the author, being named, and protection against distortion. They cannot pass to anyone, even if the author wants them to. Property rights (Article 19) cover reproducing, distributing, showing publicly and adapting the work. These are what a contract gives you.

Third, the contract must be in writing (Article 42). A Telegram message saying "use the video however you like" is weak evidence in a dispute.

Fourth, what is not written is not transferred. Under Articles 38–39, any right of use that the contract does not name stays with the author.

Type of rightWhat it meansDoes it pass to you
Authorship and nameThe right to say "I made this"No, never
Integrity of the workThe work cannot be changed in a way that harms the author's reputationNo
Reproduction and distributionUsing it on your site, on Instagram, on a bannerYes, if the contract says so
AdaptationCropping, re-editing, adding textYes, if the contract says so
Passing it onGiving it to a dealer or franchise partnerOnly with direct permission in the contract

What the law assumes when the contract is silent

Most disputes begin with "we never agreed on that". The law fills those gaps itself, and usually not in the client's favour.

Missing from the contractResult under the law
Method of useA method that is not named has not passed to you
TerritoryThe right applies only within Uzbekistan
ExclusivityThe right is non-exclusive: the author can give the same work to others
TermAfter five years the author may terminate the contract with six months' written notice
Transfer to third partiesNot permitted
Methods of use that do not exist yetCannot be covered by the contract

In practice it looks like this: you agreed with a photographer on "photos for the website". A year later the same photo appears on a billboard. If outdoor advertising is not in the contract, the photographer is entitled to object.

Five contractors, five different situations

The starting position depends on who created the content.

Who created itStarting positionWhat to check
Freelance photographerThe photographer holds the rights; you get what the contract listsMethods of use, term, adaptation
Videographer or studioFor an audiovisual work, property rights pass to the producer unless the contract says otherwise (Article 40)The producer is usually the studio. Write the transfer to you as a separate clause

8 clauses to put in the contract

You do not need a separate long document. A one-page section or an annex to the service contract is enough.

  1. What is delivered. A list of works: "30 retouched photos", "3 clips of 30 seconds". Repeat the list in the acceptance certificate.
  2. Methods of use. List them all: social media, website, paid social and search ads, marketplaces, outdoor advertising, print, television, in-store screens.
  3. Territory. If you sell or advertise outside Uzbekistan, write "worldwide".
  4. Term. A specific period or "for the full term of copyright".
  5. Exclusive or non-exclusive. For a logo, a brand character and ad videos, exclusive. For ordinary stock-style photos a non-exclusive right is enough and costs less.
  6. Right to adapt. Cropping, changing the format, adding subtitles and text, editing into another video. Without it, even a Reel cut from a 16:9 clip is open to dispute.
  7. Transfer to third parties. Dealers, branches, your ad agency, a marketplace. The agency you work with today may change tomorrow, and the new one must be able to use the material.
  8. Fee. The law treats the amount of the fee, or the way it is calculated, as an essential term of the contract. Write: "the fee for the rights is included in the total price".

Two more clauses prevent later arguments: whether the contractor may show the work in a portfolio (usually yes, after publication) and whether the author's name is credited (in ads usually not, so agree on this as well).

Illustrative example. A furniture store in Tashkent commissions a catalogue shoot. The contract says: 40 photos; use on the website, Instagram, paid social ads, a marketplace and a printed catalogue; territory Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan; unlimited term; non-exclusive, but the photographer will not give the photos to other furniture stores; cropping and adding text are allowed; the photographer may place 10 photos in a portfolio. The example is invented for clarity.

Source files, music and people in the frame

Three things are often confused with copyright.

Source files. RAW photos, the editing project, Figma or PSD files are materials, not rights. The law does not oblige the contractor to hand them over. If you need them, write it into the contract: which files, in what format, how long they are kept. Many contractors charge separately for source files, and that is normal practice.

Music, fonts and stock materials. The music in a video or the font in a layout is someone else's work. Have the contractor guarantee that these were obtained legally and that the licence permits use in advertising. Trending music from Instagram may not work in an ad account, so plan for this at the creative production stage. For images and voices generated by neural networks, see AI and the law.

People in the frame. Even when you hold the rights to a photo, the consent of the person in it is a separate matter. The advertising law prohibits using a person's image and name in advertising without their consent. Get written consent from models, customers and employees. Agree in advance what happens to the ads if an employee leaves.

5 common mistakes

  1. Assuming "I paid, so it is mine". The payment is for the service. Rights pass only to the extent written in the contract.
  2. Agreeing verbally or in a messenger. The law requires a written contract. For a small job a two-page document is enough.
  3. One sentence saying "all rights pass to the client". If the methods are not listed, that sentence is weak in a dispute. Write the full list.
  4. Remembering about rights only when parting with an agency. From day one, record who keeps the content, the accounts and the source files after the contract ends. For accounts, see giving an agency access to your ad account.
  5. Using someone else's content "from the internet". Article 65 lets the rights holder claim compensation instead of damages, from 20 to 1,000 base calculation units. A competitor's photo or a blogger's video is not free material.

Questions and answers

I paid the photographer. Are the photos automatically mine?

No. You receive only the rights of use written in the contract. If there is no contract, or it says nothing about rights, your position is weak.

We stopped working with our SMM agency. Can we keep the old posts?

It depends on the contract. If it records the transfer of rights to you and the term, yes. If not, sign an additional agreement with the agency. It is best to settle this at the start.

Can I demand the source files?

Only if the contract says so. Under the law the finished work and the rights to it are one thing, and working files are another.

Who owns the videos my employee films on a phone?

If creating content is part of the job, it is a work made for hire and the employer usually holds the right of use. If the duty is not written anywhere, a dispute is possible, so add it to the job description.

What if the author later says "take the video down"?

If there is a written contract with a stated term, you are protected. If no term is stated, the law lets the author terminate the contract after five years.

Conclusion

Content copyright passes through the contract, not through the payment. Before the shoot, prepare a one-page section: what is delivered, where and for how long it is used, whether the right is exclusive, whether you may adapt it and pass it on, and whether the fee is included in the price. Get consent from the people in the frame and a guarantee on music and fonts from the contractor. Then a year later you can still put your own video into an ad without worry.
