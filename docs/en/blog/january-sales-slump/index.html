The January sales slump: getting through the post-holiday lull

Sales peak in December, and after 1–2 January the phone goes quiet for many businesses. People have spent a lot over the holidays, they're on leave or busy making plans. This "January lull" is predictable — which means you can prepare for it. Here's how to keep sales going in January.

Why sales drop in January

  • Post-holiday thrift: after December's spending, people are cautious.
  • Holidays: many rest in early January, and decisions get postponed.
  • B2B: companies don't buy until the annual budget is approved.
  • Ad fatigue: after December's flood of promotions, audiences tune out ads.

But not for everyone: for fitness, education, language courses, healthcare and planning services, January is the season of "fresh starts".

6 moves for January

  1. A "new year, new start" offer: a course, membership or service package themed around setting goals.
  2. Reach out to your customer base: give December buyers a next step — care products, accessories, a service — repeat sales and LTV.
  3. Clear leftover stock: New Year items and seasonal surplus — without turning your brand into a permanent sale — the discount trap.
  4. For B2B: in early January, offer companies an annual contract or package while they're setting budgets.
  5. Behind-the-scenes content: the year's results, the team, plans for the new year — trust without ads.
  6. Collect reviews and referrals: ask December customers for reviews — collecting customer feedback.

The ad budget: pause or reduce?

Switching ads off completely is risky: campaigns lose their learning, and restarting in February may cost more. A better approach:

  • reduce the budget but keep your best campaigns running;
  • refresh audiences and creatives (switch off holiday creatives);
  • judge results weekly, not daily.

Preparing in December

January's problem is solved in December:

  • collect December buyers' contacts (with consent) in your CRM;
  • have the January offer ready by mid-December;
  • shoot January creatives in advance;
  • plan team holidays with sales cover.

Questions and answers

Do ads get cheaper in January?

Competition often eases after the holidays, so ad costs may fall. Demand falls too, so judge results by cost per lead.

Can discounts rescue January?

Briefly, yes — but constant discounts damage margin and brand. Adding value works better.

Is January a lost month for B2B?

No — it's when budgets are set. Meetings and proposals in January, contracts in February and March.

Conclusion

The January lull is a known season. Work out which group your business is in, then use a fresh-start offer, your customer base, B2B packages and a careful ad budget. Most importantly, prepare in December. In a free audit, Level Marketing will review your January plan.
