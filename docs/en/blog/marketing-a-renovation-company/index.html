Renovation company marketing: trust, estimates and a steady flow of leads

Renovating a home or office is a big expense and a big risk. Clients fear three things most: the estimate doubles mid-project, deadlines slip, and quality problems show up six months later. So renovation company marketing is less about pretty photos and more about trust: showing the process, explaining the price and putting promises in writing. This article covers how to build a steady flow of leads for a renovation company or crew in Tashkent.

Clients' fears and the answers

FearMarketing's answer
"The estimate will balloon"A detailed estimate, a fixed price or clear limits in the contract
"It will take forever"A stage schedule, responsibility for delays
"The work will be poor"Process videos, the materials used, a warranty
"It won't look like I imagined"3D visualisation or a design project upfront
"They'll take the money and vanish"An office, a contract, staged payments, reviews

Every fear should have an answer in your content and your offer.

Step 1. A specific offer

Everyone writes "renovations of any complexity". Stronger:

  • By format: "A new-build apartment, turnkey, in 60 days".
  • By pricing model: "The price per square metre is fixed in the contract".
  • By specialisation: kitchens and bathrooms, offices, restaurants, new builds.
  • By warranty: a written warranty for a set period.

Framing the offer — unique selling proposition.

Step 2. Content — process and result

Results are easy to show in renovation, but the process is what builds trust:

FormatExample
Before / afterSame angle: the bare shell and the finished room
StagesWiring, plumbing, plastering — "what's inside the wall"
The craftsman explains"Why you must never cut corners on waterproofing"
MaterialsWhich brand and why
Site of the weekA short weekly video report from one project
The clientHanding over the keys, a review (with consent)

Make it a habit to shoot every project from the same spot — that's how before/after pairs build up. Shooting on a phone — shooting Reels on a phone; a content matrix example — AutoRobot case (the same approach for services with visible results).

Step 3. 3D visualisation — show the result in advance

3D visualisation removes the fear that "it won't look like I imagined": clients see the finished room before work begins. Visualisations are strong content too — "here's the project, here's the result" posts raise trust and bring leads.

Step 4. The estimate and contract are part of marketing

Many companies lose a lead by being vague about price in the first conversation. A process that works:

  1. On the phone or in DMs — an approximate price range per square metre and an invitation to a site visit.
  2. Site visit — free, or with the price stated upfront; a craftsman comes.
  3. Detailed estimate — works and materials separately, within 2–3 days.
  4. Contract — price, timeline, stages, payment schedule, warranty.
  5. Weekly report — photos and videos for the client.

A sample estimate and contract (without personal data) on your website or in highlights increases trust.

Step 5. Paid ads

  • Audience: a radius around new-build developments, housing and interior interests; broad audiences often work well.
  • Creative: a before/after video and a specific offer: "Apartment renovation — illustrative example: from … UZS per m², with a 3D project".
  • Objective: a lead form — area, property type (new build/resale), start date; or messages.
  • Retargeting: case studies and the warranty for people who watched a video.

Partnering with developers — a special offer for new-build buyers — brings a steady flow. Choosing lead forms — lead form or messages; setup — Instagram ads.

Step 6. Maps, reviews and referrals

In renovation, referrals are the strongest channel: neighbours, friends, clients in the same building. Encourage them:

  • ask for a review after hand-over — on maps and Instagram;
  • a bonus for clients who refer someone (a discount on the next job or a gift);
  • if you work in one building, show the project in the building's Telegram group (with management's permission).

Working with reviews — online reputation and reviews; maps — adding your business to maps.

Commercial projects and seasons

Office, shop and restaurant fit-outs are a separate market. Here a company head or manager decides, and the key questions differ: timelines (every day means rent and lost revenue), the option to work outside business hours, paperwork and invoices. Prepare a separate page or highlights, case studies and a commercial proposal for commercial clients — more on the B2B approach in B2B marketing.

Renovation demand is seasonal: leads rise in spring and early summer, and spike when new buildings are handed over. Watch developers' hand-over schedules and time ads to those dates. In winter, offer interior work and design projects so the crew isn't idle.

What to measure

  • number and cost of leads;
  • lead-to-site-visit and site-visit-to-contract conversion;
  • average contract value;
  • share of projects handed over on time (this drives reviews);
  • share of clients from referrals.

Common mistakes

  • Only photos of finished interiors — no process, no team.
  • "We can't say without seeing it" when asked about price in the first conversation.
  • Working without an estimate and "unexpected" extras at the end.
  • Someone else's photos or stock images in ads — clients find out anyway.
  • Losing touch with clients after handing over the keys.

Questions and answers

Which ads work best for a renovation company?

Paid ads with before/after and process videos, around new-build developments. Ask for area and property type in the lead form — it filters out unsuitable leads.

Should ads show a price?

An approximate "from … per m²" reduces cold leads. Give the exact price after the site visit and estimate.

Is 3D visualisation a must?

Not a must, but on pricier projects it noticeably raises trust and conversion. For small renovations, standard design options may be enough.

What to do about a negative review?

Reply calmly, acknowledge the problem and say how you're fixing it. A well-handled complaint gives future clients confidence.

Conclusion

Renovation company marketing answers clients' fears: show the process, show the result in 3D upfront, provide a transparent estimate and contract, and meet deadlines. Then ads bring leads and happy clients bring new projects through referrals.
