What does a marketer do? A guide for business owners

"We need a marketer," many business owners say — but they mean different things: someone to run Instagram, someone to set up ads, or someone to own the whole sales strategy. The wrong person gets hired, and the conclusion becomes "marketing doesn't work". Here's what a marketer actually is, what they do and when you need one.

What a marketer does

A marketer is responsible for the system that runs from finding customers to closing sales:

  • researching the market and the customer — marketing research;
  • shaping the offer and positioning — brand positioning;
  • choosing channels and allocating the budget — planning a marketing budget;
  • coordinating specialists: social media, paid ads, design, video;
  • measuring results: leads, sales, ROMI — how to calculate ROMI;
  • working closely with the sales team — marketing and sales alignment.

The skills needed

  • Analytics: reading numbers, spreadsheets, the CRM.
  • Customer understanding: interviews, objections, motivations.
  • Channel knowledge: at least the main ones, in practice.
  • Copywriting and creative thinking.
  • Project management: deadlines, tasks, the team.

When a business needs a marketer

  • Several channels are running, but nobody connects them.
  • There's advertising, but no visible link to sales.
  • The founder can't find time for marketing.

If the business is small and one channel is enough, start with one specialist or an agency — agency, freelancer or in-house.

How to judge results

Judge a marketer not by the number of posts but by:

  • trends in leads and sales;
  • customer acquisition cost — CAC;
  • a clear monthly report with next steps — what an ad report should include.

Agree KPIs in advance — what is KPI.

Hiring mistakes

  • Looking for one person who "does everything": strategy, design, video and ads.
  • Looking only at portfolio images without asking for numbers.
  • Hiring without a probation period or measurable goals.
  • Keeping marketing separate from sales.

Questions and answers

Do you need a degree to be a marketer?

Not necessarily. Practical results and analytical thinking matter more.

Should a marketer set up ads personally?

In a small team, often yes. In a large one, they manage specialists.

How soon does a marketer deliver results?

The first clear conclusions usually come within 2–3 months — what to expect from marketing in 90 days.

Conclusion

A marketer isn't a post-writer or an ad technician but the person responsible for the system from customer to sale.
