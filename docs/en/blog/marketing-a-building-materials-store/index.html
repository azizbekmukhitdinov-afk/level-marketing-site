Marketing a building materials store: tradespeople and homeowners

Building materials stores compete on price. At the market, dozens of shops side by side sell the same products, and buyers phone around asking "where's it cheapest?" If your marketing is nothing but discounts, your margin disappears. Here's how a building materials store can escape the price war and build a base of regular customers.

Two kinds of customer, two kinds of marketing

A building materials store has two main kinds of buyer:

Tradesperson / site foreman / crewHomeowner (renovating)
How oftenEvery weekOnce every few years
What mattersStock, delivery, price, credit termsAdvice, trust, choice
Who decidesThemselvesOften on a tradesperson's advice
Where they find youContacts, TelegramMaps, Instagram, the tradesperson

The key insight: many homeowner purchases happen on a tradesperson's recommendation. Relationships with tradespeople are your strongest channel.

A programme for tradespeople

  • A trade card: special prices or bonuses for registered tradespeople.
  • Fast ordering: send a list via Telegram, and a salesperson prices it and replies.
  • Delivery to site at a fixed time.
  • A Telegram channel for tradespeople: new products, stock, price changes.

Put bonuses and special terms into written rules — who gets what, how and when. That ties the relationship to a system rather than personal acquaintance.

For homeowners, advice sells

People renovating are anxious: "How much material do I need? Which brand is better? Will my money stretch?" Turn that into marketing:

  • An estimate service: material quantities based on room size, free of charge.
  • Useful content: "Choosing laminate: 5 questions", "How to choose tile adhesive" — what is content marketing.
  • Before/after videos of your customers' renovations.
  • Bundles: "A complete bathroom kit" — everything in one place.

Maps come first

"Building materials near me" searches often end on a map. On Google Maps, Yandex Maps and 2GIS, make sure you have:

  • the right category, opening hours and phone number;
  • photos inside and outside the store, plus access directions;
  • a list of product categories;
  • reviews, with replies.

Advertising: what works

  • Search and map ads for specific-need queries like "plasterboard price" or "buy tiles" — search ads in Tashkent.
  • Instagram/Facebook — bundles and before/after videos for homeowners nearby.
  • Retargeting — showing ads again to people who visited your site or profile — how retargeting works.

Escaping the price war

To avoid competing on price alone, add other value:

  • fast delivery, including carrying materials upstairs;
  • estimates and advice;
  • the option to return unused material;
  • bundles and guarantees.

These services make direct price comparison harder. More in the discount trap.

Repeat sales

A renovation lasts months, so one customer comes back several times. Collect phone numbers (with consent), keep purchase history in a CRM and offer the next stage: "You bought tiles — need plumbing too?" — repeat sales and LTV.

Questions and answers

Do I need an online store?

Not necessarily a full one. A catalogue, a "get a price" button and Telegram ordering are often enough.

Do building materials sell on Instagram?

Less directly, but Instagram is good for showing trust and choice: before/after, advice, new collections.

Is it legal to give tradespeople bonuses?

If the terms are open and documented, it's an ordinary partner programme. Check the tax side with your accountant.

Conclusion

A building materials store has two kinds of customer: the tradesperson and the homeowner. Tradespeople need a structured programme; homeowners need advice and bundles.
