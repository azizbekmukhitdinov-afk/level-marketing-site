New Year shop window: a store decoration plan, Nov to Jan

A New Year shop window is a once-a-year chance: people walk more slowly, look for gifts and pay more attention to stores than usual. Yet many shops hang their decorations in a rush on 25 December and take them down in February. This article shows how to plan a New Year shop window and store decoration from late November to January: a calendar, a budget, 6 steps and a checklist.

Why a New Year shop window needs its own plan

A regular window can be changed once a month. A holiday window works differently, for three reasons:

  • the season is short — most purchases are squeezed into 3–4 weeks, and every week of delay is expensive;
  • everyone decorates — your neighbours also have a tree and fairy lights, so simply "having decorations" does not set you apart;
  • there is a clear end — after 1 January a holiday window ages fast and has to be replaced on time.

Is the effort worth it? Researchers at the Stockholm School of Economics (Lange, Rosengren, Blom, 2016) ran a field study with 1,834 participants and an online experiment with 480 to test whether more creative windows attract more store visits — and the results supported that hypothesis (Journal of Business Research). The study was done in Sweden and was not about the holiday season, but the takeaway is useful: what stops a passer-by is the idea, not the amount of decoration.

The general window rules — one idea, a focal item, lighting, a readable price — are covered in the shop window display article. Here we focus only on what is specific to the holiday season.

New Year shop window calendar: 4 stages

The most common mistake is treating decoration as a one-day job. In practice there are four stages, and the window has a different task in each.

StageApproximate datesThe window's taskWhat you do
1. Preparation15–25 November—Idea, measurements, budget, ordering materials
2. Opening25 November – 5 DecemberSay "gifts are here"Install the decor, put out gift sets
3. Peak15–31 DecemberA quick decision: ready gift, price, opening hoursSwap the focal item, add "last days" copy
4. Closing2–14 JanuaryA winter window or a new collectionRemove holiday symbols, keep the lighting

The dates are approximate — adjust them to your niche. Gift and clothing stores do better opening in late November. For slow-decision purchases such as furniture or home appliances an early start also helps: people want delivery before the New Year.

Note the difference between stages two and three. In early December the shopper is choosing and needs ideas and range. In late December they are in a hurry and need a ready solution, a price and a sign saying "open on 30 December too". One set of decorations, two messages.

Budget: three levels of decoration

There is no single price for decoration — it depends on the window size and materials. But you can split the spending into three levels in advance.

LevelWhat is includedWho it suits
MinimalWarm-white fairy lights, one focal composition, gift boxes, price cardsA small shop with one window
MediumThe above + a backdrop, a separate light on the product, entrance decor, a checkout zoneA street-front shop with 2–3 windows
FullThe above + a decorator, facade lighting, a photo zone, branded packagingA large store or showroom

One rule helps when dividing the money: lighting first, the focal composition second, small decor last. In December it gets dark soon after 5 pm, and that is exactly when the window works hardest. A beautiful display with no light is invisible in the evening.

Illustrative example. A furniture showroom in Tashkent split its decoration budget like this: half on lighting and fairy lights, a third on the focal composition (a laid table and chairs), the rest on price cards and the entrance door. The figures are for illustration only; your proportions may differ.

A tip: good fairy lights, an artificial tree and a backdrop are bought for three or four years, not one. Plan the storage for next season ahead of time — a box, a label, a place.

Store decoration in 6 steps

1. Write the idea in one sentence

"New Year" is a theme, not an idea. The idea is what you tell the shopper: "everything for the family table", "gifts under 500,000 soums", "a warm home for winter". Once the sentence exists, every item in the window should serve it.

2. Keep your brand colour

Everyone has the red, green and gold kit. If your brand colour is blue, build a blue and silver New Year window — people will recognise you from a distance and tell you apart from the neighbours. Decoration should extend your visual identity, not replace it.

3. Don't hide the product behind the decor

More than half of the window should be product. The tree, snow and ornaments are a background. If a person cannot tell what the shop sells within three seconds, there is too much decoration.

4. Show gift sets and prices

In December shoppers walk around asking themselves what to give. Offer a ready answer: 2–3 gift sets, each with a price. The price should be in soums and large enough to read. If you plan to advertise a discount, read about the discount trap first — in the holiday season you can sell without cutting the price.

5. Decorate inside too, but in three spots

You don't have to decorate the whole store. Three spots are enough: the entrance (first impression), the shelf with gift sets and the checkout area. Small gifts and a wrapping service belong at the checkout — add-on purchases often happen there.

6. Photograph it by day and by night

On the day the decoration is finished, shoot the window twice. These photos are Instagram content, a fresh image for your map listing and a reminder for next year.

Rules: facade, language and safety

Check three things before installing anything.

Facade and the design code. Tashkent has had a design code in force since May 2026, and anything that goes on the facade — lettering, illuminated signs, structures — may fall under it. The city administration has launched the Telegram bot @DizaynKodBot, where you can look up the requirements for your street (Spot.uz, 7 November 2026). We found no separate rules for temporary holiday decoration in open sources, so the safest route is to keep the decor inside the glass and to ask the bot or the district administration about the facade. The difference between a storefront sign and outdoor advertising is explained in a separate article.

Copy and prices. "Sale" and "discount" signs and prices in the window should meet advertising requirements: the state language, prices in soums, and clear promotion dates and terms. The main bans are summarised in the article on the advertising law.

Electrics and walkways. Buy factory-made fairy lights and check whether they are rated for outdoor or indoor use, don't plug everything into one extension lead, and switch indoor decor off at night or put it on a timer. The tree and the photo zone must not block the entrance, the exit or the fire extinguisher. If your shop is in a mall, agree the decoration and power connection with the management in advance — they have their own rules.

Linking the window to Instagram and ads

When the window on the street and the post in the feed say the same thing, they reinforce each other. In practice:

  • on opening day — a Reel or Stories showing the decorating process and the result;
  • gift sets — a separate post on the profile and a Highlights section;
  • a QR code in the window — leading to a gift list or a Telegram bot;
  • in ads — the same sets and the same colours as in the window.

What to post week by week is in the New Year content plan, and how to split ad spend is in the December ad budget article. Put the window plan into the same calendar.

Measure the result too. A simple way: the salesperson asks every customer "where did you hear about us?" and marks the answer "saw the window". Compare the number of visitors and receipts for the two weeks before and after the decoration went up.

When to take the decorations down

In Uzbekistan 31 December 2026 has been declared a day off and 1 January is a public holiday (more on working through the holidays). After that people stay in a festive mood for another week or two, but gift buying nearly stops.

So take the decor down in two steps:

  1. 2–5 January: remove "Happy New Year" signs, gift boxes, holiday price cards and promotion copy. Replace the focal item with a new product.
  2. By 10–14 January: pack away the tree and ornaments. Warm lighting and a neutral winter backdrop can stay — they still look right in February.

A tree standing in late January makes a store look neglected. Put the take-down date in the calendar on the day you put the decorations up, and name the person responsible.

6 common mistakes

  • Starting late. Decorations hung on 25 December work for one week.
  • Lots of decor, no product. Pretty, but nobody can tell what is sold here.
  • A dark window in the evening. In December most foot traffic passes after dark.
  • The brand disappears. The same red and green kit as everyone else.
  • No prices or gift sets. Shoppers have to work it out themselves — and they walk on.
  • Decor stays until February. Nobody set a take-down date.

Checklist

  • The idea is written in one sentence
  • Dates for the four stages are in the calendar, with an owner
  • Lighting is planned first and the timer is set
  • More than half of the window is product
  • 2–3 gift sets with prices are visible
  • Copy is in the state language, prices in soums, promotion terms stated
  • Facade requirements checked, mall management informed
  • Fairy lights do not block walkways or the fire extinguisher
  • The window is photographed by day and by night and posted on Instagram
  • The take-down date and storage place are set

Questions and answers

When should the New Year window open?

For most stores, late November or the first days of December. Gifts, clothing and children's goods benefit from an earlier start because people look for presents in advance. Decor that goes up too early — in early November — feels stale by late December, so swap the focal item at the peak stage.

Should we decorate ourselves or hire a decorator?

A small shop with one window can manage alone: an idea, warm lighting, one composition and price cards. A decorator is useful when there is a large window, a facade and a photo zone. Either way, write the idea yourself — a decorator does not know your product better than you do.

Do I need permission to hang fairy lights on the facade?

It depends on the street and the building. In Tashkent, check the design code requirements through @DizaynKodBot or the district administration; in rented premises ask the owner, and in a mall ask the management. We found no separate official guidance on temporary holiday decoration in open sources, so it is better to ask beforehand.

How do I know the window worked?

Three simple indicators: the number of visitors, the number of receipts and the "saw the window" answers. Compare the period before and after the decoration. Traffic rises in the holiday season anyway, so compare with last December as well.

Conclusion

A New Year shop window is not a one-day decoration but a four-stage plan: preparation in November, opening in early December, a quick-decision window in late December and a timely close in January. Write the idea in one sentence, spend the first money on lighting, show the product and the price, and set the take-down date in advance.
