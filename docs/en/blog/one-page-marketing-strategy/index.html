A one-page marketing strategy: a 9-block template

In many businesses the marketing strategy either doesn't exist or is a 40-page document nobody reads. The result is the same: random ads, unplanned content, a budget set by gut feeling. The fix is a one-page strategy. It's equally clear to the owner, the marketer and the sales team, and it gets updated every month. This article gives a 9-block template and an illustrative example of filling it in.

Why one page

  • It gets read. Anyone can read a page in 3 minutes.
  • It forces choices. Space is limited, so you keep only what matters.
  • It gets updated. One page is easy to revisit every month.
  • It aligns people. The owner, marketing and sales look at the same goal.

The 9 blocks

BlockMain question
1. GoalWhat do we want in 3–6 months (in numbers)?
2. Point AWhere are we now?
3. AudienceWho is our core customer?
4. OfferWhy do people choose us?
5. CompetitorsWho do we compete with and how are we different?
6. ChannelsWhere do we find customers?
7. ContentWhat do we talk about, and in what formats?
8. BudgetHow much do we spend, and on what?
9. KPIs and planHow do we measure results, and who does what?

Blocks 1–2. Goal and point A

The goal must be a number with a deadline: not "more customers" but "grow monthly sales from 40 to 70 in 3 months" (illustrative example). Point A is your current numbers: leads, cost per lead, sales, average order, budget. A strategy without point A is a trip without a map. How to gather it — how to fill in a marketing brief.

Block 3. Audience

Write down 1–2 core segments, not everyone:

  • who they are (age, role, situation);
  • what problem they have;
  • what worries them when deciding;
  • where they spend time.

More — target audience and customer persona.

Blocks 4–5. Offer and competitors

The offer in one sentence: what + for whom + what makes it different. Then list 3–5 main competitors with your difference from each in a single line. If you can't find a difference, the strategy's first task is to create one. Helpful reading — unique selling proposition, competitor analysis.

Block 6. Channels

Don't try to be everywhere. Pick 2–3 main channels and write down each one's job:

ChannelJob
Instagram (content)Trust and brand
Meta adsNew leads
TelegramWarming the base and repeat sales
Google / mapsCustomers already searching

Choosing a channel — Google Ads vs Meta Ads.

Block 7. Content

Write down 3–4 core themes and the formats. For example: product and results, customer reviews, expert tips, team and process. Formats: Reels, carousels, Stories. More — Instagram content plan.

Block 8. Budget

Split the budget in two:

  • ad budget — money paid to the platforms;
  • production and team — content, video, specialists.

You can work the budget back from the goal: required sales → required leads → cost per lead. More — planning a marketing budget.

Block 9. KPIs and plan

3–5 key metrics and the main tasks for each month. Also note who owns what: marketing, sales, the owner. Choosing KPIs — KPIs in an agency contract.

Illustrative example: a filled-in strategy

Say, an education centre in Tashkent (illustrative example):

BlockContent
GoalGrow new students from 40 to 70 a month in 3 months
Point A200 leads a month, 20% convert, cost per lead 35,000 so‘m
AudienceStudents aged 16–24 and their parents
OfferA free trial lesson and level-based groups
Competitors4 centres; difference — small groups and teacher videos

ContentTeachers, student results, lesson clips

KPIsLeads, trial-lesson attendance, conversion to sale, cost per student

The numbers are only an example — for your business, start from point A. More on the industry — marketing an education centre.

How to use the strategy

  • Every month — compare results with the KPIs and update next month's tasks.
  • Every quarter — revisit the goal, channels and budget.
  • Every new hire or agency — gets this page on day one.

Common mistakes

  • A strategy with no goal or no numbers.
  • An audience of "everyone".
  • Too many channels at once.
  • Budgeting only for ads and forgetting content production.
  • Writing the strategy and never opening it again.

Questions and answers

Does a small business need a strategy?

Especially so: with a small budget, mistakes cost more. A one-page strategy protects against wasted spend.

Can I write it myself?

Yes — the 9 blocks above are enough. An outside specialist adds an independent view of the market and competitors, plus experience from other industries.

What period should a strategy cover?

Usually 3–6 months with monthly plans. Markets move fast, so revisit longer-term plans every quarter.

Is a strategy the same as a content plan?

No. Strategy is what and why; a content plan is when and how. The content plan follows from the strategy.

Conclusion

A one-page strategy puts goal, point A, audience, offer, competitors, channels, content, budget and KPIs on a single sheet. It gets read, used and updated every month.
