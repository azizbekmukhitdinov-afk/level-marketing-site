Google Ads vs Meta Ads: which is better for a small business

October 22, 2026 · 3 min read

  3. Businesses that suit Meta Ads
  4. How to choose: 4 questions
  5. When to run both
  6. Conclusion

With a limited ad budget, the first question is: spend it on Google, or on Instagram and Facebook? Both bring leads, but they work completely differently. Here is how Google Ads and Meta Ads differ, which businesses each suits and how to choose.

The core difference: demand and supply

Google Ads shows when people are already searching. Someone typed "air conditioner installation Tashkent" — they are looking for the service right now. This is capturing existing demand.

Meta Ads (Instagram, Facebook) shows while people scroll their feed. They may not be looking for anything, but their interests and behaviour resemble your customers. This is creating demand: show the product and spark desire.

Businesses that suit Google Ads

  • Urgent services: plumbers, tow trucks, computer repair — people search when a problem appears.
  • Services with clear demand: dentistry, freight, training courses, driving schools.
  • B2B: equipment, wholesale, corporate services.
  • If your website is ready — Google ads mostly lead to a site.

And before you advertise, don't forget the free option: a complete Google Business Profile on Google Maps brings many customers from local search.

Businesses that suit Meta Ads

  • Visual products: furniture, clothing, cosmetics, restaurants, interiors.
  • Impulse purchases and new products people don't know about yet.
  • Events and promotions: openings, discounts, seasonal offers.
  • Businesses without a website: leads via Direct, instant forms or Telegram.

We cover Instagram ads in detail in our step-by-step guide.

How to choose: 4 questions

  1. Do customers search for you? Yes — start with Google.
  2. Is your product easy to sell by showing it? Yes — Meta.
  3. Do you have a website that collects leads? No — start with Meta (Direct, forms), add Google when the site is ready.
  4. What is your budget? A small budget split across two platforms often gives neither enough data to test. Get stable results on one first.

The method for calculating budget from cost per lead and customers needed is in our ad cost guide.

When to run both

As a business grows, the platforms complement each other. A typical flow:

  • Meta shows the product to new people and sparks interest.
  • Later, interested people search for your brand or product on Google.
  • Google Ads and Maps put you first at exactly that moment.
  • People who visited the site without leaving a lead see ads again via retargeting.

In this setup, judge the overall result — total leads and sales — rather than each platform on its own.

Conclusion

Google Ads captures existing demand; Meta Ads creates new demand. With a small budget, start with the one platform that matches how your customers buy, then add the second once results are stable. At Level Marketing we decide together during strategy which platform and budget fit your business.
