Google Ads vs Meta Ads: which is better for a small business

With a limited ad budget, the first question is: spend it on Google, or on Instagram and Facebook? Both bring leads, but they work completely differently. Here is how Google Ads and Meta Ads differ, which businesses each suits and how to choose.

The core difference: demand and supply

Google Ads shows when people are already searching. Someone typed "air conditioner installation Tashkent" — they are looking for the service right now. This is capturing existing demand.

Meta Ads (Instagram, Facebook) shows while people scroll their feed. They may not be looking for anything, but their interests and behaviour resemble your customers. This is creating demand: show the product and spark desire.

Google AdsMeta Ads
Customer stateActively searchingNot searching yet
Main formatSearch text ads, mapsVideo, images, Reels, Stories
Strength"Hot" leadsVisual products, broad audience
Main workKeywords and adsCreatives and audiences
Leads toUsually website or callDirect, form, Telegram or website

Businesses that suit Google Ads

  • Urgent services: plumbers, tow trucks, computer repair — people search when a problem appears.
  • Services with clear demand: dentistry, freight, training courses, driving schools.
  • B2B: equipment, wholesale, corporate services.
  • If your website is ready — Google ads mostly lead to a site.

And before you advertise, don't forget the free option: a complete Google Business Profile on Google Maps brings many customers from local search.

Businesses that suit Meta Ads

  • Visual products: furniture, clothing, cosmetics, restaurants, interiors.
  • Impulse purchases and new products people don't know about yet.
  • Events and promotions: openings, discounts, seasonal offers.
  • Businesses without a website: leads via Direct, instant forms or Telegram.

How to choose: 4 questions

  1. Do customers search for you? Yes — start with Google.
  2. Is your product easy to sell by showing it? Yes — Meta.
  3. Do you have a website that collects leads? No — start with Meta (Direct, forms), add Google when the site is ready.
  4. What is your budget? A small budget split across two platforms often gives neither enough data to test. Get stable results on one first.

The method for calculating budget from cost per lead and customers needed is in our ad cost guide.

When to run both

As a business grows, the platforms complement each other. A typical flow:

  • Meta shows the product to new people and sparks interest.
  • Later, interested people search for your brand or product on Google.
  • Google Ads and Maps put you first at exactly that moment.
  • People who visited the site without leaving a lead see ads again via retargeting.

In this setup, judge the overall result — total leads and sales — rather than each platform on its own.

A plan for testing both platforms (example)

PeriodGoogle AdsMeta Ads
Weeks 1–2A search campaign with 2–3 ad groupsA leads or messages campaign with 3 creatives
Week 3Add irrelevant queries as negative keywordsKeep the best creative and add new versions
Week 4Compare resultsCompare results

Give both platforms the same budget, and at the end of the month compare the cost per lead and per sale. Next month, move budget towards whichever did better.

Your first Google Ads campaign: what to watch

  • Start with a search campaign only; test broader formats later.
  • Specific keywords, not broad ones, and a negative keyword list from day one.
  • Location and schedule — only where you serve customers and during working hours.
  • Conversion tracking — the form, calls and the messenger button.
  • Call and location assets — so people can call straight from the ad.

Your first Meta Ads campaign: what to watch

Choose leads or messages as the objective, don't narrow the audience too much and prepare 3–5 different creatives. If you have a website, install the Pixel. Above all, reply to leads quickly: customers from Meta are still "colder" and cool off fast. Step-by-step setup is covered in How to set up Instagram ads.

Compare results with the same yardstick

Compare the cost of a qualified lead and a sale, not the cost per click. Tag the source of every lead with UTM tags and your CRM. Remember that the platforms help each other: someone may see your ad on Instagram and later search for your brand on Google. So ask customers "Where did you hear about us?" as well.

Common mistakes

  • Broad keywords on Google without negative keywords.
  • "Boosting" posts on Meta without an objective.
  • Comparing platforms by cost per click.
  • Stopping after three days because "it doesn't work".
  • Sending Google traffic to the home page instead of a specific page.

Questions and answers

With a small budget, where should you start?

If demand already exists — people search for your product — start with Google. If you need to create demand yourself, or the product is visual, start with Meta.

Are Google Maps ads separate?

If you add a location asset in Google Ads, your ads can also appear on Maps. But first complete your Google business listing — see Add your business to maps.

Should you add Yandex too?

If your customers also search on Yandex, yes — especially Russian-speaking and B2B audiences. More in Yandex Direct in Uzbekistan.

Can you run Google Ads without a website?

There are call-only ads, but most of the time you need at least a simple page. One basic landing page — offer, price, reviews and a form — makes Google ads far more effective.

Which is cheaper?

Compare by cost per sale, not cost per click. Sometimes pricier Google clicks produce cheaper sales, because the person is already ready to buy.

Can you start with a Performance Max campaign?

Performance Max shows ads across all of Google's placements automatically and relies on conversion data. While data is scarce, results can be unstable. So start with a search campaign, and once conversions build up, test Performance Max separately.

Should ads run around the clock?

If you can only answer calls during working hours, limit the ads to those hours or collect leads through a form at night. An unanswered call is a customer you paid for and lost.

Conclusion

Google Ads captures existing demand; Meta Ads creates new demand. With a small budget, start with the one platform that matches how your customers buy, then add the second once results are stable. At Level Marketing we decide together during strategy which platform and budget fit your business.
