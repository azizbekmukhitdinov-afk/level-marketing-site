Sales team motivation: base salary, bonus and KPIs

When the sales team underperforms, many owners raise the commission. It works for a month or two, then things slip back or a new problem appears: reps hand out discounts, close difficult leads as "low quality" and push deals into next month. The reason is simple: a motivation system steers behaviour, and the wrong system rewards the wrong behaviour. This article shows how to combine base pay, bonus and KPIs, which scheme fits which business, and what works beyond money.

Motivation has three layers

To a rep, "pay" is one number. As a system, split it into three parts:

LayerWhy it is neededWhat it drives
Base salary (fixed)Stability, keeping good peopleShowing up, following the rules
Variable pay (bonus, commission)Focus on resultsSales volume, average order, margin
Non-cash motivationLong-term loyaltyStaff turnover, learning, team atmosphere

Many companies only work with the second layer. As a result a strong rep moves wherever "the commission is higher", because nothing else holds them.

Base-to-bonus ratio depends on the business

There is no universal split. The decision rule: the more a rep influences the result and the faster a deal closes, the larger the variable part can be.

Business typeSales cycleRough base : bonusWhy
Retail, selling at the tillMinutes70 : 30The customer comes in anyway, the seller's influence is limited
Selling to leads from ads (education, services)Days50 : 50The result depends heavily on the rep
Expensive goods, real estate, custom furnitureWeeks40 : 60Each deal is large, negotiation matters
B2B with a long cycleMonths60 : 40Results arrive late; without a base people quit

These figures are an illustrative guide, not a rule. What matters is the logic: if the sales cycle is three months and the bonus is monthly, a new rep lives on base pay alone for the first months and leaves.

Four bonus schemes

SchemeHow it worksStrengthRisk
Flat commission on salesThe same % on every saleSimple and clearNo push towards a plan, discounts grow
Plan-attainment scaleThe % changes with plan attainmentFocus on the planA "jump" at each threshold, deals get shifted
Commission on margin% of profit, not revenueStops discounting by itselfReps must know and trust the margin figure
Team bonusEveryone gets it if the team hits planMutual helpUsed alone, strong performers resent it

In practice a mix works best: a personal scale plus a small team bonus. If you pay a share of revenue to reps who can give discounts, they are effectively buying their bonus with your margin. In that case tie the bonus to margin, or make discounts require a manager's approval.

Choosing KPIs: few, but precise

A bonus based only on sales value sees the result but not how it was achieved. So 2–3 process KPIs are added to the result KPI. The general idea is covered in What is a KPI?.

Four signs of a good KPI:

  • the rep can influence it directly (the ad budget is not their KPI);
  • it comes from the CRM automatically, not typed in by hand;
  • it is hard to game ("number of calls" is easy to inflate, "lead to meeting" much harder);
  • there are no more than four in total, otherwise the rep does not know what to focus on.

An illustrative example:

KPIWeightSource
Plan attainment (sales with payment received)60%CRM + till
First response time to a lead15%CRM
Lead → meeting conversion15%CRM
CRM hygiene (statuses, loss reasons)10%CRM review

Why response speed deserves its own KPI is explained in lead response time. Without a CRM you cannot run this table at all, so settle which CRM suits a small business first.

Illustrative example: the calculation

The numbers are invented and only show the mechanics.

A company has three reps. Each rep's monthly plan is 200 million soums of sales with payment received. Base pay is 4 million soums. The scale:

Plan attainmentBonus on actual sales
Below 70%0%
70–99%1.5%
100–119%2.5%
120% and above3%

A process multiplier is added on top: 1.1 if process KPIs are good, 1.0 if normal, 0.8 if poor.

Rep ARep BRep C

Now two important checks.

First: the cost of the sales team. The total payroll is 20.2 million soums against 510 million soums of sales, about 4%. If your gross margin is 25%, the sales team takes roughly 16% of gross profit. Whether that is acceptable is a call for your financial plan, but you need to know the number.

Second: the threshold jump. At 199 million soums the rep earns 1.5%, or 2,985,000 soums. At 200 million it is 2.5%, or 5,000,000 soums. One small deal lifts the bonus by more than 2 million. That is strong motivation, but at month end a rep may "hold" a deal for next month or close it with an unnecessary discount. Fixes: a progressive calculation (the higher rate applies only to the part above the threshold), an extra quarterly bonus, or discounts approved by a manager.

Non-cash motivation

Money brings a rep in, but other things often keep them:

  • Fair lead distribution. If "hot" leads always go to one person, the rest lose motivation. Round-robin distribution is the cheapest motivator there is.
  • A visible career path. Trainee → rep → senior rep → team lead, with clear criteria for each step: how many months, what conversion, which skills.
  • Learning. A weekly call review with examples of good conversations. How to review recordings: AI call analysis.
  • Recognition. Naming a good result at the weekly meeting. It costs nothing, yet many managers forget. How to run that meeting: the daily sales meeting.
  • Decent tools. A slow CRM, a broken phone and no saved replies annoy people more than a small bonus pleases them.

Rewarding long-term results

A plain monthly bonus only sees "today's sale". If your business depends on repeat purchases, it makes sense to tie part of the bonus to customers coming back. At Level Marketing, specialists' bonuses are tied to whether the client renews the contract, because for an agency the main result is not one month but a long partnership. In a sales team this can mean a separate rate for repeat purchases, membership renewals or returning customers. The economics of repeat sales: repeat sales and LTV.

Common mistakes

  • Changing the scheme mid-month. Do it once and reps will not trust the next scheme.
  • Paying the bonus late. A bonus paid two months later is no longer linked to the result.
  • Paying for "agreed". Count the bonus only when money arrives, or you will pay for returns too.
  • Penalties only. A penalty system brings order quickly but drives good people out over time.
  • Only the manager knows the maths. Reps should see their bonus during the month, in the CRM or a simple sheet.

How to roll out a new system

  • 1. Calculate the last 3 months: each rep's sales, conversion and pay.
  • 2. Apply the new scheme to those 3 months: who would have earned what, and what the total payroll would have been.
  • 3. Write the scheme on one page and discuss it with the reps.
  • 4. Run a 2–3 month trial and guarantee that nobody's pay drops sharply.
  • 5. Adjust once after the trial, then leave it unchanged for at least six months.

Questions and answers

Can I pay a rep commission only?

You can, but it rarely works: only with a large, stable lead flow and an experienced person. More often a commission-only rep leaves after the first weak month, and you spend time hiring and training again.

Should the bonus be monthly or quarterly?

The main bonus should be monthly, because reps need to see results quickly. In long-cycle businesses a quarterly bonus is added on top; it reduces the shifting of deals from month to month.

If ads bring few leads, is the rep to blame?

No. That is why you track conversion as well as sales value. Few leads is a marketing question; low conversion is a sales question.

Do new reps need a separate scheme?

Yes. In the first 1–2 months the plan is reduced or the base is slightly higher. Otherwise a new rep earns no bonus while learning and loses motivation at the start.

Conclusion

Sales team motivation is not a percentage but a system: a stable base, a bonus linked to plan and margin, 3–4 KPIs pulled from the CRM, and non-cash motivation. Test any scheme on past months' numbers first, check the threshold jumps and do not change it too often.
