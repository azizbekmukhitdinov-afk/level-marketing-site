After-hours leads: replying at night and on weekends

Ads run around the clock, but the sales team usually works from 9:00 to 18:00. As a result, after-hours leads — requests left at night and on weekends — sit unanswered until morning, and many of those people go to another company in the meantime. This article covers four tools for handling leads outside working hours, what to promise in an auto-reply and how to measure response time.

Why night-time leads are a separate problem

People see ads after work, after dinner, lying on the sofa. That's exactly when they leave a request. But if the reply comes the next day at 10:00, three things happen:

  • interest fades — in the evening the person needed it "now", in the morning they're busy with other things;
  • a competitor gets there first — people often submit requests to two or three companies at once and pick whoever answers first;
  • the person forgets — when you call in the morning they ask "what request?" or don't pick up at all.

How response speed affects sales is covered in detail in our article on lead response time. Here we only look at the out-of-hours situation.

Four tools: which one fits you

ToolWhat it doesWho it suits

On-duty repA real person replies in the evening and at weekendsUrgent services: clinics, repair, delivery

These tools don't replace each other. The minimum set is an auto-reply plus a morning queue. If you get lots of questions, add an AI assistant; for urgent services you need someone on duty.

The auto-reply: what to write and what to promise

An auto-reply is the message that goes out the moment a request is submitted: via Telegram, SMS or the thank-you screen at the end of a lead form. Its job is to make sure the person isn't left hanging and to hold their interest until morning.

A good auto-reply has five elements:

  • thanks and a name — "Thank you, Dilnoza, we've received your request";
  • an exact time — "Our manager will contact you tomorrow by 9:30";
  • something useful right now — a link to the price list, catalogue or portfolio;
  • one question — "Which room is the furniture for: kitchen, bedroom or living room?";
  • an urgent route — "If it's urgent, write here and the person on duty will reply" (only if someone is on duty).

Template (for a furniture store):

  • Thank you, Dilnoza! We've received your request.
  • We're closed for the day; our manager will call you tomorrow by 9:30.
  • Prices and a catalogue of finished projects: link.
  • To save time: which room is the furniture for, and roughly what size?

Never promise in an auto-reply what you can't deliver: writing "we'll contact you in 5 minutes" and calling the next morning destroys trust instantly.

One more rule: you can give a "from…" price in an auto-reply, but don't promise a final price. The rep gives the exact figure once they understand the need.

When to call and when to message

Our site has a rule for cold calls: only between 9:00 and 18:00 (see the article on cold calling). Someone who left a request is a different case: they asked to be contacted. But that doesn't mean you can call them at 23:00.

Time of requestWhat to do
18:00–21:00Someone on duty — call or message; nobody — auto-reply
21:00–09:00Written message only; call in the morning
Saturday–SundayThe on-duty rep messages; call only if the person named a convenient time
Public holidaysState the exact date you'll be back in the auto-reply

In the evening a message works better than a call: people read it when it suits them and reply. Often they answer that same night, and the morning call becomes a prepared conversation.

The morning queue: night leads first

The most common mistake with night leads is mixing them with daytime leads in the morning. The result: yesterday's request waits until lunch. A simple routine prevents this:

  1. 09:00–10:00 — only night and weekend leads. Reps don't switch to anything else.
  2. Order by level of interest. Those who replied to the message overnight and answered the question go first.
  3. Open with yesterday's context: "Last night at 22:00 you left a request for kitchen furniture and even gave us the size — thank you."
  4. No answer — message and a second attempt. If they don't pick up, send a Telegram message right away and call again after lunch.
  5. Record the status in the CRM. Every lead needs an outcome: contacted, meeting booked, declined, wrong number.

If people still don't pick up in the morning, the cause may lie elsewhere — the form, the creative or the audience. That's covered in the article on why leads don't answer the phone.

The AI assistant and the on-duty rep

An AI assistant can handle most night-time questions: price range, address, opening hours, delivery, free booking slots. It collects the details you need, and in the morning the rep gets a ready lead card. How to build one for Telegram is described in detail in the article on a Telegram AI assistant.

Three rules for the AI:

  • it answers only from the knowledge base you provide and never invents prices or terms;
  • for a complex question or a complaint, it says "a manager will sort this out tomorrow";
  • read its conversations every week and fix wrong answers.

An on-duty rep is needed in urgent services: clinics, repair and handyman work, freight, food delivery. Agree on separate pay for being on duty and a clear schedule — for example, weekdays 18:00–22:00 plus Saturday. The person on duty replies to every lead but can leave final decisions on price and contracts until the morning.

How to measure response time

If you don't measure it, you won't know whether it improved. Three metrics are enough:

MetricWhat it showsIllustrative target
Time to first response (automatic)Whether the auto-reply worksUnder 1 minute
Time to first human responseHow quickly a rep gets involved15 minutes in working hours; night leads by 10:00
Conversion of after-hours leadsHow far night leads lag behind daytime onesClose the gap with daytime

The targets are illustrative — start from your own baseline. Calculate the metrics separately for leads inside and outside working hours, otherwise the average hides the problem. Compare once a month and add them to your monthly report.

Common mistakes

  • No auto-reply at all — people don't know whether their request arrived.
  • The auto-reply says "we'll be in touch soon" without giving a time.
  • Calling at 23:00 — the person gets annoyed.
  • Night leads get lost among daytime leads in the morning.
  • The AI assistant makes up prices and nobody checks.
  • Response time isn't measured, and everyone assumes "we reply quickly".

Questions and answers

When is the right time to call someone who left a request at night?

In the morning, right after the working day starts — between 9:00 and 10:00. At night, send a written auto-reply immediately. If the person mentioned a convenient time in their message, call at exactly that time.

Does a small business need an on-duty rep?

Usually not. An auto-reply and a morning queue are enough. Having someone on duty pays off for urgent services or when the share of evening leads is large. Count first, then decide.

What if we just switch the ads off at night?

That's an option, but don't rush. Evening leads are often good quality — people are relaxed and making a considered decision. First get your handling of them right, then adjust the ad schedule based on the data.

Can an AI assistant replace a sales rep?

No. It answers questions and collects details, but trust, negotiation and closing the deal stay with a person. The AI covers the night-time gap; the rep closes the sale.

Conclusion

After-hours leads are part of your ad spend. You don't need a complex system to stop losing them: an instant auto-reply with an exact time, a message rather than a call at night, night leads handled first between 9:00 and 10:00, and an AI assistant or on-duty rep where needed. Above all, measure response time and conversion for out-of-hours leads separately.
