Holiday pay in Uzbekistan: a short guide for employers

In late December, shops, restaurants and service businesses all face the same question: who works over the holidays, and how much do they get paid? Holiday pay in Uzbekistan is set out clearly in the Labour Code, yet many owners settle it with a verbal promise and end up arguing with staff later. This article covers the rules in plain language, a sample calculation and a ready-made process for 31 December and 1 January.

This is not legal advice. The rules come from official sources, but check your own situation with an accountant or lawyer.

End of 2026: which days are off

Start with the calendar. Of the non-working public holidays listed in the Labour Code, two fall in December–January: 8 December (Constitution Day) and 1 January (New Year). On top of that, a presidential decree of 24 December 2025 set extra days off and day swaps for 2026 (president.uz).

For a team on a five-day week this means Saturday 12 December is a full working day, while 31 December to 3 January is four days off in a row. The decree on extra days off for 2027 usually comes out in late December, so keep an eye on president.uz.

The catch for retail is that these are exactly the days when customers come in most. Someone has to work, and that has to be done by the book.

Can you call staff in on a holiday?

The general rule: work on days off and non-working holidays is prohibited, except in cases set out in the Code (gov.uz). For a small business, three cases matter:

  1. Serving the public. On non-working holidays, work serving the public, continuous operations and work that cannot be stopped for technical reasons are allowed. Shops, cafés, pharmacies and car services usually fall here.
  2. Unforeseen urgent work. If the normal running of the business or a unit depends on it, an employee can be called in with their written consent. The list of such work is set in the collective agreement, or, for a sole trader, in the employment contract.
  3. By rota. In shift work, days off follow the rota. If a holiday falls on a rostered working day, the employee works, but is paid at holiday rates.

In practice, the safest route is written consent from each employee and an order calling them in on the holiday. The Code has separate restrictions for staff under 18 and certain other groups, so check the text on lex.uz before putting them on the holiday rota.

Holiday pay: who gets what

Pay is governed by Article 263 of the Labour Code. According to the official explanation on gov.uz, the rules are as follows (gov.uz):

How the employee is paidFor work on a day off or public holiday
Piece rate (per item, per order)at least double piece rates
Daily or hourly rateat least double the daily or hourly rate
Monthly salaryon top of salary, at least one daily or hourly rate for each day or hour worked

That is the floor. A collective agreement, internal policy or employment contract can set a higher supplement, never a lower one.

Illustrative example: a salaried sales assistant

Illustrative example. A furniture store pays a sales assistant 6,000,000 soums a month, and the month has 20 working days. Daily rate: 6,000,000 / 20 = 300,000 soums. The assistant works 8 hours on 1 January. On top of the usual salary they get at least 300,000 soums. If they work both 31 December and 1 January, at least 600,000 soums.

How you calculate the daily rate (by working days or working hours in the month) depends on your accounting method. What matters is that it is the same for everyone and announced in advance.

One more point: the working day before a holiday is usually one hour shorter. Plan the store schedule for 30 December accordingly, or provide the legal compensation for that hour.

A day off instead of money: when it works

At the employee's request, work on a holiday or day off can be compensated with another day off. In that case the work is paid at no less than the single rate, and the day off itself is unpaid.

When this option suits both sides:

  • January is quiet. In many shops, footfall drops after the first days of January (we wrote separately about the January sales slump). Giving a sales assistant a day off in mid-January costs the payroll little and means a lot to the employee.
  • The employee asks for it. For a student or a young parent, a free day may matter more than money.

Important: the choice belongs to the employee. An order saying "everyone gets a day off instead of pay" goes against the spirit of the law. Get each employee's choice in writing.

Holiday duty rota: a 5-step process

Do this in early December and you will avoid the "who's coming in?" argument on the 31st.

  1. Estimate the workload. Use last year's till data and enquiries to see which days and hours are busiest. For a sales team, this calculation is covered in detail in sales team during the holidays.
  2. Ask for preferences. Who is happy to work, who wants pay, who wants a day off: collect it in a simple table. If you have enough volunteers, you will not need to push anyone.
  3. Rotate. No one should work 31 December, 1 January and 2 January all together. You can split days into half-shifts.
  4. Put it on paper. Written consent, the order calling staff in, and the chosen compensation (pay or a day off). Mark holiday hours separately on the timesheet.
  5. Announce in advance and pay on time. State the amount before the duty starts and show it as a separate line in the December–January payroll.

For hours with nobody on duty, set up automated responses on Instagram and Telegram: staff get their rest and customers still get a reply.

5 common employer mistakes

  • A verbal deal. "Come in on the holiday and I'll pay you extra" is a promise with no amount. By January everyone remembers the "extra" differently.
  • Not paying salaried staff extra. "They're on a salary anyway" is wrong: a salaried employee is paid for a holiday on top of their salary.
  • No consent. Calling someone in for urgent work requires written consent. Without it, the employer is in a weak position in any dispute.
  • The same people every year. If your most experienced sales assistant works every holiday, they burn out and leave. Keep a record and rotate year to year.
  • Forgetting 12 December. On a five-day week, Saturday 12 December is a working day. Build it into the rota, or you will come up a day short.

Questions and answers

Is 31 December a public holiday or an ordinary day off?

In 2026, 31 December is not on the Code's list of holidays, but a presidential decree made it a day off: on a five-day week it was moved from 12 December, and on a six-day week it is an extra day off. Anyone who works that day is paid under the rules for working on a day off.

How much does a piece-rate craftsman get?

For work on a holiday or day off, at least double piece rates. In other words, the usual amount per order at least doubles.

An employee chose a day off instead of pay. Can they change their mind?

The law sets no specific procedure for this. So record the choice in writing and agree in advance by when you will give the day off, for example by the end of January.

Does a restaurant or shop need permission to open on a holiday?

The law allows work serving the public on non-working holidays. But staff hours and pay are still calculated under the holiday rules.

Conclusion

Holiday pay rests on three rules: calling staff in is documented, pay is at least double (for salaried staff, at least one daily rate on top of salary) or, if the employee prefers, another day off, and everything is agreed in advance. Draw up the duty rota in early December and announce the amounts, and the holidays become extra income for your team rather than a source of stress.
