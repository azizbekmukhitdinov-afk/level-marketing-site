Music for video ads: where to get it and 5 mistakes

Your Reel with a trending song did well, you tried to run it as an ad — and the system rejected the music. That is not a glitch, it is the rule. Music for video ads comes from a different place than music for an ordinary post. This article compares 5 legitimate sources, lists what to check in a licence and covers the 5 most common mistakes.

Why trending music does not work in ads

The big music library inside Instagram and TikTok — the one with famous songs — is cleared with rights holders for personal, non-commercial use. When a business sells a product to that song, the purpose is commercial and needs separate permission.

So there are three situations:

SituationTrending songWhy
Ordinary Reel on a personal accountUsually fineThat is what the platform library is for
Organic post on a business accountOften restrictedSome business accounts get no access to the licensed library
Paid ad (Ads Manager, the "Boost" button)Not allowedCommercial use, a separate licence is needed

Meta says so directly: its requirements for Reels ads state that ads must not contain licensed music, and it recommends original audio or royalty-free music from the Meta Sound Collection instead (Meta ads guide).

In practice, when you try to boost a Reel with a trending song, the system may ask you to replace the music or refuse the ad. The worst part: the video is already edited, the cuts follow the beat of that song, and everything has to be rebuilt.

It is not only a platform rule either. Music is a work protected by copyright, and using it requires the rights holder's permission (Uzbekistan's Law on Copyright and Related Rights). How rights to content are divided in general is covered in content copyright.

1. Meta Sound Collection

This is Meta's own library of tracks and sound effects, and it is what Meta recommends for ads. You download a track, add it in your editing app and upload the finished file to Ads Manager.

The downside: there are no famous songs, only background music. But that is what an ad needs — music sets the mood, while text and voice carry the message.

2. TikTok Commercial Music Library

TikTok also has two libraries: the general one (trending songs) and the commercial one. According to TikTok, tracks in the Commercial Music Library are pre-cleared for branded content and ads, and Business Account holders access it inside the app (TikTok for Business). TikTok also warns that promoting a business through a personal account with music from the general library carries copyright risk.

One important point: one platform's library gives you no rights on another platform. Before you put a video with a TikTok library track into an Instagram ad, read the terms — usually it is not allowed. For getting started with TikTok advertising, see TikTok Ads in Uzbekistan.

3. Paid stock library

If the same video runs on Instagram, TikTok, YouTube and a screen in your shop, a paid music library is the most convenient route. You pay for a subscription or a single track and receive a licence.

This is where most mistakes happen: "royalty-free" does not mean "free" or "allowed for anything". It only means you do not pay per play. Whether you can use the track in advertising is decided by the licence text (see the list below).

4. Custom track

For a brand film or a campaign that runs for months, you can commission music from a composer or sound designer. The advantage is that nobody else has it. But the rights do not pass to you automatically: the contract must say where, for how long and in what ways you may use the track.

AI-generated music sits close to this group: whether commercial use is allowed depends on the service's plan and terms. On free plans it often is not. More in AI and the law in Uzbekistan.

5. Video without music

The most underrated option. A specialist talking, the sound of a saw, a box being opened, kitchen noise — none of it needs a licence, and it often works better than music. When to use speech and when music is covered in sound in video ads.

A separate note on the YouTube Audio Library: YouTube calls it "copyright-safe" for videos on its own platform, and some tracks require crediting the artist (YouTube Help). Before using such a track in an ad on another platform, check that track's terms.

What to check in a licence: 6 points

If you work with a paid library or a composer, find answers to these questions before paying:

  1. Is advertising allowed? A "personal" or "social media" plan often does not cover paid ads. Look for the words "commercial" and "advertising".
  2. Which platforms? Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, website, in-store screen, television — each may be listed separately.
  3. Term. What happens to old videos when the subscription ends? In some libraries a video published during the subscription stays covered, in others it does not.
  4. In whose name? If the licence belongs to the agency, does it cover your ad account? Use for a client may be a separate plan.
  5. Territory. Most licences are worldwide, but it is worth checking.
  6. Paperwork. Keep the licence certificate or receipt. If a platform questions the music, that is the document you will need.

If you work with an agency or a videographer, add one sentence to the contract: the contractor guarantees the music was obtained legally and the licence permits advertising use, and hands over the document together with the video.

About the last row: you can take part in trends, you just need two versions. One for the organic Reel with audio the platform permits, one for ads with different music or voice. Adapting trends to a business is covered in how to use trends.

Illustrative example: a furniture shop

A furniture shop in Tashkent made a 20-second video about a new kitchen set (this is an illustrative example; the numbers are for explanation only).

  • The editor cut the video to the beat of a trending Instagram song. As an organic Reel it turned out well.
  • When it went into ads, the music did not pass. Without the song the cuts lost their rhythm and the video looked "empty".
  • Re-editing took another day.

The right order looks like this: before the shoot, write down where the video will run (Instagram ads plus the in-store screen), pick the music source to match (paid stock with an advertising licence), and edit to that track from the start. The trend version is assembled separately for organic. Fifteen minutes of planning instead of a day of rework.

5 common mistakes

  • Choosing music at the end of editing. The cuts follow one track, and then it fails in ads. Settle the source before the shoot.
  • Reading "royalty-free" as "free and unrestricted". The track is downloaded and nobody reads the licence.
  • The "up to 30 seconds is fine" myth. A short excerpt also needs permission — there is no safe length.
  • Using a track from one platform's library on another. Each library is for its own platform.
  • Not keeping the paperwork. The editor left, the subscription was in their name, and nobody knows where the licence is.

Questions and answers

Can I boost a Reel with a trending song?

Under Meta's requirements, Reels ads must not contain licensed music. So to promote that post you will need to replace the music or upload another version of the video.

What if we sing or play it ourselves?

The performance is yours, but the song itself — melody and lyrics — belongs to its author. A cover of a famous song also needs permission. An original tune you composed yourself is fine.

Can we use a song by a local Uzbek artist?

Only with a written agreement with the rights holder — the performer, the author or the production company. Being local does not make a song free to use.

Does a video without music perform worse?

It depends on the type of video. Meta strongly recommends sound for Reels ads, but sound is not only music: live speech and natural sound count too. A test will show which works better for you.

Conclusion

Music for video ads is not the last decoration in editing but one of the first lines of the plan. First write down where the video will run, then choose a matching source: Sound Collection for Meta, the Commercial Music Library for TikTok, a paid track with an advertising licence for several platforms, or no music at all. Keep the paperwork and add a music clause to the contract. At Level Marketing we decide on the music source at the script stage, so the finished video does not get stuck in the ad account.
