Marketing a cosmetics store: selling through advice

Cosmetics is one of the most competitive markets: the same brands are sold in dozens of shops, on marketplaces and on Instagram. Competing on price eats your margin. What sets a cosmetics store apart is advice and trust — the answer to "what will actually suit me?". Here's how to build that kind of marketing.

Customers' two main fears

  1. "What if it's fake?" — trust in genuine products.
  2. "What if it doesn't suit me?" — skin type, allergies, results.

Every element of your marketing should answer these two questions.

Proving products are genuine

  • Information about the official distributor or supplier (if you have one).
  • Videos showing packaging, batch numbers and expiry dates.
  • Open return and exchange terms.
  • Useful content on "How to tell a genuine product from a fake".

Advice is the main product

  • Matching by skin type: "a 3-step routine for dry skin", sold as bundles.
  • A consultant in store and in DMs, giving fast, precise answers — handling comments and DMs.
  • A chance to try: samples or testers make buying easier.

Content that works

  • Application videos: how to apply it, with close-up results.
  • Before/after — real ones only, with the customer's consent, and no "miracle" promises.
  • Ingredient explainers: "what retinol is and who needs it".
  • Q&A Reels answering customers' real questions — content from customer objections.

Exaggerating results in ads may count as misleading advertising — advertising law in Uzbekistan.

Influencers

Cosmetics is the category where influencer advertising works best. But the audience must match, and results should be measured with promo codes — influencer advertising, promo codes in marketing.

Marketplaces vs your own channels

Marketplaces such as Uzum bring new customers, but the customer base doesn't stay with you. The balance: marketplaces for discovery, Instagram and Telegram for regulars and advice — building a brand on Uzum Market.

Repeat purchases

Cosmetics run out, so customers naturally return. Make the most of it:

  • reminders timed to when a product runs out (with consent);
  • bonus points — loyalty programmes;
  • early access to new collections.

Questions and answers

Should we compete with marketplaces on price?

Usually not. Advice, testers, fast delivery and trust are stronger advantages than price.

Which ad format works best?

Application and results videos aimed at a local audience, with the goal of a consultation via DM or Telegram.

How do we collect reviews?

Ask 1–2 weeks after purchase, once results are visible — collecting customer feedback.

Conclusion

Cosmetics store marketing means trust in genuine products, advice matched to skin type, application videos, measurable influencer campaigns and a repeat-purchase system. Advice sells, not price. In a free audit, Level Marketing will review your page and ads.
