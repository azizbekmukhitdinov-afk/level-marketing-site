A sales script and objection handling with AI

Many small businesses have no sales script — or one was written once and sits in a file nobody opens. So every salesperson improvises: one sells, another loses the customer. AI turns writing a script into a few hours' work — and, more importantly, helps you train your team. Here's how to build a sales script with AI, how to test it and where AI gets it wrong.

What to give the AI

Script quality depends on context. Material for the AI:

  • product and prices: what, how much, what makes it different;
  • the customer: who they are, where they come from (ads, referrals), what they expect;
  • your best salesperson's conversations: 3–5 chats or call transcripts (anonymised);
  • objections: the 10 most frequent, in the customer's words — content from customer objections;
  • the conversation's goal: a meeting, a measurement visit, an order or payment.

Customers' names, phone numbers and other personal data don't go to the AI — anonymise the chats. More in AI and the law in Uzbekistan.

Script structure: 6 stages

Ask the AI to build the script around these stages:

StageGoalWhat the AI writes
1. GreetingThe customer knows who's writing2–3 options, using their name
2. DiscoveryLearn what's needed with 2–3 questionsA list of questions
3. OfferAn option that fits the needA short description + price with context
4. ObjectionsAnswer doubts2–3 answers per objection
5. Next stepA concrete actionProposing a meeting, measurement, order
6. Follow-upIf they say "I'll think about it"14 days of messages

The objection bank: where AI helps most

Prompt:

The customer said: "[objection]". Our product: [short description]. Write 3 different replies without arguing or pressure: one using a question, one using proof, one offering an alternative. 2–3 sentences each.

Three replies per objection — the salesperson picks one to suit the situation. But edit the AI's answers together with your team: they know which phrases work with real customers.

Role-play: AI plays the customer

This is the most underrated use. Prompt:

You're a customer who wants to buy [product] but has doubts about the price. Keep answers short and raise objections now and then. I'm the salesperson. At the end, give me 3 tips: what went well and what could be better.

If a new salesperson practises like this for 20–30 minutes, their first real conversations go far more confidently. Create different "customers": rushed, sceptical, comparison shopping, consulting their family — family purchase decisions.

Call analysis

If calls are recorded (with customers informed) and transcribed, AI can help analyse them:

  • did the salesperson go through the script stages;
  • was a next step offered;
  • which objection went unanswered.

Important: anonymise transcripts and discuss the findings with the salesperson — the AI's conclusion isn't a grade, it's a starting point for a conversation. Measuring salespeople's results — sales rep conversion.

Where AI goes wrong

  • Overly "salesy" language: "Today only!", "Don't miss this unique opportunity" — pressure that puts customers off.
  • Invented facts: a warranty, deadline or discount that doesn't exist.
  • A translated tone: Uzbek replies sound artificial.
  • No local context: instalments, delivery, the regions — AI doesn't know.

So every script is read by your best salesperson and a manager before use.

Rollout: 2 weeks

  1. Days 1–2: gather material and draft with AI.
  2. Days 3–4: edit with the sales team.
  3. End of week 1: role-play, 3–5 conversations per salesperson.
  4. Week 2: test in real conversations, with a short daily debrief.
  5. End of week 2: compare conversion with the previous period.

Common mistakes

  • Writing the script with AI without the sales team and handing it down "as an order".
  • Requiring word-for-word reading — customers sense a robot.
  • Entering customer data into the AI.
  • Writing the script once and never updating it.
  • Not measuring results.

Questions and answers

Is an AI script better than an experienced salesperson?

No. The best script is your best salesperson's experience; AI quickly turns it into a structure and helps teach others.

How often should the script be updated?

Every quarter, or when prices, the product or the main objections change.

Can role-play be done in Uzbek?

Yes, but AI replies are sometimes unnatural. You can also do it in Russian or a mix — whatever language your customers use.

Does it work for Telegram chats?

Yes. A written script is shorter: short messages, one question, a clear next step.

Conclusion

AI builds a sales script quickly, fills the objection bank and trains your team through role-play. But the script's foundation is your best salesperson's experience and real customer questions, and the final edit is human.
