Competitor analysis: what you can learn in one day

You don't need expensive research to understand your market: in a single working day, open sources can tell you a great deal about competitors. The goal isn't to copy them but to find what they do badly and how you can stand out. Here is a one-day plan and a summary table.

Morning: find 5–7 competitors

  • Google and Yandex searches for your main queries.
  • "Near me" searches in Google and Yandex Maps — we have a separate article on maps.
  • Instagram search and hashtags, Telegram channels.
  • If you sell products — marketplaces such as Uzum Market.
  • Ask your customers: "Who did you compare us with?"

Split competitors into two groups: direct (same product, same audience) and indirect (a different product that solves the same problem).

In the Meta Ad Library (facebook.com/ads/library), choose Uzbekistan as the country and enter a competitor's page name: you'll see their active ads and when each one started running. An ad that has been running for a long time is probably working.

Afternoon: mystery shopping and reviews

Message a competitor as an ordinary customer: how fast did they reply, did they ask questions, did they follow up later? Compare it with your own service — see our article on lead response time.

Complaints in reviews are your opportunity. If customers complain about a competitor's late deliveries, fast delivery could become your USP.

The last column matters most: every competitor weakness is something you can do better. For example, to help the Global Avenue residential complex stand out from competitors, a unique offer for the market was developed together with the client — a 3-year interest-free instalment plan.

Ground rules

Use only public information. Don't copy texts, photos or designs — the analysis is for understanding the market, not for cloning it.

Conclusion

Repeat your competitor analysis every 3–6 months: markets change and new players appear. The findings feed your USP, ad copy and pricing. The next step is to define who you're selling to: target audience and customer persona.
