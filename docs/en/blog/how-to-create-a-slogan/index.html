How to create a slogan for your business: a memorable line

A slogan is a brand's short voice: next to the logo, on the sign, at the end of an ad. A good slogan helps people remember the brand and tell it apart from others. But many small-business slogans sound alike: "Quality is our motto", "The best for you". Here's how to create a slogan people remember.

When you need a slogan — and when you don't

A slogan is a short expression of your positioning. Positioning comes first, then the slogan — brand positioning. If the positioning isn't clear, the slogan will be pretty but empty.

For a small business, a clear description sometimes works better than a slogan: "Custom kitchens — in 14 days" (an illustrative example). It does a slogan's job too.

Signs of a good slogan

  • Short: 2–6 words.
  • States your difference: it wouldn't fit a competitor.
  • Easy to say and remember.
  • True: the promise can be proven.
  • In your audience's language.

The test: swap your brand name for a competitor's. If nothing changes, the slogan doesn't work.

6 types of slogan

  1. Benefit: what you get — "A new kitchen in 14 days".
  2. Difference: how you're different — "We make it ourselves".
  3. Audience: who it's for — "Furniture for young families".
  4. Action: a call — "Refresh your home".
  5. Emotion: a feeling — "Comfort every day".
  6. Wordplay: rhyme or a pun — easy to remember, but keep the meaning.

(All illustrative examples.)

The creation process

  1. Raw material: your positioning, unique offer and words from customer reviews — unique selling proposition.
  2. Lots of options: 30–50, without judging.
  3. Filter down to 5 using the signs above.
  4. Test on customers and the team — which one stuck? A/B test them in ads — A/B testing ad creatives.
  5. Apply it consistently: logo, website, bio, ads.

AI can help with ideas, but choosing and checking are up to you — marketing prompts.

In two languages

  • a strong slogan in one language and a matching meaning in the other — not a literal translation — AI translation for business;
  • or a short line tied to the brand name that needs no translation.

Legal checks

  • Don't use "the best" or "No. 1" without proof.
  • Don't repeat another brand's slogan or trademark.
  • For an important slogan, discuss trademark registration with a lawyer.

Common mistakes

  • Words everyone uses: quality, reliability, professionalism.
  • Too long or unclear.
  • An English slogan your audience doesn't understand.
  • A new slogan every year, so none sticks.

Questions and answers

Is a slogan mandatory?

No. Clear positioning and a strong offer matter more.

When should I change my slogan?
