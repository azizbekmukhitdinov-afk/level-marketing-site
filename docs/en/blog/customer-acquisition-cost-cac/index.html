Customer acquisition cost (CAC): how to calculate and lower it

Many business owners judge advertising by cost per lead. But a lead isn't money yet. The real business question is different: how much does one new paying customer cost me? That metric is CAC — customer acquisition cost. It rolls advertising, marketing and sales costs into one number and shows whether your growth is profitable or loss-making. This article covers how to calculate CAC, what to compare it with and how to bring it down.

CAC vs CPL: the difference

MetricWhat it showsFormula
CPL (cost per lead)What one lead costAd spend ÷ number of leads
CPA / cost per saleWhat one sale from ads costAd spend ÷ number of sales
CACTotal cost of one new customer(Ads + marketing + sales costs) ÷ new customers

CPL judges the ads; CAC judges the whole customer acquisition system. On cost per lead — cost per lead (CPL).

How CAC is calculated

CAC = acquisition costs for the period ÷ new customers in the same period

Costs usually include:

  • ad budget (Meta, Google, Telegram, influencers);
  • content and video production;
  • agency or marketer fees;
  • the share of the sales team spent on new customers (salaries, bonuses);
  • the CRM and other tools.

Count only new customers — repeat purchases artificially lower CAC.

Illustrative example

ItemMonthly amount
Ad budget12,000,000 so‘m
Content and marketing services8,000,000 so‘m
Sales team (share for new customers)5,000,000 so‘m
Total25,000,000 so‘m
Leads400
New customers50
  • CPL = 12,000,000 ÷ 400 = 30,000 so‘m
  • CAC = 25,000,000 ÷ 50 = 500,000 so‘m

As you can see, a 30,000 so‘m lead actually turns into a 500,000 so‘m customer. That's why decisions based on cost per lead alone are risky.

What to compare CAC with

CAC on its own isn't "good" or "bad". It has to be compared with the profit a customer brings.

LTV (customer lifetime value) — the gross profit one customer brings over the whole relationship. More — repeat sales and LTV.

Continuing the illustrative example:

  • average order 400,000 so‘m at a 40% margin → 160,000 so‘m profit per purchase;
  • a customer buys about 4 times a year → LTV ≈ 640,000 so‘m;
  • LTV ÷ CAC = 640,000 ÷ 500,000 ≈ 1.3.

In practice the target is often an LTV at least several times higher than CAC. A ratio of 1.3 means the business barely covers its acquisition costs.

Payback period

Another useful question: after how many purchases or months is CAC paid back?

Payback = CAC ÷ profit per purchase

In the example: 500,000 ÷ 160,000 ≈ 3.1 purchases. If a customer buys every 3 months, the cost is recovered in roughly 9 months. That's a long time for cash flow — especially if you want to raise the ad budget.

7 ways to lower CAC

  1. Reply to leads fast. More customers from the same leads — CAC drops immediately — lead response time.
  2. Raise conversion to sale. Scripts, follow-ups, handling objections — sales script.
  3. Refresh creatives. A strong creative brings cheaper, better leads — A/B testing ad creatives.
  4. Strengthen the offer. A clear, valuable offer lifts conversion.
  5. Use retargeting. People who already know you buy more cheaply — how retargeting works.
  6. Invest in organic content. AutoRobot, for example, grew to 11,000 followers in 8 months with no paid ads — AutoRobot case.
  7. Encourage referrals. A customer brought by a happy customer is one of the cheapest you'll get.

When rising CAC is fine

CAC doesn't always have to fall. It may rise when:

  • you're entering a new, more expensive audience;
  • you're scaling the budget (the cheap audience runs out);
  • new customers have a higher LTV.

What matters is the LTV-to-CAC ratio and the payback period.

Common mistakes

  • Looking only at CPL instead of CAC.
  • Leaving out content, agency and sales costs.
  • Counting repeat customers as new ones.
  • Looking only at overall CAC, not by channel.
  • Judging CAC without LTV.

Questions and answers

What should my CAC be?

There's no universal number. It depends on average order, margin and repeat purchases. A good CAC is well below LTV, with a payback period your business can live with.

How often should I calculate CAC?

Every month, by channel. For long sales cycles, quarterly as well.

How should sales team costs be split?

A simple approach: roughly estimate the share of managers' time spent on new customers. Precision isn't the point — calculating it the same way every month is.

How do CAC and ROMI relate?

ROMI shows how much profit each so‘m spent on marketing brought; CAC shows the price of one customer. Together they give the full picture — how to calculate ROMI.

Conclusion

CAC is the real price of one new customer: advertising, marketing and sales costs divided by the number of new customers. Compare it with LTV and the payback period, look at it by channel, and lower it first through conversion to sale and response speed.
