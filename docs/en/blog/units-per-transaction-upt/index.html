Store owners usually watch two numbers: daily revenue and average transaction value. But when the average transaction goes up or down, you can't see why: did people buy more items, or just more expensive ones? Units per transaction (UPT) answers that question. Below: what UPT is, how to get it from your till report, how to read it and how to set a target for sales staff.

What UPT is and how it differs from average transaction value

UPT (units per transaction) is the average number of items in one receipt. The formula is simple:

UPT = units sold ÷ number of transactions

If a store rang up 500 receipts in a week with 800 items in total, UPT = 1.6. In other words, the average shopper comes to the till with one item, and sometimes two. It is a standard retail metric (StoreForce glossary).

Average transaction value has two parts:

Average transaction value = UPT × average price per item

MetricWhat it showsHow to calculate
Average transaction valueAmount spent per purchaseRevenue ÷ transactions
UPTItems per purchaseUnits ÷ transactions
Average item priceWhat one sold item costRevenue ÷ units

So there are two ways to grow the average transaction: shoppers take more items (UPT) or choose pricier ones (price). Each needs a different approach. The overall store sales formula — visitors, conversion, transaction value and repeat purchases — is in how to increase sales in a store.

Why track UPT separately

Retail in Uzbekistan is growing: official statistics show retail turnover for January–July 2026 exceeded UZS 310 trillion (stat.uz). When the market grows, your revenue may grow too — but that doesn't mean the work on your shop floor got better. UPT shows exactly that work.

Three things drive UPT:

  • Sales staff. Showing a shopper a matching extra item is their job. UPT is the simplest number that measures it.
  • Merchandising. If belts hang next to trousers and sweets sit next to tea, people add them on their own.
  • Bundles and checkout items. They lift UPT with almost no staff involvement.

If the average transaction grew but UPT did not, the growth came from price (a pricier collection, a price increase). That's fine too, but the shop floor work hasn't changed.

How to get UPT from your till report

Many point-of-sale systems don't show a "UPT" column, but every report has the two numbers you need: number of transactions and units sold (pieces or line items). The steps:

  1. Pick a period. One day is too little; the number jumps. At least a week, ideally 4 weeks.
  2. Take the number of transactions. Sales receipts only. Exclude refund receipts.
  3. Take units sold. Pieces sold in that period, minus returned items.
  4. Remove what doesn't count. Bags, gift wrapping, delivery fees, staff purchases and wholesale orders — they distort UPT up or down.
  5. Divide and record. Units ÷ transactions. Every week, the same way.

Be careful with goods sold by weight (groceries, dried fruit): 1.5 kg of apples is not one piece but one line item. Such stores count line items per receipt instead: how many different products are in one receipt. Whichever method you choose, use it every week.

For UPT per salesperson, each receipt must be linked to a salesperson in the till. If your system can't do that, you can add a staff code as a receipt note or split by shifts — but accuracy drops.

  • B sells expensive items but doesn't suggest extras. They need a list of matching products and one ready phrase — the script in upselling and cross-selling is for exactly that.
  • C has the highest UPT, but 20 transactions are too few to conclude anything. Don't judge staff on a handful of receipts.

How to read the number: 4 situations

Never read UPT on its own. Look at it together with average transaction value and conversion rate.

UPTAvg transactionLikely causeWhat to do
UpUpAdd-on selling worksWrite the method down and train everyone
UpDownCheap small items get added, the main item got cheaperCheck discounts and "2 for 1" deals
DownUpExpensive items sell, but aloneList accessories for your premium items
DownDownSomething broke on the floor: stock-outs, too few staff, queuesCheck stock, shift schedule and the till

One more risk: if staff start pushing shoppers to raise UPT, conversion drops — people say "just looking" and leave. That's why you should count store visitors and watch conversion alongside UPT.

Setting a UPT target for staff: 6 rules

  1. Measure first, then set a target. Record UPT for at least 4 weeks without a target. That's your baseline.
  2. Small steps. Not from 1.6 straight to 2.5, but 1.7 next month (illustrative example). An unreachable target pushes staff into pressure selling or "adjusting" the numbers.
  3. Minimum transactions. Don't judge someone on UPT if they rang up fewer than, say, 30 receipts a week — the number is random.
  4. UPT shouldn't be the only KPI. Pair it with average transaction value or revenue. Otherwise staff will "hit" the target with cheap small items. On choosing metrics — what is a KPI.
  5. Give them the method. Along with the target: 2–3 matching items for each main product, one ready phrase and bundles. A target without a method is just a demand.
  6. 10 minutes a week. Go through the weekly table with your staff: who did well, which pairs sold most.

Common mistakes

  • Not subtracting returns. Returned items stay "sold" and UPT comes out too high.
  • Counting bags and services as items. If every receipt has a bag, UPT rises by a whole unit.
  • Mixing in wholesale. One 40-piece order breaks the weekly number.
  • Reacting to daily numbers. UPT jumps day to day — draw conclusions from weeks.
  • Bonuses for UPT alone. Pressure and pushy selling begin, and shoppers don't come back.
  • Changing the counting method. Pieces today, line items tomorrow — nothing is comparable.

Questions and answers

What is a good UPT?

There's no universal norm: a grocery receipt can have dozens of line items, while in a furniture store one sofa is a normal purchase. Use your own 4 weeks as a baseline and compare the store with itself.

Which matters more — UPT or average transaction value?

Both together. Average transaction value shows the result, UPT shows the reason. Revenue is what counts in the end; UPT is a tool for managing the shop floor.

Does UPT apply to online stores?

Yes, the same way: items in orders ÷ number of orders. Just exclude cancelled and returned orders.

How can I raise UPT quickly?

The fastest methods don't need staff: placing matching items side by side, ready-made bundles and small items at the checkout. To bring regular customers back — a loyalty programme.

Conclusion

UPT is the number inside average transaction value: how many items a shopper takes per purchase. You can calculate it from two numbers in your till report, but returns, bags and wholesale must be excluded. Read UPT together with average transaction value and conversion, give staff small-step targets and a ready method.
