Private kindergarten and school marketing: earning parents' trust

Choosing a private kindergarten or school is one of parents' most important decisions. Discounts and pretty banners don't work here: parents want to know who they are trusting their child with. So private kindergarten and school marketing is about openly showing safety, people and results. This article sets out a plan for a private education provider in Tashkent to earn parents' trust and fill its places.

What parents look at

QuestionWhat the parent wants to see
"Will my child be safe?"Premises, entry control, cameras, a nurse
"Who will they be with?"Teachers and carers: experience, attitude
"What will they learn?"Curriculum, daily routine, languages, clubs
"What's the food like?"Menu, kitchen, cleanliness
"Are there results?"Graduates, pupils' achievements, parents' views
"What does it cost and what's included?"Transparent fees and extras

When your content and ads answer these questions, parents take the first step — a visit.

Step 1. Position and difference

"Modern education, qualified teachers" is what everyone writes. Be more specific:

  • Language environment: "English all day, with a native-speaker teacher".
  • Method: Montessori, STEM, school readiness.
  • Format: full day, half day, small groups (how many children per group).
  • Location and convenience: hours, transport, an evening group.

Framing the offer — unique selling proposition; the audience — target audience and customer persona.

Step 2. Open days and trial days

The key conversion for a kindergarten or school is a visit. Parents won't decide until they've seen the place. So the goal of ads and content is a booking for an open day or a personal tour:

  1. A tour — rooms, kitchen, playground, security system.
  2. A talk with the head or curriculum lead — programme and approach.
  3. A trial day or lesson — a gentle settling-in for the child.
  4. A concrete next step — documents, a deadline to reserve the place.

Send reminders to those who booked, and call within 24 hours after the visit.

Step 3. Content — people and everyday life

Important: photos and videos of children are published only with parents' written consent. Children without consent should not be filmed, or their faces should not be shown. Handling personal data — customer database and personal data.

Preparing teachers for the camera — expert interview videos.

Step 4. Parents' views and maps

Parents trust other parents. Ask current families for reviews — on maps and social media. Recommendations in parent chats often work better than ads. Keep the maps listing complete: photos, hours, age groups. More in adding your business to maps and online reputation and reviews.

Step 5. Paid ads

  • Radius: 3–5 km around the premises — parents won't travel far;
  • Audience: broad, with parenting interests; the creative does the filtering;
  • Creative: the real daily routine, a teacher's face, safety, a concrete offer: "Open day — Saturday, 10:00";
  • Objective: tour bookings — a lead form (child's age, convenient time) or messages.

Retargeting: parents' views and the menu for people who watched videos or visited the profile. Setup — Instagram ads.

Step 6. The admissions season

For private schools, the main season is spring and summer — admissions for the new school year. Kindergartens enrol all year, but August–September and January are busier. Plan ahead: a schedule of open days, ad budget for those months and honest information about available places. More on seasonality in education — marketing an education centre.

Step 7. Retention and referrals

A happy parent brings a younger sibling and recommends you to friends. For that:

  • regular contact — weekly updates, photos in the parents' group;
  • one or two parent meetings and surveys a year;
  • a sibling discount and a referral bonus.

Collecting feedback — collecting customer feedback.

The first call: what the receptionist says

A parent's first question often arrives by phone or DM: "How much is it? Are there places?". That conversation decides whether the family books a visit. A short standard for the receptionist:

  1. Ask the child's name and age — the conversation immediately becomes personal.
  2. Find out the family's needs — working hours, language, whether it's the child's first nursery.
  3. State the fees openly — what's included and what's extra.
  4. Offer a specific visit time — "Thursday 17:00 or Saturday 10:00".
  5. Send the address and a reminder.

Parents call several providers at once and remember whoever answered warmly and clearly first. On reply speed — lead response time; conversation structure — sales script.

What to measure

  • tour bookings and attendance;
  • visit-to-contract conversion;
  • cost to enrol one pupil;
  • share of children still enrolled at year end;
  • share of families from referrals.

Common mistakes

  • Only interior photos; teachers and the daily routine are invisible.
  • Posting children's photos without consent.
  • Vague fees and extras.
  • No reminders or follow-up calls for tour bookings.
  • Starting ads after admissions season has begun.

Questions and answers

Which channel works best for a kindergarten?

Maps, Instagram and parents' recommendations. Paid ads within a radius increase open day bookings.

Should fees be published?

Yes, at least "from…" and what's included. Parents trust a listing without prices less. In ads, the price must be in UZS.

Can children appear in videos?

Only with parents' written consent. Without it — film from behind, hands, wide shots, or footage without children.

How can a small kindergarten compete with big chains?

With small groups, personal attention and the head talking openly with parents. Show that in your content.

Conclusion

Private kindergarten and school marketing is about parents' trust: openly showing safety, teachers, the daily routine and results, with the visit as the main goal. Happy families bring the next ones.
