"How to build an AI agent" has become one of the most searched AI topics in recent months. Many assume it's a job for programmers. In reality, a small business can assemble its first agent with no-code tools in a few days — if it picks the right task. What an agent is — AI agents for business. This article covers the practical part: how to build one.

Step 1: choose the right task

A good first task:

  • repeats — dozens of times a day;
  • has a clear input and output — e.g. lead text → category and reply;
  • is cheap to get wrong — a person can check it.

Examples: sorting incoming leads, analysing reviews, summarising calls, content ideas, drafting reports.

Bad first tasks: payments, contracts, replying directly to customers without review.

Step 2: choose a tool

  • Custom assistants inside ChatGPT or Gemini — configured with instructions and files; the simplest start.
  • No-code automation platforms (such as Make, n8n or Zapier) — connect AI to your CRM, Telegram or Google Sheets.
  • A Telegram bot plus AI — for customer conversations — a Telegram AI assistant.

Step 3: write the agent's instructions

An agent gets instructions much like an employee's job description:

  • Role: "You are an assistant to a furniture store's sales team" (an illustrative example).
  • Task: "Read each enquiry and categorise it: kitchen, wardrobe, other."
  • Rules: "Never quote a price yourself", "If it's a complaint, flag it for a manager."
  • Output format: "Category, urgency 1–3, a two-sentence draft reply."
  • Knowledge: price list, FAQ and opening hours, as files.

Prompt structure — marketing prompts.

Example: an agent for incoming leads

  1. A lead arrives from a lead form or Telegram — connect a Meta lead form to your CRM.
  2. The agent reads it: what's needed, when, the budget.
  3. It sets the category and urgency.
  4. It drafts a reply for the salesperson.
  5. It logs everything in the CRM and assigns a task.
  6. The salesperson checks and sends.

The result: the first reply takes seconds rather than minutes — lead response time.

Step 4: testing

  • Test on 20–30 real (anonymised) examples.
  • Record every error and improve the instructions.
  • For the first 2–4 weeks, a person checks every result.

Security and the law

  • Give the agent only the data and access it needs.
  • Follow the law when handling personal data — fines apply for unlawful processing with AI — AI law in Uzbekistan.
  • Important actions (payments, deletions, final replies to customers) only with human approval.

Measuring results

  • Time saved: how long the task took before and after.
  • Quality: what share of the agent's decisions a person corrects.
  • Business metrics: response speed and conversion.

Questions and answers

Do I need a developer?

Not for a first, simple agent. Complex integrations and large volumes may need one.

Can the agent reply to customers on its own?

For simple questions (opening hours, address), yes. Prices, disputes and complex questions go to a person.

How much does it cost?

At small volumes, AI and automation tools can cost a few tens of dollars a month; the exact price depends on the plan and volume.

Conclusion

A first AI agent needs a repetitive, clear and safe task, a no-code tool, well-written instructions, testing on real examples and human oversight. Start small and measure time saved and conversion. In a free audit, Level Marketing will show you which step of your lead handling could be automated.
