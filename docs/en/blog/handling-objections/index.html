Handling objections: “too expensive”, “I'll think about it”

"It's too expensive", "I'll think about it", "It's cheaper elsewhere" — every sales rep hears these dozens of times a day. An objection isn't a rejection. It means the customer still has a question that hasn't been answered. Handling objections is the skill of finding that question and answering it honestly.

A 4-step method

  1. Listen — don't interrupt or argue.
  2. Acknowledge and clarify — "I understand. What exactly felt expensive?"
  3. Answer — the customer's specific concern, not with generalities.
  4. Propose a next step — "Shall I send you the warranty terms and call you tomorrow?"

7 common objections (illustrative examples)

  • "It's too expensive" → "What are you comparing it with?" Then show the value: lifespan, warranty, cost per day — replying to "how much?".
  • "I'll think about it" → "Of course. Which question is still open?" There's often another objection behind it — the "I'll think about it" customer.
  • "It's cheaper elsewhere" → "Which option did you see? Let's compare what's included." Never badmouth a competitor.
  • "I don't have time now" → "When would 5 minutes suit you?" — a specific time.
  • "I need to talk to my spouse / boss" → "What questions might they ask? I'll send a short summary."
  • "I don't trust you" → reviews, a guarantee, a contract, an invitation to the office — social proof in ads.
  • "We don't need it" → "How do you handle this today?" — maybe they really don't need it, and that's fine.

Real versus hidden objections

Customers often say "too expensive" when they actually don't trust you or don't understand. A test question: "If price weren't an issue, would you start today?" If the answer is no, the objection lies elsewhere.

Closing objections in advance

The best answer comes before the objection does:

  • show price, guarantees and timelines in your ads and on your website;
  • turn frequent questions into content — content from customer objections;
  • add the 10 most common objections to your script — sales scripts.

Collecting objections

Every month, listen to calls and update your list of objections: which come up most and which answers worked — AI call analysis. This data is gold for marketing too: your ad messages should answer exactly these questions.

Common mistakes

  • Arguing, or saying "you've misunderstood".
  • Offering a discount straight away — it cheapens the product — the discount trap.
  • Mechanically repeating a memorised answer.
  • Not setting a next step after an objection.

Questions and answers

Is an objection a bad sign?

No — a customer who objects is interested. The one who leaves in silence is worse.

When can I offer a discount?

After the value has been explained, and in exchange for something: pre-payment or a longer contract, for example.

What if the same objection keeps coming up?

Then the problem is in the offer, the price or the ad message — work through it with marketing.

Conclusion

Handling objections means listening, clarifying, answering specifically and setting a next step. The best results come when objections are answered in advance in your ads, content and script. In a free audit, Level Marketing will check whether your ad messages answer customers' questions.
