An online shopper's biggest fear is "the photo shows one thing, and something else arrives". An unboxing video addresses exactly that: the buyer sees in advance what the order will look like when it arrives. This article covers how to make an unboxing video for an online store in 6 steps, a 30-second script template, examples for three niches and the most common mistakes.

What an unboxing video is and why an online store needs one

An unboxing video is a short clip that starts with a sealed box and goes through opening it, showing what is inside and using the product for the first time. Unlike a regular product video, it recreates the buyer's experience: the box from the courier, the tape, the inner packaging, what comes in the set, the first impression.

For an online store, it answers three questions:

  • What condition does the product arrive in? Is the box intact, is the packaging solid, is anything fragile protected?
  • What is in the box? Charger, manual, warranty card, a gift, extra parts.
  • What does it look like in real life? Colour, size, material — without studio lighting, in ordinary conditions.

Competition demands it too. According to figures Spot.uz published in October 2026, Uzum Market lists more than 2 million products from more than 18,500 sellers (Spot.uz). When dozens of stores sell the same item, buyers go with the one they trust more. An unboxing video is an inexpensive way to earn that trust.

You can use it in three places: Reels and TikTok, paid social ads, and a marketplace listing or Telegram channel. Each has its own job — we covered this in detail in 7 product video mistakes.

How to make an unboxing video: 6 steps

Step 1. Choose the product

Not every product suits an unboxing. These work best:

  • products with attractive packaging — gift boxes, cosmetics sets, branded packaging;
  • products with a rich set of contents — gadgets, home appliances, children's toys;
  • products buyers have doubts about — clothing (size, fabric), furniture accessories, fragile items.

Start with your store's 3–5 best sellers: they also generate the most questions.

Step 2. Pick one main message

Each video should answer one question. For example: "Will the box get crushed in transit?", "Is everything in the set?", "Is the colour the same as in the photo?". Try to say everything in one clip and nothing will stick.

The easiest way to find these questions is to open your Direct and Telegram chats and list what customers ask most often before buying.

Step 3. Prepare the set and lighting

  • Table — clean, one colour: white, grey or wood. Nothing extra in the frame.
  • Light — by a window in the daytime. In the evening, one ring light or softbox; switch off other lamps so colours stay true.
  • Camera — a phone on a tripod, shooting from above or at a 45-degree angle. Hands in the frame; a face is optional.

For phone settings, lighting and sound in detail, see shooting Reels on a phone.

Step 4. Plan the sound

Half the appeal of an unboxing is the sound: tape tearing, a lid clicking, film rustling. So:

  • shoot in silence, turn off the air conditioner and TV;
  • place the microphone (a lavalier or the phone) close to the table;
  • record any voice-over separately so it does not mix with the unboxing sounds.

Meta strongly recommends sound for Reels ads and also recommends captions (Meta Ads Guide). So the video must make sense both with and without sound: repeat the main message as short on-screen text.

Step 5. Shoot from a shot list

Before shooting, write a shot list and take each shot 2–3 times:

  1. Sealed box — whole, as the courier delivered it.
  2. Cutting the tape or opening the box.
  3. Inner packaging: protection, paper, film.
  4. Taking the product out — slowly, by hand.
  5. The full set laid out on the table.
  6. Close-ups: material, stitching, buttons, ports.
  7. First use: trying it on, switching it on, holding it.
  8. Final shot: the whole product.

From these eight shots you can cut several videos: a short one for Reels and a full one for the listing.

Step 6. Editing and format

  • Format: vertical 9:16. For Reels ads, Meta advises keeping text and key elements out of the top 14%, bottom 35% and 6% on each side of the frame (Meta Ads Guide).
  • Length: 15–30 seconds for Reels and ads; the listing version can be longer.
  • Cut pauses and speed up slow moments, but keep the "best" moment — lifting the lid — at real speed.
  • Music should not drown out the unboxing sounds.

For editing tools, see video editing apps.

Unboxing video script: a 30-second template

The table below is an illustrative example: the timings are approximate, adapt them to your product.

TimeShotOn-screen text or voice
0–3 secThe most interesting moment of openingA question or promise: "Will a laptop fit in this bag?"
3–8 secSealed box, cutting the tape"This is how your order arrives"
8–15 secInner packaging, taking the product outOne line about protection and packaging
15–23 secThe set and close-ups"Inside: …" — a list
23–28 secFirst useThe answer to the main question
28–30 secThe whole productHow to order: "Message us on Direct"

The first 3 seconds matter most. TikTok's ad guidance advises showing the key offer within the first 3 seconds (TikTok Ads Help). So don't open with a sealed box — start with the most interesting shot, then show everything from the beginning. Ready-made openers are in 10 Reels hooks.

Examples for three niches

Clothing store

The buyer's main question is fabric and size. In the video: the bag, the label, a fabric close-up, stretching it by hand, then the item on a model or mannequin. Put the size on screen: "Model's height … cm, wearing size M". Use real data only.

Gadgets and home appliances

The main question is what's in the box and whether it works. In the video: the sealed box, inner protection, the set on the table, the warranty card, switching it on for the first time. Briefly put the warranty and service terms on screen — it adds trust.

Cosmetics and gift sets

Here the packaging is part of the product. In the video: the box design, untying the ribbon, taking out each item separately, the texture (cream on the back of a hand). Before holidays, such a video can open with "here's what the gift looks like".

Customer unboxing videos: how to ask

The most convincing unboxing is one the customer filmed themselves. To get them:

  • Put a card in the parcel: "Film yourself opening the box and send it to us".
  • Message after delivery: 1–2 days after the order arrives, say thank you and ask for a video.
  • Offer a small thank-you: a discount on the next order or a small gift — for any honest video, not only positive ones.

An important rule: before using a customer's video in ads, get their written consent (a Direct conversation counts). Uzbekistan's advertising law prohibits using a person's name or image without their consent — more in advertising law in Uzbekistan. If a blogger does the unboxing, their post must be labelled as advertising — see influencer advertising.

5 common mistakes

  1. A slow start. The box is turned around for 10 seconds while the viewer has already scrolled away. Put the most interesting shot first.
  2. Dark or yellow light. The colour looks different from reality — and then the buyer returns the item saying "the colour is wrong".
  3. Hidden flaws. A video that is too polished lowers trust. Show the product as it is.
  4. Meaningless without sound. Everything is in the voice-over, nothing on screen. Repeat the main message with captions or a short line of text.
  5. No call to action. The video ends and it's unclear how to order. In the last shot, give one clear step: Direct, a Telegram bot or the marketplace listing.

Checklist: is your unboxing video ready?

  • The video answers one customer question.
  • The first 3 seconds show the most interesting shot or a question.
  • Daylight, true colours.
  • Unboxing sounds are audible; music doesn't drown them out.
  • The main message is also shown as on-screen text.
  • Text and key shots are inside the safe zone.
  • The full set of contents is shown.
  • There is one clear call to action at the end.
  • Consent has been obtained for customer or blogger videos.

Questions and answers

Do I need to show a face in an unboxing video?

No. Many unboxing videos show only hands and the product. If you want a face — for example, an expert or the store owner recommending the product — add it in the final shots.

Do I need an expensive camera?

No, a modern phone is enough. A tripod, good light and a quiet room affect quality more than the camera does.

How many videos per product?

At least two from one shoot: a dynamic 15–30-second version for Reels and ads, and a full, calmer version for the listing or Telegram. In ads, it's worth testing 2–3 versions with different openings.

Can I use an unboxing video in paid ads?

Yes, it's one of the common types of video ads. Just edit a separate ad version: a strong opening, captions, the safe zone and a clear offer at the end.

Is an unboxing worth it for a cheap product?

Yes, if buyers have doubts about size, fabric or quality. With a cheap product, the fear of "what if it's poor quality" is often even stronger, and a video removes it quickly.

Conclusion

An unboxing video lets the buyer "hold the order" before buying. Pick one customer question, film 8 shots from the list, open with the most interesting moment and end by saying how to order.
