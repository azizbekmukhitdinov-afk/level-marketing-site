What is NPS: how to calculate and improve customer loyalty

Do your customers recommend you to friends? The best-known metric for answering that is NPS (Net Promoter Score). It's a single question, but used properly it reveals service problems early and helps you find sources of reviews and referrals. Here's how to calculate and use NPS.

The core question

"On a scale of 0 to 10, how likely are you to recommend us to a friend or colleague?"

An optional follow-up: "Why that score?" — this is where the most valuable information lies.

Three groups

ScoreGroupWho they are
9–10PromotersLoyal, recommend you
7–8PassivesSatisfied, but could leave
0–6DetractorsUnhappy, may spread negativity

The formula

NPS = % promoters − % detractors

An illustrative example: 100 responses — 50 promoters (50%), 30 passives and 20 detractors (20%). NPS = 50 − 20 = 30. Scores range from −100 to +100. Passives aren't in the formula, but they count towards the total.

When to ask

  • After a purchase or service, while the experience is fresh.
  • Regular customers, for example once a quarter.
  • In B2B, at the end of a project stage.

How to ask: SMS (with consent), a Telegram bot, a call or a short form — collecting customer feedback.

Acting on results — the main part

NPS is for action, not for the number:

  • Detractors (0–6): call within 24 hours and fix the problem. This often wins the customer back.
  • Passives (7–8): "What was missing for a 10?" — ideas for improvement.
  • Promoters (9–10): ask for a review and invite them into a referral programme — online reputation and reviews, affiliate programmes.

Every month, categorise the "why?" answers — price, service, quality, timing — and fix the most common first.

Where to store it

In the CRM: the NPS score and comment on each customer record. Then you can see who recommends you and who's at risk of leaving — CRM for small business.

Common mistakes

  • Tracking the number but not reading the "why?".
  • Not responding to detractors.
  • Asking only happy customers — a pretty but false result.
  • Asking too often and tiring customers out.
  • Comparing NPS directly across industries.

Questions and answers

What's a good NPS?

There's no universal benchmark — it depends on industry and region. What matters most is the trend in your own NPS.

How is NPS different from a 1–5 rating?

A 1–5 rating measures a specific service; NPS measures the overall relationship and likelihood of recommendation.

Is there an NPS for employees?

Yes — eNPS asks whether employees would recommend the company as a place to work.

Conclusion

NPS is one question, three groups and a simple formula. Its value lies in action: fast responses to detractors, ideas from passives, reviews and referrals from promoters.
