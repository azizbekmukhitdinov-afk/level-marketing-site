What crowd marketing is and how it brings customers to your website

Before buying, people look for other people's opinions: they ask in Telegram chats "does anyone know a good dentist?", share experiences on forums and read reviews on Google and Yandex Maps. Crowd marketing is the work of making sure your brand comes up in those conversations in a useful, natural way. Here is how it works and how to keep it from turning into spam.

What crowd marketing is

Crowd marketing means mentions of your company, product or website where your audience talks. Usually that is:

  • helpful answers to questions, with a link to an article on your site where relevant;
  • managing review platforms and map listings;
  • taking part in topic-specific Telegram chats and groups as an expert;
  • bloggers and experts mentioning your product in their own words.

Crowd marketing delivers two results: direct customers (people who follow the link) and SEO value — natural links and brand mentions on other sites tell search engines your site can be trusted.

Who it suits best

  • New websites — to earn search engines' trust faster.
  • Products people deliberate over — education, healthcare, real estate, electronics, B2B services.
  • Expert businesses — lawyers, doctors, marketers, teachers show their knowledge and earn trust.
  • Local businesses — "where in Chilanzar can I…" questions come up in chats all the time.

Who it barely helps

  • Cheap impulse products — paid ads work faster for them.
  • Niches whose audience doesn't discuss them online.
  • Businesses not ready to spend time and expertise on answers — low-quality mentions do harm.

How to do it right: 6 rules

1. Find where your audience talks

In Uzbekistan that is usually topic-specific Telegram chats, comments under experts' Instagram posts, reviews on Google and Yandex Maps, and industry forums. For the first week or two, just observe: which questions repeat, which problems come up most.

2. Value first, link second

A good crowd mention is a complete answer on its own. Add a link only when more detail is genuinely needed. A bare "message us" gets deleted fast and hurts the brand.

3. Be honest

Don't hide that you represent the company. Praise from fake "customers" and fake reviews get exposed, break platform rules and destroy your reputation.

4. Tailor every answer

Posting the same text across dozens of chats is spam. Read the question and answer exactly that.

5. Work with reviews

Reply to every review on maps and platforms, good and bad. A calm reply to criticism with a concrete fix often impresses new customers more than praise does. Ask happy customers to leave a review — but never buy reviews.

6. Measure results

Add UTM tags to your links and track where site visitors come from. Every month: how many mentions, how many clicks, how many leads.

A sample answer (illustrative)

A question in a Telegram chat: "Is there a good dentist in Chilanzar?"

A bad answer: "Come to us, we're the best!"

A good answer: "I work as an administrator at [clinic], so I'm not impartial 🙂 But whichever you choose, look at three things: the doctor's experience, whether prices are given upfront and how sterilisation is handled. Our first consultation is free — if you're interested, here's our listing [link]."

A good answer introduces itself, gives something useful and only makes an offer at the end.

A weekly routine

  • 30 minutes a day: search topic chats and groups for your keywords.
  • 3–5 useful answers — quality matters more than quantity.
  • Reply to reviews on maps and marketplaces.
  • Log it in a spreadsheet: where, when and on what topic you answered.

Common mistakes

  • Praising yourself from fake accounts.
  • The same copy-pasted answer everywhere.
  • Useless messages that are nothing but a link.
  • Arguing with critics.
  • Ads and spam that break a chat's rules.

Where to start: a list of platforms

  • Telegram chats — district chats, parents' chats, industry groups.
  • Q&A sites and forums — especially Russian-language ones.
  • Map listings — reviews and the questions section.
  • YouTube — comments under videos on your topic.
  • Marketplaces — product questions.

Pick one or two places first and be active there regularly — one-off answers in ten places achieve little.

Replying to reviews is crowd marketing too

Future customers read every reply you post to a review on maps. A calm, precise and helpful reply is the cheapest advertising in a public space. Reply templates are in Online reputation and reviews.

Linking crowd marketing to your website

For questions that keep coming up in chats, write a detailed article on your website and link to it in your answers. That way the short answer is useful, and over time the article brings visitors from search too. Your list of questions becomes a ready-made content plan for the site.

Questions and answers

Can you use fake accounts?

No. It deceives people, platforms block such accounts, and if you're exposed your reputation takes serious damage. Write only under your own name, openly.

Who should do crowd marketing?

Ideally the company's own expert, or a marketer whose work the expert checks. Answers need to be at an expert level — otherwise they do more harm than good.

What rules apply in Telegram chats?

Every chat has its own rules: some ban advertising entirely, others allow it on certain days. Read the rules and ask the admins if needed.

Are the links useful for SEO?

Links on some sites pass no weight to search engines. The main benefit is traffic and trust, so only leave a link where it genuinely helps.

When will you see results?

After a few months: crowd marketing builds up over time. A useful answer posted today can keep bringing people from search months later.

Is working with a blogger also crowd marketing?

Partly. If a blogger mentions your product honestly, it works like a recommendation. But if the collaboration is paid, they must clearly mark it as an ad.

What if a competitor badmouths you in a chat?

Reply once, calmly and with facts, and don't keep the argument going. If there are insults or false information, contact the chat admin. Readers often pay more attention to the tone of the reply than to the attack.

Conclusion

Crowd marketing isn't advertising — it's useful participation. Answer as an expert where your audience talks, be honest, work with reviews and measure results, and mentions gradually turn into customers and search rankings.
