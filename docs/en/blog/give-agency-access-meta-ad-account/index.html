Giving an agency access to your Meta ad account, the safe way

The first technical question when starting with an agency or a media buyer is access: the Instagram page, the ad account, the pixel. Many business owners make one of two mistakes here: they either send a password or let the agency create everything under its own name. Both cause trouble later. Here's how to grant access in Meta properly so the assets stay yours, and what to do when the partnership ends.

The core rule: assets live in your portfolio

In Meta, business assets — the Facebook Page, Instagram account, ad account, pixel (dataset) and catalogue — live inside a business portfolio (formerly Business Manager). Whoever owns the portfolio owns the assets.

So:

  • create the business portfolio yourself and keep full control of it;
  • create the ad account in your own portfolio, then share it with the agency;
  • connect the pixel and the Instagram account to your portfolio too.

If the agency creates the ad account in its own portfolio, the account and its history — audiences, pixel data, results — stay with the agency. When the partnership ends, you start from zero.

Handing over a password is the worst option

Giving the agency your Instagram password or your Facebook profile is risky:

  • Meta may flag a login from an unknown device as suspicious and lock the account;
  • you can't see who did what;
  • to revoke access you have to change the password, which breaks other services.

The right way is to grant access as a partner. The agency works through its own accounts, and you can remove access with one click at any time.

Giving a partner access: the steps

According to Meta's Business Help Center (Meta Business Help):

  1. Ask the agency for its business portfolio ID. The partner needs a portfolio of its own.
  2. In Meta Business Suite, open your portfolio settings: Users → Partners.
  3. Click Add and choose the option to give a partner access to your assets.
  4. Enter the partner's ID.
  5. Select the assets (ad account, Page, Instagram, dataset) and the access level for each.
  6. Click Assign assets. Someone else with full control of the portfolio may need to approve the action.

You need full control of the portfolio yourself to do this.

Full or partial access

Meta offers two levels: partial access (specific tasks only) and full control (everything except sharing the asset with others).

Practical advice:

AssetWhat the agency needsRecommendation
Ad accountCreate and edit campaigns, reportsPermission to manage ads
Facebook Page and InstagramAds, comments, messages (as agreed)Only the tasks needed
Pixel / datasetView events, use in campaignsPermission to use
CatalogueIf you run product adsOnly if needed

There's no need to give the agency full control of your portfolio. Portfolio admins should be you and a trusted employee only.

Billing: whose card is it

The ad account's payment method is a separate topic. There are two models:

  • Your card. You add your own card and Meta charges you directly. Transparent, but card limits and foreign-currency payments are your problem.
  • Through the agency. The agency pays the ad budget and invoices you. Convenient, but compare the invoice with actual spend in Ads Manager every month.

Whatever the model, you must be able to see spend in the account. What a report should contain — what an ad report should include.

Checklist before granting access

  • [ ] The business portfolio is in your name and you are the admin.
  • [ ] Two-factor authentication is on.
  • [ ] The portfolio has at least two admins (the second a trusted employee), so access isn't lost if one person disappears.
  • [ ] The ad account was created in your portfolio.
  • [ ] The pixel / dataset is in your portfolio.
  • [ ] The agency has only the assets and levels it needs.
  • [ ] You've written down who got what, and when.

When the partnership ends

Any partnership can end — that's normal. With access set up properly, leaving is easy:

  1. In Settings → Partners, select the agency and remove its access.
  2. Save a list of active campaigns in the ad account.
  3. Ask the agency for an archive of creatives, copy and reports — ideally agreed in the contract up front.
  4. Grant access to the new team the same way.

Audiences, pixel history and campaign results stay in your account — the new team doesn't start from scratch.

Common mistakes

  • Letting the agency create the ad account in its own portfolio.
  • Sending the Instagram password.
  • Being the only portfolio admin — if that person leaves, access goes with them.
  • Not removing access for former employees and old freelancers.
  • Forgetting to share the pixel, so the agency works without seeing conversions.

Questions and answers

Is it risky when an agency asks for access?

Partner access is the standard, safe route. The risk lies in sharing passwords or creating assets in the agency's portfolio.

Can the agency see my whole portfolio?

No — only the assets you shared, at the level you granted.

What if the ad account was created in the agency's portfolio?

Talk to the agency. The most reliable path is to create a new ad account in your own portfolio and run future campaigns there.

Can I give several agencies access at once?

Technically yes. But when two teams work in one ad account, results get mixed up and accountability disappears.

Conclusion

Granting an agency access takes 10 minutes, but the consequences last for years. Keep the portfolio, ad account and pixel in your hands, and give the agency only the partner access it needs.
