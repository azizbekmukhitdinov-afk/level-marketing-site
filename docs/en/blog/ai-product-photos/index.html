AI product photos: a catalogue without a studio

A studio, a photographer, lighting gear — for a small online store, that's expensive for every new product. That's why searches for "AI product photos" are growing fast: shoot on a phone, let AI replace the background, fix the light and "place" the product in a room. It genuinely works — with one strict limit. Here's how to do AI product photography and where to stop.

The core rule: the product doesn't change

AI may change what's around the product: background, light, shadows, setting. But it must not change the product itself — its colour, shape, material, size or details.

Why:

  • buyers expect what's in the photo — a mismatch means returns and negative reviews;
  • the Law "On Advertising" prohibits misleading advertising (Article 5, lex.uz);
  • marketplaces require the product's real appearance.

Step 1: a good original photo

AI doesn't turn a bad photo into a good one — it makes a good photo look better. The minimum for phone photography:

  • natural light: by a window, without direct sun;
  • a plain background: white paper or a single-colour wall;
  • a tripod or support: so the shot isn't blurred;
  • several angles: front, side, back, a detail, a shot showing size;
  • true colour: compare the colour against the actual product.

Step 3: settings and context

A photo in a real setting helps buyers picture the product at home: a lamp in a room, a kettle in a kitchen, a bag in someone's hand. AI creates these shots without a studio. Rules:

  • proportions: a lamp shouldn't look bigger in the room than it really is;
  • honest context: don't show items as "included" if they aren't in the set;
  • one real shot: alongside setting shots, include a real photo on a plain background.

A catalogue workflow

  1. Shoot in one day: 20–30 products, 4–6 shots each.
  2. Naming: product codes in file names so nothing gets mixed up.
  3. AI editing: backgrounds, light, formats — in batches.
  4. Checking: every image compared with the original.
  5. Upload: website, marketplace, Instagram.
  6. Monitoring: return reasons — if it's "different colour" or "smaller than expected", revisit the photos.

For ads

AI-edited photos can work well in ad creatives, but real-use shots often work better: in a customer's hands, at home, in action. Customer videos — UGC content. Product video mistakes — product video mistakes.

Common mistakes

  • AI making the product's colour "prettier".
  • AI "smoothing" the texture of fabric, wood or metal.
  • Settings with broken proportions.
  • Only AI settings, no real plain-background photo.
  • Not reading the AI tool's commercial-use terms.

Questions and answers

Will AI replace photographers?

For simple catalogue photos, it speeds up a lot of work. For premium products, campaigns and brand shoots, a photographer is still preferable — premium segment marketing.

Is a phone camera good enough?

A modern phone is enough for a catalogue if the light and background are right.

Must AI-edited images be labelled?

The final text of Law ZRU-1115 contains no such requirement. But marketplaces and ad platforms may have their own rules. More in AI and the law in Uzbekistan.

How many photos per product?

Usually 4–6: main, sides, a detail, size, in use. Check the marketplace's requirements.

Conclusion

AI makes product photography faster and studio-free: backgrounds, light, settings and formats. But the product itself — colour, shape and size — must stay real, or the time saved goes on returns.
