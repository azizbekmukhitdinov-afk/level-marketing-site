Marketing a wedding venue: booking season and family trust

A to‘yxona — a wedding venue — is a business unique to Uzbekistan. Customers come not once a year but once or twice in a lifetime; the decision is made not by one person but by two families; and bookings happen months before the wedding. So ordinary restaurant marketing isn't enough. Here's how to build marketing for wedding venues and restaurants with large banquet halls.

An important detail: every guest at a wedding is a future wedding host. A wedding that goes well is the strongest advertising.

The decision: two families, many voices

Choosing a venue is the clearest example of a family decision: the couple, the parents, sometimes other relatives. Each has their own criteria:

  • the couple — how the hall looks, modernity, photo spots;
  • the parents — capacity, menu, price, service, reputation;
  • relatives — location, parking, recommendations from people they know.

So your marketing has to speak to several "voices". More in family purchase decisions.

Content: show the hall

What people look for most on a venue's Instagram:

  1. The fully decorated hall — shots from real weddings (with families' consent), not stock photos.
  2. The empty hall and its dimensions — capacity, table layout.
  3. Menu and dishes — close-ups, cakes, service.
  4. A video tour — from the entrance to the hall, 30–60 seconds.
  5. Different formats — a big wedding, a small nikoh oshi (wedding-morning pilaf), a corporate event.
  6. Customer reviews — videos of parents and couples speaking — customer testimonial video.

Important: if guests' faces appear in content, you need consent. Many families may object to wedding photos being posted publicly. Agree in advance and, if needed, use faceless shots — decor, tables, food.

Ads: who and when

Audience

  • Geography: districts around the venue and the city; if weddings come from the regions, those regions too.
  • A broad audience + strong creative: there's no reliable "planning a wedding" interest; the creative finds the right people.
  • Retargeting: for people who engaged with your profile or videos — prices, reviews, "dates are filling fast".

Timing

Bookings start long before the wedding. Ramp up ads before the peak wedding season, not once it has begun. Seasonal planning — advertising in the low season and marketing calendar 2027.

The offer

The best ad goal is a visit to the hall. Nobody books without seeing it:

Come on Saturday with your family to see the hall and taste the menu. (illustrative example)

Handling leads

A venue lead is big money. Every lost lead is a lost wedding. Standards:

  • response speed: as fast as possible, including outside working hours — more in lead response time;
  • first questions: date, number of guests, format — then the salesperson can give an accurate price;
  • price: a "from" price or a per-guest range — hiding the price sends people to another venue; more in replying to "How much?";
  • an invitation to visit in every conversation;
  • follow-up: the family is consulting — "I'll think about it" follow-up.

A Telegram bot is handy for checking date availability and collecting leads — Telegram bot for leads.

Filling empty days

A venue's main problem is uneven demand: weekends and the season are full, weekdays and the off-season empty. For empty days:

  • corporate events: year-end parties, anniversaries, conferences;
  • smaller events: birthdays, nikoh oshi, memorial gatherings;
  • weekday terms: instead of a discount, an extra — decor, a cake or music;
  • a B2B channel: partnerships with event agencies.

What to offer instead of a discount — the discount trap.

Reviews and referrals

Wedding venues live on referrals. A system:

  1. Two or three days after the wedding, thank the family.
  2. Ask for a review on Google and Yandex Maps.
  3. With consent, a short video review.
  4. Partner with professional photographers and videographers — they create the best shots of your hall.

Working with reviews — online reputation and reviews.

Common mistakes

  • Only photos of the decorated hall — no information on capacity, menu or price.
  • "Price on visit" — families won't spend time touring five venues.
  • Switching ads on once the season has started.
  • Posting wedding videos without guests' consent.
  • Accepting empty weekdays as "normal".

Questions and answers

Is Instagram enough for a wedding venue?

Instagram is often the main channel, but the maps listing and referrals matter a lot too. A website is a useful extra, especially for corporate clients. More in does a business need a website.

Should I post prices on Instagram?

At least a range or "from ... per guest". Even if the exact price depends on guest numbers and the menu, a guide brings people in for a visit.

Do influencers work for wedding venues?

Partnerships with local influencers and wedding photographers can help. The ads must be labelled — influencer advertising.

If a restaurant also hosts weddings, does it need a separate page?

If weddings are a significant share of revenue, yes: the restaurant and the banquet hall have different audiences and content. Restaurant marketing — marketing a restaurant or cafe.

Conclusion

Wedding venue marketing starts before the season, speaks to two families and ends with a visit. Show the hall honestly, give a price guide, reply to leads fast, create separate offers for empty days and collect a review after every wedding.
