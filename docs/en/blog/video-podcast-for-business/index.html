Video podcasts for business: expertise and a content engine

Video podcasts are one of the fastest-growing content formats: people watch long conversations on YouTube and short clips on Reels and Shorts. For a business, a podcast delivers two things: expertise and trust (people listen to you for an hour) and a "content engine" (dozens of short videos from one recording). But many podcasts stop after 3–4 episodes. Here's how to make a podcast work as a business tool.

What a podcast does for a business

  • Trust: in a long conversation, people see how you think — something ads can't show.
  • A source of short content: 10–15 Reels from a 40-minute conversation — one shoot, 10 ad creatives.
  • B2B connections: guests are potential clients and partners.
  • Search traffic: long videos on YouTube get watched for years — YouTube for business.

Who it suits

  • B2B companies and services: legal, finance, IT, construction.
  • Experts and personal brands — personal brand: where to start.
  • Education centres and clinics, in a Q&A format.

Who doesn't need it yet: simple retail, and businesses without capacity for content.

Choosing a format

FormatAdvantageDifficulty
Guest interviewsNew audiences, B2B connectionsFinding and preparing guests
Two hostsRegular and easyTopics can run out quickly
Q&AAnswers customers' questionsYou need to collect questions
Case breakdownsProof of expertiseRequires client consent

Topics and guests

  • The 20 questions customers ask most are a ready-made topic list — content from customer objections.
  • A guest should be a specialist useful to your customers, but not a competitor.
  • Each episode tackles one specific question: "Expanding to a second city: 5 mistakes" (an illustrative example).

Equipment: the minimum kit

  • Two microphones (sound matters most).
  • One or two cameras or phones, plus a tripod.
  • Lighting and a quiet room.
  • Editing software — video editing apps.

You don't need a studio — a well-equipped corner of the office is enough.

One recording, a week of content

  1. YouTube — the full episode.
  2. Reels/Shorts — the 5–10 strongest points (30–60 seconds each).
  3. Telegram — a short summary and a link.
  4. A post or carousel — the episode's 5 key ideas.
  5. A blog article — based on the episode, for your website.

Turning viewers into clients

  • End every episode with one action: a consultation, a free audit, a lead magnet — lead magnet.
  • Put a link with UTM tags in the description — UTM tags.
  • Partner with guests: they share the episode with their audience too.

Questions and answers

How often should we publish?

Consistency matters more: two episodes a month for three months beat six random ones.

How long should an episode be?

For a business podcast, 25–45 minutes is often enough. What matters is density of content.

When will we see results?

A podcast is a long-term tool. Short clips pay off faster; long episodes build trust and B2B relationships.
