Handling comments and DMs: how replies turn into sales

Ads and content bring people into your comments and DMs — that's where the sale starts. An unanswered question or a curt "price in DM" loses a customer who was ready to buy. Here are standards for handling comments and messages, ready templates and how to respond to criticism.

Why it matters

  • Everyone sees comments. One reply informs dozens of readers.
  • Speed decides the sale. Customers message several pages at once. More in our article on lead response time.
  • Engagement affects reach. A post with a lively discussion gets shown to more people.

Response standards

SituationStandard
Message during working hoursWithin a few minutes
Outside working hoursAuto-reply + first thing in the morning
Question in the commentsA public answer + an invitation to DM if needed
ComplaintCalm, with a concrete fix

Ready templates

Price: "Hello! It starts from 1,200,000 som; the exact amount depends on the size. Send me the size and I'll calculate it now."

Availability: "Yes, this model is in stock. Order today and we'll deliver tomorrow. Which colour would you like?"

Delivery: "Delivery within Tashkent is free; regions take 2–3 days. Which city are you in?"

A template is only a starting point: always adapt it to the customer's name and question. If every reply ends with a question, the conversation keeps going.

Negative comments

  1. Reply quickly and calmly — don't delete (except spam and abuse).
  2. Acknowledge the problem and offer a solution.
  3. Sort out the details in DM or by phone.
  4. Once resolved, post a short update in the comments.

A well-handled complaint often impresses new customers more than praise. For working with reviews, see our online reputation article.

Spam and automation

  • Turn on the hidden words filter in Instagram settings.
  • On a professional account, set up saved replies and FAQ buttons.
  • With high message volume, a chatbot can answer first questions and hand over to a manager.

What to measure

Weekly: number of messages, leads from them, sales from leads and average response time. For turning a chat into a sale, see our sales script article.

From DM to sale: the stages of a conversation

  1. Answer the question — price, availability or timing, without dodging.
  2. Clarify the need — one or two questions: size, colour, when they need it.
  3. Offer an option — with a photo or a short video.
  4. Handle the objection — price, trust, delivery.
  5. Ask for the next step — an address, a phone number or a payment method.
  6. Log it in the CRM — with the source "Instagram Direct", so the conversation isn't lost.

3 more templates

"That's expensive": "I understand. The price includes [what's included]. If you have a different budget in mind, there's also [a more affordable option] — shall I send a photo?"

"I'll think about it": "Of course! What would help you decide — photos in another colour or customer reviews? I'll send them now."

"Where are you located?": "We're at [address, landmark], open [hours]. Shall I send a map link? If you message us before you come, we'll have the model ready for you."

Common mistakes

  • Writing "price in DM" instead of answering the question in the comments.
  • Deleting negative comments.
  • Replying a week later.
  • Sending a template without a name or any changes.
  • Ending the conversation without a next step.

Questions and answers

Should you post the price openly in the comments?

Yes, at least a "from" price. A hidden price annoys many people, and others see the question too — an open answer saves their time as well.

Who should reply: the SMM manager or a salesperson?

Usually the SMM manager handles comments and first questions, while a sales manager takes the conversation about buying. What matters is a clear handover rule; how to split the tasks is covered in What an SMM manager does.

How do you get a customer from DMs into the CRM?

Through an integration or manually: name, contact, source and what they asked for. The key is that every real enquiry is in the CRM; choosing a system is covered in CRM for small business.

Can you reply with voice messages?

Yes, and sometimes they build more trust than text: the customer hears a real voice. But keep them short and repeat the key details (price, address) in text — not everyone can listen to a voice message right away.

Can you turn off comments?

When ad posts attract lots of spam, it's tempting to turn comments off. But comments are social proof and a source of questions. Instead, set up the hidden words filter and moderate comments daily: hide insults and spam, and answer the questions.

Conclusion

Comments and DMs are your page's checkout. Fast, specific and friendly replies, ready templates and calm handling of criticism bring more sales from the same ad budget.
