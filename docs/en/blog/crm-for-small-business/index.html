What a CRM is and which one suits a small business

Leads arrive from Instagram, Telegram, the phone and your website, managers keep them in notebooks and chats — and some get lost. A CRM (customer relationship management system) collects every lead in one place and tracks it through to the sale. Here is when you need a CRM, how to choose one and how to roll it out.

Signs you need a CRM

  • Leads get lost or go unanswered.
  • You don't know which ads bring sales.
  • When a manager leaves, the customer base leaves too.
  • Follow-ups get forgotten.
  • Comparing the sales plan with actual results is hard.

Start with a spreadsheet

While lead volume is low, Google Sheets is enough: date, name, phone, source, stage, amount, next step, manager. If there's no discipline with a spreadsheet, a CRM won't help either.

How to choose a CRM

CriterionWhy it matters
IntegrationsInstagram, Telegram, website forms, telephony — so leads arrive automatically
Stages (pipeline)Configurable to your sales process
Tasks and remindersSo follow-ups aren't forgotten
ReportsSources, conversion, managers' results
Mobile appManagers work from their phones
Language and supportA clear interface and help
PriceUsually a monthly fee per user

In Uzbekistan, small and medium businesses often use systems like amoCRM (Kommo) and Bitrix24; try several during free trials before choosing.

Rolling it out: 5 steps

  1. Define pipeline stages: new lead → contact made → offer sent → payment → closed.
  2. Mandatory fields: source, amount, reason (if lost).
  3. Connect integrations to cut manual entry.
  4. Train managers and set a rule: "If a lead isn't in the CRM, it doesn't exist."
  5. A weekly report based on your sales funnel.

Common mistakes

  • Over-complicated setup — managers stop using it.
  • Buying a CRM without a defined process.
  • Not recording the source — you can't evaluate your ads; for website leads, UTM tags help.

Weekly CRM reports

ReportThe question it answers
Leads by sourceWhere leads come from and which channel is stronger
Conversion by stageWhere customers drop out of the funnel
Reasons for losing a dealWhy people don't buy: price, timing, a competitor
Response timeHow long it takes from a lead to the first contact
Manager performanceHow many leads each manager got and how many became sales

Loss reasons are especially useful: if many customers leave saying "too expensive", the problem may be the offer or the sales conversation — see Sales script.

A first-month plan

  1. Week 1: set up stages, fields and integrations.
  2. Week 2: move current leads over and train the managers.
  3. Week 3: check daily CRM use and fix awkward fields.
  4. Week 4: the first report and a decision on what to change.

Will a CRM pay off? A simple calculation

An example (illustrative numbers): you get 100 leads a month and lose 10 of them. If 20% of leads turn into sales, that's 2 lost sales a month. Compare the profit from two sales with the cost of the CRM subscription and setup — often a single saved sale covers the cost of the system. How to measure ad efficiency is covered in How to calculate ROMI.

Questions and answers

Does a small business need a CRM, or is a spreadsheet enough?

If there are few leads and one person handles them, a spreadsheet is enough. Once there are several managers and channels and more follow-ups, a CRM saves time and reduces lost leads.

What should you do with your old customer list?

Import it into the CRM with name, phone, last purchase and a note. That list is the cheapest source of repeat sales.

What if managers don't use the CRM?

First find out why — often the setup is too complicated. Simplify the fields, then enforce a firm rule: bonuses are paid only for sales recorded in the CRM.

Should you set up the CRM yourself or hire a specialist?

You can often set up a simple funnel and one or two integrations yourself — the systems come with guides and templates. If you need telephony, several branches or complex automation, working with an integrator saves time. Either way, write down your sales process on paper first — nobody knows it better than you.

Conclusion

A CRM is the tool for not losing leads and managing sales by the numbers. Start with a spreadsheet, define the process, then move it into a CRM. Combined with fast replies, it grows sales quickly — read our lead response time article.
