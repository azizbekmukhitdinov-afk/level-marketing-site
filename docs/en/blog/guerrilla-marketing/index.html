Guerrilla marketing: winning attention on a small budget

Big brands spend millions on billboards and TV. Small businesses don't have that budget — but they do have ideas. Guerrilla marketing means winning attention with little money but in an unexpected, memorable way. The term became popular in the 1980s through Jay Conrad Levinson's book of the same name. Here's what it is and how a small business can use it.

The core idea

  • Surprise — people stop and take photos.
  • Low cost — creativity and time instead of money.
  • Sharing — a good guerrilla stunt spreads across social media by itself — viral marketing.
  • Local focus — a specific place and audience.

7 tactics for small businesses (illustrative examples)

  1. A message in an unexpected place: a café puts a playful sign in nearby office lifts — "Lunch is 3 floors down, fresh bread at 12:30" (with permission).
  2. A live demonstration: a furniture store sets up a "try it" sofa zone on the street.
  3. An interactive shop window: a question or game in the window — shop window display rules.
  4. Collaboration: a joint promotion with neighbouring businesses — a café and a florist.
  5. A street "gift": free branded umbrellas when it rains.
  6. An online challenge: a fun task involving your product, on Reels — using trends for business.
  7. Standing out at events: a memorable stand at a city event — event marketing.

Legal and ethical limits

Guerrilla marketing doesn't mean "anything goes":

  • Ads and structures in public spaces may need permits — check local rules.
  • Never place anything on someone else's property or wall without permission.
  • Disparaging competitors or making unfair comparisons is prohibited — advertising law in Uzbekistan.
  • Avoid stunts that frighten or mislead people.

Measuring results

A guerrilla stunt has to do more than look good. Measure:

  • visits and sales on the days of the stunt;
  • people arriving via a special promo code or QR code — promo codes in marketing;
  • social media mentions and shares.

Measuring offline advertising — measuring offline advertising.

Common mistakes

  • A great idea with no link to the brand — people remember the stunt but not who did it.
  • Placing things without permission — fines and reputational damage.
  • No call to action — attention but no leads.
  • A one-off stunt with no follow-up.

Questions and answers

Does guerrilla marketing suit every business?

Most local B2C businesses, yes. Conservative fields (healthcare, for example) need care.

How much money does it take?

The main resources are ideas and time. The costs go on materials and permits.

Is there online guerrilla marketing?

Yes: surprising Reels, creative replies in comments, challenges.

Conclusion

Guerrilla marketing wins attention on a small budget through surprise and memorability. A clear link to the brand, legal limits, a call to action and measurement are the conditions for success. In a free audit, Level Marketing can discuss ideas for your brand.
