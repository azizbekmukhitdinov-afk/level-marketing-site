How the Instagram algorithm works: what matters for reach

"The algorithm cut my reach" is the most common complaint in SMM. But Instagram doesn't have one "algorithm": Feed, Stories, Reels and Explore each run on their own rules. Instagram itself has explained in general terms how these systems work. Here is what affects reach and what to do in practice.

What Instagram takes into account

Each surface ranks content for a user using several groups of signals:

SignalExample
The user's activityWhich posts they like, save and watch
Information about the postHow quickly it gathers engagement, when it was posted
Information about the creatorHow often people interact with the account
Interaction historyWhether the user has messaged you or commented

What matters in Reels

Reels are shown mostly to people who don't follow you, so content quality decides everything:

  • Watch time and completion — the first seconds matter most; see our hooks article.
  • Shares — people sending the video to friends is a strong signal.
  • Saves, likes and comments.
  • Originality — videos reposted with other platforms' watermarks are usually shown less.

What matters in Feed and Stories

Here relationships decide: followers who regularly interact with you see your posts higher. So replying to comments and using interactive stickers in Stories helps your Feed reach too.

Habits that increase reach

  1. Post consistently — keep the rhythm.
  2. Strong first seconds and short, dynamic editing.
  3. Useful content worth saving and sharing.
  4. Replying to comments in the first hours.
  5. Collab posts with another account.
  6. Only your own videos, no watermarks.

Myths

  • "30 hashtags will boost reach." Hashtags help define the topic, but they're not a magic button.
  • "There's one perfect minute to post." Your audience's active hours matter, but not more than content quality.
  • "Any drop in reach is a shadowban." More often the content just isn't interesting to the audience.

If reach drops: a checking routine

  1. Widen the period — compare the last 1–3 months, not one week.
  2. Find out which format dropped — Reels, the feed or Stories.
  3. Recall what changed — topics, hooks, video length, posting rhythm.
  4. Check your account status — the Account Status section in settings shows whether there are limits on your content being recommended.
  5. Remove reposted videos and videos with watermarks.
  6. Test 2–3 new formats over two weeks.

What to look at in your insights

These tell you more than likes:

MetricWhat it means
Reach among non-followersWhether content is reaching recommendations
Average watch timeHow well the video holds attention
Shares and savesHow useful or interesting the content is
Profile visitsWhether the content sparks interest in the author

Common mistakes

  • Deleting and reposting a post when reach is low.
  • Buying followers and likes.
  • "Engagement pods" — trading artificial likes and comments.
  • Going quiet for a month, then posting ten times in one day.
  • Changing topics every week — the audience doesn't know what to expect.

Questions and answers

Does deleting and reposting a post help?

Usually not. Put the effort into the next post instead: improve the hook, the first seconds and the topic.

Do bought followers hurt?

Yes. They don't watch your content or engage, so your statistics get distorted and it's harder to understand your real audience. It also breaks the platform's rules.

How often should you post?

At a rhythm you can keep up. For example, 3–4 posts or Reels a week for months on end beat one busy month followed by silence. How to use trends well is covered in Using trends for business.

Does Reel length affect reach?

Length itself matters less than watch time and completion. If the idea fits into 15–30 seconds, don't stretch the video; if the topic needs a longer video, tell viewers in the first seconds why it's worth watching to the end.

Is there an honest way to boost reach quickly?

Yes — ads and collaborations. Promote an organic post that has already performed well, or publish a Collab post with relevant creators. Both show your content to new people, while services that promise to "trick" the algorithm only spoil your statistics and put the account at risk.

Conclusion

Don't fight the algorithm — feed it signals: content people finish, save and share. For planning, use a content plan; for growing reach fast, paid ads.
