Marketing a hotel: more direct bookings and higher occupancy

For many hotels, international booking sites are the main source of reservations. They bring guests, but take a commission on every booking — and the relationship with the guest stays with them. The main job of hotel marketing isn't to abandon booking sites but to gradually grow the share of direct bookings and fill empty dates. Here's how.

Booking channels: who brings what

ChannelAdvantageDrawback
Booking sitesInternational audience, trustCommission, limited guest data
Direct (website, phone, Telegram)No commission, you own the guest listYou have to bring the traffic
Travel agencies and corporate clientsLarge groups, planned aheadDiscounts and contracts
Maps"Hotel near me" searchesDepends on reviews and photos

The insight: a guest from a booking site is a first introduction. Give them a reason to book directly with you next time.

How to grow direct bookings

  • A website or booking page: rooms, prices, photos and an easy booking form — a landing page that converts.
  • A direct-booking perk: breakfast, late check-out, a transfer — extra value not available on booking sites (check rate-parity terms in your contract with the platform).
  • Fast replies on Telegram, WhatsApp and phone — answering enquiries within minutes — lead response time.
  • A returning-guest offer: a card or message at check-out.

Maps and reviews

Travellers in a new city search "hotel near me" or browse a map. On Google Maps, Yandex Maps and 2GIS, make sure you have:

  • the right category and address;
  • photos of every room type, the lobby, breakfast and entrance;
  • a phone number and website link;
  • replies to reviews — especially negative ones.

Handling reviews — online reputation and reviews. Getting listed — add your business to maps.

Photos and video are your main salesperson

Guests book without seeing the room, so photos decide:

  • daylight and a clean room;
  • an honest view: the window view, bathroom, bed size;
  • a short video tour from the entrance to the room;
  • what's nearby: metro, sights, restaurants.

Photos prettier than reality lead to negative reviews. What you show should match what guests find.

Content and advertising

  • Instagram/Telegram: city guides, guest stories, room-tour Reels.
  • Search ads for queries like "hotel in Tashkent" or "Samarkand hotel" — search ads in Tashkent.
  • Retargeting people who visited your site without booking — how retargeting works.
  • Language: English and Russian too, for foreign guests.

Seasonality and empty dates

An empty room is a loss you never get back. In the low season, look to:

  • corporate clients: business trips, training events;
  • local audiences: weekend packages, wedding guests;
  • long-stay rates.

More in advertising in low season.

A returning-guest database

Data from direct-booking guests is your asset. Collect it with consent and follow the personal data law — customer database and personal data.

Questions and answers

Should I drop booking sites?

No. They bring new guests. The goal is to grow your share of direct bookings.

Does a small hostel need a website?

A simple booking page and Telegram may be enough. What matters most is fast replies and good photos.

Which metrics should I track?

Occupancy, average daily rate and the share of bookings by channel.

Conclusion

Hotel marketing isn't a fight with booking sites but a balance with them: maps, reviews, honest photos, fast replies and direct-booking perks. Corporate and local offers fill the empty dates. In a free audit, Level Marketing will show you where your booking journey breaks down.
