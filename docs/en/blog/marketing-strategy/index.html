What is a marketing strategy and how to build one

People often picture a "marketing strategy" as a thick document, or something "for big companies". In reality, a strategy answers one question: who we sell to, what we sell, how, and with what budget. Marketing without a strategy means a new "let's try this" every month: an influencer today, a discount tomorrow, a new page after that. Here's what a marketing strategy is and how to build one. A ready template — one-page marketing strategy.

Strategy vs plan vs tactics

The 7 core parts

  1. Goal — in numbers: "X sales a month", "revenue of Y". KPIs — what is KPI.
  2. Audience — who buys and why — target audience and customer persona.
  3. Positioning — how you're remembered — brand positioning.
  4. Offer — what you sell, at what price and how you differ — unique selling proposition.
  5. Channels — where your customers are: paid social, search, maps, Telegram, influencers.
  6. Budget — by channel and by month — planning a marketing budget.
  7. Measurement — which metrics, when, and who reports — how to calculate ROMI.

How to build it in 5 steps

  1. Analyse the situation: numbers, competitors, customers — SWOT analysis, competitor analysis.
  2. Set the goal: realistic and measurable.
  3. Make choices: 2–3 core channels and one main message — not everything at once.
  4. Turn it into a plan: monthly actions and owners.
  5. Review monthly: compare plan and actuals, and adjust if needed.

For small businesses

You don't need a thick document. One page is enough if it's specific: who, what, where, how much and how we'll measure. What matters is coming back to it every month.

Common mistakes

  • Treating a list of channels ("Instagram, Telegram, TikTok") as a strategy.
  • A goal without numbers: "more customers".
  • One message for every audience.
  • A document nobody revisits all year.
  • A strategy built without the sales team — marketing and sales alignment.

Questions and answers

Can I build the strategy myself?

Yes, with a one-page template. But an outside view often spots mistakes you can't see.

When should the strategy change?

When the market, the product or the results shift sharply; otherwise, do a big review once a year.

How does a strategy differ from a marketing system?

The strategy is the direction; the system is the processes that carry it out every day — marketing system for growth.

Conclusion

A marketing strategy is a concrete answer to who you sell to, what, how and with what budget: goal, audience, positioning, offer, channels, budget and measurement.
