Instagram Content Protection: how a brand guards its Reels

You spent money and time shooting a Reel, and a week later the same video is circulating on someone else's page under someone else's logo. It happens in Tashkent more often than people think. Instagram Content Protection is Meta's new tool for this: it finds copies of your original videos on Instagram and Facebook, and you decide what happens to them. Here is how it works, who can use it and what a brand should prepare.

What Content Protection is

Content Protection launched on Instagram on 17 November 2026 (TechCrunch). Before that, it had been running on Facebook since November 2025. Now it checks both platforms together.

The mechanics are simple. You enrol, and Meta scans Instagram and Facebook for videos that visually match your originals. Each match appears in a list showing which profile posted it and how many views it got. Then you decide what to do with each copy.

One important difference: this is not YouTube's Content ID. On YouTube a rights holder can claim the revenue from a copy. On Instagram you cannot monetise copies — you can only track them, hide them or require a credit.

Your options when a copy is found

For each match there are four actions:

ActionWhat happensWhen to choose it
TrackThe copy stays up and you see its viewsHarmless, but worth keeping an eye on
HideNobody else can view the copyA competitor or unknown page passes your video off as its own
Add creditA link to your profile is added to the copyThe page may even promote you, but the author must be visible
ReleaseYou allow the copy and it leaves the listA partner, a creator or your own second account

There is also an allow list. Add your own second accounts and partner brands to it, and their posts of your videos won't show up as matches.

Not every copy is an enemy: some are free reach, as long as you get the credit.

Who can use it and how to enrol

The tool doesn't open for everyone automatically. Meta grants eligibility based on its "originality and integrity standards". Eligible accounts get a notification in their activity feed; others can apply.

You can't assume it's open to every business account in Uzbekistan. So:

  1. Check your Instagram notifications and professional dashboard for an invitation.
  2. If there isn't one, look for the option to apply.
  3. Tidy up the account: pages that repost a lot of other people's content will struggle to count as "original".

Once enrolled, new original videos are protected automatically. Older ones can be added manually — start with your most-viewed Reels and the ones you ran as ads.

Why it matters for a brand

Content Protection isn't only for creators. A business has three clear reasons to care.

Ad creatives. If a competitor posts the Reel that worked in your ads, buyers get confused: which shop is the real one? Product videos get lifted especially often in furniture, fashion and beauty.

The algorithm favours original content. On 30 April 2026 Instagram stopped recommending accounts that mostly repost other people's content — aggregators (Tubefilter). Meta rewards originality, and a brand that shoots its own video wins. What else drives reach is covered in The Instagram algorithm in 2026.

Evidence. A list of matches with their views is useful for a complaint or a conversation. But first make sure who actually owns the video: is it written into your contract with the agency, videographer or creator? That question is covered in Content copyright.

AI-modified videos

According to TechCrunch, Meta is also testing detection of AI videos that reuse a creator's audio. But for now this is a limited test, not a full feature. If someone "redraws" your video with a neural network or puts your voice over different footage, the tool won't always catch it.

The practical takeaway: don't rely on automation alone. Once a week, search for the title or key phrase of your main Reels, and collect customer messages like "I saw your video on another page".

Preparing your videos for protection

Whether or not you get into Content Protection, these steps pay off anyway.

1. Put the brand inside the frame

A small logo in the corner is easy to crop. Make the brand part of the shot: the shop sign, staff uniforms, the logo on the packaging, a recognisable interior. Whoever copies that video still advertises you.

2. Keep the originals

For every Reel, keep the source file, the shoot date and the edit project in one folder. In a dispute, what matters is who posted first and who holds the original.

3. Add partners to the allow list in advance

If a creator, distributor or branch officially posts your videos, add them to the allow list. Otherwise they'll show up as "copies" too. The rules for running creator content as ads are in Instagram partnership ads.

4. A separate rule for UGC

If a customer films a video about your product, it's their content, not yours. Ask permission before reposting. How to get it is explained in UGC content.

5. Control account access

Who can open the Content Protection settings? If a former manager still has access, they can "release" copies too. Sorting out access is covered in Business account security.

Found a copy: a decision guide

Illustrative example. A Tashkent furniture store's Reel about a sofa shows up on a page from another region — with a different phone number in the caption.

Here's how to decide:

  • Does the copy pretend to be you and give a different contact? Hide it. That's straightforward customer theft.
  • Is it on an entertainment page, with no contact details and lots of views? Add credit — that's free reach.
  • Was it posted by a partner or dealer? Release it and add them to the allow list.
  • Few views, harmless? Leave it on track.

Give one person responsibility for these decisions and review the match list once a month.

A 20-minute monthly review

Content Protection runs on its own, but someone has to look after it. Otherwise the match list piles up and nobody decides anything. Once a month, the social media manager or the owner does the following:

  1. Reviews new matches and picks one of the four actions for each.
  2. Re-checks tracked copies. A page that was harmless last month may have added its own number under your video.
  3. Updates the allow list. Is there a new partner, dealer or branch? Has a creator you stopped working with been removed?
  4. Adds older videos. Makes sure the Reels that ran as ads this month are protected.
  5. Writes a short note. How many copies were found, how many hidden, which video gets copied most.

The last step gives you a useful insight: the most-copied video is usually your strongest content. Think about using it more in ads and shooting more like it.

Common mistakes

  • Hiding every copy. Some of them could have brought you customers for free.
  • Not adding partners to the allow list. It leads to awkward conversations later.
  • Forgetting older videos. Automatic protection only covers new videos after enrolment.
  • Not knowing who owns the video. If rights aren't written into the agency or videographer contract, a dispute gets messy.
  • Expecting full protection from AI copies. That part is still a test.
  • Reposting other people's content yourself. It cuts your reach and makes it harder to qualify for Content Protection.

Questions and answers

Does Instagram Content Protection work in Uzbekistan?

The tool opens to eligible accounts; no exact country list has been published. Check your Instagram notifications and professional dashboard: if an invitation arrived, enrol; if not, look for the option to apply.

Can I earn money from copies?

No. Unlike YouTube's Content ID, Instagram doesn't let you monetise copies. You can only track, hide, add credit or release.

Are my older videos protected?

New original videos are protected automatically after enrolment. Older ones need to be added manually — start with your most-viewed and advertised Reels.

What if the copy is on Telegram or TikTok?

Content Protection only checks Instagram and Facebook. On other platforms, use their own reporting process.

Conclusion

Instagram Content Protection gives a brand control over its original Reels: it finds copies on Instagram and Facebook, and you choose whether to track them, hide them, add a credit or release them. It's open to eligible accounts, copies can't be monetised, and AI-copy detection is still a test. The main protection is still in your hands: the brand inside the frame, the originals stored safely and the rights written into your contracts.
