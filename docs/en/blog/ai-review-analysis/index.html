Analysing customer reviews with AI: 5 insights from 100 reviews

Reviews are the cheapest, most honest research a business can get: customers write down what they liked and what bothered them. Yet many businesses use them only at the level of "good review — great; bad review — reply". Reading 100 reviews and spotting recurring patterns takes time. AI cuts it down to an hour. Here's how to analyse reviews with AI and use the results.

Where to collect reviews

  • Maps listings: Google and Yandex Maps;
  • marketplaces: product reviews and questions;
  • Instagram and Telegram: comments, feedback in DMs;
  • surveys: short post-purchase questions — collecting customer feedback;
  • sales: lost reasons from the CRM.

Put them in one table: date, source, rating (if any), text.

Anonymising is mandatory

Before giving reviews to AI, remove:

  • names and surnames;
  • phone numbers, addresses;
  • order numbers and other identifiers.

The review text is enough for analysis. Uzbekistan has a specific fine for unlawfully processing personal data using AI (ZRU-1115, lex.uz). More in AI and the law in Uzbekistan.

The analysis: 4 prompts

1. Themes

Group these reviews by theme (e.g. quality, price, delivery, service, waiting time). How many reviews mention each theme? Give 2 example quotes per theme.

2. Sentiment

For each theme, what share of reviews is positive, negative and neutral?

3. Recurring problems

What are the 5 most frequent specific problems in the negative reviews? How many reviews for each, with a typical quote.

4. The customer's language

Which words and phrases do customers use when praising us? 15 phrases.

The last prompt is gold for marketing: your customers' language makes the best ad copy.

Checking the conclusions

AI's conclusion is a hypothesis. Check:

  • compare quotes with the original reviews — AI sometimes "polishes" a quote;
  • spot-check the numbers — group 10–20 reviews yourself and compare with the AI;
  • don't draw big conclusions from little data — 15 reviews aren't a trend.

Replying to negative reviews

AI quickly drafts a reply to a negative review: acknowledge, fix, contact. But:

  • a human reads and adapts every reply;
  • identical template replies are visible to everyone and lower trust;
  • if the problem isn't actually fixed, even the best reply won't help.

Rules for reviews — online reputation and reviews.

The rhythm

  • Monthly: analyse new reviews, 30 minutes.
  • Quarterly: a deep analysis and an action list.
  • After every major change: new product, price, process — how did reviews change?

Common mistakes

  • Uploading reviews with names and phone numbers to AI.
  • Analysing only negative reviews — positive ones teach a lot too.
  • Making decisions on AI conclusions without checking.
  • Analysing and then changing nothing.
  • Trying to "balance" things with fake reviews — that's a violation and a reputational risk.

Questions and answers

How many reviews do I need?

At least 50–100. Analysis is useful with fewer, but treat the conclusions with caution.

What if reviews are a mix of Russian and Uzbek?

AI understands both. Ask for the results in one language.

Can I analyse competitors' reviews?

Yes, public ones. They reveal market gaps — AI competitor analysis.

What if I have few reviews?

First set up a system for collecting them: post-purchase requests, a QR code, a salesperson's reminder.

Conclusion

Review analysis with AI is an hour's work: collect, anonymise, group by theme and sentiment, check the conclusions and turn them into actions — in service, ads, content and copy. Your customers' language is the best marketing material. When preparing creatives and content, Level Marketing draws on customers' real reviews and questions.
