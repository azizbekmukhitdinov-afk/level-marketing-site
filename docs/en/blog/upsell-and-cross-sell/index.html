Upselling and cross-selling: simple ways to raise order value

Winning a new customer is expensive. Offering something extra to a customer who's already buying is almost free. Upselling and cross-selling are the simplest ways to raise your average order value — from the café cashier asking "any dessert?" to the online store's "frequently bought together" block. Done badly, though, they annoy customers. Here's the right approach.

The difference

UpsellCross-sell
WhatA pricier or more complete versionAn extra, related product
ExampleAn 8 kg washing machine instead of 6 kgInstallation and an extended warranty with the washing machine
The question"Do you need something better?""What else do you need with this?"

The key rule: customer benefit

Upselling and cross-selling work when they solve the customer's problem better. An offer made "to hit the till target" destroys trust. Ask: "Does this offer genuinely help the customer?"

When to offer

  • After the decision is made, before payment — the customer has already said yes.
  • After the purchase — care products, accessories, services — repeat sales and LTV.
  • While uncovering needs — for upsells: "How many people are in your family?" helps show whether a larger model is needed.

Examples by industry (illustrative)

  • Café: a combo — meal + drink + dessert.
  • Beauty salon: a care treatment with a haircut.
  • Car service: a filter and diagnostics with an oil change.
  • Online store: a "bought together" block and add-ons in the basket.
  • Education: a package of one-to-one lessons with the main course.
  • B2B services: an extended report or an extra channel on top of the core package.

Bundles: the easiest cross-sell

"A complete bathroom set", "A starter kit" — the customer makes one decision and you get a bigger order. Price options — how to set prices.

A salesperson script

  1. Uncover the main need — sales script.
  2. Offer the main product.
  3. Make one additional offer, with a reason: "For the humidity you mentioned, this protective layer keeps the furniture in shape for 5 years."
  4. If they decline, no pressure.

More than one extra offer just confuses people.

Measurement

  • Average order value, before and after.
  • The share of orders where the extra offer was accepted.
  • Returns and complaints — if they rise, the offer doesn't suit customers.

Common mistakes

  • The same add-on offered to every customer.
  • Pressure and manipulation: "today only".
  • Offering extras before the main sale is closed.
  • Upsell targets for salespeople without training on customer benefit.

Questions and answers

Don't upsells annoy customers?

Not if the offer is useful and made once. Customers are often grateful.

Which is easier: upselling or cross-selling?

Usually cross-selling: a small extra decision doesn't change the main price.

How do you upsell online?

Options on the product page, recommendations in the basket and a post-purchase message.

Conclusion

Upselling and cross-selling are the cheapest way to raise order value — as long as the offer solves the customer's problem better.
