Shooting Reels on a phone: light, sound and framing

You don't need an expensive camera for Reels that sell — a modern phone is enough. A professional look comes not from gear but from three things: light, sound and framing. Here is how to set up your phone, shoot and quickly edit Reels.

Phone settings

  • Format: vertical 9:16, 1080p or 4K.
  • Frame rate: 30 fps for normal video, 60 fps for lots of movement.
  • Clean the lens — blurry video is often down to this.
  • Turn on the grid to frame shots evenly.
  • Lock exposure and focus — tap and hold on the face so the light doesn't "jump".
  • The main camera is better than the front one — use it where you can.

Light

  • Film by a window, facing the window.
  • A bright window behind you makes your face dark.
  • In the evening, use an inexpensive ring light or softbox.
  • Don't mix lamps of different colours.

Sound — the most important part

Viewers forgive an imperfect picture but not bad sound.

  • A wireless lavalier mic is the best investment.
  • Film in a quiet room with little echo.
  • Turn off the air conditioner, street noise and music.

Framing

Stability

A tripod is the cheapest and most essential piece of gear. For moving shots, use a gimbal or your phone's stabilisation mode.

The shooting process

  1. Write a script first — Reels script templates help.
  2. Make a shot list: the main shot and B-roll.
  3. Record each line 2–3 times.
  4. Film several videos in one day.

Quick editing

Cut the pauses, add captions, keep music quieter than your voice and aim for 15–45 seconds. For apps, see video editing apps. For the first seconds, see Reels hooks.

A pre-shoot checklist

  • The phone is charged and has at least a few gigabytes of free storage.
  • Do Not Disturb or airplane mode is on — a call won't ruin a take.
  • The lens is clean, the grid is on and exposure is locked.
  • You've made a 5-second test recording with the mic and listened to it on headphones.
  • The background is tidy: no clutter, cables or boxes.
  • The script or a list of key points is at hand.

B-roll: a list of extra shots

B-roll is the extra footage shown instead of the person talking. It hides cuts, keeps the pace and backs up what you say. On every shoot, capture a few of these:

  • hands working with the product;
  • close-ups of details: material, stitching, packaging;
  • the entrance and interior of the shop or office;
  • the team at work;
  • a customer receiving an order (with their permission);
  • a screen recording: the website, an app or a report.

Common mistakes

  • Shooting with your back to a window or lamp.
  • Digital zoom — quality drops sharply; move the phone closer instead.
  • Talking without a mic and far from the phone.
  • Looking at the screen instead of the lens — you lose eye contact.
  • Long handheld shots without a tripod.

Questions and answers

Can you shoot with the front camera?

Yes, especially if you need to see yourself. On modern phones the front camera is good enough for talking-head videos, but the main camera usually gives a sharper picture. Make a test recording with both and compare.

Do you need a teleprompter?

Teleprompter apps for phones help with longer text. But so the reading doesn't show, memorise short phrases and record the video in parts.

4K or 1080p?

4K gives you room to crop when editing, but the files are heavy. Instagram compresses video anyway, so 1080p is enough for most Reels. How to use one video in different formats is covered in Types of video ads.

How many videos can you shoot in one day?

With ready scripts, you can shoot 4–6 short videos in a few hours. Change clothes between them so the videos look as if they were filmed on different days.

Can you shoot outdoors?

Yes, but wind and noise are the main problems. Put a windscreen on the mic, choose a spot away from busy roads, and shoot in the shade or on an overcast day so the sun doesn't hit your face directly. Outdoor shots add life to a video, especially as B-roll.

Conclusion

Good light, clean sound, a stable shot and a script turn a phone into a studio. Level Marketing's videographers shoot many Reels by exactly these rules.
