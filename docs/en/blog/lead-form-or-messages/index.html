Lead form or Direct messages: which brings more sales

Instagram advertising has two main ways to collect leads: an instant lead form and messages in Direct. Both bring leads, but they suit different businesses. Here is how they differ, when each works better and how to improve lead quality.

The two approaches in brief

Instant form — the user leaves a name and phone number without leaving Instagram. Your manager then calls them.

Messages — the ad button opens a chat in Direct. The customer asks a question and your manager answers in the chat and leads them to a sale.

Cost per leadOften cheaperUsually higher
Lead qualityVariableOften higher
Manager's jobCall backSell in the chat

When the instant form wins

  • The service is sold by phone: clinics, education centres, real estate, renovation.
  • You have someone who calls leads back quickly.
  • You need volume and have a process for qualifying leads.

When messages win

  • Customers ask first: price, size, colour, delivery.
  • Visual products: clothing, furniture, cosmetics.
  • A small team is used to selling in chat.

How to improve form lead quality

  1. Try the "higher intent" form type instead of "more volume" — it adds a step where people confirm their details.
  2. Add one qualifying question: "Which district are you in?" or "When do you plan to start?".
  3. State the offer and the next step at the top of the form: "A manager will call you within 15 minutes".
  4. Call back as fast as possible — leads cool off quickly.

How to get more sales from messages

  • Set up an automatic greeting and buttons for frequent questions.
  • Response standard: a few minutes during working hours.
  • Prepare a chat script — greeting, discovery, offer, close.

How to choose

The most reliable way is to test both in parallel and compare cost per sale, not cost per lead. If cheap form leads don't convert, pricier message leads may turn out more profitable. For running the test, see our A/B testing guide; for CPL, our CPL guide.

Sample questions for a lead form

An example (illustrative): a form for a dental clinic.

  1. Name — filled in automatically from Instagram.
  2. Phone number — filled in automatically; the customer checks it.
  3. "Which service are you interested in?" — options: treatment, implants, whitening, children's dentistry.
  4. "When would you like to come in?" — options: this week, next week, not sure yet.

Thank-you screen text: "Thank you! Our administrator will call you within 15 minutes. While you wait, have a look at our prices" — with a link to the price page.

Multiple-choice questions are easy for the customer and let you sort the leads.

A sample greeting for messages

"Hello! Thanks for getting in touch. Choose one of the questions below or type your own."

Quick-question buttons: "Prices", "Address and opening hours", "Talk to a manager". Prepare a ready answer for each button, but end it with a question that keeps the conversation going.

Common mistakes

  • Calling form leads back at the end of the day.
  • Too many questions in the form — people leave without filling it in.
  • Not a single qualifying question — random leads multiply.
  • Nobody answering messages in the evenings and at weekends.
  • Comparing the two approaches by cost per lead alone.

Questions and answers

How do you get form leads into the CRM?

Ideally through a CRM integration: the lead reaches a manager within seconds. Without one, you can download leads from Meta Business Suite, but that increases the risk of delays and lost leads.

Can you send people to WhatsApp or Telegram?

Meta ads offer WhatsApp as a separate destination option. Telegram is usually reached through a regular link, which gives you less data in the ads manager, so track the results separately.

Can you use both approaches at once?

Yes, and that's the best test. Run them in separate ad sets with the same budget and compare the cost per sale after a week or two.

What if a form lead doesn't answer the phone?

Call 2–3 times at different times of day, then send a short SMS or messenger note: "You left a request about…, when would be a good time to talk?" If there are many such leads, try the "higher intent" form type.

How many questions should a form have?

Name, phone and one or two qualifying questions are usually enough. Each extra question reduces the number of leads but can raise their quality. If you get plenty of leads but few sales, add a question; if leads are scarce, shorten the form.

Conclusion

Forms bring fast, cheap leads; messages bring warmer conversations. The right choice depends on your sales process. At Level Marketing we test both in every project and decide by sales figures.
