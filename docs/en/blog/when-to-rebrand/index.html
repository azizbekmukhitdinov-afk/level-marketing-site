When you need a rebrand and how to do it

A rebrand is a big decision: a new name, logo or positioning can attract customers or drive them away. Here is when you need a rebrand, how it differs from restyling and how to carry it out without losing customers.

Signs you need a rebrand

  • Your audience has changed: you now sell to a different segment.
  • The brand looks dated and doesn't stand out from competitors.
  • The name causes confusion or resembles another company.
  • The business has moved into a new direction or merged.
  • A bad reputation has attached itself to the old name.

If the only problem is a "boring logo", a restyle is often enough rather than a full rebrand.

Restyle vs rebrand

RestyleRebrand
What changesVisual style: logo, colours, fontsPositioning, promise, sometimes the name
RiskLowHigh
TimelineA few weeksA few months
WhenThe brand is right but datedThe brand no longer fits the business

The stages

  1. Audit. Ask customers how they know you, what they value and what they dislike.
  2. Strategy. A new positioning and promise, using the one-page platform from what a brand is.
  3. Name (if needed) — rules in how to name a business.
  4. Visual identity — logo, colours, templates; more in visual identity for small business.
  5. A launch plan — a list of every touchpoint and a date.
  6. Communication — explain to customers what changed and why.

Touchpoint checklist

ChannelWhat to update
Social mediaAvatar, name, bio, highlight covers
WebsiteLogo, colours, copy
MapsName, photos, description
OfflineSignage, packaging, business cards, uniforms
DocumentsContract and invoice templates

How not to lose customers

  • Keep recognisable elements: a colour, symbol or slogan.
  • Announce the change in advance: "We're refreshing — here's why".
  • For a while, add "formerly …" so people searching the old name can find you.
  • Update every channel at once — a mixed style confuses people.

Conclusion

A rebrand is a strategic decision, not a visual one. Listen to customers first, change the positioning next and only then move on to design. For a branding project that delivered, see our Global Avenue case.
