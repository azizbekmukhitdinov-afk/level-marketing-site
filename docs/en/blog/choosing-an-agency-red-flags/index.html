Choosing a marketing agency: 10 red flags and the questions to ask before you sign

When business owners choose a marketing agency, they fear two things: losing money and losing time. The second is often more expensive: 3–4 months with the wrong agency means a missed season, a demoralised team and the false conclusion that "marketing doesn't work for us". This article covers how to vet an agency before signing, which signs should worry you, and which ones build trust.

Why the choice is so hard

An agency's service is a product you can't try in advance. You're buying a promise. So many agencies package the promise beautifully: logo lists, "full service", "guaranteed results". None of it proves your business will get results.

For a confident choice, check three things:

  1. Proof — what was done for businesses like yours, and with what numbers.
  2. Process — what happens in the first month, what the report looks like, who's responsible.
  3. Terms — who owns the ad account, and on what terms you can end the contract.

10 red flags

1. Results are "guaranteed"

Ad results depend on the offer, price, sales team and market. An agency doesn't control all of that. A "100% guarantee" is a sign of either inexperience or manipulation.

2. A logo list instead of case studies

"We've worked with these brands" isn't a result. Ask: what exactly was done, over what period, and how did the numbers change?

3. Not a single case in your niche

That's not a reason to walk away immediately, but it is a reason to ask questions. How will they learn your niche? What experience do they have in related fields?

4. "We do everything"

The longer the list of services, the more specifically you should ask: who actually does this? Who's on the team, and how many projects do they handle?

5. Reviews without numbers

"Great team, highly recommend" is nice but useless. A good review has a "before → after": leads, sales, timeframe.

6. The ad account is in the agency's name

The ad account, page and pixel should always be in your name, with the agency as a partner. Otherwise, when the contract ends, all the data and history stay with the agency.

7. They won't show a sample report

A report should include spend, leads, cost per lead, sales and next steps. Reach and likes alone are a warning sign — what an ad report should include.

8. No questions in the first meeting

If an agency names a price straight away without asking about your numbers, customers and sales process, it works from a template.

9. The only suggestion is a bigger budget

"No results — raise the budget." A good agency first looks at where the money leaks — before you increase your ad budget.

10. Running down others

An agency that sells itself by criticising competitors says little about its own results. A good agency speaks through proof.

Green signs

  • case studies with numbers in your niche or a related one: what was done, how long it took, the result;
  • lots of questions in the first meeting, and pinning down point A;
  • a written first-month plan — first 30 days with a marketing agency;
  • a sample report and a reporting schedule;
  • the ad account in your name;
  • willingness to say "ads won't help you right now" if that's the truth.

10 questions to ask before signing

  1. Can you show a case in my niche or a related one, with numbers?
  2. Who will work on my project, and who's accountable?
  3. What exactly happens in the first month?
  4. What will the report look like, and when? Show me a sample.
  5. Which KPIs will we agree on? — KPIs in an agency contract
  6. Who will own the ad account and the content?
  7. What do you need from me: approval times, data, the sales team?
  8. What will you do if there are no results?
  9. On what terms can the contract be ended?
  10. Which client left you most recently, and why?

The last question is uncomfortable but very useful: an honest agency will answer openly.

How to check: practical steps

  • Prepare a brief and give 2–3 agencies the same information — comparing becomes easier — how to fill in a marketing brief.
  • Use a free audit or consultation. You'll see how the agency thinks: does it ask questions, does it work with your numbers?
  • Talk to their clients. Ask for contacts of 1–2 current clients.
  • Treat the first 2–3 months as a trial period, with specific KPIs and a report date.

Agency, freelancer or in-house?

Sometimes the problem isn't the agency but the model: a freelancer may be enough for one specific task, while at larger scale an in-house marketer and an agency work together — agency, freelancer or in-house.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing on price alone.
  • Expecting results within a month, then switching agencies.
  • Opening the ad account in the agency's name.
  • Leaving reporting and KPIs out of the contract.
  • Forgetting your own obligations (approvals, the sales team).

Questions and answers

Is the cheapest agency always a risk?

Not necessarily, but a low price often means little time, a template approach or an inexperienced specialist. Compare the price with the scope of work and the team.

Can I work with an agency that has no cases in my niche?

Yes, if it has results in related fields and proposes a concrete plan to learn your business.

How soon can I judge an agency?

On at least 2–3 months of data. But the quality of the process, reporting and communication is visible in the first month.

How do I end work with an agency?

Within the timeframe and procedure set out in the contract. Make sure in advance that the ad account, content and data stay with you.

Conclusion

Choose an agency on proof, process and terms — not promises. Guarantee promises, cases without numbers, an ad account in the agency's name and no sample report are warning signs.
