7 types of video ads: which one will sell your product

The same product can be advertised with different video formats — and results can differ several times over. It's hard to guess in advance which format will work, so you need to prepare several types and test them. Here are the 7 video ad types that sell best and when each works.

The 7 types

TypeWhen it worksExample
ReviewThe product sells by being shown"5 advantages of this sofa"
Before / afterThe result is visibleRenovation, beauty, cleaning
Customer testimonialTrust decidesClinics, education, expensive services
ProcessQuality and craft matterFurniture making, kitchens
ExpertA complex choiceDoctor, lawyer, technical advice
Problem → solutionThe customer has a specific pain"Back pain? Here's why"
OfferA warm audience, a promotion"This week only: free measurement"

Tips for each type

  • Review: one video, one product, its 3–5 key features.
  • Before / after: shoot from the same angle and light so the difference is obvious.
  • Testimonial: a real customer in their own words — don't make them read a script. For collecting customer videos, see our UGC content article.
  • Process: fast editing, 10–20 seconds, rhythmic music.
  • Expert: open with a question and explain in plain words.
  • Problem → solution: the problem in the first second, a specific action at the end.
  • Offer: terms, deadline and how to order — clear at a glance.

Which suits you

Ask three questions:

  1. Can you show the product's result? If yes: before/after and review.
  2. Do customers think long before deciding? If yes: testimonial and expert.
  3. Does the audience already know you? If yes: offer.

How to test

Prepare 3–4 videos of different types at once and compare them on the same audience and budget, following the rules of A/B testing. Next month, make several variations of the winning type.

A sample script for each format

  • Review (20 seconds): one line about the problem → the product in close-up → 3 advantages → price and call to action.
  • Before / after (10–15 seconds): the "before" shot → transition → the "after" shot → a short line about the result.
  • Customer testimonial (30 seconds): the customer introduces themselves → what the problem was → why they chose you → what result they got.
  • Problem → solution (20 seconds): open with a question → the cause → the solution → the offer.

For detailed templates, see Reels scripts: 5 ready-made templates.

Length and format

Vertical 9:16 is the main format for Reels, Stories and TikTok. Most ad videos need 15–30 seconds; testimonials and expert videos can run a little longer. Put the key idea in the first 3 seconds, and always add captions for people watching without sound.

Common mistakes

  • All videos in one format — nothing to compare.
  • Opening with a logo in the first second.
  • Trying to say everything in one video.
  • Showing stock footage instead of the real product.

Questions and answers

Do you need professional production?

Usually not. A phone video with good light and a clear script performs no worse than a studio one. For shooting tips, see Shooting Reels on a phone.

How many videos do you need a month?

For testing, at least 3–4 videos in different formats. Once you find a winner, make several variations of it and add new ones every month as creatives start to wear out.

Which format gives the cheapest lead?

You can't know in advance — it depends on the product, audience and offer. That's why formats need to be compared under the same conditions.

Can you use one video on different platforms?

Yes. A vertical 9:16 video fits Reels, Stories, TikTok and YouTube Shorts. But adapt it for each platform: check that interface buttons don't cover the text and captions, remove another app's watermark, and write the post text for that platform's audience. That way one shoot gives you content for several channels. For ads, check each platform's requirements and formats separately.

Conclusion

Rotating video types is the simplest way to stop creatives wearing out and keep cost per lead in check. Every video needs a strong opening — see our hooks article.
