Advertising in the low season: stop or keep going?

Almost every business has a season: summer for air-conditioner sellers, autumn and spring for wedding venues, September for education centres, 8 March for flower shops. When the low season arrives, the natural reaction is "there's no demand — switch off the ads and save money". Sometimes that's right. But often, stopping ads completely costs you dearly in the next high season. Here's what to do with advertising in the low season.

Why switching off completely is risky

1. The account and algorithm "go cold"

Ad campaigns learn from data. An account paused for several months goes through learning again in the high season — and results are unstable in exactly the weeks that matter most. More in Meta learning limited.

2. Your audience forgets you

Even in the low season, people are in the middle of decisions. For example, couples marrying in autumn look for a venue in summer. If they don't see you then, they see a competitor.

3. Your retargeting pool empties

Site visitors, video viewers and people who engaged with your profile — these audiences age over time. Stop completely, and at the start of the high season you'll have little to retarget. More in how retargeting works.

4. Ads are often cheaper in the low season

When many competitors pause, competition in the auction may be lower. It's a good time to build awareness and collect an audience.

When stopping is right

  • The business doesn't operate at all in the low season (say, a summer-only resort) and customers don't need long to decide.
  • Cash flow is tight and every soum is needed to survive.
  • There's no product: the warehouse is empty and new stock arrives for the high season.

Even then, a small "maintenance" budget for awareness and retargeting is often worthwhile.

Off-season offers

The low season isn't an absence of demand — it's different demand. Examples (illustrative):

  • Air conditioners in winter: "Install in winter — no queue in summer, lower price."
  • Wedding venues in winter: corporate events, birthdays, small gatherings.
  • Education centres in summer: summer intensives, trial lessons.
  • Furniture after the holidays: "A new kitchen after New Year, ready by spring."
  • Clinics — preventive check-ups, family packages.

Off-season customers are often thrifty or planners — "order early, get better terms" works well for them.

What to spend low-season time on

The low season is a time to work on the system, not the ads:

  1. Creative tests: try new ideas in a cheaper auction — run only proven ones in the high season. More in A/B testing ad creatives.
  2. A content reserve: film videos and posts for the high season in advance.
  3. The sales team: scripts, CRM, response speed — there won't be time in the high season.
  4. Existing customers: repeat sales, collecting reviews, a referral programme — repeat sales and LTV.
  5. An audit: what worked last season and what didn't — marketing audit checklist.

Switching ads on once the high season has started is too late. A plan:

Common mistakes

  • Switching ads off completely and restarting from zero at the start of the high season.
  • Showing the high-season offer in the low season — people don't need it now.
  • Treating the low season as "time off" and not working on the system.
  • Comparing month to month without accounting for seasonality, then panicking.
  • Raising the budget sharply all at once in the high season.

Questions and answers

How much should I cut the budget in the low season?

It depends on the business, but a "maintenance" mode often beats zero. Calculate the budget from your unit economics and cash flow.

If results are poor in the low season, does that mean ads don't work?

No. Compare results with the same period last year, not with last month.

Should the off-season offer be cheaper?

Not necessarily. Instead of a discount, offer another advantage: no queue, faster service, an extra service. More in the discount trap.

Should I pause my agency contract in the low season?

That's your call. But the low season is the best time for testing and preparation; stopping completely makes the high-season restart more expensive.

Conclusion

The low season isn't the time to switch ads off — it's the time to change mode: a small maintenance budget, an off-season offer and preparation for the high season. Use the period to test creatives, build a content reserve and tune your sales system.
