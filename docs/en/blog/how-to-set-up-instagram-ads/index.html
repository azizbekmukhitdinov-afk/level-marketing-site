How to set up Instagram ads: a step-by-step guide

For many businesses in Uzbekistan, Instagram is the main sales channel: customers discover products there, message in Direct and buy. But pressing "Boost" under a post is not real targeted advertising. This guide walks through setting up Instagram ads properly in Meta Ads Manager.

Why the Boost button is not enough

The in-app button is quick but limited: almost no fine audience control, no testing of multiple creatives, no lead forms and no deep analytics. Meta Ads Manager (adsmanager.facebook.com) is the full tool: you manage campaigns, audiences and creatives separately and see which ad brings leads.

Before you start you need:

  • an Instagram professional account (business or creator);
  • a linked Facebook Page;
  • Meta Business Suite (business.facebook.com) with an ad account;
  • a payment card (Visa or Mastercard; some local cards may not work).

Step 1: choose the campaign objective

When you create a campaign, Meta offers six objectives: awareness, traffic, engagement, leads, app promotion and sales. The objective matters because the algorithm looks for people most likely to take exactly that action.

Your goalObjective
People message you in Direct or TelegramEngagement → messages
Collect phone numbersLeads → instant form
Purchase or form on your websiteSales or leads (Pixel required)
Introduce a new brandAwareness

For most small businesses the clearest start is messages or leads via an instant form.

Step 2: set up the audience

At the ad set level you decide who sees the ad:

  1. Location. Only the city or area you serve. If you work in Tashkent, advertising to the whole country wastes budget.
  2. Age and gender. Based on your real buyers, not "everyone".
  3. Interests or Advantage+ audience. Meta now recommends giving the algorithm more freedom. In a new account, test one or two broad interest groups against Advantage+.
  4. Language. Separate ad sets for Uzbek and Russian creatives make results easier to compare.

Step 3: placements and budget

Instagram's main placements are Feed, Stories and Reels. With Advantage+ placements on, Meta distributes delivery itself; to show ads only on Instagram, choose placements manually.

Set a daily budget and avoid changing it often in the first days: each big change sends the algorithm back into learning. We explain budget planning in detail in our guide to ad costs.

Step 4: creative and copy

On Instagram the creative drives most of the result:

  • Vertical format (9:16 for Reels and Stories, 4:5 for Feed).
  • The first 2–3 seconds are the hook: a problem, a result or an intriguing question. See our Reels hooks article.
  • A specific offer: price, discount, free consultation or measurement.
  • A call to action: "Message us in Direct", "Fill in the form".
  • Launch 3–5 creatives at once — you cannot predict the winner in advance.

Step 5: lead form or message settings

In an instant form keep questions to a minimum: name and phone are usually enough. One qualifying question ("Which district are you in?") filters out poor leads. For message campaigns, set up an automatic greeting and FAQs.

Above all, reply fast. People message several companies at once and choose the first one to answer.

What to watch in the first week

  1. Cost per lead (CPL) — how much one lead costs. See our CPL guide.
  2. CTR — the share of viewers who clicked. A low CTR means a weak creative or offer.
  3. Frequency — how many times one person saw the ad. Too high means the audience is tired.
  4. Lead quality — ask your sales team whether leads turn into purchases.

Switch off the worst creatives, move budget to the best and add new creatives every week.

Conclusion

Instagram advertising is not a single button but a system: objective, audience, creative and lead handling. When all four parts work, ads bring a steady flow of leads. At Level Marketing we build that system for every project and review results with the client in person every month.
