How to set up Instagram ads: a step-by-step guide

For many businesses in Uzbekistan, Instagram is the main sales channel: customers discover products there, message in Direct and buy. But pressing "Boost" under a post is not real targeted advertising. This guide walks through setting up Instagram ads properly in Meta Ads Manager.

Why the Boost button is not enough

The in-app button is quick but limited: almost no fine audience control, no testing of multiple creatives, no lead forms and no deep analytics. Meta Ads Manager (adsmanager.facebook.com) is the full tool: you manage campaigns, audiences and creatives separately and see which ad brings leads.

Before you start you need:

  • an Instagram professional account (business or creator);
  • a linked Facebook Page;
  • Meta Business Suite (business.facebook.com) with an ad account;
  • a payment card (Visa or Mastercard; some local cards may not work).

Step 1: choose the campaign objective

When you create a campaign, Meta offers six objectives: awareness, traffic, engagement, leads, app promotion and sales. The objective matters because the algorithm looks for people most likely to take exactly that action.

Your goalObjective
People message you in Direct or TelegramEngagement → messages
Collect phone numbersLeads → instant form
Purchase or form on your websiteSales or leads (Pixel required)
Introduce a new brandAwareness

For most small businesses the clearest start is messages or leads via an instant form.

Step 2: set up the audience

At the ad set level you decide who sees the ad:

  1. Location. Only the city or area you serve. If you work in Tashkent, advertising to the whole country wastes budget.
  2. Age and gender. Based on your real buyers, not "everyone".
  3. Interests or Advantage+ audience. Meta now recommends giving the algorithm more freedom. In a new account, test one or two broad interest groups against Advantage+.
  4. Language. Separate ad sets for Uzbek and Russian creatives make results easier to compare.

Step 3: placements and budget

Instagram's main placements are Feed, Stories and Reels. With Advantage+ placements on, Meta distributes delivery itself; to show ads only on Instagram, choose placements manually.

Set a daily budget and avoid changing it often in the first days: each big change sends the algorithm back into learning. We explain budget planning in detail in our guide to ad costs.

Step 4: creative and copy

On Instagram the creative drives most of the result:

  • Vertical format (9:16 for Reels and Stories, 4:5 for Feed).
  • The first 2–3 seconds are the hook: a problem, a result or an intriguing question. See our Reels hooks article.
  • A specific offer: price, discount, free consultation or measurement.
  • A call to action: "Message us in Direct", "Fill in the form".
  • Launch 3–5 creatives at once — you cannot predict the winner in advance.

Step 5: lead form or message settings

In an instant form keep questions to a minimum: name and phone are usually enough. One qualifying question ("Which district are you in?") filters out poor leads. For message campaigns, set up an automatic greeting and FAQs.

Above all, reply fast. People message several companies at once and choose the first one to answer.

What to watch in the first week

  1. Cost per lead (CPL) — how much one lead costs. See our CPL guide.
  2. CTR — the share of viewers who clicked. A low CTR means a weak creative or offer.
  3. Frequency — how many times one person saw the ad. Too high means the audience is tired.
  4. Lead quality — ask your sales team whether leads turn into purchases.

Switch off the worst creatives, move budget to the best and add new creatives every week.

Campaign structure: three levels

In Ads Manager, every ad has three levels:

  1. Campaign — the objective: messages, leads or sales.
  2. Ad set — audience, placements, schedule and budget.
  3. Ad — creative, copy and button.

A simple structure is enough for a first test: one campaign, two ad sets by language (Uzbek and Russian) and 3 different creatives in each. Within a month you'll see which language and which creative work best.

Sample ad copy (illustrative)

  • Primary text: "A sofa made to your measurements — in 10 days. Instalments and free delivery across Tashkent. Message us for the price."
  • Headline: "Made-to-order sofas".
  • Button: "Send message".

The first line of the text matters most — it's visible without tapping "More". Keep the offer and promises true.

A pre-launch checklist

  • Business account and payment method set up.
  • Instagram account connected to Meta Business Suite.
  • If you have a website, the Pixel is installed and events are checked.
  • The form is tested: a test lead was submitted and received.
  • An automatic greeting and quick replies are ready for DMs.
  • It's decided who answers leads and how quickly.
  • Links carry UTM tags.
  • Budget and dates are checked.

Weeks 2–4: optimisation

  • Once there's enough data, switch off ads with a high cost per lead.
  • Add new creatives to the winning ad set.
  • Test one new audience or offer.
  • Don't change settings every day — let the algorithm learn.
  • Raise the budget in stages.

Common mistakes

  • Boosting a post with the "Boost" button instead of using Ads Manager.
  • A very narrow audience and a single creative.
  • Restricting placements manually far too much.
  • Replying to leads late.
  • Running without tracking — not knowing which ad brought the sale.

Add a retargeting campaign

After 2–3 weeks, once the main campaign has built up an audience, add a second campaign for people who engaged with your profile, watched a video or visited your site but didn't leave a request. It needs different creatives — reviews, answers to objections and a time-limited offer. Exclude people who already submitted a lead, and give this campaign a small share of the budget. More in How retargeting works.

The key columns in your report

Customise the columns in Ads Manager and save them as a preset: spend, results, cost per result, link click-through rate (CTR), frequency and cost per thousand impressions (CPM). Those six columns show the state of your ads at a glance.

Questions and answers

How long does ad review take?

Usually a few hours, sometimes up to a day. If an ad is rejected, read the reason, bring the ad in line with the policies and request a review — more in Ad account blocked.

Is a Facebook Page required?

Working through Ads Manager usually requires a Facebook Page with an Instagram account connected to it. Link the two in Meta Business Suite — it makes management easier too.

Should ads be in Uzbek or Russian?

It depends on your audience. If it's bilingual, create separate ad sets in both languages. Make each language's creative feel natural — machine translation shows immediately.

Daily or lifetime budget?

A daily budget suits ongoing campaigns. For promotions tied to a specific date, a lifetime budget with an end date works better.

Ads aren't showing — what could be wrong?

The ad is still in review or was rejected, there's a payment problem, the audience is too small, the budget is too low or the account is restricted. Check the status and warnings in Ads Manager.

When should you stop an ad?

If, despite changes, the cost per lead stays above your maximum affordable level for a week or two, or the product is out of stock. One bad weekend day is no reason to stop.

Where should you look at results?

In Ads Manager: spend, leads and cost per lead. But your CRM gives the final answer — how many leads turned into sales. Look at both together; the cost per lead guide helps with this.

Should you use Advantage+ campaigns?

They choose the combination of audience, placements and creatives automatically and work well once there are enough conversions. In a new ad account, start with a standard campaign, then test Advantage+ separately and compare the results.

Can you advertise several products in one campaign?

A separate ad set for each product or offer is better — then you can see what's working. For stores with a large range, catalogue ads are more convenient.

Conclusion

Instagram advertising is not a single button but a system: objective, audience, creative and lead handling. When all four parts work, ads bring a steady flow of leads. At Level Marketing we build that system for every project and review results with the client in person every month.
