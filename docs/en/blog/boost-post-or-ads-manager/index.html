Instagram's Boost button or Ads Manager: which should you use?

Every Instagram post has a "Boost" button: a few taps and the post becomes an ad. Many business owners start paid social with that button. But the result is often "lots of views, no leads". Here's the difference between the button and Meta Ads Manager — and when to use which.

The key difference

The Boost buttonAds Manager
WhereIn the Instagram appIn a browser or the Meta Ads Manager app
ObjectivesLimited: profile visits, website, messagesAll: leads (lead forms), sales, traffic, reach and more
CreativeAn existing postNew ads, multiple variants, A/B tests
AudienceAutomatic or simple settingsFull: custom, lookalike, exclusions
PlacementsMainly InstagramInstagram, Facebook, Messenger, Threads and more
ReportingBasicDetailed, by ad and audience
Pixel and conversionsLimitedFull

When the button is enough

  • Showing a post that's already performing well to more people.
  • A small test: "Is this topic interesting?"
  • Local news: a new branch opening, an event.
  • When the goal is DMs and you can reply quickly.

When you need Ads Manager

  • You need leads: lead forms can only be fully set up in Ads Manager — lead form or messages.
  • Several creatives and tests: which brings cheaper leads? — A/B testing ad creatives.
  • Website and sales, with the Pixel and Conversions API — Meta Pixel and Conversions API.
  • Retargeting website visitors and video viewers — how retargeting works.
  • Working with an agency: access rights and reporting — give an agency access to your Meta ad account.

Using the button properly

If you do use the button:

  1. Objective: "Messages" or "Website" — "Profile visits" rarely bring leads.
  2. Audience: a city and radius; very narrow interests aren't necessary.
  3. Post: a Reel with a clear offer in the first seconds — Reels hooks.
  4. Duration: at least 5–7 days — one day won't show results.
  5. Replies: answer DMs fast.

Common mistakes

  • Boosting for "more people" — lots of likes, no customers.
  • Boosting a different post every day, so the system never learns.
  • Judging results by reach alone.
  • Running boosts and Ads Manager campaigns at once without a plan, spreading the budget thin.

Questions and answers

Are boosted posts cheaper?

Prices are set in the same auction. The difference lies in settings and objectives: the wrong objective gives "cheap but useless" results.

Do boosted posts show up in Ads Manager?

Yes — reports and payments appear in your Meta ad account.

What budget should I start with?

It depends on your industry and goal — paid ads cost in Uzbekistan.

Conclusion

The Boost button is for quickly lifting a good post; for leads, tests, retargeting and measuring sales, you need Ads Manager. Choose the right objective and judge results by leads and sales. In a free audit, Level Marketing will check your ads account.
