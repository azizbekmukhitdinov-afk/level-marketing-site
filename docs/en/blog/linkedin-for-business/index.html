LinkedIn for business: who needs it and how to start

LinkedIn for business is not a replacement for Instagram. People come here to find jobs, partners and suppliers, not to shop. That means LinkedIn is not for everyone, but for a company in B2B, IT, exports or one that hires a lot, it can become a strong channel. Below: who needs it, where to start and which mistakes to avoid.

Who needs LinkedIn in Uzbekistan

There are not many LinkedIn users from Uzbekistan, but they are the "right" people: managers, IT specialists, staff of international companies, HR and procurement teams. According to NapoleonCat's ad audience estimate, in 2026 LinkedIn could reach roughly 1.4 million users in Uzbekistan (NapoleonCat). It is an estimate, and the number of active users is noticeably lower.

Which businesses it suits:

Business typeWhat LinkedIn gives
IT company, outsourcingForeign clients, hiring developers
Exporting manufacturerContact with foreign buyers and distributors
B2B services (audit, legal, logistics)Access to decision-makers, expertise
Training centre (corporate training)Reaching HR directors
Large companyEmployer brand, vacancies

Who can skip it for now: cafés, beauty salons, clothing shops, apartment sales — B2C in general. Their buyers are on Instagram and Telegram. If your customer is a company, add LinkedIn to the plan alongside the basics of B2B marketing.

Company page or founder profile

Many companies start LinkedIn with a company page and stop there. The problem is that on LinkedIn people trust a person more than a brand.

Company pageFounder or executive profile
PurposeOfficial business card, vacancies, adsIntroductions, trust, sales conversations
ReachUsually lowUsually higher
Who runs itMarketing or HRThe founder (with an assistant)
What gets postedNews, case studies, vacanciesOpinions, experience, mistakes, process

Rule of thumb: you need both, but put your effort into the founder's profile. Keep the company page complete and tidy, and let the main content come from the leader. It is the same logic as in building a personal brand: the expert's face makes the company known.

Fix your profile in 30 minutes

Before writing posts, prepare the profile. A stranger will open it before replying to you.

  • Photo — face clearly visible, good light, plain background.
  • Headline — a benefit, not a job title: "I set up export logistics for Tashkent manufacturers" says more than "CEO".
  • About — 4–6 sentences: who you help, what problem you solve, what experience you have, how to get in touch.
  • Featured — your best case study, a presentation or a website link.
  • Language — if you want foreign clients, write the profile in English and add a second-language version if needed.
  • Company page — logo, cover, short description, website, employee profiles linked to the page.

Illustrative example: a furniture factory in Tashkent is looking for dealers in Kazakhstan and Russia. The founder's headline reads "We make furniture for hotels and offices and are ready to export", and Featured holds the catalogue and a factory video. A dealer opening the profile understands the offer in 10 seconds.

LinkedIn content: what works

Posts that sound like ads perform poorly on LinkedIn. People value experience, numbers and takeaways. Two or three posts a week is enough to start.

Formats that work

  • Lesson post — "The mistake we made with our first export shipment and what we learned".
  • Process — photos from the factory, office or a project with a short comment.
  • Opinion — your stance on the market, backed with arguments.
  • Case study — with the client's permission: problem → solution → result. Write about the process, not the client's sales.
  • Carousel (PDF document) — a checklist or guide over 6–10 pages.
  • Team — a new hire, internal training, a team achievement.

Post structure

  1. The first 1–2 lines must hook. That is all the feed shows.
  2. Short paragraphs of 1–3 sentences.
  3. One idea, one example.
  4. End with a question or a concrete action: "download the catalogue from our website".

Copying an Instagram post to LinkedIn rarely works: too many emojis, salesy text, little value. Take the topic and rewrite it for LinkedIn in a business tone. The ideas from Founder on camera apply here too — just in a more formal voice.

Outreach etiquette: connecting without spamming

LinkedIn's strength is the ability to message the right person directly. This is also exactly where most people ruin it.

Don't sell in the first message — first explain why you are writing to this person.

A bad message: "Hello! Our company provides high-quality services at low prices. Shall we have a call?"

The structure of a good message:

  • Reason — "I read your post about warehouse automation".
  • Common ground — "We have been working with logistics companies this year too".
  • Small value — a useful link, an observation or a question.
  • Light ask — "If it's of interest, let's swap notes on a 15-minute call".

A few rules:

  • Don't send connection requests without a note, especially to senior people you don't know.
  • If there is no reply, one follow-up is enough, not five.
  • Don't send hundreds of requests a day — LinkedIn may restrict that kind of activity.
  • Ask permission before calling. The rules of cold contact are in our cold calling guide — they apply on LinkedIn too.

If you are targeting foreign clients, write in their language and mind the time zone.

LinkedIn ads: when they are worth the money

The main advantage of LinkedIn ads is targeting: by job title, industry, company size, even a list of specific companies. The downside is price. LinkedIn's own guidance puts the minimum daily campaign budget at $10 (LinkedIn). Clicks usually cost noticeably more than on Meta, and the exact figure depends on audience and competition — check it with a test campaign.

When ads make sense:

  • one customer brings in a lot of money (a B2B contract, a foreign order, corporate training);
  • the audience is clearly defined by job title ("production directors", "HR directors");
  • the offer is ready: a landing page, a lead magnet or a meeting invitation.

When you don't need them: a cheap product, ordinary consumers, or a website and offer that aren't ready yet. In that case Meta or Google will bring leads more cheaply, and on LinkedIn you focus on organic content and messages. Other ways to reach companies are covered in finding B2B clients.

A weekly plan: 2 hours

LinkedIn doesn't give fast results, but steady work brings noticeable new contacts in 2–3 months. A simple weekly plan:

DayTaskTime
MondayWrite and publish a post30 minutes
TuesdayThoughtful comments on posts by 10 relevant people20 minutes
Wednesday5–10 connection requests with a note20 minutes
ThursdaySecond post or carousel30 minutes
FridayReply to messages, log results20 minutes

What to measure: profile views, new connections with the right job titles, incoming messages, booked meetings. Likes are secondary.

Common mistakes

  • Company page only — the leader's profile is empty and nobody sees the page.
  • Copying Instagram posts — wrong tone and format.
  • Selling in the first message — no reply, and the profile gets marked as spam.
  • Bursts of activity — 10 posts in a month, then three months of silence.
  • Ads without a ready offer — expensive clicks go to waste.
  • An English profile full of errors — for a foreign client that's the first impression.

Questions and answers

Does LinkedIn work in Uzbekistan?

Yes, LinkedIn is accessible in Uzbekistan and there is an ad audience. But it is much smaller than on Instagram or Telegram and consists mainly of specialists, managers and people in IT.

Do I have to create a company page?

Yes, create one: employee profiles link to it, and it is required for vacancies and ads. But don't stop there — introductions and trust come mainly through the leader's profile.

Which language should I use on LinkedIn?

It depends on your audience. For foreign clients and partners — English. For local managers and HR, Russian or Uzbek work too. Try not to mix languages within one post.

How much do LinkedIn ads cost?

The minimum daily budget is $10, but that guarantees nothing. Cost per click and per lead depend on audience, industry and creative. Run a small test campaign and compare the cost per lead with what one customer is worth to you.

Conclusion

LinkedIn for business makes sense for companies in B2B, IT, exports and those that hire actively. Tidy up the company page, but put most of the effort into the founder's profile: a clear headline, 2–3 useful posts a week, connection requests with a note and a first message that doesn't sell. Launch ads only when the offer is ready and one customer is worth a lot.
