Search ads and SEO start with the same question: what words does a customer type when looking for what you sell? Keyword research answers that question as a list — what Russian-speaking marketers call a "semantic core". This guide shows how to collect keywords for the Uzbek market, sort them, and map them to Google Ads ad groups and website pages.

What a keyword list is and why you need one

A keyword list is an organised set of search queries relevant to your business. Each query carries three notes: how often it is searched, what the person wants, and which page or ad group it belongs to.

Without one, things usually go wrong in two ways:

  • in ads — money goes to queries like "free", "DIY" or "jobs", while the queries that matter are not covered at all;
  • on the website — one page tries to talk about everything and ranks for nothing.

Opening an account and launching a first campaign is covered in our guide on how to set up Google Ads. Here we focus only on keywords — the most important preparation before any campaign.

Step 1: a seed list

Start without tools. Open a spreadsheet and fill in three columns:

ColumnQuestionExample for a furniture store in Tashkent
Product or serviceWhat do you sell?kitchen furniture, sliding wardrobe, bedroom set
ModifierWhat do customers add?made to order, cheap, instalments, ready-made
LocationWhere do they search?Tashkent, Chilanzar, Yunusabad

Take seed words from customers, not only from your head. Listen to sales calls and read first messages in Telegram and Instagram Direct. A customer rarely says "sliding wardrobe"; they say "wardrobe for the hallway" — and that is a keyword too.

Step 2: three scripts, plus typos

In Uzbekistan the same query is typed in at least three ways: Uzbek in Latin script, Uzbek in Cyrillic, and Russian. Add typos and the apostrophe problem on top: the letter "oʻ" is written with different marks or without one at all.

Illustrative example for one product:

  • Latin: oshxona mebeli, oshxona mebel narxi;
  • Cyrillic: ошхона мебели;
  • Russian: кухонная мебель Ташкент, кухня на заказ;
  • typos and short forms: oshxona mebl, kuxnya mebel.

The difference between Latin and Cyrillic search, the apostrophe issue and how to prepare a site for both are covered in our article on Uzbek search in Latin and Cyrillic. For keyword research the takeaway is simple: check every seed word in all three forms and look at volumes in a tool instead of guessing.

Step 3: tools — where the data comes from

ToolWhat it givesWho it's for
Google Keyword PlannerSearch volume, related queries, bid estimatesAnyone with a Google Ads account

Yandex WordstatYandex search volume by regionIf the Yandex audience matters

Keyword Planner lives inside Google Ads under "Tools". Enter seed words or a competitor's site and you get related queries and average monthly searches. New or low-spending accounts may see ranges instead of exact numbers — that is normal and good enough for sorting. Set the location to Uzbekistan or your city and double-check the language setting.

Search Console is the most valuable source because it shows real queries: the Performance report lists the searches your site appeared for, with clicks and average position. It often surfaces queries you never thought of.

Yandex Wordstat helps with Yandex Direct and SEO for Yandex. Set the region to Uzbekistan and check Russian-language queries. When Yandex is worth it at all is covered in Yandex Direct in Uzbekistan.

In small Uzbek-language niches, tools often show "0" or tiny numbers — that does not mean there is no demand.

In that case, test the query with a small ad budget: real impressions are more reliable than a tool's estimate.

Step 4: sorting by intent

Your list may now run to several hundred rows. Ask one question of every query: what does this person want to do right now?

For search ads, start with the "buy" group — it costs more but sits closest to an enquiry. The "compare" group is great raw material for SEO articles. The general difference between ads and SEO is explained in what is SEO.

Step 5: match types and ad groups in Google Ads

In Google Ads a keyword is added with one of three match types:

  • broad match — Google interprets the meaning of the query itself; maximum reach, but plenty of irrelevant searches;
  • phrase match — the query must include the meaning of the keyword; written in quotes: "kitchen made to order";
  • exact match — the query must be very close in meaning to the keyword; written in square brackets: [kitchen made to order].

A practical route for a small budget: start with phrase and exact match, and test broad match once conversion tracking works and enquiries start coming in. Broad match works best with automated bidding, but automated bidding needs data.

Grouping rule: one ad group, one theme. Kitchens, wardrobes and bedroom sets go into separate groups, each with its own ad copy and its own page. That way the ad matches the query, and after the click the person sees exactly what they searched for — more on that in a landing page that converts.

Step 6: negative keywords

Negative keywords are queries your ad should never show for. They are the cheapest way to save budget. Prepare the list before launch and add to it every week.

A starter negative list that suits many niches:

  • free, download, photos, pictures, video, film;
  • job, vacancy, training, course (unless you sell courses);
  • DIY, drawing, diagram;
  • used, second-hand, OLX (if you sell new goods);
  • names of other cities (if you only serve Tashkent).

Write every negative in all three forms: "бесплатно", "bepul", "бепул". Once the campaign runs, open the Search terms report weekly — it is the only place that shows what people actually typed when they saw your ad.

Step 7: mapping queries to pages for SEO

SEO works differently: one main query, one page. If two pages compete for the same query, both get weaker.

Build a simple table:

Put the main query in the title, URL and first paragraph, and let supporting queries appear naturally in the text. There is no need to repeat words artificially — search engines understand meaning.

Common mistakes

  • Collecting in one script only. Latin queries are there, Russian and Cyrillic are not — part of the market is invisible.
  • Everything on broad match. Without conversion tracking, that means paying for irrelevant searches.
  • Launching without negatives. The first week's budget goes to "free" and "jobs".
  • Informational queries in ads. Selling to someone asking "how to" is hard and expensive.
  • Build once, forget forever. Queries shift with seasons and trends.

Questions and answers

How long does keyword research take?

For a small business, a first working version takes a day or two: seed words, variants, tools, sorting. After that it is topped up monthly with data from ads and Search Console.

Is higher search volume always better?

No. High-volume generic queries ("furniture") are expensive and vague. Low-volume, specific ones ("kitchen made to order Chilanzar") often bring cheaper enquiries. How click prices form is covered in our CPC article (link below).

Which matter more for ads — Uzbek or Russian queries?

It depends on the niche and audience. Collect both, then split the budget based on tool data and the first weeks of ads. Many niches have demand in both languages.

Is Keyword Planner paid?

The tool itself is free inside a Google Ads account. Exact numbers, however, tend to be shown to accounts running active campaigns; others see ranges.

Conclusion

Keyword research is a system, not a list: seed words come from customers' own language, get checked in three scripts, expanded with tools, sorted by intent and mapped to ad groups and website pages. Without negative keywords and a weekly search terms review, even a good list won't save money.
