Visual identity: the minimum a small business needs

When every post uses a different font, colour and style, the page looks chaotic and the brand isn't memorable. Big companies produce brand books hundreds of pages long, but a small business needs only a one-page minimum kit. Here it is.

The minimum kit

ElementWhat you need
LogoA main version and a simplified mark for small sizes
Colours1–2 primary, 1 accent and neutrals — with exact codes
FontsOne for headings, one for body text
Photo styleLight, background, framing — what "our photos" look like
TemplatesPost, Stories, Reels cover, price list
RulesDos and don'ts

Logo

  • It should look good on white and black backgrounds.
  • It must stay legible at small sizes — avatars, favicons.
  • Files: vector (SVG or PDF) and PNG with a transparent background.

Colours

The primary colour makes the brand recognisable, the accent is for buttons and key elements, and neutrals are for backgrounds and text. Write down every colour's code (HEX, for example) so your designer, SMM manager and printer all use the same colour.

Fonts

Don't use more than two fonts. Check that they display the Uzbek Latin letters o‘ and g‘ correctly.

Photo style

Consistent light, similar backgrounds and tones make your grid look unified. For phone filming rules, see shooting Reels on a phone.

Templates and tools

  • Canva — a brand kit (colours, fonts, logo) and ready templates; convenient for an SMM manager.
  • Figma — for working with a designer.

Templates save time on every post and keep the style consistent.

A one-page brand book

Collect everything into one PDF or page: the logo and its versions, colour codes, fonts, sample photos, templates and "don't do this" examples. Share it with staff, designers and partners.

Common mistakes

  • Too many colours and fonts.
  • A low-quality, blurry logo file.
  • Every designer working in their own style.
  • A visual style that doesn't match the brand's voice.

Conclusion

A visual identity makes your brand recognisable at every touchpoint. Start with the minimum kit, document it on one page and apply it consistently across channels. For what a brand means, see what a brand is; for refreshing it, when to rebrand.
