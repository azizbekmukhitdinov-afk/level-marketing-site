What is marketing? A plain-language guide for business owners

Everyone uses the word "marketing", but people mean different things by it: for one it's Instagram ads, for another a nice logo, for a third discounts. That's why you so often hear "we did marketing and it didn't work". Here's what marketing is in plain words: how it differs from advertising, what it's made of and where a small business should start.

A simple definition

Marketing is a system for getting the right offer to the right people in a way they understand, and turning them into customers. It answers three questions:

  1. Who are we selling to? (audience)
  2. What do we offer, and on what terms? (product, price, offer)
  3. How do people find us and buy? (channels, sales)

Advertising is just part of the third question. Without answers to the first two, even the best ads burn money.

All three form one chain. Break one link and there's no result. That's why marketing and sales must work together — marketing and sales alignment.

The classic model: the 4Ps

The best-known model for understanding marketing is the 4Ps:

  • Product — what you sell and what problem it solves;
  • Price — how much, and on what terms;
  • Place — where people can buy: a store, a website, a marketplace, delivery;
  • Promotion — how people find out: ads, social media, referrals.

Many owners work only on the fourth P — advertising. But the problem is often in the first three: the product's difference is unclear, the price has no context, buying is inconvenient.

The modern view: the customer journey

Today it's easier to see marketing through the customer journey: how people find out about you and what stages they go through before buying.

  1. Awareness — ads, referrals, search.
  2. Interest — profile, website, reviews.
  3. Comparison — with other options.
  4. Decision — salesperson, price, terms.
  5. Purchase — payment, delivery.
  6. Repeat purchase and referral — service, staying in touch.

People can drop out at any stage. Marketing's job is to find and remove the barriers at each one. More in customer journey map and what is a sales funnel.

What marketing looks like in a small business

Small-business marketing doesn't have to be complicated. The minimum:

  • a clear offer: for whom, what, what's different — unique selling proposition;
  • trust: profile, reviews, real photos — social proof;
  • one or two channels: for example, Instagram ads + a maps listing;
  • fast replies to leads — lead response time;
  • measurement: how much was spent, how many leads and sales.

Where to start: 5 steps

  1. Describe your customer in one sentence — target audience and customer persona.
  2. Write your offer in one sentence: what, for whom, what's different.
  3. Find the weakest stage of the customer journey.
  4. Choose one channel and test it for 4–6 weeks.
  5. Record the numbers weekly: spend, leads, sales.

Common mistakes

  • Treating marketing as advertising only.
  • Launching ads without thinking through the offer and price.
  • Trying to be in every channel at once.
  • Not measuring results.
  • Giving up if the first month shows no results — marketing is a system, not a one-off. More in marketing system for growth.

Questions and answers

Why do you need marketing?

So you don't have to wait for customers to come on their own: marketing makes demand manageable and measurable.

Are marketing and SMM the same?

No. SMM is social media work — one part of marketing. More in what an SMM manager does.

How much money does marketing need?

It depends on goals and niche. How to calculate it — planning a marketing budget.

Can I do marketing myself?

At the start, yes. As the business grows you'll need a team or an agency — agency, freelancer or in-house.

Conclusion

Marketing isn't advertising — it's a system that gets the right offer to the right people and turns them into customers: audience, offer, price, channels, sales and measurement. For a small business, the essentials are a clear offer, trust, one or two channels and the numbers.
