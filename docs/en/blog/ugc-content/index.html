UGC content: when customer videos sell better than ads

A simple video a customer films on their phone often sells better than a studio-made ad. This kind of content is called UGC — user-generated content. Here is why UGC works, how to collect it and how to use it in ads.

What UGC is

UGC is photos, videos, reviews and comments about your product created by customers. In recent years UGC creators have appeared too: for a fee, they use the product and film videos "in the style of a customer".

Why UGC works

  • Trust. People believe other buyers more than the brand itself.
  • Naturalness. It doesn't look like an ad in the feed, so people watch it through.
  • Cheaper. A few UGC videos cost less than a big production.
  • Many variations. Different people mean different hooks — ready material for A/B tests.

How to get UGC from customers

  1. Ask. A few days after purchase, ask for a short video review.
  2. Make it easy. Give 2–3 prompts: "Why did you choose it? What did you like? Who would you recommend it to?"
  3. Incentivise. A discount on the next purchase or a gift — but never make a positive review the condition.
  4. Ask to be tagged. Keep track of posts that tag your page.
  5. Get permission. Ask the author before reposting or using their content in ads.

Honesty matters: the creator should genuinely use the product and not exaggerate results.

Using UGC in ads

  • Run each video as a separate creative and A/B test them.
  • Partnership ads show the ad under the creator's account name.
  • Save your best UGC to a "Reviews" highlight on your profile.

Ready-made messages to ask for UGC

After a purchase (Telegram or DMs): "Hello! Thank you for your purchase. If you like the product, could you send us a short 20–30 second video? It would really help us: why did you choose us and what did you like? As a thank-you, we'll give you a discount on your next order."

On a post where a customer tagged you: "Thanks for the post! May we use it on our page and in our ads?"

Keep the reply and the permission in the chat — you'll want them if questions come up later.

How to measure UGC results

MetricWhat it tells you
Views past the first 3 secondsWhether the hook works

Number of new reviewsWhether customers start posting after seeing UGC

Compare each UGC video with a studio creative on the same audience — that shows which format works for your business specifically.

Common mistakes

  • Using a customer's video in ads without permission.
  • Over-polishing the edit until it loses its natural feel.
  • Testing a single UGC video and judging the whole format by it.
  • Asking a creator for a made-up review without giving them the product — that's deception and damages your reputation.

For collecting reviews regularly, see Online reputation: how to collect reviews and respond to criticism.

Questions and answers

What if customers don't want to send videos?

Lower the bar: instead of a video, ask for a product photo and a couple of sentences — some people find that easier. For video, work with UGC creators.

Where do you find UGC creators?

Watch small accounts in your niche on Instagram and TikTok, look for active people among your own customers, or ask around. What matters is being natural on camera; follower count is secondary.

How long can you use UGC?

As long as you agreed with the creator. Agree the term and the places of use (your page, ads) in writing — a chat message will do. When the term ends, remove the video from ads or renew the agreement.

5 UGC ideas

  • Unboxing — the first look at the product.
  • A day with the product — how it fits into everyday life.
  • Before and after — the problem and the result.
  • Q&A — a customer answers the most common question.
  • Comparison — what they used before and why they switched.

Conclusion

UGC is content that turns trust into sales. Ask customers regularly, give creators a clear brief and test every video in your ads. For more on video formats, see 7 types of video ads.
