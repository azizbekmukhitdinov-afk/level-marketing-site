Franchise marketing: selling franchises, supporting franchisees

Franchising is a way to expand into other cities using investors' money. But franchise marketing involves two different jobs: first, selling the franchise (finding investors); second, bringing customers to every franchisee location. Many franchisors focus only on the first — and when the second fails, the whole network's reputation suffers. Here are both.

Part 1: selling the franchise

Investors don't buy a franchise; they buy a return. They want to know:

  • how much investment is needed and what it's spent on;
  • how long payback takes (on real numbers);
  • what support the franchisor gives: training, supply, marketing;
  • what the risks are.

The investor presentation

  • Results from existing locations — verifiable figures only.
  • Unit economics — unit economics.
  • The stages: contract → site selection → fit-out → training → opening.
  • Reviews from current franchisees.

Promising investors "a guaranteed X% a year" is dangerous: results depend on many factors. Honest numbers build trust.

Where to find investors

  • Search ads: "franchise Tashkent", "buy a business" — search ads in Tashkent.
  • LinkedIn and business Telegram channels.
  • The founder's personal brand — personal brand: where to start.
  • Your existing customers — the best franchisees are often brand fans.

Part 2: marketing for franchise locations

Central vs local marketing

Central (franchisor)Local (franchisee)
Brand book and templatesLocal maps profile
Core ad creativesArea-targeted ads
National campaignsLocal events and partners
Standards and trainingService and reviews

A brand book keeps the brand consistent — what is a brand book.

The marketing fund

In many franchises, a share of franchisee revenue goes into shared marketing. Spell out the rules in the contract: how much, what it's spent on and how it's reported.

The minimum for every location

  • A complete profile on Google and Yandex Maps — add your business to maps.
  • An opening campaign — marketing before opening.
  • A central CRM or a single reporting format: leads, sales, cost per lead.

The law

A franchise (complex business licence) agreement is governed by civil law — draw it up with a lawyer.

Questions and answers

When can I start selling franchises?

When several locations show stable profit and your processes are documented.

Can franchisees advertise themselves?

Yes, within the brand book and agreed rules.

What's the most common mistake?

Selling franchises without building a system that brings franchisees customers.
