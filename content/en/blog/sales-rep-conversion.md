"The ads bring poor-quality leads" — many owners hear this from their sales team. Sometimes it's true. But often something else is going on: leads from the same ad convert at 15% for one salesperson and 4% for another. The problem isn't the lead but who handles it and how. Here's how to measure your salespeople's conversion and what to do with the numbers.

## Why it matters

In the chain between advertising and sales, the salesperson is the strongest lever. Illustrative example:

| | Rep A | Rep B |
|---|---|---|
| Leads per month | 100 | 100 |
| Conversion to sale | 12% | 5% |
| Sales | 12 | 5 |
| Ad cost per sale (lead = 50,000 soum) | ~417,000 soum | 1,000,000 soum |

Same ads, same leads — and the cost per sale differs by more than double. Bring rep B closer to rep A's level and sales nearly double without spending a single extra soum on ads.

## Step 1: distribute leads evenly

To compare conversion, salespeople must work **under equal conditions**. If one gets the "hot" leads and another gets the rest, the numbers mean nothing.

- Leads are assigned in rotation or at random.
- From the same sources: ads, website, DMs.
- Reps can't cherry-pick leads.

## Step 2: key metrics

For each salesperson, monthly:

| Metric | What it shows |
|---|---|
| First response time | Speed — more in [lead response time](/en/blog/lead-response-time/) |
| Share of leads reached | Persistence with calls and messages |
| Conversion to meeting / visit / measurement | Conversation quality |
| Conversion to sale | The final result |
| Average order value | Ability to upsell and cross-sell |
| Share of leads marked "low quality" | If too high, it needs checking |

The last metric matters: some reps mark difficult leads as "low quality" so their conversion looks better. If one rep's share is much higher than others', spot-check their "low quality" leads.

## Step 3: set up the CRM

These numbers are only possible with a CRM. Minimum setup:

- a responsible rep on every lead;
- the source (ad, campaign);
- statuses: new → contacted → meeting → sale / lost;
- a lost reason, chosen from a list;
- first response time recorded automatically.

If ad account and CRM numbers don't match — [ad leads vs CRM mismatch](/en/blog/ad-leads-vs-crm-mismatch/). Choosing a CRM — [CRM for small business](/en/blog/crm-for-small-business/).

## Step 4: a weekly table

Every Monday, one table:

| Rep | Leads | Response time | Contact % | Meeting % | Sale % | Avg order |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 25 | 8 min | 88% | 40% | 12% | 4.2m |
| B | 25 | 45 min | 64% | 24% | 5% | 3.8m |
| C | 25 | 12 min | 80% | 36% | 9% | 5.1m |

The numbers are illustrative. The table isn't for blaming anyone — it shows **where sales are lost**: rep B struggles with response time and contact, rep C has a good average order but loses deals between meeting and sale.

## Step 5: review conversations

Numbers answer "where"; the conversations themselves answer "why":

- each week, review 3–5 chats or calls per rep;
- use your best rep's conversations as examples for the others;
- look for typical mistakes: a price with no context, no next step, arguing with objections.

Answering price questions well — [replying to "How much?"](/en/blog/replying-to-how-much/). Handling "I'll think about it" — ["I'll think about it" follow-up](/en/blog/i-will-think-about-it-follow-up/). Script structure — [sales script](/en/blog/sales-script/).

## Motivation: what to reward

If reps earn bonuses only on number of sales, they'll cling to "easy" leads and drop difficult ones. Better:

- a bonus based on sales value, but conditional on minimum response speed and contact rate;
- a team bonus for hitting the overall plan;
- recognition for sharing best practice.

## Ads and sales: one table

Rep conversion affects your ad reporting too. If the agency or media buyer shows only cost per lead, sales losses stay hidden. A good report shows ads and sales together: leads, who got them, and how many became sales. More in [what an ad report should include](/en/blog/what-an-ad-report-should-include/) and [marketing and sales alignment](/en/blog/marketing-and-sales-alignment/).

## Common mistakes

- Not distributing leads evenly, then comparing results.
- Measuring only number of sales, without intermediate stages.
- Never checking "low quality" labels.
- Using the numbers to punish — reps will start hiding them.
- Never reading the conversations.

## Questions and answers

### What should conversion be?

There's no universal benchmark — it depends on the niche, ticket size and lead type. Compare your reps with each other and with previous months.

### What if I have only one salesperson?

Compare their results month to month and look for the stage where sales are lost. Reviewing conversations matters even more here.

### A rep says "the ad leads are bad" — what now?

Compare their leads with other reps' leads from the same source. If conversion is low for everyone, the issue may be the ads or the offer. More in [why ads don't bring sales](/en/blog/why-ads-dont-bring-sales/).

### Should calls be recorded?

It's very useful for reviews. Tell customers calls are recorded and store the data in line with the law.

## Conclusion

Rep conversion is the strongest lever for growing sales without growing the ad budget. Distribute leads evenly, measure by stage, keep a weekly table and review conversations.
