December is the busiest month for advertising and sales, so the year-end review often gets postponed to January — and then forgotten. The new year then starts with last year's mistakes. A year-end review takes only an hour or two if you have the right questions. Here's a ready-made structure for reviewing your 2026 marketing and drafting a 2027 plan.

## Numbers first: the year on one page

Fill in one table, month by month:

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Marketing spend (ads + services) | Total investment |
| Number of leads | Marketing volume |
| Cost per lead (CPL) | Efficiency — [cost per lead](/en/blog/cost-per-lead-cpl-how-to-lower/) |
| Sales and revenue | The result |
| Customer acquisition cost (CAC) | The real cost — [CAC](/en/blog/customer-acquisition-cost-cac/) |
| Share of repeat purchases | Retention |
| ROMI | Return on marketing — [how to calculate ROMI](/en/blog/how-to-calculate-romi/) |

If some of these numbers are missing, that's your first conclusion: in 2027 you need a system for collecting them.

## 12 questions

**Channels**
1. Which channel delivered the cheapest *sale* (not lead)?
2. Which channel got money but produced almost no sales?
3. Which channel haven't we tried, although competitors are active there?

**Creative and content**
4. Which 3 creatives or posts worked best — and why?
5. Which format should we drop?

**Sales**
6. How did lead-to-sale conversion change — [what is conversion rate](/en/blog/what-is-conversion-rate/)?
7. Where were leads lost: response, meeting, price?
8. Which objections came up most often?

**Customers**
9. Who are the most profitable customers, and where did they come from?
10. What were customers unhappy about (reviews, complaints)?

**Seasons and team**
11. Which months were strong and which weak?
12. Which skills did the team lack?

## Three lists: stop, continue, start

Sort your answers into three lists:

- **Stop** — channels, formats and spending that didn't deliver.
- **Continue and scale** — what worked best.
- **Start** — one or two new tests, no more.

A common mistake is starting everything at once. Two new experiments are enough for 2027.

## A one-page 2027 plan

1. **Goal**: sales or revenue, by month.
2. **Budget**: by channel and season — [planning a marketing budget](/en/blog/planning-a-marketing-budget/).
3. **Core channels**: two or three.
4. **Seasonal campaigns**: Ramadan, 8 March, Navruz, back to school, Black Friday, New Year — [marketing calendar 2027](/en/blog/marketing-calendar-2027-uzbekistan/).
5. **Metrics and reporting**: what is measured, by whom and when.

A ready template — [one-page marketing strategy](/en/blog/one-page-marketing-strategy/).

## With an agency or your team

If you work with an agency, year end is a good moment for a "point B" conversation: where we were at the start of the year, where we are now and what next year's goal is — [monthly marketing meeting](/en/blog/monthly-marketing-meeting/).

## Questions and answers

### What if I don't have the numbers?

Start with what you have: the ads manager, bank statements, the CRM. The key decision for next year is to record the source of every lead — [UTM tags](/en/blog/utm-tags/).

### Who should do the review?

The founder, the head of sales and the marketer together. A marketer alone won't see the sales side.

### When should we do it?

In early December, leaving time to plan the 2027 budget and January campaigns.
