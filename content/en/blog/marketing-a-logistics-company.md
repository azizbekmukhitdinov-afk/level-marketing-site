Logistics is a business of trust. A shipper hands goods — sometimes worth millions of UZS — to a company they don't know and has to believe they'll arrive on time and intact. So logistics marketing relies less on pretty ads and more on precision, speed and proof. This article covers how to build a client flow for city delivery, interregional and international freight companies.

![What clients fear](/img/blog/illus/logistika-marketing-1.jpg 1200x675)

## What clients fear

| Fear | Marketing's answer |
|---|---|
| "The cargo will be late" | Exact timelines, tracking, responsibility for delays |
| "It will be damaged or lost" | Insurance, a packing standard, loading videos |
| "The price will rise on the way" | Transparent pricing, a calculator, a written agreement |
| "Paperwork problems" | Customs and documentation experience, templates |
| "They won't answer the phone" | A dedicated manager, hours and a contact standard |

## Step 1. Specialise

"Any cargo, any route" doesn't inspire trust. Stronger:

- **Route:** "Tashkent — Moscow", "consolidation from China", "daily runs to the regions".
- **Cargo type:** refrigerated goods, building materials, furniture, e-commerce parcels.
- **Client type:** marketplace sellers, manufacturers, wholesalers.
- **Service:** warehousing + delivery, customs clearance, door to door.

Framing the offer — [unique selling proposition](/en/blog/unique-selling-proposition/); defining the ideal client — [target audience and customer persona](/en/blog/target-audience-and-customer-persona/).

## Step 2. Trust signals

- **Fleet or partners** — photos and videos of vehicles, the warehouse, the team.
- **Case studies** — which client, which route, what volume (with permission).
- **Documents** — licence and insurance (where required), a sample contract.
- **Tracking** — can clients see where their cargo is.
- **Reviews** — on maps and Telegram.

A maps listing matters for B2B too — [adding your business to maps](/en/blog/add-your-business-to-maps/); reviews — [online reputation and reviews](/en/blog/online-reputation-and-reviews/).

## Step 3. Search and the website

"Freight Tashkent Moscow", "shipping from China", "book a truck" — logistics clients often start with search. You need:

- a separate page for each route and service;
- a price calculator or a table of indicative prices;
- a quick request form and a Telegram button;
- Google and Yandex search ads — with precise keywords.

Search ads — [Google Ads vs Meta Ads](/en/blog/google-ads-vs-meta-ads/), [Yandex Direct](/en/blog/yandex-direct-in-uzbekistan/).

## Step 4. Fast replies and pricing

In logistics, clients ask 3–5 companies for a price at once. The first to give an exact quote often wins the order. A standard:

1. **Reply within 15–30 minutes** during working hours.
2. **Clarifying questions** — cargo type, weight, volume, addresses, deadline.
3. **Price and timeline in one message** — what's included and what's extra.
4. **Next step** — contract, loading date.
5. **Follow-up** — the next day if there's no reply.

Log every request in the CRM: who asked, which route, what volume. It becomes a base for future offers. Reply speed — [lead response time](/en/blog/lead-response-time/); CRM — [CRM for small business](/en/blog/crm-for-small-business/).

## Step 5. Direct sales

Large, regular clients — manufacturers, wholesalers, marketplace sellers — often come through direct outreach:

- a list of 50–100 companies matching the ideal profile;
- a concrete benefit in the first message: "A weekly run on your route, 7-day transit";
- a trial shipment — start with a small volume;
- after each successful delivery, a report and a referral request.

More on B2B selling — [B2B marketing](/en/blog/b2b-marketing-uzbekistan/).

## Step 6. Content

Logistics content should also be useful and specific:

| Format | Example |
|---|---|
| Guide | "Shipping goods from China: 6 steps and the paperwork" |
| Process | Loading, securing, the warehouse — on video |
| Price explained | What a freight rate is made of |
| Case study | What problem was solved and how |
| Updates | Changes to routes and transit times |

A Telegram channel works well for regular clients and shippers: schedules, free capacity, transit times. Growing a channel — [grow a Telegram channel](/en/blog/grow-a-telegram-channel/).

## Step 7. Paid ads

Meta and Telegram ads help in logistics too, especially for marketplace sellers and small businesses:

- audience — entrepreneurs, online retail, import interests;
- creative — route, transit time and an indicative price: "China to Tashkent — illustrative example: from … UZS per kg, 18–25 days";
- objective — messages or a lead form (cargo type, volume, route).

Show prices in UZS in ads — it's a legal requirement ([advertising law in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/advertising-law-uzbekistan/)).

## What to measure

- price requests and their source;
- request-to-order conversion;
- average order size and revenue;
- share of shipments delivered on time;
- share of regular clients and repeat orders.

![Common mistakes](/img/blog/illus/logistika-marketing-3.jpg 1200x675)

## Common mistakes

- No information on prices or transit times on the website.
- Taking hours to answer a price request.
- "Any cargo" — no specialisation.
- Promising overly optimistic transit times — then bad reviews.
- Not recording requests — losing the chance to follow up.

## Questions and answers

### Which channel matters most for a logistics company?

Search and direct sales. Paid ads and Telegram are a good addition for small businesses and marketplace sellers.

### Is a price calculator a must?

Not a must, but it raises both the number and quality of requests: clients know the indicative price and come better prepared. At minimum, publish a table of indicative prices.

### How can a small company compete with big ones?

Through specialisation, fast replies and personal service: knowing one route very well and having a dedicated manager follow every shipment.

### When will results show?

Search ads bring requests in the first week; regular B2B clients can take 2–4 months.

Logistics marketing is about trust and precision: specialisation, proof, visibility in search, fast and exact quotes, direct sales and a CRM that never loses a request.
