**Meta Muse Image** is Meta's new image-generation model, and sooner or later it will reach your ad account. For a business owner that means two things: creative variations get faster and cheaper, but responsibility for brand control grows too. This article covers what Muse Image is, where it helps in advertising, how to brief it and what you should not automate.

## What Meta Muse Image is and where it works

Meta announced Muse Image on 7 July 2026. It is the first image model from Meta Superintelligence Labs. It works inside Meta AI, Instagram and WhatsApp: an ordinary user types a prompt and gets an image, or edits one of their own ([Meta newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/07/introducing-muse-image/)).

For advertisers, the key point is different: Meta said advertisers and agencies would get Muse Image in Advantage+ creative "in the coming weeks" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/07/meta-ai-muse-image.html)). So it is not a separate app — it plugs into the same automatic creative enhancements you have already seen. Whether it is switched on for your account, check in the creative settings in Ads Manager: new features do not reach every account at once.

Three more facts:

- according to Meta, more than 8 million advertisers use at least one of its generative AI creative tools;
- Muse Video is in development, with no release date announced;
- in September 2026 Meta launched a personal AI agent called Muse (a shopping assistant), but it has not announced any ad placements in it.

How Advantage+ creative itself works, and which enhancements to keep or switch off, is covered in detail in [our article on Advantage+ creative](/en/blog/advantage-plus-creative-ai-edits/). Here we focus on how Muse Image changes creative production.

## What changes for ad creatives

A single static banner used to take a designer, a photographer and a few days. A generative model shortens part of that chain. Not all of it.

| Task | Usual way | With a model like Muse Image | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product background | Studio or Photoshop | 5–10 backgrounds in minutes | Product shape or colour drifts |
| Lifestyle variation | Shoot in an interior or with a model | AI-generated scene | Scene doesn't match reality |
| Static from video | Crop a frame and retouch it | New composition based on a frame | Quality loss, garbled text |
| Format adaptation | Designer resizes by hand | Automatic expansion | Logo or price gets cropped |

The main shift is the number of variations. Meta's algorithm likes to show different creatives to different people, as we explained in [the article on Meta Andromeda](/en/blog/meta-andromeda-creative-diversity/). AI makes multiplying variations easy, but many variations are not many ideas. One product on ten backgrounds is not ten ideas — it is one idea in ten outfits.

## Where Muse Image helps: 4 scenarios

### 1. Backgrounds and settings for a product

The safest use. You have a clean, sharp photo of the product, and the AI adds a setting: a kitchen, an office, a holiday table. A tableware shop in Tashkent can show one teapot in "morning breakfast", "expecting guests" and "gift box" scenes. How to shoot the original on a phone is covered in [our guide to AI product photos](/en/blog/ai-product-photos/).

### 2. Lifestyle variations for different audiences

The same furniture set — for a young family in a bright flat and for a large family in a spacious living room. It is a cheap way to speak to different segments differently. Be careful with human faces, though — more on that below.

### 3. Static creatives from video

You have shot Reels but have no static banners. Take the best frames, use AI to clean the background, extend the composition and adapt it for feed and Stories. Your video and static ads then share one style.

### 4. Quick replacement of a worn-out creative

When an ad runs too long with the same image, people stop noticing it. With AI you can change the background, colour or scene and refresh the creative while keeping the idea. To know when it's time, see [our article on creative fatigue](/en/blog/creative-fatigue/).

## A brief for the AI: template

A model does what you write and invents what you don't. The more precise the brief, the less rework. Here is a template for one image:

- **Product:** what it is, colour, size, which side should be visible;
- **Scene:** where (kitchen, office, street), time (day, evening), season;
- **Mood:** warm, premium, energetic, family;
- **Colours:** brand colours with HEX codes or "mostly blue and white";
- **Composition:** product in the centre, empty space at the top for text, 4:5 or 9:16;
- **Don'ts:** no other brands' logos, no lettering, no faces, the product shape must not change;
- **Goal:** for which audience and with which message.

The last line is often forgotten, yet it matters most. "A nice picture" is not a brief. "For a young family who just moved: this sofa fits a small living room" is a brief.

> Don't ask the AI to write text in the image: add the price and offer in design afterwards.

## Brand control: what must not change

A generative model tries to make the product "prettier": it smooths buttons, brightens colours and garbles the lettering on packaging. In advertising that is risky, because the buyer expects exactly what they saw in the picture.

Five things that must not change:

1. **The product itself** — shape, colour, size, text on the packaging.
2. **The logo** — only from the original file, never one drawn by AI.
3. **Price and offer** — added by a designer, not by the model.
4. **Real conditions** — if your shop is small, a huge AI-generated showroom misleads the buyer.
5. **People** — don't present an AI-generated face as a real customer's testimonial.

Before any AI image goes live, have one person — a salesperson or manager — compare it with the real product. It takes five minutes and saves you from "it didn't look like the picture" complaints. If your brand colours and logo rules are written down, checking is easier — that is what a brand book is for.

## What not to hand over to AI

Muse Image and other models speed up creative production, but three things should stay with people.

**The idea and the offer.** The model doesn't know why a buyer should choose you. The offer, your competitive advantage and the core message are your job.

**Testimonials and results.** AI-made before-and-after photos, customer reviews or examples of work mislead buyers and may break advertising rules. For a beauty salon, clinic or renovation company — real photos only.

**Final approval.** If automatic enhancements are on in Advantage+ creative, the system may alter the creative at delivery. Check regularly which features are enabled and look at the ad preview.

In sensitive niches — healthcare, finance, education results — think twice before using AI images. Here trust depends on the picture being real.

## How to test it: a 2-week plan

Don't guess whether an AI creative works — test it. A simple plan:

| Week | What you do | What you watch |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 original creatives + 2 AI-background variations, same copy and offer | CTR, cost per lead, questions in comments |
| 2 | 2–3 more variations in the winning style, losers switched off | Lead quality, conversion to sale |

> **Illustrative example.** A furniture store in Tashkent launched four creatives for a sofa: two real showroom photos and two with the sofa in an AI-generated interior. After a week the AI variations got more clicks, but also more "is this sofa actually in the showroom?" questions. The figures and outcome are illustrative.

The lesson: don't look at CTR alone. An AI image may bring more clicks but also unexpected questions and weaker leads. We have written separately about how to set up a proper test — one variable, enough budget and enough time.

## Questions and answers

### Is Meta Muse Image available in ad accounts in Uzbekistan?

Meta said it would roll it out gradually to advertisers inside Advantage+ creative. No country list has been published, so when you create an ad in Ads Manager, open the creative settings and check whether new generative features appear.

### Do AI images perform better than real photos?

There is no universal answer. For backgrounds and format adaptation, AI saves time. Where trust matters — services, results, your team — a real photo may be stronger. Only a test in your own account will tell.

### So we don't need a designer any more?

You do, but the job changes. The designer picks the right AI variations, checks the product, adds the logo, price and copy, and keeps the brand style consistent. Generation gets faster; taste and control stay human.

### Can we advertise inside the Muse agent?

Not for now: Meta has not announced ad placements in the Muse personal agent it launched in September 2026. If an official announcement comes, that will be a separate topic.

## Conclusion

Meta Muse Image helps you make ad creatives faster and in more variations: product backgrounds, lifestyle scenes, statics from video and refreshes of worn-out creatives. But the idea, the offer, real testimonials and the final check must stay with people. Write a precise brief, keep the product and logo unchanged, and test AI variations against real photos.
