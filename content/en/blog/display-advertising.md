You're reading a news site and, in the sidebar, there's the sofa you looked at yesterday. That's **display advertising** — an ad in the form of an image or animation, shown on websites, apps and channels while the person isn't searching for anything. This guide covers where display (banner) ads run, who they help, when they waste money and how to prepare them.

## What display advertising is and how it differs from search

Search ads respond to demand: someone types "kitchen made to order price" and you answer. Banners create or remind people of demand: the person is busy with something else and you put yourself in front of them.

| | Search ads | Display ads |
|---|---|---|
| Person's state | Actively searching | Reading or watching something else |
| Main job | Enquiry, call | Awareness, reminder, bringing people back |
| Payment | Usually per click | Usually per impression |
| Click-through rate | Higher | Usually much lower |
| Creative | Text | Image, animation, video |

That's why judging banners by search-ad standards is a mistake. The logic behind both formats is covered in [what is search advertising](/en/blog/what-is-search-advertising/) — what it calls network ads is exactly display advertising.

## Where display ads appear

For a business in Uzbekistan there are four main routes.

**1. Google Display Network.** Partner sites and apps, plus YouTube and Gmail. Campaigns are created in Google Ads; audiences are set by interests, topics, remarketing lists or specific placements.

**2. Yandex Advertising Network.** Ads on Yandex partner sites and apps, managed through Yandex Direct. Useful for Russian-speaking audiences. When Yandex makes sense in Uzbekistan at all is covered in [Yandex Direct in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/yandex-direct-in-uzbekistan/).

**3. Buying directly from websites.** News portals and niche sites sell banner slots by the day or week. You can't fine-tune the audience, but some of the portal's credibility rubs off on you. Ask in writing for the price and statistics up front: impressions, clicks and the report format.

**4. Image posts in Telegram channels.** Not a classic banner, but the job is similar: an image and a short text while the audience reads something else. Choosing channels and measuring results is covered in [advertising in Telegram channels](/en/blog/buying-ads-in-telegram-channels/).

## When display ads work

Display advertising pays off in four situations.

- **Remarketing.** Showing ads again to people who visited your site, added a product to the cart or abandoned an enquiry form. This is the banner's most valuable job: the person already knows you and just needs a reminder. The mechanics are explained in [how retargeting works](/en/blog/how-retargeting-works/).
- **Awareness.** A new store, branch or brand — people don't know you yet and don't search for you. Banners deliver wide reach through cheap impressions.
- **Long-consideration purchases.** Real estate, furniture, education, cars — people think for weeks. During that time your brand needs to stay top of mind.
- **Tightly themed sites.** A building-materials seller showing on renovation sites, for example, reaches the right audience.

> A banner rarely closes the sale on its own — it sends people back to search, your site or your store.

## When display ads waste money

Many business owners try banners once and conclude they "don't work". Usually the cause is one of these:

- **expecting enquiries immediately.** A banner shown to a cold audience won't produce leads from day one the way search does;
- **spreading a small budget over a wide audience.** Everyone sees the banner once and forgets it;
- **no placement control.** Ads end up on low-quality sites or in gaming apps full of accidental clicks;
- **no frequency limit.** One person sees the banner ten times a day and gets annoyed;
- **a vague creative.** There's a logo but no offer — nobody can tell what's being sold.

A word on frequency: how many times to show an ad to one person, and when it becomes too much, is covered in [ad frequency](/en/blog/ad-frequency/). Watch it closely in remarketing — the audience is small and fatigue sets in fast.

## Banner sizes

Google Ads has two kinds of display ads: **responsive** ads and **uploaded image ads**.

In a responsive display ad you upload images, a logo and headlines separately, and Google assembles them to fit each placement. Google's help pages recommend:

| Asset | Aspect ratio | Recommended size |
|---|---|---|
| Landscape image | 1.91:1 | 1200 × 628 |
| Square image | 1:1 | 1200 × 1200 |
| Square logo | 1:1 | 1200 × 1200 |
| Landscape logo | 4:1 | 1200 × 300 |

If you upload finished banners, the most common standard sizes are 300 × 250, 336 × 280, 728 × 90, 300 × 600, 160 × 600, plus 320 × 50 and 320 × 100 for mobile. There are file-size and format limits that change from time to time, so check the [Google Ads help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7005917) before uploading. Yandex and portals have their own requirements — ask each time.

Practical tip: start with a responsive ad. It's less work and reaches more placements. Fixed-size banners are worth it when your brand design is strict.

## Good banner creative: 6 rules

1. **One idea.** A banner is read in a second. One offer, one image, one action.
2. **Show the product.** Not an abstraction — the sofa, the dish, the room, the result.
3. **Little text.** A 4–6 word headline, plus a price or condition if needed. Small text on a small screen is unreadable.
4. **Logo present, but not centre stage.** People should know whose ad it is, but the offer takes the main spot.
5. **A clear button or call to action.** "See prices", "Open catalogue", "Leave a request".
6. **Contrast.** The banner shouldn't disappear against the site's background — check it on a phone screen.

> Illustrative example. A furniture store in Tashkent made three remarketing banners: one with the viewed sofa and "The sofa you looked at is still in stock", one with instalment terms and one with free delivery.

Refresh creatives every 3–4 weeks, especially in remarketing: the same image quickly becomes "invisible".

## Setting up a display campaign: a short plan

1. **Pick a goal:** remarketing or awareness. Don't mix them in one campaign.
2. **Prepare audiences:** for remarketing, install site tags (Google tag, Yandex Metrica) in advance — lists need time to fill.
3. **Limit placements:** turn on content exclusions, then after a few days open the placements report and exclude low-quality sites and apps.
4. **Manage frequency:** check the per-person impression limit in campaign settings.
5. **Choose the right metrics:** for awareness — reach, CPM and frequency; for remarketing — return visits, enquiries and cost per enquiry.
6. **Watch the knock-on effect:** compare branded searches and direct site visits before and after the banners start.

## How to judge the results

The most common question about banners is "how many enquiries did they bring?". For remarketing that's the right question; for an awareness campaign it isn't enough. Pick metrics by goal:

| Goal | Main metric | Supporting | When to judge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remarketing | Enquiries and cost per enquiry | Return visits, cart purchases | After 2–3 weeks |
| Awareness | Reach and frequency | Branded searches, direct site visits | After 4–6 weeks |
| New branch | Reach in that area | Map direction requests, calls | A month after opening |

A low CTR is normal for banners, so don't compare it with search. Within one campaign, though, comparing creatives by CTR is useful: it shows which image grabs attention.

One more rule: a banner's effect often shows up later and through another channel. Someone sees the banner, doesn't click, and two days later types your brand name into Google or finds you on Instagram. So judge results not only by last click but also by the trend in total enquiries and branded searches.

## Questions and answers

### Is display advertising expensive?

Impressions are usually cheap, but clicks are few. So on a cost-per-click basis banners can look more expensive than search. Judge them by reach and remarketing results instead.

### Does a small business need display ads?

For many small businesses, banners aren't the first channel. Start with search or Instagram ads, then add remarketing banners once your site gets traffic. Banners aimed at cold audiences need a bigger budget and patience.

### Can I make banners myself?

For a responsive ad you don't need a designer: good product photos, a logo and short headlines are enough, and Google assembles them. Fixed-size banners need a designer who knows your brand style.

### How do banners differ from Instagram ads?

Instagram ads run in the social feed to Meta's audience. Banners run on websites, apps and other platforms. They complement each other: someone who saw you on Instagram and then meets your banner on a news site remembers the brand faster.

## Conclusion

Display advertising doesn't capture demand — it creates it and keeps you top of mind. It works for remarketing, awareness for a new brand and long-consideration purchases, and it burns budget when you expect "enquiries today". Set a clear goal, control placements and frequency, keep each creative to one idea and judge results by reach and return visits.
