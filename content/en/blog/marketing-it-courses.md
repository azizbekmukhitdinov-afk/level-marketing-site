Programming, design, social media, data analysis — IT courses are growing fast in Uzbekistan, and so is ad competition: "become a developer in 3 months", "guaranteed job". People trust such promises less and less. The strongest marketing for an IT course is showing results honestly and making the first step easy. Here's how.

## Who studies, and what they fear

| Audience | Goal | Fear |
|---|---|---|
| School pupils and parents | A profession, university | Wasted money |
| University students | Side income, a first job | "What if it's too hard?" |
| Career changers (25–35) | A new profession, a higher salary | "Is it too late? Can I do it?" |

Each audience needs its own creative and offer — [target audience and customer persona](/en/blog/target-audience-and-customer-persona/).

## The first step: a free trial lesson

"Sign up for the course" is an expensive decision. "Build your first website in a free trial lesson" is easy and appealing. A trial lesson:

- lets people feel they can do it;
- introduces the teacher and the atmosphere;
- leads naturally into a conversation with a salesperson.

That's an entry offer — [entry offer](/en/blog/entry-offer/).

## Show results honestly

- **Student portfolios**: real projects — websites, designs, apps.
- **Graduate stories**: "where I started, where I am now" (with consent) — [customer testimonial video](/en/blog/customer-testimonial-video/).
- **Numbers**: verifiable ones only — before claiming "X% of graduates found jobs", have the calculation and a source.

Promises like "guaranteed job" or "a high salary in 3 months" risk being misleading advertising — [advertising law in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/advertising-law-uzbekistan/).

## Content ideas

- "A day in the life of a developer" — a teacher or graduate.
- "Which IT path suits you?" — a test or quiz — [lead magnet](/en/blog/lead-magnet/).
- Clips from lessons.
- Myths about the profession: "you can't code without being good at maths".

## Ad channels

- **Instagram and Telegram**: young people and parents, with a lead form for the trial lesson — [lead form or messages](/en/blog/lead-form-or-messages/).
- **Search ads**: "programming course Tashkent", "frontend course" — [search ads in Tashkent](/en/blog/search-ads-tashkent/).
- **YouTube**: long free lessons for trust and search traffic.

## The sales team

Decisions about IT courses take time, since people compare several. The salesperson should:

- call within 24 hours of the trial lesson;
- offer payment options (instalments, following the law) — [installment advertising rules](/en/blog/installment-advertising-rules/);
- talk to parents separately for school pupils — [family purchase decisions](/en/blog/family-purchase-decision/).

## Questions and answers

### Does advertising online and offline courses differ?

Yes. Offline means district targeting and maps; online means all of Uzbekistan, but with stiffer competition.

### Which metric matters?

Conversion from trial sign-up → attendance → payment, and student retention.

### Should we offer a free course?

A short free mini-course works well as a lead magnet; a completely free course can devalue your main product.

## Conclusion

Marketing IT courses means audience-specific messages, a free trial lesson, honest portfolios and graduate stories, promises without "guarantees" and a fast sales process. Level Marketing works in education too — see the Express School case.
