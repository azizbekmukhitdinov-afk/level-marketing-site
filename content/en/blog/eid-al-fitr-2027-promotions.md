Eid al-Fitr, known in Uzbekistan as Ramazon hayiti, is one of the biggest gifting and hospitality seasons of the year: people buy presents for parents and relatives, new clothes for children and sweets for the festive table. If you start planning your **Eid al-Fitr 2027 promotions** now, you won't be scrambling at the end of February. This article covers the dates, gift sets, corporate orders, delivery, ad timing and a respectful tone.

## When is Eid al-Fitr 2027: tentative dates

The Islamic calendar follows the moon, so the dates move roughly 10–11 days earlier every year. The tentative estimate for 2027 is:

| Event | Tentative date |
|---|---|
| Start of Ramadan | around 8 February 2027 |
| Last 10 days of Ramadan | roughly 27 February – 8 March |
| Eid al-Fitr (Ramazon hayiti) | around 9–10 March 2027 |

These dates are **tentative**. The exact day is announced by the Muslim Board of Uzbekistan based on moon sighting, and the public holiday is confirmed by a government decree. So build a buffer of a day or two into your plan and watch for the official announcement in February.

A marketing plan for the month of Ramadan itself — iftar timing, content, etiquette — is in our article on [Ramadan 2027 marketing](/en/blog/ramadan-2027-marketing/). This piece is only about the pre-Eid gift and promotion season.

## How pre-Eid shopping is different

Before New Year, people buy for themselves and their family, often on impulse. Before Eid it's different: purchases are mostly **for others** — parents, older relatives, neighbours, children. This buyer cares about three things:

- **appropriateness** — the gift must not look cheap, and the packaging should be beautiful;
- **trust** — quality, freshness, the shelf life of sweets;
- **timing** — a gift that arrives the day after Eid loses its meaning.

That is why the main lever of an Eid promotion is not a discount but a **ready-made solution**: a nicely packed set, a clear price and guaranteed delivery. How to sell without cutting prices is covered in the article on [the discount trap](/en/blog/discount-trap/).

## Calendar: the four stages of the Eid season

| Stage | Tentative timing | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Preparation | January – first week of Ramadan | Sets, packaging, prices, stock, corporate offer |
| 2. Warm-up | Weeks 2–3 of Ramadan | Content, corporate orders, pre-orders |
| 3. Peak | Last 10 days | Main advertising, gift sets, delivery deadlines |
| 4. Eid and after | Eid days + 3–5 days | Greetings, opening hours, clearing leftover stock, thank-you message |

Preparation for corporate gifts and large orders is best finished by early February. Companies order gifts for staff and partners in the middle of Ramadan, and by the last week there is no time left for production and packing.

## How to build a gift set

A good set has three price tiers and one clear idea. For example: "An Eid gift for parents", "An Eid gift for kids", "For the guest table".

| Tier | What's included | Illustrative price |
|---|---|---|
| Small | 2–3 items, simple packaging, greeting card | 150–250 thousand sum |
| Medium | 4–5 items, branded box, name inscription | 300–500 thousand sum |
| Large | Premium items, special packaging, delivery included | from 700 thousand sum |

The prices are illustrative — calculate from your own cost and margin. The key is to make the medium tier look like the best deal so that it becomes the bestseller.

## Corporate gifts: the B2B offer

Many companies give staff food hampers or gifts for Eid and send greetings to partners. These are large orders planned well in advance. To prepare:

1. **Make a one-page offer:** three sets, prices, minimum order, logo printing, delivery terms.
2. **Send it in late January – early February:** to HR and office managers and to last year's corporate clients.
3. **State an order deadline:** for example, "up to 10 days before Eid".
4. **Prepare paperwork in advance:** contract, invoice, payment options.

The corporate sales script and steps are described in the article on [corporate gifts](/en/blog/corporate-gifts-sales/) using New Year as the example — the same logic works for Eid.

## Delivery and opening hours

The most common complaint in the days before the holiday is late delivery. Prepare like this:

- **announce an order cut-off:** "For delivery before Eid, order by 12:00 on the day before Eid";
- **book couriers early:** your own couriers or a contract with a delivery service;
- **give a precise delivery window:** "morning" or "evening", not "we'll bring it today";
- **update your opening hours everywhere:** Instagram, Telegram, Google Maps and Yandex Maps;
- **draw up a sales team rota:** who is on duty during Eid and who answers incoming leads.

A detailed plan for not losing leads over the holidays is in the article on [the sales team during the holidays](/en/blog/sales-team-during-holidays/).

## Ad timing: the last 10 days

You don't need to run ads at full budget from the first day of Ramadan. Ramp the budget up in stages:

| Period | Budget share (illustrative) | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 of Ramadan | 20% | Reach, introducing the sets, corporate offer |
| Week 3 | 30% | Pre-orders, retargeting |
| Last 7–10 days | 50% | Sales, delivery deadlines, "last days" |

The shares are an illustrative example — adjust them to your niche and stock. During the fast people's daily routine changes: many pick up their phones more after suhoor and after iftar. Check ad delivery hours and lead arrival times in your own stats and plan your sales rota around them.

What to show in the creatives:

- the set itself — an open box and what's inside;
- the price and the contents;
- the delivery deadline and how to order: a button, a link or a lead form;
- not a person holding a gift but the moment of giving — a son or daughter handing a present to their parents.

## A respectful tone: what to avoid

Eid is a religious holiday, and people pick up on the tone of an ad instantly. A few rules:

- **don't use religious symbols as sales tools.** A mosque, prayer or Quran verses should not become the background of a discount banner;
- **keep greetings and selling apart.** On Eid day, post a pure greeting; commercial offers belong to the days before;
- **don't tie unsuitable products to Eid.** An offer involving alcohol or gambling in an Eid context, for example, is a crude mistake;
- **don't manufacture urgency.** If "Today only!" repeats every day, trust disappears. Name the real deadline — the delivery cut-off is reason enough;
- **don't mix charity with commerce.** If you donate, don't make it a condition of purchase.

> The best tone for Eid advertising is warm and family-focused, not loud.

## Common mistakes

- Sets are invented in the final week, and packaging and stock run short.
- The corporate offer goes out late, after clients have already ordered elsewhere.
- No delivery deadline is given, so complaints arrive after Eid.
- Opening hours for Eid aren't updated, and customers arrive at a locked door.
- The whole season is built on discounts alone, and the margin disappears.

## Questions and answers

### When will the exact date of Eid al-Fitr 2027 be known?

Usually close to the end of Ramadan, based on moon sighting. Watch for the announcement from the Muslim Board of Uzbekistan and the government decree on the day off. Plan around 9–10 March with a buffer of a day or two.

### Should an Eid promotion include discounts?

Not necessarily. In this season buyers value a ready-made set, nice packaging and on-time delivery more. Instead of a discount, add value: free gift wrapping, a greeting card, a name inscription or free delivery.

### When should I start taking corporate orders?

Send the offer in late January or early February and close orders at least 10 days before Eid. That leaves time for production, logo printing and delivery.

### Is advertising during Ramadan unethical?

No, as long as the tone is respectful. People are preparing for Eid and looking for gifts, so a useful offer is perfectly normal. What matters is not turning religious symbols into sales tools and keeping the tone sincere.

## Conclusion

Eid al-Fitr 2027 is expected around 9–10 March, but the exact date will only be known after the official announcement, so plan with a buffer. Prepare your sets and corporate offer in January, collect orders in mid-Ramadan, switch on the main ads in the last 10 days, announce delivery deadlines early and keep the tone sincere.
