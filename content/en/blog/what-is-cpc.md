**CPC** (cost per click) is the average cost of one click on an ad. In Google search ads it's the main pricing unit: you usually pay per click. In Meta ads, CPC is a derived metric — even when you pay for impressions, you can see what each click cost. Here's what CPC is and how to judge it properly.

## The formula

**CPC = spend ÷ clicks**

An illustrative example: you spend $40 and get 200 clicks. CPC = 40 ÷ 200 = **$0.20**.

## CPC on Google vs Meta

| | Google Search | Meta (Instagram/Facebook) |
|---|---|---|
| Payment | Usually per click | Usually per impression |
| What drives CPC | Keyword competition, ad quality, bids | CPM and CTR |
| The person's intent | High — actively searching | Low to medium — scrolling a feed |

Google CPCs can be higher, but the clicks are warmer — [Google Ads vs Meta Ads](/en/blog/google-ads-vs-meta-ads/).

## What affects CPC

- **CPM and CTR**: on Meta, CPC = CPM ÷ (CTR × 10), so a higher CTR means cheaper clicks — [what is CTR](/en/blog/what-is-ctr/), [what is CPM](/en/blog/what-is-cpm/).
- **Competition**: many advertisers bidding for the same keyword or audience.
- **Ad quality**: on Google, ads and pages that match the query cost less.
- **Season**: prices rise around holidays and promotions.

## The cheap-click trap

A cheap CPC isn't always good. A "traffic" campaign, for example, can deliver very cheap clicks from people who close the page immediately. The real question is **what one lead and one sale cost** — [cost per lead](/en/blog/cost-per-lead-cpl-how-to-lower/), [the cheap leads trap](/en/blog/cheap-leads-trap/).

## How to lower CPC properly

1. Raise CTR with a strong hook and a clear offer.
2. On Google: clear-intent keywords and negative keywords — [how to set up Google Ads](/en/blog/how-to-set-up-google-ads/).
3. Match the ad to the landing page.
4. Don't narrow the audience too much.
5. Refresh creatives regularly — [creative fatigue](/en/blog/creative-fatigue/).

## Questions and answers

### How does CPC differ from CPL?

CPC is the cost of a click; CPL is the cost of a lead. CPL = CPC ÷ landing page conversion rate.

### What should my CPC be?

There's no universal benchmark. Work backwards from your target cost per lead: if a lead must cost no more than $5 and the page converts at 10%, CPC must stay under $0.50 (an illustrative example).

### Lots of clicks but few leads — what now?

Check the page, the form and your response speed — [a landing page that converts](/en/blog/landing-page-that-converts/).
