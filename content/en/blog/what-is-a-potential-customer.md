A **potential customer** is a person or company that may need your product but hasn't bought anything from you yet. Business owners use the term all the time, yet they often mix up potential customers, leads and customers — and then the ads, the sales team and the monthly report end up speaking different languages. Here's a plain guide to recognising potential customers, finding them and moving them towards a purchase.

## What is a potential customer: a simple definition

Two questions define a potential customer:

1. **Do they need your product?** They have a problem or a wish, and you solve it.
2. **Can they buy from you?** They can afford it, they make the decision, and you can deliver to them.

If both answers are "yes", you have a potential customer. They may not even have heard of you yet. A family that has just bought a flat in Chilanzar is a potential customer for a furniture store, even if the store's name means nothing to them.

Being a potential customer is a stage, not a permanent label. Today someone is a potential customer; tomorrow they leave an enquiry and become a lead; once they pay, they're a customer. Working with new, repeat and loyal customers is covered in [our article on types of customers](/en/blog/types-of-customers/) — here we focus on what happens before the first purchase.

## Potential customer, lead and customer: the difference

Put the three terms in one table and the confusion disappears.

| Stage | Who | What you have | Main task |
|---|---|---|---|
| Target audience | A broad group your product suits | Just a description | Know who to talk to |
| Potential customer | A specific person or company with a need and the means | No contact, or just a follow | Get attention, build trust |
| Lead | A potential customer who showed interest and left contact details | Name, phone or a chat | Reply fast, qualify |
| Customer | Has paid at least once | Receipt, contract | Good experience, repeat purchase |

A target audience is a description you use when setting up ads: "women aged 25–40 in Tashkent with children". How to define one is explained in [our guide to target audiences and customer personas](/en/blog/target-audience-and-customer-persona/). A potential customer is a real person inside that group: the mum who watched your Reel about your courses to the end yesterday.

For more on leads, see [what is a lead](/en/blog/what-is-a-lead/). In short: a potential customer becomes a lead the moment they leave contact details. The difference matters in practice because each needs different work. Content and ads work on potential customers; a call from a sales manager works on leads.

## 5 signs of a potential customer

Not everyone who is curious is a potential customer. Check five signs.

- **A need.** The problem or wish is clear: a toothache, a teenager who has to sit IELTS, an office that needs 20 chairs.
- **The means to pay.** Your price fits their budget, either upfront or in instalments.
- **Decision power.** They make the decision or influence whoever does. Big purchases often involve several people — a couple, parents, or a director and an accountant in a company.
- **The right timing.** The need will appear in the coming months. If the wedding is in autumn 2027, they're a distant potential customer for a banquet hall for now.
- **You can serve them.** They live in your area, you deliver there, you speak their language.

Four signs out of five means a strong potential customer. One or two — keep them in view, but don't spend your sales team's time on them.

> Pick potential customers by asking "who can buy from us?", not "who likes us?".

## Where to find potential customers: 4 examples from Tashkent

To find potential customers, first understand where and when the need appears. Four illustrative examples:

| Business | Potential customer | When the need appears | Where to find them |
|---|---|---|---|
| Furniture store | A family that bought a home or is renovating | After getting the keys, at the end of renovation | Instagram, Uzum Market, outdoor ads near new buildings |
| Dental clinic, Chilanzar | Adults and parents within 3–5 km | When a tooth hurts or they think about their smile | Google and Yandex search, maps, Instagram |
| IELTS school | Students aged 15–22 and their parents | When planning to study abroad, 3–6 months before the exam | Instagram, TikTok, Telegram channels |
| Wholesale building materials | Contractors, construction companies | When a new project starts | LinkedIn, trade shows, referrals, cold calls |

The table shows that no single channel fits everyone. A dental clinic's potential customer often searches on their own, so search and maps matter. An IELTS school's potential customer isn't searching yet but scrolls a video feed, so targeted ads matter.

## Estimating how many potential customers you have

Before planning an ad budget or a sales target, answer one question: how many potential customers are around you? You don't need an exact number — the order of magnitude is enough.

**Illustrative example.** A children's development centre opens in Yunusabad.

1. Around 6,000 families with children aged 3–7 live within 3 km (illustrative figure).
2. One in ten is willing to pay for after-school classes — 600 families.
3. The centre can take 120 children at a time.

Conclusion: there are five times more potential customers than places, so market size isn't the problem. If groups aren't filling up, look at the offer, the price or the ads. The reverse also happens: 200 potential customers and 150 places — then every lost enquiry is very expensive and response speed decides everything.

Where to get the numbers: open district statistics, the estimated audience size in your ad account, the number of competitors and the history in your own CRM.

## Moving a potential customer to a purchase: 6 steps

### 1. Describe your potential customer in one sentence

"Everyone" isn't an answer. Write something like: "parents in Mirzo Ulugbek district looking for an English course for a child aged 6–10". That sentence becomes the basis for your ads, content and sales script.

### 2. Match the channel to the moment of need

If people search on their own — search, maps, marketplaces. If they don't search but care about the topic — Instagram, TikTok, Telegram. In B2B — personal contacts, trade shows and referrals. Start with one channel and get it working properly.

### 3. Offer a small first step

A potential customer isn't ready for a big purchase straight away. Give them an easy step: a free check-up, a trial lesson, a free measuring visit, a catalogue or a price list. The easier the step, the more people leave their contact details.

### 4. Collect contact details the convenient way

Lead form, Telegram bot, call button, website form — use whichever suits your potential customer. Every extra question reduces the number of enquiries but raises their quality; you choose the balance.

### 5. Reply fast and qualify

A potential customer who has left contact details is now a lead. They have probably messaged your competitors at the same time. Replying within minutes during working hours and checking the five signs in the conversation is the cheapest way to sell more.

### 6. Bring back those who didn't leave contact details

Someone who visited your site or watched a video to the end but didn't write is a warm potential customer. Bring them back with [retargeting](/en/blog/how-retargeting-works/): reviews, before-and-after results, answers to common questions. People who said "I'll think about it" need a separate plan — that's what the [14-day follow-up plan](/en/blog/i-will-think-about-it-follow-up/) is for.

## What to measure

Three numbers show how well you work with potential customers:

- **potential customer to lead conversion** — how many of the people who saw your ads or content left contact details;
- **lead to customer conversion** — how many your sales team turned into buyers;
- **cost per customer** — money spent on ads and sales divided by the number of purchases.

If the first number is low, the problem is the offer or the channel. If the second is low, the problem is the sales process. Without looking at them separately, it's easy to blame the ads for nothing.

## Common mistakes

- **"Everyone needs our product."** Ads aimed at everyone reach no one.
- **Counting only leads.** People who were interested but didn't leave contact details are forgotten — and go to a competitor.
- **Pushing too early.** "Pay today and get a discount" scares off someone who doesn't trust you yet.
- **Treating existing customers as potential ones.** People who already bought see a "first purchase" discount and the money is wasted.
- **Buying contact lists.** A list of strangers' numbers isn't potential customers — they never asked to hear from you.

## Questions and answers

### Is a potential customer the same as the target audience?

No. A target audience is a broad description, such as "young families in Tashkent". A potential customer is a specific person matching that description who has the need and the means to buy. You set up ads by audience; you talk to potential customers.

### When does a potential customer become a lead?

When they leave contact details or write first: they fill in a lead form, send "how much?" in a DM, share their number with a bot or call you. From that point they should be in your CRM and get a reply.

### Can I buy a database of potential customers?

We don't recommend it. People on such lists haven't agreed to hear from you, contacting them may conflict with personal data laws, and conversion is usually very low. A base you build yourself — through ads, your site, a bot or your shop — is far more valuable.

### Who is a potential customer in B2B?

The company and the decision-makers inside it. One company usually involves several people: the user, the manager and procurement. Each needs their own arguments, so in B2B it's better to define potential customers by company and job role.

## Conclusion

A potential customer is someone who needs your product and can buy it but hasn't yet. They're more specific than a target audience and one step before a lead. Recognise them by the five signs, reach them in the right channel at the moment of need, offer an easy first step and don't forget people who didn't leave contact details.
