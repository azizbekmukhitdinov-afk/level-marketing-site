You don't need expensive research to understand your market: in a single working day, open sources can tell you a great deal about competitors. The goal isn't to copy them but to find what they do badly and how you can stand out. Here is a one-day plan and a summary table.

## Morning: find 5–7 competitors

- Google and Yandex searches for your main queries.
- "Near me" searches in Google and Yandex Maps — we have a [separate article on maps](/en/blog/add-your-business-to-maps/).
- Instagram search and hashtags, Telegram channels.
- If you sell products — marketplaces such as [Uzum Market](/en/blog/building-a-brand-on-uzum-market/).
- Ask your customers: "Who did you compare us with?"

Split competitors into two groups: **direct** (same product, same audience) and **indirect** (a different product that solves the same problem).

## Midday: what to check

| Area | What to look at | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Offer and USP | What they promise, what guarantees they give | Website, profile bio |
| Prices | Price level, instalments, discounts | Website, DMs, marketplace |
| Ads | Active ads, formats, offers | Meta Ad Library, Telegram channels |
| Content | Posting frequency, Reels, engagement | Instagram, TikTok, YouTube |
| Reviews | Praise and complaints | Maps, comments, marketplace |
| Service | Speed and quality of replies | A message posing as a customer |

In the Meta Ad Library (facebook.com/ads/library), choose Uzbekistan as the country and enter a competitor's page name: you'll see their active ads and when each one started running. An ad that has been running for a long time is probably working.

## Afternoon: mystery shopping and reviews

Message a competitor as an ordinary customer: how fast did they reply, did they ask questions, did they follow up later? Compare it with your own service — see our article on [lead response time](/en/blog/lead-response-time/).

Complaints in reviews are your opportunity. If customers complain about a competitor's late deliveries, fast delivery could become your [USP](/en/blog/unique-selling-proposition/).

The last column matters most: every competitor weakness is something you can do better. For example, to help the [Global Avenue](/en/blog/case-global-avenue/) residential complex stand out from competitors, a unique offer for the market was developed together with the client — a 3-year interest-free instalment plan.

## Ground rules

Use only public information. Don't copy texts, photos or designs — the analysis is for understanding the market, not for cloning it.

## Conclusion

Repeat your competitor analysis every 3–6 months: markets change and new players appear. The findings feed your USP, ad copy and pricing. The next step is to define who you're selling to: [target audience and customer persona](/en/blog/target-audience-and-customer-persona/).
