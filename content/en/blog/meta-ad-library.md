When a business needs an idea for a new ad, the usual method is scrolling Instagram and remembering whatever ad happened to appear. That is luck, not research. The **Meta Ad Library** shows, in one place and with filters, which ads are running in your niche right now. This guide covers how to search it, what conclusions it can and cannot support, and where the line is: borrowing ideas is fine, copying is not.

## What the Meta Ad Library is

The library is a public database of ads shown across Meta's platforms: Facebook, Instagram, Messenger and others. It lives at [facebook.com/ads/library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/) and exists for transparency: anyone can see what ads a given page is running.

According to [Meta's official page](https://transparency.meta.com/researchtools/ad-library-tools), all active ads are searchable. Ads about social issues, elections or politics are kept for 7 years whether they are active or not, and they come with spend, reach and information about who paid for them. Ads delivered in the European Union get extra information and stay archived for one year after their last impression.

The practical takeaway for an ordinary commercial ad in Uzbekistan: you can see it **only while it is running**. Once it is switched off, it disappears from search. That is why the library is something to check regularly, not once.

## What you can and cannot see

The most common mistake is assuming the library shows results. It does not.

| Visible | Not visible |
|---|---|
| The creative: video, image, carousel | Ad cost, CPL and sales |
| Ad copy and the button (CTA) | Audience settings and budget |
| When the ad started running | Which variant is winning |
| Platforms: Facebook, Instagram and others | Number and quality of leads |
| Several versions of one ad | Commercial ads that have stopped |
| The advertiser's page | What happens in sales after the click |

In other words, the library answers "what are they doing", not "what is working". Forget that difference and you end up importing someone else's mistakes.

## How to search: 5 steps

1. **Pick the country.** Uzbekistan, or wherever your customers live.
2. **Pick the ad category.** For commercial ads you want "All ads", not the political category.
3. **Type a keyword or a page name.** Searching by page name is more precise. When searching by keyword, try the Russian spelling and the Uzbek spelling in both Latin and Cyrillic script separately; each one surfaces different ads.
4. **Apply filters.** Narrow by platform, media type (video or image) and dates. Keep active ads only.
5. **Save what you find.** Put the link and library ID of every interesting ad into a sheet. It may be gone tomorrow.

Draw up your competitor list in advance: 5–7 direct competitors plus 3–5 strong players from other cities or countries. How to build that list is covered in our [competitor analysis](/en/blog/competitor-analysis/) guide.

## What to look at: a creative research sheet

Just looking at ads is not enough. If you log each one in the same columns, patterns start to appear after 20–30 ads.

| Column | What you record |
|---|---|
| Hook | What happens in the first 2–3 seconds: question, result, problem, face |
| Format | Reels, static image, carousel, UGC, screen recording |
| Offer | Discount, free consultation, instalments, gift |
| Angle | Price, quality, speed, trust, status, convenience |
| CTA | Lead form, website, DM, phone call |
| Start date | How many days it has been running |
| Versions | How many variants sit inside one ad |

What a creative is made of is explained in [what is an ad creative](/en/blog/what-is-an-ad-creative/). Once the sheet is filled, answer two questions: what does everyone in the niche repeat, and what does nobody show? The second matters more, because the empty space may become your angle.

## Illustrative example: a niche review in 30 minutes

Illustrative example. The owner of a dental clinic in Tashkent reviewed 6 competitors in the library and found 34 active ads:

- 22 (65%) open with a discount and a price: "implants 30% cheaper".
- 8 (24%) show a doctor talking to camera.
- 4 (12%) tell a patient's story or show the procedure.
- None of them shows a guarantee or follow-up care after treatment.
- 3 ads have been running for more than 60 days, each with 4–5 versions.

Conclusion: the niche is saturated with price messages. Instead of joining the discount race, the clinic tests a "one year of follow-up after treatment" angle: a doctor video, the guarantee terms and a lead form. From the three long-running ads it borrows the format (a short question-and-answer video), not the content.

Note that "running for 60 days" is a signal, not proof. Maybe the ad really performs well. Maybe nobody bothered to switch it off. Test the hypothesis in your own account; the method is in [A/B testing ad creatives](/en/blog/ab-testing-ad-creatives/).

## From research to a creative brief

Turn the finished sheet into a short brief for your designer or videographer. One page at most:

- **What the niche does:** 2–3 sentences on the most common angle and format.
- **What we will test:** 2–3 hypotheses, each phrased as "if we show X, Y will change".
- **Two creatives per hypothesis:** same offer, different hooks, so you know what explains any difference in results.
- **What we will not do:** a list of specific phrases, colours and shots competitors already use.
- **How we will measure:** cost per lead, lead quality and a 7–14 day test period.

This routine turns the library from an inspiration feed into a working tool. Research without a brief usually ends with the request "make it look like theirs".

## Using it without copying: the ethics

The library is a source of ideas, not a template. Set the boundary in advance:

- **Fine to borrow:** a format (such as a problem, solution, result structure), a type of hook, video length, a type of offer, gaps in the niche.
- **Not fine to borrow:** someone else's video, photos, copy, slogans, music, design or brand elements.
- **Be careful with:** repeating a competitor's exact offer with "but cheaper". It drags you into a price war and weakens your brand.

Using other people's content is also a legal risk, as explained in [content copyright](/en/blog/content-copyright-uzbekistan/). There is a practical reason too: a copied creative is something the audience has already seen, and near-identical creatives give the algorithm little to choose between. Why variety matters is covered in our piece on [Meta Andromeda](/en/blog/meta-andromeda-creative-diversity/).

A simple test: remove your logo from the ad. If people would think it belongs to a competitor, you copied.

## Limits and common false conclusions

- **"Many versions means success."** Many versions mean testing, not results.
- **"The competitor isn't advertising."** They may be running ads from another page or targeting another country. Try several search methods.
- **"What I see is their whole strategy."** You will not see Google, Telegram, influencer or offline advertising there.
- **One-off research.** Commercial ads vanish when they stop. Set aside 30 minutes a month and update the sheet.
- **Local competitors only.** Formats used by strong brands abroad are often still unused in the local market.

AI can speed this up: give a model the entries from your sheet and ask it to find recurring patterns. The approach is described in [AI competitor analysis](/en/blog/ai-competitor-analysis/).

## Questions and answers

### Does the Meta Ad Library cost anything?

No, it is free and public. You open it in a browser and search by country and keyword. There is also an API for deeper analysis, but it is mainly aimed at political ads and ads delivered in the EU, and it is limited to authorised users.

### Can I find out how much a competitor spends on ads?

Not for commercial ads in Uzbekistan. Spend and reach are shown mainly for ads about social issues, elections or politics. An indirect signal is the number of active ads and how long they have been running.

### Can competitors see my ads too?

Yes. Your active ads are visible to everyone in the library. So keep customers' personal data and internal pricing policy out of your creatives.

### How often should I check the library?

A structured review once a month is enough. Add an extra check before a new season, a holiday or a big promotion, since that is when competitors usually launch fresh creatives.

## Conclusion

The Meta Ad Library shows what competitors are doing, not what is working for them. Using it well comes down to three steps: a systematic search, a filled-in research sheet, and testing the hypotheses you find in your own ads. Copying feels like a shortcut but makes your brand indistinguishable from everyone else.
