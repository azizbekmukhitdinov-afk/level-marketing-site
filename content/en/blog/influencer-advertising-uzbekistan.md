Influencer advertising is a fast way to get noticed, yet many business owners pay for it blindly: they pick bloggers by follower count and judge the result with "it got lots of views". This guide walks through how to choose a blogger, what to agree on, how to meet the legal requirements in Uzbekistan and how to count the customers an integration actually brought in.

![Who needs influencer advertising and when](/img/blog/illus/blogerlar-bilan-reklama-1.jpg 1200x675)

## Who needs influencer advertising and when

A blogger does not replace targeted ads — they do a different job. Targeting shows your ad to a precise audience, many times, under your control. A blogger brings trust: people treat a recommendation from someone they know differently from a banner.

Working with bloggers pays off especially when you have:

- **A new brand or a new branch.** Say a coffee shop opens in Chilanzar — a city blogger can introduce it to thousands of people in a day.
- **A product that needs trust.** Clinics, training centers, cosmetics — the feeling that "someone already tried it" matters here.
- **A product that needs to be shown.** Furniture, appliances, clothing — a blogger shows it in real use.

If you don't yet have a profile that sells, a manager who replies quickly and a clear offer, hold off on paying a blogger. People will visit your profile after the integration and decide based on what they see. Get your [Instagram profile that sells](/en/blog/instagram-profile-that-sells/) and your [unique selling proposition](/en/blog/unique-selling-proposition/) ready first.

## Influencer advertising: choosing a blogger in 5 steps

### 1. Are these your customers?

The first question isn't how many followers, but who follows. For a furniture store in Tashkent, a home and interior blogger with 30,000 followers can do more than a comedy blogger with 500,000 followers who are mostly schoolkids. Define your [target audience](/en/blog/target-audience-and-customer-persona/) first, then look for a blogger who reaches it.

Ask the blogger for screenshots of their statistics (on Instagram, the "Professional dashboard"):

- which cities the audience is in (what share is Tashkent);
- age and gender;
- average Stories and Reels reach over the last 30 days.

Have the blogger send the screenshots with the date visible. A short screen recording is even better — it's harder to fake.

### 2. Compare reach and engagement

Followers can be bought; real reach is harder to fake. Look at:

- **Stories views relative to followers.** If there are 100,000 followers but only a few hundred people watch Stories, most of the audience is inactive.
- **What the comments say.** "🔥🔥🔥" and identical template comments are a bad sign. Questions and debates are a good one.
- **Follower growth.** A sharp jump in one day followed by a flat line often means bought followers.

### 3. Review past sponsored posts

Scroll through the blogger's sponsored posts from the last 2–3 months. If a blogger runs 4–5 ads a day, their audience has stopped trusting them. How the ads are delivered matters too: does the blogger just read a script, or weave the product into their life?

Stay away from bloggers who have promoted prohibited things such as betting services or alcohol. Bloggers in Uzbekistan have already been held liable for such ads, and sitting next to them hurts your brand.

### 4. Do values and tone match?

A children's training center shouldn't work with a blogger who swears or builds content on scandals, however big the reach. Watch their last 10–15 videos and ask: "Would I be happy with this person speaking on behalf of my brand?"

### 5. One big blogger or several small ones

Small and mid-sized bloggers (10,000–100,000 followers) are usually closer to their audience and charge less. It's safer to test 3–4 smaller bloggers first and return to the one who performed best than to spend the whole budget on one big name.

## What to put in the agreement

A verbal deal is a source of future disputes. Even for a small integration, agree on these in writing (at least in chat):

| What | Example |
|---|---|
| Format | 3 Stories + 1 Reel |
| Date and time | November 15, evening 19:00–21:00 |
| Key messages | Free delivery, 12-month installments |
| Off-limits | No mentions of competitors, no "cheapest" claims |
| Link and promo code | Personal UTM link, promo code BLOGER10 |
| Approval | Script or video reviewed before posting |
| How long it stays up | Reel stays on the profile for at least 30 days |
| Statistics | Reach and click screenshots after 24 hours |
| Content rights | Right to use the video in our own ads (with a time limit) |

The dates, terms and promo code in the table are an illustrative example.

The last row deserves special attention. A blogger's successful video can later work in your targeted ads — it builds trust much like [UGC content](/en/blog/ugc-content/). But that right has to be agreed in advance.

Instead of a ready-made script, give the blogger a brief: what the product is, who it's for, what problem it solves and which 2–3 points must be said. Let the blogger say the rest in their own words — that's what the audience believes.

## The legal side: the "advertising" label

This matters more than many people think. In September 2026, Uzbekistan's Committee for Competition Development and Consumer Protection reminded social media users that advertising must be clearly identified: promotional materials must carry the label "advertising" ("reklama") or "on the rights of advertising". The Committee cited Article 5 of the Law "On Advertising" and noted that the format alone doesn't decide whether a post is an ad — a review or "personal opinion" made for commercial purposes can count as advertising too. According to the Committee, measures were applied to more than 20 bloggers and group and channel administrators ([UzA](https://uza.uz/ar/posts/reklama-v-socsetyax-dolzhna-byt-zametnoy-komitet-po-konkurencii-predupredil-blogerov_903570)).

In practice this means:

- The "advertising" label should be clearly visible in Stories, Reels and posts, not hidden among hashtags.
- On Instagram, also use the **"Paid partnership"** label: the blogger tags you as a partner and you approve it. Instagram's branded content policies require this label ([Instagram Help Center](https://help.instagram.com/android-app/1109894795810258)).
- Alcohol, tobacco, betting services and medicines are subject to separate bans and restrictions — if you work in one of these niches, talk to a lawyer first.

There's also news on the tax side. The Tax Committee and the Ministry of Justice are drafting a document on labeling online advertising and tracking bloggers' income — it's still at the draft stage ([Spot.uz](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2026/03/13/bloggers-income/)). The takeaway for business is simple: pay bloggers with paperwork (a contract, an invoice and, where possible, a blogger registered as a sole proprietor or self-employed). As the rules tighten, cash-only deals without documents will become a risk for the business as well.

## How to measure results

"Lots of views" is not a result. Each blogger needs their own tracking tool, or you'll never know which one brought in money.

**1. A personal UTM link.** Give every blogger their own link: `utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=bloger&utm_campaign=noyabr&utm_content=blogger_name`. Your website or Telegram bot analytics will show how many people came through it. How to build one — in our article on [UTM tags](/en/blog/utm-tags/).

**2. A personal promo code.** Customers who walk into a store or call don't click links. A promo code works for them: "Say BLOGER10 for 10% off". Always record the code at the till or in your CRM.

**3. The manager's question.** Ask every new customer "How did you hear about us?" and record the answer in the CRM. Simple, but many businesses skip it.

**4. Compare with the baseline.** Compare inquiries, profile visits and follower growth for the 7 days before and the 7 days after the integration. Some customers will come without a promo code — the difference shows them.

Collect the results in a table:

| Blogger | Price | Clicks | Leads | Sales | Cost per lead |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (city blogger) | 3M UZS | 420 | 35 | 9 | ~86K UZS |
| B (interior blogger) | 2M UZS | 150 | 22 | 7 | ~91K UZS |

The numbers in the table are an illustrative example, only to show how to calculate.

Compare the cost per lead with your [cost per lead](/en/blog/cost-per-lead-cpl-how-to-lower/) from targeted ads. But don't decide on that number alone: influencer advertising builds awareness and its effect lasts for weeks — afterwards your targeted ads may also get cheaper because people already know you.

## 5 common mistakes

1. **Choosing by follower count.** Paying without checking reach and audience geography.
2. **A one-off integration.** Expecting big results from a single Story. Audiences usually act after 2–3 touches with a brand — several appearances with one blogger beat a single one.
3. **Handing the blogger a script.** The blogger loses their voice and the audience notices immediately.
4. **Ads without tracking.** No UTM, no promo code — you'll never know the result.
5. **Posting without the "advertising" label.** The blogger skips it "to look more natural" — that breaks the law and puts your brand at risk.

## Questions and answers

**How much should I pay a blogger?**
There's no single price: it depends on reach, niche and format. Judge the price by real reach, not follower count, and compare offers from several bloggers. Put the deal in writing before paying.

**Can I pay with products (barter)?**
Yes, especially with smaller bloggers. But even with barter, the format, date and "advertising" label need to be agreed — it's still advertising.

**How soon will I see results after an integration?**
Clicks and inquiries from Stories mostly arrive within the first 24 hours. Reels keep working longer. Brand awareness can build over weeks, so track results for at least 1–2 weeks.

**Which is better — a blogger or targeted ads?**
They complement each other. A blogger brings trust and awareness; targeted ads bring a steady, controlled flow of leads. A good combination is running a blogger's successful video as a targeted ad.

## Conclusion

Influencer advertising should be a system, not a lucky pick: check the audience, agree in writing, label the ad and give every blogger a separate UTM link and promo code. Then a month later you'll know exactly which blogger brought in money and which one only brought views.
