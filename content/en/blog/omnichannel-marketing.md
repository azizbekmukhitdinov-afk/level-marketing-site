Today a customer sees you on Instagram, asks for a price on Telegram, visits your store and later reorders from your website. If each channel lives on its own, the customer has to reintroduce themselves every time, and the salesperson doesn't know their history. **Omnichannel marketing** joins all channels into one customer experience. Here's what it is and how a small business can start.

## Multichannel vs omnichannel

| | Multichannel | Omnichannel |
|---|---|---|
| Channels | Many, but separate | Many and connected |
| Customer history | Separate in each channel | In one place (the CRM) |
| Experience | Varies by channel | Consistent and continuous |
| Example | Different prices on Instagram and in store | A question from Instagram continues in store |

## Examples for businesses in Uzbekistan (illustrative)

- A customer sees furniture on Instagram → sends measurements on Telegram → the salesperson sees that chat during the in-store meeting.
- An online order → collected in store → a bonus for the next purchase, automatically.
- A clinic: booking via the website → an SMS reminder (with consent) → an NPS survey after the appointment — [what is NPS](/en/blog/what-is-nps/).

## The core parts of omnichannel

1. **A single customer base** — each customer's channel, conversations and purchases in the CRM — [CRM for small business](/en/blog/crm-for-small-business/).
2. **The same offer and price** across all channels.
3. **The same service standard** — [customer service standards](/en/blog/customer-service-standards/).
4. **Bridges between channels** — a QR code in store, a Telegram link on Instagram, a promo code offline — [promo codes in marketing](/en/blog/promo-codes-marketing/).
5. **Measurement** across the customer journey, not one channel — [customer journey map](/en/blog/customer-journey-map/).

## Where to start

1. List which channels customers come from and where they buy.
2. Collect all enquiries in one CRM.
3. Align prices and promotions across channels.
4. Train staff to reply with the customer's history in view.
5. Keep in touch for repeat purchases: reminders, bonuses — [loyalty programmes](/en/blog/loyalty-program/).

## Common mistakes

- A separate employee and a separate database for each channel.
- One price on Instagram, another in store.
- Customers having to re-explain in store what they wrote on Telegram.
- Crediting only the last channel as "the result".

## Questions and answers

### Does a small business need omnichannel?

Not a full technology stack. But a single customer base and consistent prices help any business.

### Which channels are essential?

The ones your customers use. Usually Instagram, Telegram, maps and a store or website.

### What about personal data?

Collect a cross-channel database with consent and protect it — [customer database and personal data](/en/blog/customer-database-personal-data-uzbekistan/).
