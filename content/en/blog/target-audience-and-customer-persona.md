"Our audience is women aged 18–45" isn't an audience description. A good customer persona tells you what to say in an ad, who to show it to and where. Here is how to segment your market, build a customer persona and use it in your ads.

![Step 1: segmentation](/img/blog/illus/maqsadli-auditoriya-1.jpg 1200x675)

## Step 1: segmentation

Split the market into groups with similar needs:

- **Demographics:** age, gender, income, city.
- **Situation:** when the product is needed — for furniture, say: moving into a new flat, renovating, fitting out an office.
- **Behaviour:** first or repeat purchase, price-focused or quality-focused.
- **Channel:** where people spend their time — Instagram, Telegram, search.

Then pick 1–2 priority segments: the biggest, the most profitable or the easiest to reach.

## Step 2: the customer persona

Describe one "typical" customer for each segment. **An example (illustrative):** an online children's clothing shop.

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Who | Malika, 29, Tashkent, two children aged 3 and 6 |
| Situation | Buys clothes for the kids every season |
| Pains | Kids grow fast, no time to go to the bazaar, hard to balance price and quality |
| Desires | Natural fabrics, delivery, easy exchanges |
| Objections | "Will the size fit?", "Is it really cotton?" |
| Where she spends time | Instagram, parenting Telegram channels |
| What drives the decision | Reviews from other mums, real photos, delivery terms |

## Where to learn pains and desires

- Interviews with 5–10 customers — see [collecting customer feedback](/en/blog/collecting-customer-feedback/) for how to run them.
- Sales managers: what customers ask most often.
- Conversations in [comments and DMs](/en/blog/handling-comments-and-dms/).
- Reviews of competitors — see [competitor analysis](/en/blog/competitor-analysis/).
- Follower insights in the Instagram professional dashboard: age, gender, cities.
- CRM data: who buys more and comes back.

## Using the persona in ads

- **Creative:** show the customer's situation and pain — our article on [types of video ads](/en/blog/types-of-video-ads/) helps you pick a format.
- **Copy:** use the customer's own words and answer objections up front.
- **Settings:** location, age and interests; build a [lookalike audience](/en/blog/lookalike-audiences/) from your buyer list.
- **One ad per segment:** the same ad won't work for people moving house and for offices.

A precise creative also helps the algorithm find the right people: the system shows the ad to people similar to those who responded. For setup basics, see [how to set up Instagram ads](/en/blog/how-to-set-up-instagram-ads/).

![8 questions for a customer interview](/img/blog/illus/maqsadli-auditoriya-2.jpg 1200x675)

## 8 questions for a customer interview

1. How did you find us, and why did you choose us?
2. What other options did you consider?
3. What held you back from buying?
4. Where did you look for information?
5. How would you describe your problem in your own words?
6. What changed after the purchase?
7. Who else was involved in the decision?
8. How would you recommend us to a friend?

Write the answers down word for word: the customer's own phrases are the best raw material for ad copy.

## How a persona differs in B2B

In B2B, the decision is made not by one person but by several: the user, a manager and the finance department. Build a separate persona for each — their questions differ. The user cares about convenience, the manager about results, and finance about price and terms. The sales cycle is longer, so you need content for every stage and every role.

## Using the persona across the team

Fit the persona on one page and share it with your SMM manager, designer, paid ads specialist and sales team. Refer to it in every brief. Before each new ad or post, ask a simple question: "Is this for Malika?" If the answer is no, rework the text or the creative.

## Common mistakes

- "Inventing" the persona in the office without talking to customers.
- A description that's too broad: "everyone aged 18 to 55".
- One ad for every segment.
- Demographics only — no situation, pain points or objections.
- Writing the persona once and never updating it.

## Questions and answers

### How many personas do you need?

One to three to start. You need the capacity to prepare a separate message and creative for each persona — otherwise they just stay in a document.

### Should you add lots of interests in your ad settings?

Not necessarily. In today's ad systems, broad audiences often perform well as long as the creative speaks to a specific person. The persona first defines what to say, and only then whom to show it to.

### How do you check whether a persona is right?

Run a separate ad for each segment and compare cost per lead and conversion to sales. The numbers will confirm or disprove your assumptions.

### When should you update a persona?

Every six months, or when a new product, price or channel appears. Add new data from your CRM and sales team.

### Are Instagram insights enough to build a persona?

No — they only give demographics: age, gender and cities. The customer's situation, pain points and objections come only from interviews, your sales team and message threads. Use the insights to check the persona: if most followers are from another city, your content or ads may be attracting the wrong audience.

## Conclusion

A persona isn't a one-off document: update it every six months with new data. A product "for everyone" is the most common mistake new entrepreneurs make; the rest are in our article on [7 marketing mistakes](/en/blog/marketing-mistakes-new-entrepreneurs/).
