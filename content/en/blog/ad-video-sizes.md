The video's finished, the ad is live — and the Instagram button is sitting on top of the price while the logo has been cropped off. It's one of the most common mistakes in video advertising. **Ad video sizes** differ from platform to platform, and knowing them before you edit changes how you plan the shoot itself. This article covers where 9:16, 4:5, 1:1 and 16:9 belong, how safe zones work and what file specs to export.

The figures come from Meta's and Google's help pages as of November 2026. Platforms update their requirements from time to time, so check in your ad account before uploading.

## The four main formats

Almost every ad video falls into one of four aspect ratios. The first number is the width, the second the height.

| Format | Recommended size | Looks like | Main use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9:16 | 1080×1920 (Meta's guide lists 1440×2560) | Vertical, full screen | Reels, Stories, TikTok, YouTube Shorts |
| 4:5 | 1080×1350 or 1440×1800 | Near-vertical | Instagram and Facebook feed |
| 1:1 | 1080×1080 | Square | Feed, some YouTube campaigns |
| 16:9 | 1920×1080 | Horizontal | YouTube in-stream, website, TV |

The practical takeaway: in Uzbekistan ads are watched mostly on phones, so 9:16 is your main format. You need 4:5 for feeds and 16:9 only for YouTube and your website.

## Instagram and Facebook: Meta's specs

Meta's ads guide has separate requirements for each placement. The key ones:

| Placement | Ratio | Duration | File |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram and Facebook Reels | 9:16 | Up to 15 minutes | MP4 or MOV, up to 4 GB |
| Stories | 9:16 | Up to 60 minutes; videos up to about 16 seconds play in full, longer ones may be split into cards | MP4, MOV, up to 4 GB |
| Facebook feed | 4:5 | 1 second to 241 minutes | MP4, MOV, up to 4 GB |

Source: [Meta Ads Guide — Instagram Reels](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/instagram-reels). The allowed duration is a technical limit, not a recommendation. How long an ad should actually run is covered in our article on [video ad length](/en/blog/video-ad-length/).

Meta also lists technical settings: H.264 compression, a fixed frame rate, progressive scan and stereo AAC audio at 128 kbps or higher. Most editing apps produce a matching file with an "Instagram" or "H.264" export preset.

If one video is running across all placements, you can upload a separate version for each in Ads Manager: 9:16 for Reels and Stories, 4:5 for the feed. Meta can crop automatically, but the result isn't always tidy.

## Safe zones: where to put text and the logo

On vertical video, the interface covers the top and bottom: the profile name, ad copy, the "Learn more" button, like and comment icons. For Reels and Stories, Meta recommends keeping these zones clear:

- **top 14%** — profile and the "Sponsored" label;
- **bottom 35%** — ad copy and the call-to-action button;
- **6% on the left and right** — edges and icons.

In pixels:

| Frame size | Top | Bottom | Sides | Safe area |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1080×1920 | ≈ 270 px | ≈ 672 px | ≈ 65 px | ≈ 950×978 px |
| 1440×2560 | ≈ 358 px | ≈ 896 px | ≈ 86 px | ≈ 1268×1306 px |

What belongs inside the safe area: price, offer, logo, subtitles, key captions and the most important part of the product. Background and movement can spill outside it; text can't.

> Don't put anything important in the bottom third of the frame — the button will be there anyway.

When editing, it helps to lay a transparent template with these zones over the frame. Ads Manager also has an ad preview — check every placement there before launch.

## TikTok: vertical and fast

TikTok is a vertical platform, so 9:16 is the main format. Square and horizontal videos are accepted, but much of the screen stays empty and the ad looks out of place.

Public guides usually list these specs for TikTok video ads: 9:16 ratio, at least 540×960 (1080×1920 recommended in practice), MP4 or MOV, up to 500 MB and 5–60 seconds long. These numbers can change — check the exact requirements in TikTok Ads Manager before uploading.

TikTok's interface is laid out differently: the like, comment and share buttons sit on the right, the account name and caption at the bottom. So:

- place text in the centre and slightly to the left;
- keep anything important away from the right edge;
- leave the bottom clear, just as on Meta.

## YouTube: in-stream, bumper and Shorts

Google Ads help lists three formats for YouTube ([Google Ads: video specs](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17091270)):

| Format | Size | Where | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16:9 | 1920×1080 | In-stream, in-feed video, bumper | Bumper — up to 6 seconds; 15 seconds recommended for skippable in-stream |
| 9:16 | 1080×1920 | Shorts | 6–60 seconds in reach campaigns |
| 1:1 | 1080×1080 | Some campaign types | 6–60 seconds |

Non-skippable in-stream ads are usually 15 seconds or shorter; longer options exist in some cases, so check in your account.

One important difference with YouTube: the video is first uploaded to your YouTube channel (public or unlisted) and linked to the ad from there. A horizontal video won't work for Shorts — you need a separate vertical version. How Shorts differ from Reels is covered in our article on [YouTube Shorts](/en/blog/youtube-shorts-for-business/).

## Telegram: channel posts and Telegram Ads

Telegram channel posts have no fixed format: vertical, square and horizontal videos all display. To take up more space in the feed, 4:5 or 1:1 works well, but you can also post a 9:16 video made for Reels.

Since September 2024, Telegram Ads has allowed images and videos in sponsored messages ([Spot.uz](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2024/09/04/tg-ads-images/)). Check the exact size and file limits for video in the ad account itself — public sources quote different figures. The difference between Telegram Ads and buying posts in channels is explained in a [separate article](/en/blog/telegram-advertising/).

## Pre-export checklist

- The format matches the placement: Reels, Stories and TikTok — 9:16, feed — 4:5
- Width of at least 1080 pixels
- Text, price and logo inside the safe zone
- Subtitles added, the video makes sense with the sound off
- MP4, H.264, stereo AAC audio
- Fixed frame rate (for example, 30 fps)
- File size below the platform limit
- The file name includes format and length, e.g. `sofa_hook1_15s_9x16`
- Every placement checked in the Ads Manager preview

## Every format from one shoot

You don't need a separate shoot for each format. Plan ahead and you can get everything in one day.

1. **Shoot vertically.** 9:16 is the main format, so mount the phone or camera vertically. For a furniture store in Tashkent, say: sofa, salesperson and price tag in the centre of the frame.
2. **Keep the main action centred.** Then nothing is lost when you crop 9:16 down to 4:5 or 1:1.
3. **If you need a horizontal version, shoot it separately.** Making 16:9 out of 9:16 means losing most of the frame. One or two horizontal takes are enough for YouTube.
4. **Add text in the edit.** Don't bake captions in while shooting — each format needs its text placed separately.

How to set up light, sound and framing on a phone is covered in [shooting Reels on a phone](/en/blog/shooting-reels-on-a-phone/).

## 5 common mistakes

- **Horizontal video in Reels.** Two-thirds of the screen is black or blurred background.
- **Price at the bottom of the frame.** The ad copy and button cover it.
- **One video for every placement.** Meta's automatic cropping can cut off the logo or a face.
- **Low-quality export.** A video that looked sharp on the phone turns soft after compression.
- **Specs from an old article.** Platforms keep updating them — use the official help pages.

## Questions and answers

### If I can only make one format for every platform, which should it be?

9:16. It's the main format for Reels, Stories, TikTok and Shorts. You can crop a 4:5 version from it for feeds, as long as the main action is centred.

### For the Instagram feed, 1:1 or 4:5?

On a phone, 4:5 takes up more of the screen, so it's the usual choice for feeds. 1:1 works too, but looks smaller.

### Can I use Meta's safe zones for TikTok too?

As a starting point, yes: keep the top, bottom and edges clear. But TikTok's buttons are on the right, so leave a bigger margin on that side and check the ad in TikTok Ads Manager's preview.

### How do I shrink a video file without losing quality?

Export in H.264, keep the size at 1080×1920 and lower the bitrate. Unnecessary 4K and an excessive bitrate often bloat the file while doing almost nothing for the picture.

## Conclusion

Ad video sizes come down to a simple rule: 9:16 for phones, 4:5 for feeds, 16:9 for YouTube in-stream. Keep text and logo inside the safe zone, upload the right format to each placement and plan the shoot so one day gives you every version. And check the exact figures against the official help pages and your ad account each time.
