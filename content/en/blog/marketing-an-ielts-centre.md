In Tashkent there's an IELTS centre in every neighbourhood, and their ads all look alike: "our student scored 7.5", "experienced teachers", "guaranteed results". To parents and students they all seem the same, so the choice comes down to price. Here's how to make an IELTS centre stand out and keep a steady flow of students.

## Who makes the decision

| | Student (16–22) | Parent |
|---|---|---|
| What matters | The teacher, atmosphere, speed of progress | Trust, results, price, safety |
| Where | Instagram, Telegram, TikTok | Telegram, friends, Instagram |
| What they fear | Boring lessons, wasted time | Money down the drain |

Tailor content to both: for students, the teacher and atmosphere; for parents, the system and results.

## A free level test is your main entry offer

"Sign up for a course" is a big step. "Take a free level test and find out how long it'll take to reach your target" is a small, interesting one. The test result gives:

- the current level;
- an estimated path to the target score;
- an offer of a suitable group.

This is a classic entry offer — [entry offer](/en/blog/entry-offer/).

## Show results honestly

Results are the strongest proof, but they need to be presented properly:

- **"From – to"**: "from 5.0 to 6.5 in 4 months" is stronger than a final score alone.
- **The official results form**, with the student's consent.
- **A student video review** — [customer testimonial video](/en/blog/customer-testimonial-video/).
- **Overall statistics** — only real, verifiable ones.

A "100% guarantee" may be a false promise: exam results also depend on the student's effort. The advertising law prohibits misleading claims — [advertising law in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/advertising-law-uzbekistan/).

## The teacher is a brand

Students often choose a teacher rather than a centre. Show your teachers:

- the teacher's own score and experience;
- short video lessons: "3 mistakes in Writing Task 2";
- clips from classes.

The risk: if a strong teacher leaves, students may follow. So build the centre's own brand too — its method, system and atmosphere.

## Content ideas

- Speaking questions and sample answers.
- "How long does IELTS preparation take?" — an honest answer.
- Reels from mock exam day.
- A student's journey from the first test to exam day.
- For parents: "How to check your child's level".

## Advertising and seasonality

- **Season**: demand rises before university admissions and school graduation — plan the budget in advance — [marketing calendar 2027](/en/blog/marketing-calendar-2027-uzbekistan/).
- **Paid social**: target the area around your centre, with a lead form to book the level test — [lead form or messages](/en/blog/lead-form-or-messages/).
- **Retargeting** people who took the test but didn't enrol.
- **Search ads** on queries like "IELTS courses Chilanzar".

## Keeping students

Attracting a new student is expensive. To keep them:

- a monthly progress report for parents;
- mock exams with concrete results;
- a next step: SAT or another language after IELTS.

## A referral system

A successful student is the best advertising. Give a bonus to both the student and the friend they refer, under written rules.

## Questions and answers

### Should I show prices in ads?

Often yes — it reduces poor-fit enquiries. Prices must be in soum.

### Do online courses compete with offline centres?

Yes. An offline centre's advantage is the atmosphere, discipline and live Speaking practice. Show that.

### How many students is "normal"?

It depends on group size and the number of teachers. The key metrics are test-to-course conversion and student retention.

## Conclusion

What sets an IELTS centre apart is a free level test, honest "from – to" results, teacher branding and the centre's own system. Plan for the season in advance and keep students with progress reports. Level Marketing works in education too — see the Express School driving school case.
