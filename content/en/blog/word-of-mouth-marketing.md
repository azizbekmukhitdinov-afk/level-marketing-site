Many business owners say "half our customers come through people we know" and put it down to luck. In fact, word of mouth is a channel you can manage: once you know when and why people recommend, you can turn recommendations from chance into a system. This article covers the psychology of recommendations, ways to trigger them, referral mechanics and how to measure the results.

## Why word of mouth beats advertising

An ad is the brand talking about itself; a recommendation comes from someone close to the customer. A friend's advice carries the friend's reputation with it: by saying "I tried it, it's good", a person puts their own name on the line. That is why a referred lead usually asks fewer questions and decides faster.

The trouble is that most word of mouth is invisible. Recommendations happen in private conversations, family Telegram groups and phone calls. If you do not measure it, nobody can answer "where did this customer come from?", and nobody owns the channel.

## Why people recommend

A recommendation benefits the person making it, too. If you know their motives, it is easier to give them a reason.

| Motive | What it looks like | What a business can do |
|---|---|---|
| Helping someone close | "You need this craftsman, I've used him" | Offer a specific service or package that is easy to recommend |
| Looking good | "I found this great place first" | Show new things to regular customers first |
| An experience worth telling | "Imagine, they called me themselves the next day" | Create an unexpected but repeatable detail |
| Gratitude | "They helped me, now I'll help them" | Record the result together, then ask |
| Answering a question | Someone in a group asks "know a good dentist?" | Make your name easy to remember and find |

The last motive is often overlooked. Many recommendations are not the customer's own idea but an answer to someone else's question. At that moment you should be easy to recall and your contact quick to share: a short name, a Telegram or Instagram link, a maps profile.

## Four conditions for a recommendation

1. **The product or service is good.** No referral programme fixes a bad experience. Use NPS to check loyalty; see [what is NPS](/en/blog/what-is-nps/).
2. **There is a reason to talk.** People rarely talk about ordinary good service. You need a specific, memorable detail.
3. **Passing it on is easy.** The customer should not have to think about what to say or where to send people: a ready link, card or promo code.
4. **The timing is right.** Ask for a recommendation when the customer has felt the result.

## A reason to talk: ideas

The detail does not have to be expensive, but it has to happen every time. Then it becomes part of the brand. Below are ideas worth testing in your own business:

| Business | Detail |
|---|---|
| Clinic | The doctor calls the day after treatment to ask how the patient feels |
| Education centre | A monthly progress card for each student that parents share |
| Café | Remembering a regular guest's usual order |
| Furniture shop | A free adjustment visit from a technician after delivery |
| Car service | Before-and-after photos of the work sent in Telegram |

A good detail can be retold as a story. If it cannot be said in one sentence, it will not spread. We discuss why content spreads in our article on [viral marketing](/en/blog/viral-marketing/).

## When and how to ask for a recommendation

The best moments:

- after the first result (which is why [customer onboarding](/en/blog/customer-onboarding/) is tied to word of mouth);
- when the customer thanks or praises you;
- when they give 9–10 in an NPS survey;
- after a repeat purchase.

A bad moment is at the till, before the service has even started. The customer has not seen anything yet.

Keep the request simple and specific: "I can see you're happy with the result. If anyone you know needs this kind of service, send them this link: they get a gift on their first visit and you get a bonus." Note that we do not ask the customer for their friends' phone numbers. We give them a link, and the decision stays with the friend.

## Referral mechanics

| Mechanic | How it works | When it fits |
|---|---|---|
| One-sided | Only the referrer gets a bonus | Low-cost, frequently bought services |
| Two-sided | Both the referrer and the new customer get a gift | The best starting point for most businesses |
| Non-monetary | Status, a closed event, first access to new products | Premium segment, where offering money feels awkward |
| Tiered | A growing reward for 3, 5 and 10 referrals | Businesses with many active "ambassadors" |
| B2B partner fee | A percentage of the contract or a fixed amount | Agencies, service companies |

Two-sided mechanics often work better because the customer feels "I'm giving my friend a gift" rather than "I'm earning from this". For a detailed scheme with partners and affiliates, see [affiliate programmes](/en/blog/affiliate-program-for-business/).

## A worked example

Illustrative example: acquiring one customer through ads costs 300,000 soums. The business has 500 active customers. It launches a two-sided programme: a 50,000 soum bonus for the referrer and a 50,000 soum discount on the new customer's first purchase.

- Of the 500 customers, 100 share the link.
- The link reaches 300 friends, and 20% of them buy: **60 new customers**.
- Rewards: 60 × 100,000 = 6,000,000 soums. Cards and bot setup: 1,000,000 soums.
- Cost per customer: 7,000,000 ÷ 60 ≈ **117,000 soums**, versus 300,000 soums from ads.

The numbers are illustrative. What matters is the rule: if a referred customer costs less than a customer from ads, it makes sense to expand the programme. To calculate your cost of a customer from ads, see [customer acquisition cost (CAC)](/en/blog/customer-acquisition-cost-cac/).

## Making it easy to pass on

- **A personal link.** A Telegram bot can give each customer their own link in the form t.me/bot_name?start=code. According to Telegram's documentation, the parameter can be up to 64 characters and may use only Latin letters, digits, underscores and hyphens ([core.telegram.org](https://core.telegram.org/bots/features#deep-linking)). When a friend opens the link, the bot knows who referred them.
- **A personal promo code.** Handy offline: the code can be said at the till or over the phone; see [promo codes in marketing](/en/blog/promo-codes-marketing/).
- **A physical card.** Two "for a friend" cards with each order. Simple, but it works well for offline businesses.
- **Shareable content.** The customer's result, before-and-after photos, a certificate: things people want to post themselves. On customer content: [UGC content](/en/blog/ugc-content/).

## How to measure word of mouth

The first step is to make "How did you hear about us?" a required field in your CRM for every new customer and add a "recommended by someone" option. Where possible, record who made the recommendation: you will see your most active referrers and can thank them personally.

| Metric | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Referral share | New customers from referrals ÷ all new customers | The size of the channel |
| Sharing rate | Customers who shared a link or code ÷ active customers | Whether the programme engages people |
| Referral conversion | Referred leads who bought ÷ all referred leads | The quality of referrals |
| Cost per referred customer | Rewards and costs ÷ referred customers | Comparison with ads |
| NPS | % promoters − % detractors | Willingness to recommend |

The NPS question is specifically about how likely someone is to recommend you. Frederick Reichheld proposed this approach in a 2003 Harvard Business Review article ([hbr.org](https://hbr.org/2003/12/the-one-number-you-need-to-grow)).

## Honesty and the law

Word of mouth runs on trust, and trust is easy to break. Do not buy fake reviews and do not present a paid recommendation as an independent opinion: if there was a reward, say so openly. Do not collect friends' phone numbers from customers; give them a link instead. When handling customer data, follow Uzbekistan's Law "On Personal Data" No. ZRU-547, including its consent rules ([lex.uz](https://lex.uz/docs/4396419)). More on trust signals in [social proof in ads](/en/blog/social-proof-in-ads/).

## Common mistakes

- Not measuring word of mouth and assuming it runs "by itself".
- Launching a referral programme before improving the service.
- Asking for a recommendation at the point of sale, before any result.
- Complicated terms: if customers do not understand how to get the bonus, they will not share.
- Paying the bonus late or forgetting it, which turns a referrer into a detractor.
- Asking for friends' contact details.

## Questions and answers

### Can word of mouth replace advertising?

Rarely. Word of mouth depends on how many customers you already have: a small base means few recommendations. Ads bring in new customers, and word of mouth multiplies each of them. The best results come when they work together.

### How big should the bonus be?

Work out the ceiling from your unit economics: the total reward per referred customer should be lower than the cost of a customer from ads. As a starting point, take a small percentage of the average order and adjust based on results.

### How does a new business start word of mouth?

Be especially careful with your first 20–30 customers: they are your first referrers. Ask each of them for feedback, record their result and give the happy ones a personal link.

### How do I know word of mouth is working?

Track the referral share in your CRM month by month. If it is growing and referred customers cost less than customers from ads, the programme is working.

## Conclusion

Word of mouth is not luck but the result of good service, a reason to talk, easy sharing and the right moment. Understand why people recommend, start with a simple two-sided mechanic and record every new customer's source in your CRM.
