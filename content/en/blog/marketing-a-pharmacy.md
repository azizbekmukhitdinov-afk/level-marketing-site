A pharmacy is a special kind of business: the law restricts advertising for much of what it sells, and customers usually pick whichever is nearest and open right now. So pharmacy marketing starts not with discounts and banners but with being found, being trusted and being easy to choose. Here's how to grow a pharmacy's sales within the law.

## The law first: what you can't advertise

Uzbekistan has specific rules on advertising medicines and dietary supplements: prescription drugs can't be advertised to the general public, and promises of cures or claims like "doctors recommend" are restricted. More in [medicine and supplement advertising](/en/blog/medicine-supplement-advertising-uzbekistan/).

The practical takeaway: a pharmacy's advertising should focus on **the pharmacy itself** — address, hours, delivery, service — not on particular drugs.

## 1. Being found: maps come first

"Pharmacy near me" and "24-hour pharmacy" searches end on a map:

- a complete profile on Google Maps, Yandex Maps and 2GIS — [add your business to maps](/en/blog/add-your-business-to-maps/);
- accurate opening hours (including holidays);
- a photo of the entrance and a landmark;
- phone and Telegram;
- replies to reviews.

If you're open at night or 24 hours, say so clearly in your name and profile.

## 2. Online orders and delivery

- Answer "do you have this medicine?" quickly on Telegram — [Telegram Business features](/en/blog/telegram-business-features/);
- state the delivery area and times;
- follow the legal procedure for prescription drugs: if a prescription is needed, say so upfront.

## 3. The pharmacist is the main "salesperson"

Customers come in with questions. A pharmacist's advice is the foundation of trust:

- polite, clear explanations;
- referring complex cases to a doctor — which builds trust;
- a service standard: greeting, question, explanation, receipt — [customer service standards](/en/blog/customer-service-standards/).

## 4. Non-medicine categories: cosmetics and care

Pharmacy cosmetics, hygiene, baby products and vitamins (within the law) offer more room for content and advertising:

- useful posts like "Winter skincare in 4 steps";
- seasonal bundles;
- short Reels about new brands.

## 5. Regular customers

Customers with chronic conditions come every month. For them:

- a bonus or cashback programme — [loyalty programmes](/en/blog/loyalty-program/);
- a reminder service (with consent): "your medicine runs out in 3 days";
- advance reservations.

Health-related customer data is especially sensitive. Consent and protection are mandatory — [customer database and personal data](/en/blog/customer-database-personal-data-uzbekistan/).

## What to measure

- Calls and direction requests from maps.
- Telegram enquiries and delivery orders.
- Average order value and the share of regular customers.

## Questions and answers

### Can a pharmacy advertise on Instagram?

About the pharmacy itself, its service and non-medicine products, yes. Check the legal limits before advertising specific drugs.

### Should we compete on discounts?

Pharmacy margins are usually thin. Service, location and speed often beat discounts.

### How does marketing a chain differ from a single pharmacy?

A chain invests in its brand and a bonus programme; a single pharmacy invests in local visibility and its pharmacists' relationships with customers.

## Conclusion

Pharmacy marketing works within the law: visibility on maps, fast online replies, pharmacist advice, content in non-medicine categories and a programme for regular customers.
