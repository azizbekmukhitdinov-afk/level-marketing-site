**CTR** (click-through rate) shows what percentage of people who saw an ad clicked on it. It's one of the most-quoted numbers in any ads account: when a media buyer says "CTR is low", they usually mean the creative or offer isn't grabbing people. Here's what CTR is, how to calculate it and when to trust it.

## The formula

**CTR = clicks ÷ impressions × 100%**

An illustrative example: an ad was shown 20,000 times and clicked 300 times. CTR = 300 ÷ 20,000 × 100% = **1.5%**.

## What CTR shows

- **How interesting the creative and offer are**: did people stop and think it worth a click?
- **Audience fit**: ads shown to the wrong people get few clicks.
- **Creative fatigue**: if CTR falls over time, it's time to refresh the creative — [creative fatigue](/en/blog/creative-fatigue/).

## What CTR doesn't show

A high CTR isn't sales. An intriguing but misleading creative promising "free" or "a gift" can get lots of clicks and no leads. So always look at CTR alongside the next stage:

- click-to-lead conversion — [what is conversion rate](/en/blog/what-is-conversion-rate/);
- cost per lead — [cost per lead](/en/blog/cost-per-lead-cpl-how-to-lower/).

## What's a "good" CTR?

There's no universal benchmark: it depends on the platform, format, industry and objective. Search ads usually have a higher CTR than social ads, because people are actively searching. The best approach is to compare your own campaigns with each other.

## Causes of low CTR

- No hook in the first seconds — [Reels hooks](/en/blog/reels-hooks/).
- A vague or generic offer.
- An audience that's too broad or a poor fit.
- A fatigued creative, shown too often to the same people.
- In search, ad copy missing the words people searched for.

## How to raise CTR

1. Test 3–5 different hooks — [A/B testing ad creatives](/en/blog/ab-testing-ad-creatives/).
2. Put a concrete benefit and a number in the headline.
3. Use a clear call to action: "Get a price", "Free measurement".
4. Refine the audience or adapt the creative to it.
5. Refresh creatives regularly.

## Questions and answers

### How does CTR differ from conversion rate?

CTR is the share of viewers who clicked; conversion rate is the share of clickers who submitted a lead or bought.

### Which CTR should I watch in Meta?

The ads manager shows CTR for "all clicks" and for "link clicks". To judge traffic to your site or form, link-click CTR is more useful.

### High CTR but no leads — what now?

Check the page or form: does it match the ad's promise? — [a landing page that converts](/en/blog/landing-page-that-converts/).
