Treating every enquiry the same way is one of the most expensive habits a sales team can have. **Hot, warm and cold leads** are at very different stages of readiness to buy, and if you answer them all with one script at one speed, the hot ones cool down and the cold ones get annoyed. Here's how to tell the types apart, how to respond to each and how to build a simple lead scoring table.

## Types of leads: hot, warm and cold

A lead is a potential customer who has left contact details. The general idea is covered in [what is a lead](/en/blog/what-is-a-lead/). Here we focus on one question: how ready is this person to buy right now?

| Lead type | Who | Typical message | When they buy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hot | Has almost decided, asks something specific | "Can you deliver tomorrow?", "Can I pay by card?" | Today or this week |
| Warm | Interested, comparing options | "What models do you have?", "How long is the warranty?" | Within a few weeks |
| Cold | Just curious or clicked by accident | "How much?" and then silence, or no reply at all | Unknown, maybe never |

An important distinction: a lead's "temperature" and its quality are different things. Quality answers "is this person a fit for us?" — budget, location, need. Temperature answers "when?". A lead can be hot and still not a fit: they want to buy today but live in a region you don't deliver to.

Don't confuse this topic with cold calling either. A cold lead is someone who left their details themselves but isn't ready yet. A cold call is a first call to someone who doesn't know you at all.

## How to read a lead's temperature: 6 signals

Judge temperature by clear signals, not by a manager's gut feeling. Six signals usually do the job:

1. **Source.** Someone who arrives from a Google search for "buy a sofa Tashkent" is usually hotter. Someone who saw a Reel in their feed and sent a pre-filled lead form is often colder.
2. **First message.** Asks about a specific product, size or date — hot. A general question — warm. A single "?" or the word "price" — possibly cold.
3. **Type of question.** Questions about payment, delivery or available slots signal a purchase is close. "Who are you, how long have you been in business?" means trust is still being built.
4. **Timeline.** "We need it this week" — hot. "We start renovating in spring" — warm or cold.
5. **Actions.** Opened the pricing page, downloaded the catalogue, wrote again — temperature rises.
6. **Response to contact.** Picked up straight away and answered questions in detail — hotter. Silent for two days — colder.

You can collect some of these signals in the lead form itself. For example, add a "When do you need it?" question with "this week", "within a month" and "not sure yet". Which questions to put in a form is covered in [lead form questions](/en/blog/lead-form-questions/).

## A simple lead scoring table

Lead scoring means giving points for each signal and using the total to decide the lead type. You don't need a complex system: a table and one field in your CRM are enough.

**Illustrative example** for a furniture store in Tashkent:

| Signal | Points |
|---|---|
| Came from search or a referral | +2 |
| Asked about a specific product or size | +2 |
| Said "we need it this week / this month" | +3 |
| Asked about payment or delivery | +2 |
| Answered the first call | +1 |
| Said "not sure yet, just looking" | −2 |
| No reply for two days | −2 |

Result: **6 or more — hot**, **3–5 — warm**, **2 or less — cold**.

The points and thresholds are illustrative — adapt them to your business. After a month, check: how many leads marked hot actually bought? If there's little difference between hot and warm, the points need adjusting.

## Response speed and scripts for each lead type

| | Hot | Warm | Cold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Response time | Within 5 minutes during working hours | The same day | Within 24 hours |
| Who replies | Your most experienced manager | A manager | A manager or an automated sequence |
| Goal | Meeting, order, payment | Understand the need, agree the next step | Get permission and give value |
| Channel | Phone call | Call or Telegram | Telegram, SMS, email |

With hot leads, speed decides the outcome: they've almost certainly messaged your competitors too. Why speed affects sales so much is explained in [lead response time](/en/blog/lead-response-time/).

### Script for a hot lead

Short and specific: answer the question, propose the next step, set a time.

- "Hello, Dilnoza. This is Javohir from the furniture store. You asked about the corner sofa — the 240 cm model is in stock."
- "We can deliver tomorrow at 2 pm or 5 pm. Which suits you better?"
- "Send me your address on Telegram and I'll place the order right now."

A hot lead doesn't need the whole catalogue — they've already chosen.

### Script for a warm lead

The goal is to understand the need and agree on a specific next step.

- "Which room is the sofa for? How many people will sit on it?"
- "I'll send you three options with photos and prices. Can I call you on Thursday to hear which one you liked?"
- "If you like, come and see them in the showroom — we're open on Saturday."

It's natural for a warm lead to say "I'll think about it". What to do after that is in the [14-day follow-up plan](/en/blog/i-will-think-about-it-follow-up/).

### Script for a cold lead

No pressure. A short message, something useful and a request for permission.

- "Hi! You left a request on our sofa page. Do you need to choose now, or a bit later?"
- "If it's later, I can send you a short guide to choosing a sofa and our new arrivals from time to time. Is that OK?"

Trying to close a cold lead in one call usually loses them for good.

## Warming up cold leads: a 30-day plan

A cold lead isn't junk — they just aren't ready yet. If they've given you permission, send them something useful now and then.

| Day | What you send | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A short hello and a useful resource: a guide, a price list | Be remembered |
| 4 | A customer review or a before-and-after result | Trust |
| 10 | Answers to frequently asked questions | Remove objections |
| 18 | A new product or a seasonal offer | Revive interest |
| 30 | A short "Is this still relevant?" | Check temperature |

Every reply means re-scoring the lead. If there's still silence after 30 days, don't contact them often: a monthly update or retargeting ads are enough. Send messages during the day, not at night, and always offer a way to opt out.

## Lead temperature changes

A lead type isn't a permanent label. Watch for two situations:

- **A hot lead cools down.** If the reply comes late or a manager forgets a promised call, today's hot lead buys from a competitor tomorrow. That's why the hot list is checked every day.
- **A cold lead warms up.** Someone who said "not sure yet" three months ago writes again today — now they're hot. If the CRM keeps the old conversation, the manager picks up where they left off instead of starting from scratch.

A practical rule: update temperature after every contact, not just on the day the enquiry arrives.

## How to mark lead types in your CRM

You don't need an expensive system. Any CRM needs just three things — there's [a separate article on choosing a CRM for a small business](/en/blog/crm-for-small-business/).

1. **A "Temperature" field**: hot / warm / cold — or a scoring total.
2. **A next-contact date** on every lead, especially warm and cold ones.
3. **A weekly report**: how many leads of each type arrived and how many turned into sales.

Marketing needs this report too. If one ad campaign brings mostly cold leads and another brings hot ones, split the budget by the share of hot leads, not by cost per lead.

## Common mistakes

- **One script for everyone.** A long introduction for a hot lead and "pay today" for a cold one both cost sales.
- **Guessing temperature.** Each manager uses different criteria and the report can't be trusted.
- **Deleting cold leads.** In a few months they may turn hot.
- **Messaging cold leads every day.** Frequent messages without permission hurt your brand.
- **Never updating temperature.** A lead marked "hot" a month ago still sits at the top of the list.

## Questions and answers

### Are there only three types of leads?

The main split is hot, warm and cold. Some companies add a fourth type — "not a fit": wrong number, a region you don't serve. That's about quality rather than temperature, but it's useful to show separately in reports.

### How can I get more hot leads from ads?

Make enquiring a little harder: add "when do you need it" and "budget" questions to the form, show price and terms openly in the ad, and test search ads. You may get fewer leads, but the share of hot ones usually goes up.

### Should lead scoring be manual or automatic?

Start manually: the manager adds points after the conversation. After a month or two you'll see which signals actually predict a purchase, and then you can set up automatic scoring based on form answers and source.

### Should I call a cold lead?

One call or message — yes, because they left their details themselves. But if there's no answer, don't keep calling; switch to Telegram or SMS and send something useful. Avoid calling late in the evening or early in the morning.

## Conclusion

Hot, warm and cold leads are three levels of readiness to buy. Tell them apart by signals and a simple scoring table, not by feel. Reply to hot leads within minutes and briefly, to warm leads by clarifying the need, and to cold leads without pressure and with something useful. Update temperature after every contact and judge your ads by the share of hot leads they bring.
