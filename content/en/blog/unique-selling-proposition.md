If customers can't see the difference between you and a competitor, they compare only price — and pick the cheapest. A **unique selling proposition (USP)** is one concrete reason to choose you. Here is how to find your USP, phrase it and where to use it.

## 3 signs of a good USP

1. **It matters to the customer** — it solves their problem or removes a fear.
2. **Competitors don't have it**, or don't talk about it.
3. **It can be proven** — with a number, a guarantee, a process or a result.

"Quality, fast and affordable" isn't a USP: everyone says it and nobody proves it.

## How to find your USP: 4 steps

1. **List customers' pains.** Ask your sales team: which questions and objections come up most? Why do people leave without buying?
2. **Analyse competitors.** What do they promise, and what do they leave out? More in our [competitor analysis](/en/blog/competitor-analysis/) article.
3. **List your strengths:** speed, guarantees, specialisation, process, payment model, service.
4. **Find the overlap** — something customers care about, competitors lack and you genuinely have.

## Formulas

- "[Result] in [time] — or [guarantee]".
- "Only we [unique feature] for [audience]".
- "[Product] for [segment] with [main benefit]".

## Examples

| Business | USP |
|---|---|
| Furniture | "A sofa in your size in 10 days — if we're late, delivery is free" |
| Clinic | "The price of every treatment stage fixed in the contract upfront" |
| Education centre | "Don't like it in the first two weeks? We refund you" |
| Real estate | "A 3-year interest-free instalment plan" |

The last example comes from our [Global Avenue case](/en/blog/case-global-avenue/), where that offer became the core of the campaign. In the [Chorsu Gold Center](/en/blog/case-chorsu-gold-center/) case, a 5-year interest-free instalment plan did the job.

Important: a USP must be true and delivered. A broken promise hurts a brand more than no advertising at all.

## Where to use your USP

- In your Instagram bio and pinned posts.
- In the first line of ads and the first seconds of videos.
- On the first screen of your website or landing page.
- In your sales script — managers should state it in every conversation.

## How to test a USP

A USP that looks good in the office may not work in the market. Test it before a wide launch:

1. **Ask customers** — ask 5–10 recent buyers why they chose you. Their words often turn out to be the best wording.
2. **Test it in ads** — run creatives with two different USPs to the same audience and compare cost per lead; see [A/B testing ad creatives](/en/blog/ab-testing-ad-creatives/) for how.
3. **Check your managers** — can each of them state the USP the same way, in one sentence?
4. **Watch the numbers** — have conversion and the share of "too expensive" objections changed?

## Ways to prove a USP

- **Specific numbers** — deadlines, years of experience, projects completed.
- **A guarantee in the contract** — stronger than a verbal promise.
- **Showing the process** — on video or in your office.
- **Reviews and cases** — in real customers' words.
- **Certificates and licences** — especially in healthcare and education.

Keep the proof next to the promise: if an ad makes the promise, the proof should be in that ad too.

## Common mistakes

- A USP about the company rather than the customer: "We've been on the market since 2015".
- Five "unique" advantages at once.
- Copying a competitor's offer.
- A promise you can't keep.
- The USP lives only on the website and managers never say it; how to use it in conversation is covered in [Sales script](/en/blog/sales-script/).

## Questions and answers

### Can one business have several USPs?

Have one main one. Different segments or product lines can have separate offers, but every ad and every page should carry one main promise.

### What if competitors offer the same thing?

Find another angle: process, guarantee or specialisation. Or prove the same promise more convincingly than others — customers remember the company that showed proof.

### When should you update your USP?

When competitors start repeating it, the market changes or you gain a new strength. Once a year, review customer questions and competitors' offers.

### Where does a small business or a new company find its USP?

Until you have big numbers and lots of cases, turn your small size into an advantage: the owner's personal involvement, fast decisions, flexibility, a narrow specialisation. For example, "the owner personally checks every order" — a large competitor can't promise that. Just make sure it's true and happens with every order. And start collecting your first customers' reviews right away — they'll become your next proof.

## Conclusion

A USP is your way out of price wars. Start with customers' pains, study competitors and choose one specific promise you can prove. For the other parts of a brand, see [what a brand is](/en/blog/what-is-a-brand/).
