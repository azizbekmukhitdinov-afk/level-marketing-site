Fitness club marketing has two separate jobs: get a new member into the gym, and make sure they are still coming three months later. Many gyms spend only on the first — ads in January and spring, discounted memberships — and then members quietly disappear. This article gives a plan for a gym, fitness club or studio (yoga, pilates, boxing) in Tashkent that handles both.

![Why people don't sign up](/img/blog/illus/fitnes-klub-marketing-1.jpg 1200x675)

## Why people don't sign up

The main competitor isn't another gym — it's the person's own doubts:

| Doubt | Marketing's answer |
|---|---|
| "I'll feel awkward there" | Real members, beginner groups, a supportive atmosphere |
| "It won't work for me" | A plan with a trainer, measurements, member stories (with consent) |
| "I have no time" | A nearby location, opening hours, short session formats |
| "It's expensive and I'll quit" | A trial session, monthly payment, the option to freeze |

When your ads and content answer these doubts, they work better than generic talk about "state-of-the-art equipment".

## Step 1. Who you serve

"A gym for everyone" is the weakest position. Specific segments:

- office workers nearby — morning and lunchtime sessions;
- mothers — a kids' room or a women-only area;
- beginners — small groups, trainer supervision;
- athletes — equipment, free weights, late hours.

Each segment gets its own ads and offer. How to define segments — [target audience and customer persona](/en/blog/target-audience-and-customer-persona/).

## Step 2. The trial session is the main entrance

The offer that works best in fitness is a free or nominally priced trial session with a trainer. But not just "come for free":

1. **Introduction:** brief questions about goals, experience, health.
2. **Workout:** pitched at the person's level — don't exhaust them.
3. **Plan:** the trainer outlines a 4–8 week direction and when the first results will show.
4. **Offer:** a specific membership and a small bonus for signing the same day (for example, an extra personal session).

Track it: how many came to a trial and how many bought a membership. That number matters even more than the ads.

## Step 3. Trainers are your strongest content

People join a trainer and an atmosphere, not a room. Content rubrics:

| Rubric | Example |
|---|---|
| Trainer tip | "3 exercises that are kind to your knees", "A 10-minute desk warm-up" |
| Inside a session | 15 seconds of a group class, music and energy |
| Member stories | A result and how it was achieved (with consent, careful with before/after) |
| The gym | Equipment, changing rooms, cleanliness, parking |
| Timetable | This week's group classes in Stories |

To prepare trainers for the camera, see [Reels script templates](/en/blog/reels-script-templates/) and [expert interview videos](/en/blog/expert-interview-videos/).

A caution: with body-related content, sharp before/after shots and promises like "lose 15 kg in 30 days" create doubt rather than trust, and may also break platform rules.

## Step 4. Maps and radius

Distance decides gym choice: people go to a gym 10–15 minutes from home or work. So:

- a complete Google and Yandex Maps listing: categories (gym, fitness, yoga studio), hours, photos, prices, reviews. More in [adding your business to maps](/en/blog/add-your-business-to-maps/);
- a 2–5 km radius for ads, around office centres and residential areas.

![Step 5. Paid ads and seasons](/img/blog/illus/fitnes-klub-marketing-2.jpg 1200x675)

## Step 5. Paid ads and seasons

Fitness demand is seasonal: January (New Year resolutions), spring (getting ready for summer) and September (after the holidays) are the busiest periods. Shift budget into those months and prepare creatives in advance.

Ad settings:

- **Objective:** trial session sign-ups (form or messages);
- **Creative:** the trainer's face and voice, the gym atmosphere, a clear offer: "Trial session with a trainer — illustrative example: free, 60 minutes";
- **Retargeting:** timetable and prices for people who watched videos or visited the profile.

How to set up Instagram ads — [step-by-step guide](/en/blog/how-to-set-up-instagram-ads/).

## Step 6. Retention is where the profit is

Most fitness revenue comes from people who renew. If a new member doesn't form a habit in the first 4–6 weeks, they leave. A retention system:

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| Week 1 | An intro session with a trainer, write down the goal |
| Weeks 2–3 | A personal message to anyone who skipped: "We miss you — shall I send the timetable?" |
| Week 4 | First measurements, show the progress |
| Month 2 | An invitation to a group class or challenge |
| 7 days before the membership ends | A renewal offer, a freeze option if needed |

A members' Telegram group — timetable, challenges, trainer tips — builds a sense of community and reduces churn. The value of repeat business — [repeat sales and LTV](/en/blog/repeat-sales-and-ltv/).

## Step 7. Referrals — the cheapest new member

A bonus for the member who brings a friend, and for the friend: an extra week or a personal session. People often join gyms in pairs — encourage it. Corporate memberships for nearby offices also bring a steady flow.

## What to measure

- trial sign-ups and attendance;
- trial-to-membership conversion;
- cost of one new member (ads + discounts);
- share still active after 3 months;
- membership renewal rate.

**Illustrative example:** ads bring 120 trial sign-ups a month, 70 attend, 25 buy a membership. Raise attendance from 70 to 90 (reminders and fast replies) and the same budget yields about 32 members.

![Common mistakes](/img/blog/illus/fitnes-klub-marketing-3.jpg 1200x675)

## Common mistakes

- Deep discounts only — when the discount ends, members leave too.
- No concrete offer at the end of the trial session.
- Trainers invisible in content; only photos of equipment.
- Nobody contacts members who stop coming.
- Members only learn their membership has expired when they show up.

## Questions and answers

### Which ads work best for a fitness club?

Videos featuring a trainer plus a trial session offer, within the gym's radius. Increase budget in peak seasons.

### Should I give discounts?

Not a large permanent discount — add value instead: a trial session, a first-month plan with a trainer, a freeze option. Members who came for a discount are often the first to leave.

### How can a small studio compete with a big gym?

Through specialisation and attention: small groups, a personal approach, a clear focus (for example, women-only pilates). Show that difference in your content.

### What retention rate is good?

It depends on format and price. What matters is tracking your own number and improving it month by month. Improving retention is cheaper than acquiring new members.

## Conclusion

Fitness club marketing is a system for winning members through trial sessions and keeping them through the first weeks. Trainer content builds trust, ads matched to radius and season bring the flow, and retention decides profit.
