A manufacturer's marketing is more complex than a retailer's or a service business's, because it has several "customers": a distributor or dealer buys the product, a shop puts it on the shelf, and the end buyer picks it from the shelf. Each needs a different argument. Many manufacturers only work on the first — price and terms — and never build a brand for the end buyer. The product then sits "nameless" on the shelf and can't escape price competition. This article sets out a marketing system for a manufacturer in Uzbekistan.

![A manufacturer's three audiences](/img/blog/illus/ishlab-chiqaruvchi-marketing-1.jpg 1200x675)

## A manufacturer's three audiences

| Audience | What they look at | Marketing tool |
|---|---|---|
| Distributor, dealer | Margin, turnover, support | Commercial proposal, terms, marketing support |
| Shop, pharmacy, point of sale | Does it sell, is there demand | Trade materials, promotions, brand awareness |
| End buyer | Quality, trust, price, convenience | Brand, packaging, content, ads, reviews |
| Specialist (if relevant) | Effectiveness, evidence | Expert materials, presentations |

When end buyers know and ask for the brand, negotiations with dealers and shops get easier: products with demand get listed.

## Step 1. Brand and packaging

At the shelf, buyers choose in 2–3 seconds. The brand and packaging should answer:

- what it is and who it's for — at a glance;
- how it differs from competitors;
- why it can be trusted — certification, ingredients, where it's made.

The identity minimum — [visual identity for small business](/en/blog/visual-identity-for-small-business/); brand basics — [what is a brand](/en/blog/what-is-a-brand/); the offer — [unique selling proposition](/en/blog/unique-selling-proposition/).

## Step 2. Materials for dealers and shops

For distributors and shop owners, marketing means help with selling:

- **A commercial proposal** — product, margin, minimum order, delivery, payment terms, marketing support.
- **Catalogue and price list** — kept up to date, as PDF and on Telegram.
- **Trade materials** — shelf stickers, wobblers, stand designs.
- **Short sales staff training** — how to recommend the product, the 3 key arguments.
- **Co-op advertising** — joint ads in the dealer's region and channels.

These materials speed up B2B sales. More on the B2B approach — [B2B marketing](/en/blog/b2b-marketing-uzbekistan/).

## Step 3. Content for the end buyer

A manufacturer's strongest content is the production itself:

| Format | Example |
|---|---|
| Inside the factory | Raw materials, the line, quality control |
| How to use the product | Video instructions, recipes, tips |
| Comparison | Your product versus a standard alternative — with a visible difference |
| Expert | A technologist or specialist answers questions |
| Buyers | Reviews and usage photos (with consent) |

If you produce locally, tell that story: the plant, the team, quality standards. Expert video formats — [expert interview videos](/en/blog/expert-interview-videos/); product video — [product video mistakes](/en/blog/product-video-mistakes/).

## Step 4. A pharmaceutical example — consumer and specialist at once

Our pharmaceutical cases show the multi-audience approach well. For the Loxen pain relief patch and the Grippomiks antiviral powder, we created the identity, showed consumers the product's benefits on video, filmed expert videos with doctors, ran promotions for medical representatives and sent commercial proposals to major pharmacy chains. In other words, for one product we worked with the consumer, the specialist and the retail chain in parallel. Details — [pharmaceutical cases](/en/blog/case-loxen-grippomiks-pharma/).

Advertising medicines and other special goods is subject to specific legal requirements — [advertising law in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/advertising-law-uzbekistan/).

## Step 5. Digital channels

- **Instagram and Telegram** — brand awareness, production content, promotions, a "where to buy" map.
- **Paid ads** — new product launches, seasonal promotions; the goal is awareness and driving people to points of sale.
- **Search** — "[product] manufacturer", "[product] wholesale" — for dealers and B2B buyers.
- **Marketplaces** — your own brand store; how to build it — [building a brand on Uzum Market](/en/blog/building-a-brand-on-uzum-market/).

Choosing a platform — [Google Ads vs Meta Ads](/en/blog/google-ads-vs-meta-ads/).

## Step 6. Trade shows and the dealer network

Industry exhibitions are the fastest way to meet new dealers and big buyers. Prepare in advance: book meetings, bring samples, a commercial proposal and a way to collect contacts. Within 48 hours of the show, message every contact and log them in the CRM — [CRM for small business](/en/blog/crm-for-small-business/).

## What to measure

- new dealers and points of sale;
- sell-out at points of sale — where possible;
- brand name searches and direct enquiries;
- marketplace sales and ratings;
- first-month sales of a new product launch.

![Common mistakes](/img/blog/illus/ishlab-chiqaruvchi-marketing-3.jpg 1200x675)

## Common mistakes

- Working with dealers on price and discounts alone, never building a brand.
- Packaging that looks like competitors'.
- Giving dealers no materials or training — the product sits "silent" on the shelf.
- Not showing production — the strongest trust factor goes unused.
- Judging marketing only by volume shipped to dealers.

## Questions and answers

### Does a manufacturer need Instagram if it only sells to dealers?

Yes. When end buyers know and ask for the brand, dealers and shops take the product more readily. Instagram is a trust signal for dealers too.

### Who should do the dealers' marketing?

Both sides. The manufacturer provides the brand, materials and co-op ads; the dealer handles sales and local promotions in its region.

### Won't a marketplace compete with dealers?

With a consistent pricing policy, a marketplace becomes an extra channel and raises brand awareness. Agree pricing with dealers in advance.

### When will results show?

Dealer work takes several months; brand awareness takes longer still. In the first months, track new points of sale and brand enquiries.

## Conclusion

Marketing for manufacturers means working three audiences in parallel: profitable terms and materials for dealers, demand and trade support for points of sale, and a brand, packaging and trustworthy content for end buyers. In our pharmaceutical cases this approach covered consumers, specialists and pharmacy chains at the same time. Level Marketing creates identities, production and expert videos for manufacturers and ties ads and trade materials into one strategy.
