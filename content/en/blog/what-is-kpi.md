"We need KPIs", "KPIs for staff" — common phrases among business owners. Yet many companies either have no KPIs at all or have so many that nobody tracks them. Here's what a KPI is, how to choose good ones and which metrics really matter for marketing and sales.

## A simple definition

A KPI (key performance indicator) is **one of a handful of important numbers** that show how close you are to your goal.

The key word is "key". A company has hundreds of numbers; KPIs are the 3–7 you actually need for decisions.

## Signs of a good KPI

| Sign | Meaning | Bad example | Good example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Specific | Clear what's measured | "Improve marketing" | "Number of sales per month" |
| Measurable | Expressed in numbers | "More trust" | "Number of reviews and rating" |
| Controllable | The owner can influence it | Ad prices for a salesperson | Response speed for a salesperson |
| Time-bound | By when | "More leads" | "200 leads in December" |
| Business-linked | Affects money | Number of likes | Cost per sale |

## Key marketing and sales KPIs

| Area | KPI | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Ads | Cost per lead (CPL) | Ad efficiency — [cost per lead](/en/blog/cost-per-lead-cpl-how-to-lower/) |
| Ads | Cost per sale | How ads turn into sales |
| Sales | Lead-to-sale rate | Sales team performance — [what is conversion rate](/en/blog/what-is-conversion-rate/) |
| Sales | First response time | Speed — [lead response time](/en/blog/lead-response-time/) |
| Finance | Customer acquisition cost (CAC) | What a customer costs — [CAC](/en/blog/customer-acquisition-cost-cac/) |
| Finance | ROMI | Marketing profitability — [ROMI](/en/blog/how-to-calculate-romi/) |
| Customers | Share of repeat purchases | Whether customers return — [repeat sales and LTV](/en/blog/repeat-sales-and-ltv/) |
| SMM | DM enquiries | Whether content turns into action |

You don't need to track everything at once. A small business is fine with 4–5: number of leads, cost per lead, lead-to-sale rate, number of sales and cost per sale.

## Staff KPIs

A staff member's KPI should be tied to something they **can influence**:

| Role | KPI (example) |
|---|---|
| Salesperson | Response time, lead-to-sale rate, sales value |
| SMM manager | Content plan delivery, DM enquiries, response time |
| Media buyer | Cost per lead, share of qualified leads, cost per sale |
| Administrator | Response time, number of reviews |

A common mistake is giving a salesperson only a "number of sales" KPI: they'll cling to easy leads and drop hard ones. More in [sales rep conversion](/en/blog/sales-rep-conversion/).

## A KPI table: illustrative example

| KPI | Plan | Actual | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leads | 200 | 180 | Below plan |
| Cost per lead | 50,000 soum | 48,000 soum | Good |
| Lead-to-sale | 10% | 8% | Low — check sales |
| Sales | 20 | 14 | Low |
| Cost per sale | 500,000 soum | 617,000 soum | Low |

The table shows the problem immediately: ads are working well; the issue is at the sales stage. That's the main value of KPIs — showing where to work. The monthly review format — [the monthly marketing meeting](/en/blog/monthly-marketing-meeting/).

## Common mistakes

- Too many KPIs — none get tracked.
- "Vanity" KPIs: likes, followers, reach — not linked to money.
- A KPI the employee can't influence.
- KPIs exist but nobody checks them weekly or monthly.
- KPIs used only to punish — staff start hiding numbers.

## Questions and answers

### What's the difference between a KPI and a goal?

A goal is where you want to get ("30 sales in December"). KPIs are the indicators that show whether you're on the right track (leads, conversion).

### How many KPIs should there be?

Three to seven at company level, two to four per employee.

### How often should KPIs be checked?

Sales and ad KPIs weekly; overall business KPIs monthly.

### Does a small business need KPIs?

Yes — especially a small one, where every soum counts. A simple table is enough.

## Conclusion

KPIs are a few key numbers that show how close you are to your goal. They must be specific, measurable, controllable by the person responsible and tied to money. In marketing and sales, the essentials are cost per lead, conversion and cost per sale.
