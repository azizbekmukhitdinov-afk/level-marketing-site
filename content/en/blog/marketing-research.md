"Marketing research" makes many people think of expensive reports commissioned by big companies. In reality, the most useful research for a small business is cheap and fast: ten conversations with customers, a look at search demand, an analysis of your sales data. The goal isn't a report — it's a concrete decision. Here's how to run marketing research.

## Which questions research answers

Define the question first — research without a question wastes time:

- Who are our customers, and why do they choose us?
- What words do they use to describe their problem?
- Which channel do they find us through?
- Why do some of them not buy?
- Is there demand for a new product or branch?

## 1. Customer interviews: the most valuable method

Ten to fifteen conversations often yield more insight than 500 questionnaires. Talk to:

- recent buyers — why you specifically;
- people who looked but didn't buy — what stopped them;
- customers who left — why they left.

Questions (open, not closed):

1. How did you solve this problem before you found us?
2. What mattered most when you chose?
3. Which alternatives did you consider?
4. What made you hesitate?
5. How would you describe us to a friend?

Customers' own words from these answers are ready-made ad copy. More in [target audience and customer persona](/en/blog/target-audience-and-customer-persona/).

## 2. Surveys

After interviews — to test hypotheses with numbers. The rules:

- 5–10 questions, no more than 3 minutes;
- one question, one topic;
- no leading questions: not "Did you enjoy our excellent service?";
- run it via Google Forms or a Telegram bot.

Collecting customer opinions — [collecting customer feedback](/en/blog/collecting-customer-feedback/).

## 3. Search demand

What people search for is free research:

- **Google Trends** — interest over time and seasonality;
- **Google autocomplete** — the words people search with;
- **Yandex Wordstat** — query volumes (especially useful for Russian-language searches).

Note the difference between Latin and Cyrillic spelling — [Uzbek search: Latin and Cyrillic](/en/blog/uzbek-search-latin-cyrillic/).

## 4. Competitors

What they offer, at what price, in which channels, and what customers write in their reviews. Reviews are especially valuable: whatever competitors' customers complain about is your opportunity. More in [competitor analysis](/en/blog/competitor-analysis/). Speeding it up with AI — [AI competitor analysis](/en/blog/ai-competitor-analysis/).

## 5. Your own data

The most overlooked source:

- the CRM: where leads come from and which turn into sales;
- sales: which product, which day, what average order value;
- salespeople: the most frequent questions and objections;
- reviews and DM conversations.

Analysing reviews with AI — [AI review analysis](/en/blog/ai-review-analysis/).

## Turning findings into decisions

At the end of the research, write down answers to three questions:

1. **What did we learn?** — 3–5 key findings.
2. **What will we change?** — the offer, copy, channel or price.
3. **How will we test it?** — a test and a metric.

Add the findings to your marketing plan — [one-page marketing strategy](/en/blog/one-page-marketing-strategy/).

## Common mistakes

- Research without a question.
- Asking only friends and family.
- Leading questions designed to "hear" the desired answer.
- Ignoring the gap between what people *say* and what they *do*.
- Writing the report and changing nothing.

## Questions and answers

### How often should we do research?

Before any big decision (a new product, branch or rebrand), and a short version at least once a year.

### Will customers agree to an interview?

Most will, if it's short and you offer a small thank-you.

### What about personal data?

Get consent and store the data securely — [customer database and personal data](/en/blog/customer-database-personal-data-uzbekistan/).

## Conclusion

For a small business, marketing research isn't an expensive report but a fast answer to a specific question: customer interviews, a short survey, search demand, competitors and your own data.
