Short vertical series — episodes of 1–2 minutes that end with "to be continued" — have grown into a format with dedicated apps and millions of viewers. Brands have picked up the idea and are making their own mini-series on Reels and TikTok. The reason is simple: people come back to find out what happens next, and they visit the profile. Here's how to make a mini-series for a business.

## Why series work

- **A habit of returning**: "Episode 2 tomorrow" — people look up the profile themselves.
- **A reason to follow**: so they don't miss the next part.
- **A deeper connection**: getting to know a character builds trust.
- **Easier content planning**: one idea becomes 5–10 videos.

## 4 kinds of series for business

1. **A real project series**: "The journey of one kitchen" — from measurement to installation, one stage per episode — [construction progress videos](/en/blog/construction-progress-videos/).
2. **A customer story**: problem → decision → process → result, with the customer's consent.
3. **A scripted micro-drama**: invented characters, a funny or relatable situation, with the product appearing naturally.
4. **A challenge or experiment**: "In 30 days…", "We tested…"

## A 5-episode script template (illustrative example: a furniture store)

| Episode | Content | Cliffhanger |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A young family is tired of their old kitchen | "But the budget is only…" |
| 2 | Looking at options, hesitating | "And then the designer asked one question…" |
| 3 | Measurement and the design | "The design turned up one problem…" |
| 4 | Production and the solution | "What happened on installation day?" |
| 5 | The result and the family's reaction | The offer: "A free measurement for your kitchen" |

Script structure — [Reels script templates](/en/blog/reels-script-templates/).

## The rules

- **Each episode must make sense alone**: a new viewer might start at episode 3. Open with one line on what happened before.
- **A strong hook**: every episode grabs attention in the first seconds — [Reels hooks](/en/blog/reels-hooks/).
- **A clear schedule**: for example, every Tuesday and Friday.
- **Numbering**: "Kitchen story | episode 2" in the title and cover.
- **A story, not an ad**: the product helps the hero; it isn't the hero.

## A shoot plan

Shoot all the episodes in 1–2 days so the characters, location and lighting stay consistent. If actors or customers take part, get written consent — [photo and video consent for advertising](/en/blog/photo-video-consent-for-advertising/). Lots of material from one shoot — [one shoot, 10 ad creatives](/en/blog/one-shoot-10-ad-creatives/).

## Measuring results

- How well views hold from episode 1 to episode 5.
- Profile visits and new followers during the series.
- Leads from the offer in the final episode.

Strong episodes can be used in ads — especially the first and last.

## Questions and answers

### Do I need professional actors?

Not necessarily. Real staff and customers often come across as more convincing.

### How many episodes should there be?

Three to five to start. If the series works, make a second season.

### Can I advertise the series?

Yes — advertise episode 1 and send viewers to your profile for the rest — [types of video ads](/en/blog/types-of-video-ads/).

## Conclusion

A mini-series brings people back, gives them a reason to follow and introduces your brand through a story. Start with a real project or customer story, make each episode understandable on its own, end with "to be continued" and post on a clear schedule.
