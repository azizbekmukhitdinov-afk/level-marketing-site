A Telegram channel poll is the cheapest way to hear from subscribers in one tap. Yet in many channels it turns into a toy like "Which colour is prettier?" and leads to no decision. Here are the poll types, 7 working ideas for a business channel, and how to turn the results into a content plan, product range and sales.

![Telegram channel polls: the types](/img/blog/illus/telegram-kanalda-sorovnoma-1.jpg 1200x675)

## Telegram channel polls: the types

Telegram lets you post polls in groups and channels. Since the [Polls 2.0 update](https://telegram.org/blog/polls-2-0-vmq) in 2020 there are three key settings: visible votes, multiple answers and quiz mode. Anonymous polls are still available.

| Type | How it works | When a business needs it |
|---|---|---|
| Anonymous | Nobody sees who voted for what | Honest feedback on price, quality, service |
| Visible votes | Everyone sees who picked which option | A small closed group: regular clients, dealers |
| Multiple answers | People can tick 2–3 options at once | "What matters to you?" questions |
| Quiz | One correct answer, an explanation on a wrong one | Product education, a playful format |

For a channel, the default choice is an anonymous poll: when names are visible, many people simply don't vote. Telegram's [developer documentation](https://core.telegram.org/api/poll) also describes settings such as closing a poll early, shuffling options and hiding results until the poll closes. Which of them you see in the app depends on your Telegram version, so check the poll creation screen.

## 7 poll ideas for a business channel

A good poll answers a **decision**, not just a question. Before posting, say it out loud: "If A wins, I'll do X; if B wins, I'll do Y." If nothing changes either way, you don't need the poll.

### 1. Choosing the next post topic

"What should we write about next week?" with 3–4 topics. The winner goes into the content plan. For a furniture store channel in Tashkent: "Furnishing a small kitchen", "Choosing sofa fabric", "How delivery works". It is the simplest and most useful format: subscribers read a post they ordered themselves.

### 2. Interest in a new product or service

A training centre: "Which course should we open in December?" — evening IELTS, maths for university applicants, English for kids. The result is a first demand signal, not an order. If interest is strong, the next step is a post with a sign-up button that leads to your bot ([how to make a post with a button](/en/blog/telegram-channel-post-with-button/)).

### 3. What stops people from buying

"What worries you most before buying?" — price, delivery time, quality, warranty. Allow multiple answers. The most common objection is a ready topic for a week of content. More on this: [content from objections](/en/blog/content-from-customer-objections/).

### 4. Convenient time and channel

For a restaurant or clinic: "When is it easiest for you to book?", "How do you prefer to contact us — a call, Telegram or the website?". The answer helps you adjust operator shifts, bot settings or the timing of a promotion.

### 5. Quiz: product knowledge

"Which mattress is firmer for your back?" or "How often should you have a professional dental cleaning?". Add a short explanation to the correct answer. A quiz teaches customers and shows your expertise. Keep it neither too easy nor a trick question: if everyone gets it wrong, subscribers feel awkward.

### 6. Testing price and package formats

"What suits you better: a one-off payment, 3-month instalments or a monthly subscription?". This doesn't set your price; it shows which format subscribers understand. Don't replace your pricing policy with a poll.

### 7. Quick rating after a service

"How was your last order?" — excellent, good, average, poor. In a channel this only shows the overall mood. Working with each customer needs a separate system, described in [how to collect customer feedback](/en/blog/collecting-customer-feedback/).

## How to write the question: 6 rules

1. **One question, one idea.** "Are you happy with the price and quality?" is two questions. Split them.
2. **2–5 answer options.** With too many, people scroll past without reading.
3. **Options must not overlap.** "Cheap" and "Affordable" mean the same thing. Use precise words or ranges.
4. **An "Other / no answer" option.** Otherwise people with no fitting answer tap a random one and skew the result.
5. **A short post above the poll.** In 2–3 lines explain why you ask: "We're preparing New Year gift sets and want to know which ones you need."
6. **State a deadline.** "Voting closes on Friday" stops people from putting it off. When the time is up, stop the poll so the result is fixed.

On language: if your channel is bilingual, don't post two separate polls in two languages — the votes split. Use your main language, or short bilingual options in a single poll.

## How to read the results

A poll is not research. The people who vote are the most active part of your channel, not your whole customer base. So read the result through three numbers:

| Metric | Where to find it | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Post views | The eye icon under the post | How many people saw the question |
| Votes | Under the poll | How many people answered |
| Votes as a share of views | Votes ÷ views | How interesting the topic is |

*Illustrative example:* a post got 1,200 views and 96 votes — a share of 8%. The next poll got 33 votes from 1,100 views — 3%. So the second topic interests subscribers less, and its result deserves less trust.

Three rules:

- **Few votes means a hypothesis, not a conclusion.** You don't change your range on 20–30 votes; you run a small test.
- **A small gap is a tie.** 52% vs 48% means "both are needed".
- **Compare words with actions.** Tapping "yes, interested" is easy. Real demand is a message to the bot, a sign-up and a payment.

## Turning results into a content plan and sales

When a poll ends, don't hide the result. When subscribers see their opinion was counted, they vote again next time.

| Result | Action | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| A topic won | A post or video on it, linking back to the poll | Within 3–7 days |
| Interest in a new course | Pre-registration in the bot with a seat limit | One week |
| An objection surfaced | A post answering it + add it to the sales script | One week |
| A convenient time found | Change booking or contact hours | Next month |
| Many wrong quiz answers | An explainer post or Reel on the topic | Next week |

The results post can be simple (illustrative example): "You chose — 61% asked for 'Small kitchen'. Here's the guide we promised." It shows the channel is a two-way conversation. Give polls one slot a week in your content plan; more often and subscribers get tired. On planning and growth: [how to grow a Telegram channel from zero](/en/blog/grow-a-telegram-channel/).

## 5 common mistakes

1. **Only "do you like it?" questions.** They lead to no decision.
2. **A poll every day.** Votes drop and the channel starts to feel like a survey form.
3. **Not announcing the result.** Subscribers feel their opinion vanished.
4. **Visible votes where they aren't needed.** Few people rate price or quality under their own name.
5. **A big decision based on one poll.** Fifty active subscribers are not the whole market.

## Questions and answers

### How do I create a poll in a channel?

Tap the paperclip in the message field of your channel and choose "Poll". Enter the question and options, then turn on the settings you need: anonymity, multiple answers or quiz mode.

### Can I see who voted?

Only if the poll was created with visible votes. An anonymous poll shows just percentages and the vote count. You choose this setting when you create the poll.

### Who closes the poll, and when?

A channel admin can stop a poll at any time. Announce the deadline in the post and stop the poll on that day so the result is fixed.

### How much can I trust poll results?

Enough for content and small tests. Not for big decisions like pricing, product range or a new branch — those need sales figures, CRM data and conversations with customers.

### What does a quiz add to a channel?

It teaches subscribers about your product and makes the post playful. Add a short explanation and, if needed, point to a detailed post.

## Conclusion

A Telegram channel poll is useful only when it serves one decision: the next topic, a new course, the objection that stops buyers. Keep the question short, the options precise, state a deadline, compare votes with views and publish the result. One poll a week turns a channel into a two-way conversation.
