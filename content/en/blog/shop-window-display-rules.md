A passer-by walks past a shop in a few seconds. In that time the window has to tell them two things: what is sold here and why they should come in now. A shop window display is the cheapest "ad space" an offline business has: hundreds of people see it every day, and the rent is already paid. This article covers 7 rules that turn passers-by into buyers, a monthly window plan and a checklist.

## Why the window matters: your brand's first screen

The top of an Instagram profile and a shop window do the same job. The person hasn't walked in, hasn't talked to a salesperson, hasn't asked about price — they decide based only on what they see through the glass. A window has three jobs:

- **introduce** — show what the shop sells and at what price level;
- **stop** — pull the passer-by's eye out of the flow of the street;
- **invite** — give a clear reason to step inside: a new collection, a seasonal offer, a gift idea.

The window is your [visual identity](/en/blog/visual-identity-for-small-business/) continued on the street. The sign says your name; the window shows your character. The legal rules for storefront signs are a separate topic — we covered them in [storefront signs and outdoor ads in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/storefront-sign-outdoor-advertising-uzbekistan/).

## Shop window display: 7 rules

### 1. One window, one idea

The most common mistake is putting everything the shop has in the window. It ends up looking like a warehouse, and the eye has nothing to rest on. Every window needs an idea you can say in one sentence:

- "the winter collection is in";
- "New Year gifts under 300,000 soum";
- "a complete living-room set".

If you can't say the idea in one sentence, the passer-by won't get it either. It works like a [unique selling proposition](/en/blog/unique-selling-proposition/): one clear message beats five vague ones.

### 2. A focal point and fewer items

The window needs one main item the eye lands on first — at eye level, in the centre or slightly off it. Everything else supports it. A simple technique is the triangle: one tall item with two lower ones beside it. Leave space between items: empty space looks premium, clutter looks like a clearance sale.

> Illustrative example: a furniture shop in Tashkent replaces six chairs and three tables in the window with one sofa, a floor lamp and a rug. Now the passer-by sees a finished room rather than separate furniture — and thinks "I want my place to look like that".

### 3. Lighting, so the window works after dark

In autumn and winter it gets dark early in Tashkent, and many people pass shops after work, in the dark. An unlit window is simply invisible in those hours. The minimum:

- a dedicated spotlight on the main item;
- general light — warm or neutral, so it doesn't distort colours and textures;
- a timer that keeps the window lit for a few hours after closing — people still walk by.

Check reflections: look from the street during the day and see whether the reflection of the street hides the display. The stronger the light inside, the less glare.

### 4. Make prices and the offer readable

If no price is visible, many people assume "it's probably expensive here" and walk on. Next to each main item, show the exact price or a "from" price. Check that the text is readable from the street at 2–3 metres: small fonts and long sentences don't work.

Check the text as advertising too: prices in soum, text in the state language (with a translation if needed), no unprovable "cheapest" or "best". Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 563 changed the rules for signs and windows on the same building — check the exact wording on [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/docs/8537985). If you advertise instalments in the window, read our piece on [installment advertising rules](/en/blog/installment-advertising-rules/).

### 5. Change it with the seasons

A window that never changes becomes "wallpaper" within a month: people who walk past every day stop noticing it. Change it on a calendar, like a content plan. Late November is the time to prepare the New Year window — people start thinking about gifts early. A matching Instagram plan for this period is in our [New Year content plan](/en/blog/new-year-content-plan/). When the window and your feed say the same thing, they reinforce each other.

### 6. Show the brand, don't repeat the logo

One logo in the window is enough. Colours, materials and style show the brand: the main colour of your Instagram profile should appear in the window too — in the backdrop, a plinth or a ribbon. When someone recognises on the street what they saw on Instagram, trust grows.

Tashkent also has a city design code: the Tashkent City Council approved it on 17 February 2026, and business owners and the Business Ombudsman raised objections over the signage rules ([kun.uz](https://kun.uz/en/news/2026/04/27/business-ombudsman-urges-overhaul-of-tashkent-design-code-over-legal-concerns)). If you plan to add a new structure to the façade, a large window film or an illuminated board, check the current requirements with the khokimiyat first. The composition inside the window — items, light, backdrop — is usually up to you.

### 7. Connect the window to online and measure the result

The window is offline, but its results can be counted. A few simple methods:

- **a QR code** in the window — to a catalogue, a Telegram channel or an offer page; count the scans;
- **"saw it in the window"** — staff ask at the till or in conversation and note it down;
- **footfall** — count visitors by hand for a week with the old window and a week with the new one, on the same days and hours.

How to set up QR codes and promo codes properly is covered in [how to measure offline advertising](/en/blog/measure-offline-advertising/). If your shop is on Google and Yandex Maps, add a fresh photo of the window to the listing — people look the shop up on the map before they head out. Here's [how to add your business to maps](/en/blog/add-your-business-to-maps/).

## A monthly window plan

Change the window on a plan, not "when there's time". A simple cycle for a small shop:

| Week | What happens | Who is responsible |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Idea for the next window: season, new product or gift theme | Owner or marketer |
| Week 2 | Items, backdrop and price cards are prepared | Salesperson and designer |
| Week 3 | The window is changed and photographed by day and at night | Salesperson |
| Week 4 | Compare QR scans, "from the window" notes and footfall | Owner |

When the window changes, show it on Instagram too: "new window" in Stories, the setup process in Reels. One piece of work gives you content in two places.

## Common mistakes

- **Everything at once.** Too many items, and none of them stands out.
- **Dust and a dead bulb.** Dirty glass and a dark lamp signal "this shop is closed".
- **Sun-faded stock.** In a south-facing window, fabric and packaging lose their colour fast — rotate the items.
- **No price, or an unreadable one.** Nobody walks in just to ask the price.
- **The holiday is over, the decorations stay.** New Year decorations left up until the end of January make the shop look neglected.
- **Not measuring.** If you don't know which window brought in more people, the next one is a guess.

## Checklist before launching a window

- The window's idea fits in one sentence
- There is one focal item with space around it
- The main item has its own light, and a timer is set
- Price and offer are readable from 2–3 metres, text in the state language, prices in soum
- Colours match the Instagram profile
- The glass is clean and reflections don't hide the display
- The QR code works and leads to the right page
- The next change date is in the calendar
- The window is photographed by day and at night

## Questions and answers

### How often should I change the window?

Once a month is a good rhythm for a small shop. Change it out of turn before holidays (New Year, 8 March, Navruz) and when a new collection arrives. What matters is that people who pass every day notice the change.

### Do I need a designer for the window?

Not at the start. With the rules above — one idea, a focal item, lighting and a readable price — the owner or a salesperson can make a good window. A designer or decorator is useful for a big holiday window and for work on the façade.

### Are prices and offers in the window advertising?

It depends: Resolution No. 563 changed the rules for signs and windows on the same building, so check the exact wording on lex.uz. The safe route is to bring window text in line with advertising requirements: state language, prices in soum, no unprovable "best".

### What if my shop is in a shopping mall?

The rules are the same: one idea, a focal item, lighting, price. But the mall will have its own requirements — agree decorations, lighting and change times with management in advance.

## Conclusion

A shop window is a brand tool that works every day and asks for no extra rent. Pick one idea, light one main item, make the price readable, change the window once a month and count results with a QR code or a "from the window" note. Then the window stops being decoration and becomes a sales channel.
