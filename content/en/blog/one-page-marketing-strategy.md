In many businesses the marketing strategy either doesn't exist or is a 40-page document nobody reads. The result is the same: random ads, unplanned content, a budget set by gut feeling. The fix is a one-page strategy. It's equally clear to the owner, the marketer and the sales team, and it gets updated every month. This article gives a 9-block template and an illustrative example of filling it in.

![Why one page](/img/blog/illus/bir-sahifalik-marketing-strategiya-1.jpg 1200x675)

## Why one page

- **It gets read.** Anyone can read a page in 3 minutes.
- **It forces choices.** Space is limited, so you keep only what matters.
- **It gets updated.** One page is easy to revisit every month.
- **It aligns people.** The owner, marketing and sales look at the same goal.

## The 9 blocks

| Block | Main question |
|---|---|
| 1. Goal | What do we want in 3–6 months (in numbers)? |
| 2. Point A | Where are we now? |
| 3. Audience | Who is our core customer? |
| 4. Offer | Why do people choose us? |
| 5. Competitors | Who do we compete with and how are we different? |
| 6. Channels | Where do we find customers? |
| 7. Content | What do we talk about, and in what formats? |
| 8. Budget | How much do we spend, and on what? |
| 9. KPIs and plan | How do we measure results, and who does what? |

## Blocks 1–2. Goal and point A

The goal must be a number with a deadline: not "more customers" but "grow monthly sales from 40 to 70 in 3 months" (illustrative example). Point A is your current numbers: leads, cost per lead, sales, average order, budget. A strategy without point A is a trip without a map. How to gather it — [how to fill in a marketing brief](/en/blog/how-to-fill-in-a-marketing-brief/).

## Block 3. Audience

Write down 1–2 core segments, not everyone:

- who they are (age, role, situation);
- what problem they have;
- what worries them when deciding;
- where they spend time.

More — [target audience and customer persona](/en/blog/target-audience-and-customer-persona/).

## Blocks 4–5. Offer and competitors

The offer in one sentence: **what + for whom + what makes it different**. Then list 3–5 main competitors with your difference from each in a single line. If you can't find a difference, the strategy's first task is to create one. Helpful reading — [unique selling proposition](/en/blog/unique-selling-proposition/), [competitor analysis](/en/blog/competitor-analysis/).

## Block 6. Channels

Don't try to be everywhere. Pick 2–3 main channels and write down each one's job:

| Channel | Job |
|---|---|
| Instagram (content) | Trust and brand |
| Meta ads | New leads |
| Telegram | Warming the base and repeat sales |
| Google / maps | Customers already searching |

Choosing a channel — [Google Ads vs Meta Ads](/en/blog/google-ads-vs-meta-ads/).

![Block 7. Content](/img/blog/illus/bir-sahifalik-marketing-strategiya-2.jpg 1200x675)

## Block 7. Content

Write down 3–4 core themes and the formats. For example: product and results, customer reviews, expert tips, team and process. Formats: Reels, carousels, Stories. More — [Instagram content plan](/en/blog/instagram-content-plan/).

## Block 8. Budget

Split the budget in two:

- **ad budget** — money paid to the platforms;
- **production and team** — content, video, specialists.

You can work the budget back from the goal: required sales → required leads → cost per lead. More — [planning a marketing budget](/en/blog/planning-a-marketing-budget/).

## Block 9. KPIs and plan

3–5 key metrics and the main tasks for each month. Also note who owns what: marketing, sales, the owner. Choosing KPIs — [KPIs in an agency contract](/en/blog/kpis-in-an-agency-contract/).

## Illustrative example: a filled-in strategy

Say, an education centre in Tashkent (illustrative example):

| Block | Content |
|---|---|
| Goal | Grow new students from 40 to 70 a month in 3 months |
| Point A | 200 leads a month, 20% convert, cost per lead 35,000 so‘m |
| Audience | Students aged 16–24 and their parents |
| Offer | A free trial lesson and level-based groups |
| Competitors | 4 centres; difference — small groups and teacher videos |
| Channels | Instagram Reels, Meta ads, a Telegram channel |
| Content | Teachers, student results, lesson clips |
| Budget | Ads + video production, split by month |
| KPIs | Leads, trial-lesson attendance, conversion to sale, cost per student |

The numbers are only an example — for your business, start from point A. More on the industry — [marketing an education centre](/en/blog/marketing-an-education-centre/).

## How to use the strategy

- **Every month** — compare results with the KPIs and update next month's tasks.
- **Every quarter** — revisit the goal, channels and budget.
- **Every new hire or agency** — gets this page on day one.

![Common mistakes](/img/blog/illus/bir-sahifalik-marketing-strategiya-3.jpg 1200x675)

## Common mistakes

- A strategy with no goal or no numbers.
- An audience of "everyone".
- Too many channels at once.
- Budgeting only for ads and forgetting content production.
- Writing the strategy and never opening it again.

## Questions and answers

### Does a small business need a strategy?

Especially so: with a small budget, mistakes cost more. A one-page strategy protects against wasted spend.

### Can I write it myself?

Yes — the 9 blocks above are enough. An outside specialist adds an independent view of the market and competitors, plus experience from other industries.

### What period should a strategy cover?

Usually 3–6 months with monthly plans. Markets move fast, so revisit longer-term plans every quarter.

### Is a strategy the same as a content plan?

No. Strategy is what and why; a content plan is when and how. The content plan follows from the strategy.

## Conclusion

A one-page strategy puts goal, point A, audience, offer, competitors, channels, content, budget and KPIs on a single sheet. It gets read, used and updated every month.
