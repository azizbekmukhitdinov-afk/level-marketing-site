A brand ambassador is a blogger who doesn't post an ad once and move on, but appears alongside your brand for months. This kind of partnership builds trust far better than a one-off integration, but the mistakes cost more too: the wrong ambassador or a half-finished contract can eat half a year's budget. Below is a step-by-step guide to launching a brand ambassador programme: who to pick, what goes into the contract, content rights, pricing and measuring results.

![What a brand ambassador is and how it differs from a one-off ad](/img/blog/illus/brend-elchisi-bloger-1.jpg 1200x675)

## What a brand ambassador is and how it differs from a one-off ad

In a one-off ad, a blogger posts a couple of Stories or a Reel, the audience sees it and soon forgets. An ambassador shows the brand regularly, as part of their life: today they use the product, a month later they talk about the result, a month after that they answer followers' questions. Repetition turns into trust.

| | One-off ad | Brand ambassador |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | 1–3 days | 3–12 months |
| Content | 1–3 posts or Stories | Every month, to a plan |
| How the audience feels | "Another ad" | "They actually use it" |
| Competitors | May advertise them tomorrow | Off-limits for the contract term |
| Payment | One-time | Monthly or quarterly |
| Evaluating results | Per campaign | By quarterly trend |

How to run a one-off campaign properly is covered in our article on [influencer advertising](/en/blog/influencer-advertising-uzbekistan/). Here we focus on the long-term format.

## When you need an ambassador, and when it's too early

Not every business needs an ambassador, and not at every stage. It pays off when:

- **The product is used repeatedly** — cosmetics, clothing, furniture, fitness, courses. An ambassador can show "before and after".
- **The decision takes a long time** — clinics, real estate, expensive appliances. Seeing the brand next to someone they know well for several months raises people's trust.
- **The brand needs a face** — a new brand, or the period after a rebrand.

When it's still too early:

- you haven't tested a single blogger in the one-off format yet, so you don't know which audience works;
- your profile has no selling structure and Direct messages get answered late;
- the product or prices change often — what the ambassador says today will be wrong a month later.

Test 3–4 bloggers in a one-off format first. Inviting the one who delivered the best result, and who was easy for your team to work with, is the safest route.

## Choosing a brand ambassador: 6 criteria

1. **Do they genuinely use your product?** The best ambassador is a blogger who is already your customer. They talk about the product in their own words instead of reading a script.
2. **Is their audience your customer?** City, age, interests. Have the blogger show their stats with a screen recording.
3. **Is their reach stable?** Reels and Stories reach over the last 2–3 months should be steady, without sharp spikes.
4. **Do they run too many ads?** A blogger who promotes a different brand every day can't be an ambassador — their audience doesn't believe them.
5. **Is their record clean?** Look through recent years of videos and comments: no scandals, no ads for prohibited products (such as bookmakers or alcohol).
6. **Discipline.** Did they meet deadlines in the test period, send stats on time, take criticism well?

A large follower count is not a criterion. A blogger with a "live" audience of 20–80 thousand who genuinely loves your product is often more useful than a star with a million.

## The contract: what to put in writing with a brand ambassador

Verbal agreements don't hold up in a long partnership. The written contract should include:

| Clause | What to agree |
|---|---|
| Term | For example, 6 months with a 3-month trial stage |
| Monthly content plan | How many Reels, Stories, posts; what goes out in which month |
| Exclusivity | Which competitors are off-limits (a specific list or category) |
| Content approval | How many days before posting you review the script or finished video |
| Ad label | An "advertising" label on every sponsored piece |
| Content usage rights | Where (paid ads, website, in-store screens) and for how long |
| Payment | Fixed part, bonus, payment dates |
| Stats | Monthly screenshots or screen recording of reach, clicks and promo codes |
| Reputation clause | Right to terminate in case of scandal or a legal violation |
| Termination | Notice period for both sides and what happens to any advance paid |

Don't make exclusivity too broad. A clause saying "no cosmetics ads at all" cuts the blogger's income sharply, and they'll raise their price. "No other facial skincare brands" is more precise and fairer.

Don't forget the law either: Uzbekistan's Committee for Competition Development and Consumer Protection, citing Article 5 of the Law on Advertising, has reminded social media users that ads must be clearly marked with a label such as "advertising" ([UzA, September 2026](https://uza.uz/ar/posts/reklama-v-socsetyax-dolzhna-byt-zametnoy-komitet-po-konkurencii-predupredil-blogerov_903570)). Even if the ambassador's content looks like "just my opinion", if it was made for money or product, it's an ad. Have a lawyer review the contract itself.

## Content rights: who owns them and for how long

A video the ambassador shoots is their work. If you want to run it in your own ads, that right has to be written into the contract separately. Uzbekistan's [Law on Copyright and Related Rights](https://lex.uz/acts/-1022944) requires copyright agreements to be in writing, and rights not expressly named in the agreement are considered not transferred. So a vague line like "the content is ours" isn't enough.

Spell out:

- **where it's used** — Instagram and Facebook paid ads, website, Telegram channel, in-store screens;
- **for how long** — for example, the contract term plus 3 more months;
- **whether it can be edited** — cutting down, adding subtitles, combining with other videos;
- **after the partnership ends** — when old videos are pulled from ads.

The easiest way to put ambassador content into paid ads is Instagram partnership ads: the ad runs under the blogger's name, while you control the budget and audience. How to set this up is in our article on [Instagram partnership ads](/en/blog/instagram-partnership-ads/).

## Pricing: fixed fee, bonus or product

The price of an ambassadorship depends on the blogger's audience, content volume, how broad the exclusivity is and how long the rights last. There's no single market rate, so choosing the right payment structure matters more:

- **Fixed monthly fee only.** Simple and clear for the blogger. The downside: it isn't tied to results.
- **Fixed fee plus a performance bonus.** For example, an extra payment for every purchase or lead with their promo code. The blogger has a stake in the outcome.
- **Payment in product or services (barter).** Works with small bloggers, but rarely lasts over the long term: the blogger starts prioritising paid offers.

> **Illustrative example.** A furniture store in Tashkent signs a 6-month contract with an interior blogger: 2 Reels and 4 Stories series each month, exclusivity in the upholstered furniture category, and the right to run the videos in paid ads for 3 months after the contract ends. Roughly 70% of the payment is fixed and 30% is a bonus on promo-code sales. The figures are for illustration only, not a market rate.

Starting with a 3-month trial stage is safe for both sides: if there are no results, the partnership ends painlessly.

## Measuring results: quarter by quarter, not month by month

An ambassador's strength is repetition, so drawing conclusions after one Reel is a mistake. Collect data every month, but make the decision at the end of the quarter.

What to track:

1. **A personal promo code** — the number of purchases from the ambassador's audience.
2. **UTM links** — visits through the link in Stories. How to build them is in our article on [UTM tags](/en/blog/utm-tags/).
3. **A source question in Direct and on calls** — how many people answer "Where did you hear about us?" with "from that blogger".
4. **Brand-name searches and profile visits** — are they growing during the partnership?
5. **Ambassador videos in paid ads** — cost per lead compared with your regular creatives.

At the end of each quarter, have an honest talk with the ambassador: which videos worked, what the audience asked about, what to change next quarter.

## 5 common mistakes

- **Writing the script word for word.** If the ambassador reads your text, the audience notices immediately. Give them the key points and let them choose the words.
- **Leaving out exclusivity.** A month later your ambassador could be advertising a competitor.
- **Not setting a term for content rights.** Using the videos in ads later turns into a dispute.
- **Judging results after one month.** The long-term format needs patience.
- **Skipping the "advertising" label.** It's a legal requirement and a matter of trust.

## Questions and answers

### What's the main difference between a brand ambassador and a one-off influencer ad?

Duration and repetition. An ambassador shows the brand regularly for months, doesn't advertise competitors, and the audience starts to associate them with the brand. A one-off ad is quickly forgotten.

### How many months should the contract run?

The first time, 6 months with a 3-month trial stage. If the results are good, renew for the next period. Signing for a full year right away is risky.

### Can an ambassador advertise other brands at the same time?

Yes, as long as they aren't your competitors and the contract says so clearly. Define exclusivity by category, not by the whole industry.

### Is one ambassador better, or several?

For a small business, one or two ambassadors are enough — they're easier to manage and results are easier to measure. If you need several, make sure they have different audiences.

### Can I run the ambassador's video from my ad account?

Yes, if the contract grants that right: where, for how long and with what editing. The most convenient route is Instagram partnership ads.

## Conclusion

A brand ambassador isn't a one-off ad multiplied — it's a system that builds trust in your brand over months. Test bloggers in the one-off format first, choose the best fit, spell out exclusivity, content rights and termination terms in the contract, and judge results by the quarter. The ambassador becomes the voice of the brand, so their values should match those of [your brand](/en/blog/what-is-a-brand/).
