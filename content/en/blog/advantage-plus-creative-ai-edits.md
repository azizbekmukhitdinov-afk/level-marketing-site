You uploaded one image and one piece of copy. Your customer may see a different ad: the background extended, music added, the text translated into another language. That is Advantage+ creative at work, the Meta features that automatically "enhance" ads. This article covers what Advantage+ creative changes, who answers for the result, and how to audit your settings.

## What Advantage+ creative is and how it changes an ad

Advantage+ creative is a group of settings in Meta Ads Manager. When they are on, the system builds several versions of your creative and shows each person the one it judges most suitable. Meta describes it in its [Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/297506218282224) as a way to improve performance.

The idea is fine. The trouble is that many enhancements are on by default, and business owners don't know they exist. According to advertiser and agency reports in 2026, the system has done the following:

| Type of change | What actually happens | Where the risk is |
|---|---|---|
| Image touch-ups | Brightness, contrast and cropping change | The product colour differs from the real one |
| Image expansion | AI "continues" the background to fit 9:16 | Details appear that don't exist, the product is distorted |
| Music | A track is added to an image or video | It doesn't fit the brand's tone |
| Text variations | Headline and copy are rewritten or swapped | A promise or condition changes |
| Translation | Copy is translated automatically | Translation errors, wrong terms |
| Extra links | Pages from your site appear under the ad | An old promotion or irrelevant page shows up |
| AI-generated image | A new image or carousel is built from your photos | The product doesn't really look like that |

The list is incomplete and it keeps moving: Meta adds features often and changes their names and location. So the goal is a checking habit, not memorising buttons.

> In summer 2026 a large US outdoor and sports retailer pulled an Instagram ad after an AI-altered image showed a bicycle with a second set of handlebars. The company said it had been enrolled in the feature without its knowledge.

## Who is responsible: what happened with Meta's terms

In October 2026 Meta published a revised version of its Commercial Terms with a new "AI Features" section. In the wording quoted by Adweek and PPC Land, the AI features "may make mistakes", and the advertiser is "solely responsible" for all risks associated with them. As reporters read it, that includes features Meta switched on itself.

Then things moved quickly:

| Date | What happened |
|---|---|
| October 2026 | New terms published, effective date 30 October |
| Late October | Date pushed to 20 November after objections from advertisers and publishers |
| 6 November | Meta paused the changes. [Reuters reported](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/meta-pauses-planned-terms-updates-keeps-existing-rules-in-place-5007156) they will not take effect on 20 November |
| As of 12 November | [Meta's page](https://www.facebook.com/legal/commercial_terms) says the update is postponed and the current terms remain in effect until further notice |

So can you relax? No, for three reasons:

1. **The current terms don't protect you either.** Under the version in force since 6 March 2024, a business answers to Meta for third-party claims tied to its content, while Meta's own liability is capped.
2. **Your customer doesn't care.** Your brand sits under the distorted image or the wrong translation. Nobody thinks "Meta did that".
3. **Local law looks at the advertiser.** In Uzbekistan, responsibility for ad content is set by the [Law on Advertising](/en/blog/advertising-law-uzbekistan/). It has no exception for "the algorithm changed the text".

Nobody knows yet when or in what form Meta will bring the new terms back. The direction is clear, though: the platform keeps adding automation, and checking the output stays your job.

## Which businesses carry more risk

The risk is not the same for everyone. Automatic edits cost the most in these categories:

- **Clinics, pharmacies, cosmetology.** If one word in the copy turns into a promise, it breaks both the law and Meta's rules. More here: [medicine and supplement advertising rules](/en/blog/medicine-supplement-advertising-uzbekistan/).
- **Credit, instalments, real estate.** Price, rate and conditions must be stated exactly. A rewritten headline can drop a condition.
- **Furniture, clothing, jewellery, cars.** The customer expects exactly what the picture shows. Change the colour or shape and you get complaints and returns.
- **Businesses that advertise in two languages.** If you wrote the Uzbek and Russian copy yourself, auto-translation only gets in the way. See [which language your ads should use](/en/blog/ad-language-uzbek-or-russian/).

For a simple service or a low-priced product, small enhancements such as brightness correction usually do no harm.

## How to audit Advantage+ creative settings: 6 steps

Menu names depend on the account language, the campaign objective and the format, and Meta changes them often. So what follows is the logic, not exact buttons.

**Step 1. Make a list.** Put every ad in a spreadsheet: live, scheduled and always-on. Don't forget catalogue ads.

**Step 2. Open the account-level setting.** According to agency guides, it sits in the ad account settings under "Creative features". This is where the defaults for new ads are set. Turn off anything you haven't approved.

**Step 3. Open every ad.** At ad level, find the Advantage+ creative menu in the creative section and **expand every collapsed group**. Many settings hide inside collapsed menus. Turning one off does not turn off the rest.

**Step 4. Decide on each enhancement.** One question: "If the system gets this wrong, can I live with the consequences?" If not, turn it off.

**Step 5. Check the preview.** Before saving, flip through every placement in the preview window: feed, Stories, Reels. Extended backgrounds and cropped images show up there.

**Step 6. Take a screenshot.** Save the state of the settings with a date. In a dispute, that is your evidence.

One detail matters a lot. The agency JumpFly has observed that enhancements can switch back on when you replace the creative, change the format or duplicate an ad. So the audit is not a one-off: you reopen the menu **after every edit**.

## Which enhancements to keep and which to turn off

Turning everything off blindly isn't right either: some features do help. Decide by risk.

| Enhancement | When you can keep it | When to turn it off |
|---|---|---|
| Brightness and contrast | Services, atmosphere, team photos | Products where colour matters |
| Image expansion | A simple image on a plain background | Product near the edge of the frame, interiors, equipment |
| Music | As a test, if there are no brand rules | Healthcare, premium segment, serious topics |
| Text variations | If you wrote every variation yourself | Prices, rates, medical and legal copy |
| Auto-translation | Almost never | When you have your own copy in both languages |
| AI-generated image | Only if you have seen and approved it | When the product must look exact |

**Illustrative example.** A furniture shop in Tashkent launched an ad for a grey sofa. The audit showed three things switched on: image touch-ups, image expansion and music. In the Stories preview the sofa's backrest had been "continued" and the colour came out warmer, closer to beige. The shop turned off touch-ups and expansion, uploaded a separate vertical photo for 9:16, and kept music as a test. This example has no figures and was made up to illustrate the point.

One more reason: with enhancements on, the result of an [A/B test of ad creatives](/en/blog/ab-testing-ad-creatives/) is harder to read. You don't know which version won, yours or the one the system changed.

## What to agree with your agency or media buyer

If a contractor runs your ads, the line of responsibility should be in writing. Include four points:

1. **Who checks.** Who looks at the Advantage+ creative settings on every new ad: the media buyer, the project manager or you.
2. **What is allowed.** Which enhancements are approved and which are banned. A one-page list is enough.
3. **How often.** At least once a week and after every edit.
4. **What happens on an error.** Who pauses the ad and how fast you are told.

For this to work, the ad account has to be yours and you need your own login. Here is how to set it up: [giving an agency access to your Meta ad account](/en/blog/give-agency-access-meta-ad-account/).

## 5 common mistakes

1. **"I never turned anything on."** Many features are on by default. Not turning it on doesn't mean it's off.
2. **Switching off just one toggle.** A master toggle may not affect settings in other groups.
3. **Duplicating an ad without checking.** A copy doesn't always keep the original's settings.
4. **Looking only at the feed view.** Problems usually appear in the Stories and Reels versions.
5. **Ignoring Meta's notices.** A new feature often arrives as "on unless you opt out". When a notice comes, review your settings.

## Questions and answers

### If I turn Advantage+ creative off completely, will results drop?

There is no firm answer: it depends on the account, the category and the creative. Meta says the features improve performance, while some agencies always turn them off. In a risky category, turn them off first, then test the safe enhancements one at a time.

### The new terms haven't taken effect. Why audit now?

Because the features run regardless of the terms. Your ads may already be going out in an altered form today, and the reputation and the legal responsibility are yours.

### Where do I find the settings?

In two places: the ad account settings (defaults for new ads) and the creative section of each ad. Names change, so look for "Advantage+ creative" or "Creative features" and open the collapsed menus.

### Can I see exactly which version the system showed?

Not always. The preview window shows only some of the versions. If you can't see the delivered versions in full, treat that ad as high risk and turn the enhancements off.

### How often should I check?

After every new ad and every edit, plus a general review once a week. It is convenient to do it together with your [advertising report](/en/blog/what-an-ad-report-should-include/).

## Conclusion

Advantage+ creative is not a bad tool, but it works without asking you. The pause on the terms doesn't move responsibility to Meta: your name is still under the ad. Set aside 30 minutes today: list your ads, open the collapsed menus, turn off what you haven't approved and take screenshots. Then make it a weekly habit.
