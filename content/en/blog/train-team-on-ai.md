In many companies, AI use is haphazard: one employee uses ChatGPT heavily, another has never tried it, a third uploads the customer database to "get it analysed". The result: little benefit and a lot of risk. Real value from AI doesn't come from one enthusiast but from the team's **shared rules and habits**. Here's a plan to train a small team to work with AI in 30 days.

## Step 1: internal rules (one page)

Write the rules before training. Keep them short — one page:

**Allowed:**
- draft copy: posts, replies, letters, descriptions;
- ideas and structure;
- analysis of anonymised data;
- draft translations.

**Not allowed:**
- customers' names, phone numbers, addresses or chats;
- passwords, card and bank details;
- complete contracts and financial reports;
- sending AI output to a customer or into an ad without checking.

**Required:**
- a work account for work;
- Uzbek copy is checked by a native-speaking colleague;
- every number and legal claim is checked against the primary source.

Why it matters: since January 2026, unlawfully processing personal data using AI carries a fine of 50–100 BRV (ZRU-1115, [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8008401)). More in [AI and the law in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/ai-law-uzbekistan-business/).

## Step 2: 2–3 tasks per role

A general "use AI" instruction doesn't work. Give each role concrete tasks:

| Role | AI tasks |
|---|---|
| Salesperson | Reply templates, objection answers, role-play — [AI sales script](/en/blog/ai-sales-script/) |
| SMM manager | Content plan, post drafts, review analysis — [AI content plan](/en/blog/ai-content-plan/) |
| Manager / owner | Report summaries, presentations, documents — [presentations with AI](/en/blog/ai-presentations/) |
| Operator / administrator | A knowledge base of answers to frequent questions |
| Accounts / office | Letters, spreadsheet formulas, document drafts (anonymised) |

## Step 3: a 30-day plan

| Week | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Introduce the rules (30 minutes); everyone learns one tool; first task — each person does one of their own tasks with AI |
| 2 | Each employee writes 3 useful prompts — a shared "prompt library" |
| 3 | Pair work: experienced staff show newcomers; review mistakes |
| 4 | Results: time saved, which tasks worked and which didn't; update the rules |

Starter material for the library — [marketing prompts](/en/blog/marketing-prompts/).

## Step 4: measuring results

Measure not "are we using AI" but "what changed":

- time per task, with and without AI (1–2 tasks per role);
- quality: a manager's or experienced colleague's assessment;
- errors: cases of wrong information caused by AI;
- business metrics: response speed, content consistency.

Illustrative example: an SMM manager spent 8 hours on the monthly content plan; with AI it takes 3. The 5 hours saved go to filming and working with customers.

## Handling resistance

Some employees fear or reject AI. Reasons and responses:

- **"They'll replace me"** — the goal isn't replacement but less tedious work. Say so openly.
- **"I don't understand technology"** — start with a simple task and work in pairs.
- **"AI makes mistakes"** — true, which is why a human checks. That's the rule.

And the opposite — tie over-enthusiastic employees to the rules too: the biggest risk is handing AI everything. What not to delegate — [what not to delegate to AI](/en/blog/what-not-to-delegate-to-ai/).

## Choosing a tool

One main tool works best for a team: shared rules, a shared library, one account. Which to pick — [ChatGPT or Gemini](/en/blog/chatgpt-or-gemini-for-business/).

## Common mistakes

- Starting without rules.
- "Let everyone learn on their own" — random results.
- Work data in employees' personal accounts.
- Not measuring results.
- Not closing AI account access when an employee leaves.

## Questions and answers

### Do we need an external course?

For a small team, an internal 30-day plan is often enough. External training can help with complex tasks.

### Who should own this?

One employee who uses AI well and follows the rules — an "AI lead". They maintain the library and answer questions.

### Does everyone need a paid plan?

Try the free version first. For employees who use it daily, a paid plan usually pays for itself.

### Should AI use be mandatory?

Rather than forcing it, show results: time saved is the best argument.

## Conclusion

Training a team on AI is a 30-day project: one page of rules, concrete tasks for each role, a prompt library, pair work and measured results. The goal isn't the tool — it's time saved and error-free work. The Level Marketing team — more than 25 specialists — uses AI tools in daily work under exactly these kinds of rules.
