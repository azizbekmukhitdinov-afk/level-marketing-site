A December ad budget doesn't behave like the rest of the year: there are plenty of buyers, but also plenty of advertisers fighting for the same feed. Businesses that plan ahead run their promotions calmly; those who decide on December 20 to "just double the budget" usually get expensive, unstable results. Here is how to plan December spend for Meta ads (Instagram and Facebook) in five steps.

![Why ads get more expensive in December](/img/blog/illus/dekabr-reklama-byudjeti-1.jpg 1200x675)

## Why ads get more expensive in December

Meta ads are sold through an auction. Several advertisers compete for every impression, and competition rises before the holidays: shops, restaurants, training centres and international brands all scale up at the same time. As a result, the cost per 1,000 impressions (CPM) usually goes up, and leads can cost more than in a normal month.

Buyers are active too. Meta's 2026 holiday planning guide says 85% of holiday shoppers worldwide use Meta apps every week ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-publishes-2026-holiday-planning-guide/822744/)). So switching ads off in December isn't the answer. The answer is spreading the budget correctly.

December in Uzbekistan has one more feature. Under Presidential Decree PF-257 of December 24, 2025, December 31, 2026 (a Thursday) is a non-working day for everyone, and for five-day-week employers the day off from Saturday, December 12 has been moved to that date ([Buxgalter.uz](https://buxgalter.uz/oz/publish/doc/text212400_2026_yil_uchun_ishlab_chiqarish_taqvimi_ish_va_bayram_kunlari)). January 1 is a Friday, so most people get December 31 to January 3 off in a row, while December 12 becomes a working day on a five-day week. These dates affect your budget schedule.

## How to plan a December ad budget: 5 steps

### Step 1. Collect your own numbers

Your plan needs your own ad account, not someone else's averages. For November and (if you advertised) last December, write down:

- daily and monthly spend;
- cost per lead or message;
- lead-to-sale conversion (from your CRM or spreadsheet);
- which days brought the most leads and sales.

If leads and sales aren't tracked separately, fix that first, or you won't be able to judge December. We covered the numbers you need in [what an ad report should include](/en/blog/what-an-ad-report-should-include/).

### Step 2. Split the month into 4 periods

Plan December as several periods with different jobs rather than one flat month:

| Period | Job | Approximate share of budget |
|---|---|---|
| Dec 1–10 | Build new audiences: video, useful content, catalogue | 25–30% |
| Dec 11–20 | Announce the promotion, gift offers | 30–35% |
| Dec 21–30 | Peak: retargeting, "last days", fast delivery | 30–40% |
| Dec 31 – Jan 3 | Minimal spend or pause (depends on niche) | 0–5% |

The shares are a guide, not a rule. For gifts, flowers and restaurants the peak shifts to the last days; in B2B and education demand may drop in the second half of December, so moving budget to the start of the month makes more sense. Align the order with your [New Year content plan](/en/blog/new-year-content-plan/).

### Step 3. Divide spend between new audiences and retargeting

The biggest December mistake is spending everything on cold audiences. Before the holidays, people who already know you buy faster: they visited your profile, watched a video, messaged you in Direct or visited the website.

**Illustrative example:** a furniture store in Tashkent sets aside $1,500 for December. The split could look like this:

| Direction | Dec 1–10 | Dec 11–20 | Dec 21–30 |
|---|---|---|---|
| New audiences | $300 | $300 | $250 |
| Retargeting | $100 | $200 | $350 |
| **Total** | **$400** | **$500** | **$600** |

The numbers only illustrate the structure. The logic: warm the audience up early in the month, then show it a concrete offer at the end. We explain the setup in detail in [how retargeting works](/en/blog/how-retargeting-works/).

### Step 4. Raise the budget gradually

Every Meta ad set first goes through a learning phase. According to Meta's Business Help Center, an ad set gets the "Learning limited" status when it's unlikely to get around 50 optimisation events (such as leads) in the week after its last significant edit ([Meta Business Help](https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/help/269269737396981)). A sharp budget change can also count as a significant edit, depending on its size.

In practice:

- don't multiply the budget in a single day; add it in small steps a few days apart;
- start scaling before the peak, on December 15–18, not on the 25th, so the algorithm has time to adjust;
- if you need a new creative, consider a separate ad set instead of adding it to one that's working;
- a daily budget may overspend on some days, but over a week Meta won't spend more than seven days' worth ([Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/updates-to-meta-ads-budgeting/)). So plan totals by week, not by day, to keep promotion days under control.

### Step 5. Prepare creatives in November

In December ads wear out fast: the same audience sees the same video again and again. Have at least 2–3 fresh creatives ready for each period: product and useful videos for the first, promotion and gift ideas for the second, "last days" and delivery deadlines for the third. Testing creatives in November and taking only the winners into December is the cheapest insurance you can buy. Here is how: [A/B testing ad creatives](/en/blog/ab-testing-ad-creatives/).

## Build retargeting audiences in advance

Retargeting only works if there's an audience. If you start thinking about who to retarget on December 20, the audience will be small. Set these up in November:

- **Instagram and Facebook engagement:** people who interacted with your profile, posts or Reels in the last 30–60 days;
- **video viewers:** people who watched most of a video (which is why video ads are useful in the first period);
- **website visitors:** if the Meta pixel and Conversions API are set up properly;
- **people who messaged you:** wrote in Direct but didn't buy;
- **customer list:** only if people gave consent. See [customer databases and personal data](/en/blog/customer-database-personal-data-uzbekistan/) for doing this legally.

Also exclude recent buyers from retargeting: showing someone an ad for the product they just bought wastes budget.

## Holidays: should you switch ads off?

From December 31 to January 3, the best call for most businesses is to cut ads to a minimum or pause them. The reason is simple: even if leads come in, money is wasted when nobody is there to answer. A lead left unanswered for a few days goes elsewhere. More on that in [lead response time](/en/blog/lead-response-time/).

There are exceptions:

- **restaurants, cafés, flower and gift shops:** December 31 is a working day for them, and ads should run too;
- **delivery services:** if you deliver during the holidays, say so clearly in the ads;
- **January promotion:** you can warm up an audience on January 1–3 with a small budget for offers starting after January 4.

Before pausing, remember that an ad set paused for a long time may go through learning again when you switch it back on. For a 3–4 day holiday, a significant budget cut is often more convenient than switching off completely.

## 4 December budget mistakes

1. **No plan, just "more money".** Raising the budget isn't a plan. Write down in advance what each period's money is for.
2. **Panicking over rising cost per lead.** Leads costing more in December is normal. Watch cost per sale and average order value instead: orders are often bigger before the holidays.
3. **Leaving everything to the last week.** Launching a new campaign on December 25 means its learning phase lands on peak days.
4. **Forgetting the sales team.** If ads bring twice as many leads, who will handle them? Plan the managers' December schedule together with the ads.

## December budget checklist

- November numbers: spend, cost per lead, conversion
- Month split into 4 periods, each with its own amount
- Clear split between new audiences and retargeting
- Scaling dates set (from December 15–18)
- 2–3 tested creatives ready for each period
- Retargeting audiences created in November
- Decision for December 31 – January 3: pause or minimal budget
- Sales team holiday schedule confirmed

## Questions and answers

### How much should I raise the budget in December?

There's no universal percentage. Work it out from the number of sales you need and your November conversion, then add a buffer for more expensive leads. The method is in [planning a marketing budget](/en/blog/planning-a-marketing-budget/).

### Should a new business start advertising in December?

It can, but testing at peak competition costs more. If possible, test in November and enter December with ready creatives and audiences.

### What if my retargeting audience is too small?

Then give video ads more budget in the first period: video viewers build up quickly and become the base for retargeting later.

### Daily budget or lifetime budget?

For a promotion with clear start and end dates, a lifetime budget can be convenient: Meta spreads the money across the period itself. For always-on campaigns, a daily budget is easier to control.

## Conclusion

A December ad budget isn't one big number. It's a four-period plan: build audiences early in the month, announce the promotion in the middle, sell through retargeting at the end and cut spend over the holidays. Scale gradually, prepare creatives in advance and judge results by sales, not by cost per lead.
