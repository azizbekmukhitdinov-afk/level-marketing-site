Ad copy often gets overlooked: video and design take up the time, and the text is written at the last minute. Yet it's the copy that tells people "this is for me" and shows them the next step. Here's how to write ad copy that sells. Common advertising mistakes — [why ads don't bring sales](/en/blog/why-ads-dont-bring-sales/).

## The 5 parts of good ad copy

1. **The headline (hook)** — the first line, which stops the scroll.
2. **The problem or desire** — in the customer's own words.
3. **The offer and benefit** — what they get: a result, not a feature.
4. **Proof** — a number, a review, a guarantee — [social proof](/en/blog/social-proof-in-ads/).
5. **A call to action** — one specific step.

## Two classic formulas

### AIDA

- **Attention**: "Has your kitchen furniture swollen within 2 years?"
- **Interest**: "The cause: material that can't handle moisture."
- **Desire**: "We only use moisture-resistant board, with a 5-year guarantee."
- **Action**: "Book a free measurement."

### PAS

- **Problem**: "Leads are coming in, but there are no sales."
- **Agitate**: "Ad money is lost every month, and salespeople complain about 'poor leads'."
- **Solve**: "In an audit, we'll show you where leads are being lost."

(Both are illustrative examples.)

## Benefits, not features

| Feature | Benefit |
|---|---|
| An 8 kg washing machine | A big family's laundry in one wash |
| Open 24 hours | You can get medicine even at night |
| Small groups | The teacher has time for every student |

## By platform

**Instagram/Facebook** — only the first 1–2 lines show; the rest sits behind "more". Put the main point first. On-screen text and the ad caption should complement each other — [Reels hooks](/en/blog/reels-hooks/).

**Telegram** — match the channel's style, useful and brief, with one link — [advertising in Telegram channels](/en/blog/buying-ads-in-telegram-channels/).

**Google search** — query words in the headline, a clear offer, character limits — [how to set up Google Ads](/en/blog/how-to-set-up-google-ads/).

## Language and tone

- Write in the language the customer thinks in — [ad language: Uzbek or Russian](/en/blog/ad-language-uzbek-or-russian/).
- Plain words and short sentences.
- More about "you", less about "us".

## The law

Prices in soum; claims like "the best" or "the cheapest" must be provable; misleading promises are prohibited — [advertising law in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/advertising-law-uzbekistan/).

## Common mistakes

- "Quality and reliability" — words that say nothing.
- Three offers in one ad.
- A missing or vague call to action.
- Copy that doesn't match the landing page.
- A single version, with no test — [A/B testing ad creatives](/en/blog/ab-testing-ad-creatives/).

## Questions and answers

### How long should ad copy be?

Cheap, simple products need short copy; expensive, complex ones need longer copy with proof. The first line matters most.

### Can I write ad copy with AI?

For drafts and variants, yes — but customer language and facts must come from you — [marketing prompts](/en/blog/marketing-prompts/).

### Should I use emojis?

In moderation, where they make reading easier. Lots of emojis cheapen a serious offer.
