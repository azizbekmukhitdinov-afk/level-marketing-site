"The ads aren't working" is the line business owners say most often. Yet in many cases the ads do work: people see them, click and write in. The sale gets lost somewhere else in the chain. Finding the problem takes not a blanket "the ads are bad" but checking each funnel stage separately. This article breaks the path from ad to sale into stages and shows what to check at each one.

![From ad to sale: 6 stages](/img/blog/illus/reklama-sotuv-bermasa-1.jpg 1200x675)

## From ad to sale: 6 stages

1. **Impression** — someone saw the ad.
2. **Interest** — they stopped, watched, clicked.
3. **Lead** — they wrote, called or filled in a form.
4. **Response** — the business replied.
5. **Conversation** — questions answered, objections handled.
6. **Sale** — payment.

People drop off at every stage. The job is to find where the biggest losses are. More on funnels — [what is a sales funnel](/en/blog/what-is-a-sales-funnel/).

## Diagnostic table

| Symptom | Likely cause | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Lots of impressions, few clicks | The creative doesn't grab attention | Hook, first 3 seconds, offer |
| Lots of clicks, few leads | The profile or page doesn't build trust | Bio, price, reviews, form |
| Lots of leads, slow replies | No lead-handling system | Response time, on-duty staff, auto-replies |
| Replies sent, conversations stall | No script, no next step offered | Chat transcripts, script |
| Many conversations, few sales | Price, offer or objections | Reasons for lost deals |
| "Poor quality" leads | Wrong audience or a too-easy form | Audience, form questions |

## Stages 1–2. Creative and audience

If people see the ad and don't stop:

- is the main idea there in the first 1–3 seconds — [Reels hooks](/en/blog/reels-hooks/);
- does the video make sense without sound;
- is the offer clear: what, how much, why now;
- when was the creative last refreshed.

If there are many leads but poor ones, check the audience or campaign objective. A cheap "reach" campaign, for example, may bring random people rather than buyers.

## Stage 3. Profile and lead form

Someone clicked but didn't write — so what they saw didn't build trust:

- is there a price, or a "from…" price, on the profile;
- are reviews and work examples visible;
- is it clear how to get in touch;
- is the form too long.

Choosing the format — [lead form or messages](/en/blog/lead-form-or-messages/); the profile — [Instagram profile that sells](/en/blog/instagram-profile-that-sells/).

## Stage 4. Responding to leads

For many businesses this is where most is lost. People message several companies at once and buy from whoever answers first. Check:

- average time to first reply;
- who answers in the evening and at weekends;
- how many leads got no reply at all.

More — [lead response time](/en/blog/lead-response-time/).

## Stage 5. The conversation

A reply went out, but the chat ends like this: "how much?" — "…so‘m" — silence. Causes:

- the manager only answers the question and offers no next step;
- objections aren't handled;
- there's no follow-up.

The fix is a simple script and a follow-up rule — [sales script](/en/blog/sales-script/).

## Stage 6. The sale

If there are many conversations but few payments, look at the reasons for lost deals in the CRM: too expensive, chose someone else, later, product unavailable. This data is gold for marketing too: offers and creatives can be reworked to answer exactly these objections. Choosing a CRM — [CRM for small business](/en/blog/crm-for-small-business/).

## Illustrative example: the problem in numbers

| Stage | Count | Conversion |
|---|---|---|
| Leads | 300 | — |
| Got a reply | 210 | 70% |
| Had a conversation | 120 | 57% |
| Sale | 18 | 15% |

In this illustrative example, 90 leads never got a reply at all. Fixing the response system is cheaper and faster than raising the ad budget.

## The order of fixes

1. **Set up tracking** — every lead's source and status in the CRM — [UTM tags](/en/blog/utm-tags/).
2. **Collect the numbers** — the count at each stage.
3. **Find the biggest loss** — the stage with the lowest conversion.
4. **Fix one thing** — and watch for 2–3 weeks.
5. **Move to the next stage.**

## Common mistakes

- Immediately raising or pausing the ad budget.
- Replacing the ads specialist without checking the sales process.
- Looking only at cost per lead, not at sales.
- Not recording reasons for lost deals.
- Changing everything at once.

## Questions and answers

### How do I know if the problem is the ads or sales?

Look at the funnel numbers. If leads come in normally but there are no sales, the problem is usually at the response and conversation stages. If leads are few or poor, it's the creative, audience or offer.

### What if leads are "poor quality"?

Define what you mean: they don't pick up, have no budget, live in another city. Then adjust the form questions, the audience and the offer in the creative.

### Should I stop the ads?

Usually not. First fix the stage with the biggest losses. Stopping ads also stops the flow of data.

### How long does this check take?

With numbers in hand, a day or two. Without tracking, you first need to set it up and gather 2–3 weeks of data.

## Conclusion

When ads don't bring sales, the problem is rarely the ads themselves: it's in lead response, the conversation or the offer. Break the funnel into stages, measure each in numbers and start with the biggest loss.
