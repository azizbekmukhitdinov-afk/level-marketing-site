In many companies the sale ends with the payment: the manager moves on to the next lead and the customer is left on their own. That is exactly when they ask themselves "did I choose right?" and, within the first weeks, decide whether to stay or leave. Onboarding is the system that takes a customer to a result quickly in the first 30 days after purchase and turns working with you into a habit. This article gives a day-by-day plan, message templates and examples by industry.

## Why the first 30 days decide everything

Two things happen at once after a customer pays. First, trust is at its peak but has not yet been confirmed by anything. Second, post-purchase doubt kicks in: "maybe I overpaid", "maybe someone else is better". If during this period the customer sees no result and nobody gets in touch, doubt wins.

That is why churn is usually highest in the first month. To check this in your own business, group customers by the month they joined and look at how many of each group remain after a month; the method is in our article on [customer churn rate](/en/blog/customer-churn-rate/).

## The four goals of onboarding

1. **Confirm the decision.** The customer should feel they chose well: who they work with, what happens next and when to expect what.
2. **Reset expectations.** Turn the promises made in ads and sales into a realistic plan: what will happen and what will not.
3. **Deliver a first result quickly.** Small but noticeable. This is called "time to first value", and you want it as short as possible.
4. **Build contact and a habit.** The customer knows where to write, regularly gets useful messages from you, and your service becomes part of their routine.

## The 30-day plan, day by day

| Day | What happens | Channel | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (purchase day) | Thanks, next steps, the owner's name, connecting to the bot | In person or call | Salesperson |
| 1 | Welcome message: what, when, where, who to contact | Telegram or SMS | Manager |
| 3 | "Is everything clear?", check the first step is done | Call | Manager |
| 7 | Show and record the first result | Meeting or message | Specialist |
| 14 | A useful tip, gather questions, spot problems | Telegram | Manager |
| 30 | Month summary, NPS question, plan for next month | Call or meeting | Head or specialist |

Turn this table into CRM tasks for every new customer. Then onboarding does not depend on a manager's memory. For choosing a system, read [what a CRM is and which one suits a small business](/en/blog/crm-for-small-business/).

An important technical point: a Telegram bot cannot message a user first. According to Telegram's documentation, the user must message the bot before it can write to them ([core.telegram.org/bots](https://core.telegram.org/bots)). So on purchase day the salesperson should check that the customer has opened the bot and pressed "Start". Asking later is much harder. How to build one: [how to create a Telegram bot](/en/blog/create-a-telegram-bot/).

## The welcome message: structure and template

A good welcome message is not an ad, it is a roadmap. It has five elements:

- the customer's name and what they bought;
- the name of the person responsible and how to reach them;
- the first step: a specific date, time and address or link;
- what to expect in the first week;
- how quickly questions get answered.

Template (education centre): "Hello, Dilnoza! You are enrolled in the English B1 group. Your curator is Kamola, and she will answer you in this chat. First lesson: Monday, 18:30, room 2. In the first week there will be a short level test, and we will show you the result on Friday. If you have questions, write here; during working hours we reply within 30 minutes."

Names and details are illustrative. The message should contain no extra sales: trying to sell something else on day one lowers trust.

## Delivering the first result fast

For each business, write down what "first value" means. It is a result the customer feels for themselves and that shows up as a number or a fact.

| Business | First value | By when |
|---|---|---|
| Education centre | A level test and a first small win (for example, 50 new words) | Week 1 |
| Gym | Starting measurements and a personal plan | Days 1–3 |
| Clinic | A clear diagnosis and a written treatment plan | First visit |
| Custom furniture | A project drawing and a production schedule | Days 3–5 |
| B2B service | Access, a brief and an approved plan | Week 1 |
| Online course | First module completed, progress visible | Days 2–3 |

Rule: if the first result is more than 2 weeks away, break the path into stages and show the customer each one. For a renovation company, "job done" comes after a month, but stages such as "measurements and estimate ready" or "materials delivered" happen every few days.

## Onboarding by industry

**Services (salon, clinic, car service).** Here onboarding happens at the first visit. Write down when the next visit is needed, book the day and send a reminder a day before. Within 24–48 hours of the first visit, ask a short question: "Are you happy with the result?" If there is a problem, this is the cheapest moment to fix it.

**B2B.** For a corporate client, onboarding means a kick-off meeting, one responsible person and a written plan. Both sides agree in advance who is responsible for what, and when and in what form reports will arrive. For example, Level Marketing structures its work in six steps: brief and point A, strategy (plan versus actual), content, launch, optimisation, and a report with point B. The monthly report is presented in the client's office. We describe what the first month with an agency looks like in [the first 30 days with a marketing agency](/en/blog/first-30-days-with-a-marketing-agency/).

**Education.** In education centres and courses, most students drop out in the first 2–4 weeks: they hit difficulties and do not notice progress. The fix is a curator, a level test in the first week, a short weekly update for parents (if the student is a child) and a call after the third lesson. More on education marketing: [marketing an education centre](/en/blog/marketing-an-education-centre/).

## A worked example

Illustrative example: an education centre takes on 100 new students a month at 600,000 soums a month. In the first month 30% drop out. Those who make it past the first month study for another 6 months on average.

- Without onboarding: 70 students stay.
- With a curator, a level test and a call after the third lesson, first-month churn falls to 18%: 82 students stay.
- The extra 12 students × 600,000 soums × 6 months = **43,200,000 soums** of additional revenue from a single monthly cohort.

If the curator's salary and time are a small fraction of that amount, onboarding pays better than raising the ad budget. The numbers are illustrative; plug in your own.

## What to measure

| Metric | How to calculate | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Time to first value | Days from purchase to the first result | The shorter it is, the lower the churn |
| Activation rate | Customers who completed the first step ÷ all new customers | Whether onboarding works |
| First-month retention | Customers remaining after 30 days ÷ new customers | The main outcome |
| Day-30 NPS | A survey on day 30 | Who will recommend you, who is at risk |
| First-month complaints | Number and topics of requests | Where misunderstandings happen |

For how to ask the NPS question and act on the results, read [what is NPS](/en/blog/what-is-nps/).

## Common mistakes

- Limiting onboarding to a welcome message followed by silence.
- Dumping everything on day one: instead of a 20-page manual, give one next step.
- No owner, so the customer does not know who to write to.
- Upselling in the first week. Offer the upsell after the first result; see [upselling and cross-selling](/en/blog/upsell-and-cross-sell/).
- One plan for everyone. First value differs in B2B, education and retail.
- Not measuring the outcome: onboarding exists, but nobody watches first-month retention.

## Questions and answers

### Does a small business need onboarding?

Yes, and in a small business it is simpler. Three messages and two calls, on purchase day and on days 3, 7, 14 and 30, are enough in many cases. The key is that they are logged in the CRM as tasks.

### Can onboarding be automated?

The messages can: a bot or CRM will send them on time. But the day-3 and day-30 calls are better made by people, because problems surface in conversation.

### What if the customer does not respond?

Try once more, then switch channels. If a customer does not get in touch at all in the first week, that is a risk signal: the responsible manager should call and help with the first step.

### How long does onboarding last?

Usually 30 days, and 60–90 days for complex B2B services. The finish line is not a date but the moment the customer has had a first result and uses the service independently.

## Conclusion

Onboarding is a continuation of the sale, not a separate department. Write the plan from purchase day to day 30, define first value for your business, assign an owner and measure first-month retention.
