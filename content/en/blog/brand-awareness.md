"Everyone knows us," say many business owners, though they've never measured it. **Brand awareness** isn't a feeling; it's a metric you can check with numbers. This article covers how to measure brand awareness, how to grow it and when spending money on it specifically pays off.

## What brand awareness is

Brand awareness is how well people know your name and whether they can recall it when they need what you sell. It has two levels:

- **Aided recognition**: show someone your name or logo and they say "yes, I've heard of it".
- **Unaided recall**: ask "where can I buy a sofa in Tashkent?" and they name you without prompting.

The second is worth more to a business. When people decide to buy, they usually choose from the 2–3 names that come to mind first. If you're not on that list, they won't even search for you.

Awareness is only one part of a brand. People can know your name and still not like you. What a brand consists of and how it differs from a logo is covered in [our article on what a brand is](/en/blog/what-is-a-brand/).

## Why brand awareness makes money

A known brand makes several things cheaper:

- **ads cost less**: people click on a familiar name faster and trust it sooner;
- **branded searches grow**: people type your name into Google or Yandex instead of a category, and that is the cheapest and warmest traffic you can get;
- **the sales cycle shortens**: your salespeople don't spend time introducing the company;
- **price pressure eases**: people are willing to pay a little more for a brand they know.

But awareness doesn't sell on its own. It makes selling easier. That's why you track it alongside sales metrics.

## How to measure it: 5 metrics

There's no single perfect number. You record several metrics the same way every month and watch the trend.

### 1. Branded search

How often people type your name into a search engine is one of the most honest signals. You can see it in two places:

- **Google Search Console**: in November 2025 Google added a filter to the Performance report that separates branded from non-branded queries ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/11/search-console-branded-filter)). It only works for sites with enough queries and impressions, so check whether it's available in your account. If it isn't, sort queries by your name manually.
- **Yandex Wordstat**: shows how many times a month your name is searched.

In Uzbekistan the same name gets written several ways: in Latin script, in Cyrillic, in Russian and with typos. Put every variant on one list; how to do that is explained in [our article on Latin and Cyrillic search](/en/blog/uzbek-search-latin-cyrillic/).

### 2. Direct visits

These are the times people found you without an ad: typing your web address, finding your Instagram profile through search, looking you up by name on Yandex Maps, calling you directly. Record each one in a single table every month. If these numbers grow even in months without advertising, awareness is working.

### 3. Surveys

The most accurate method is to ask people. A small business has two simple options:

- **Ask every new customer** "How did you hear about us?" and log the answer in your CRM. The share of "a friend recommended you" and "I've known you for a while" answers is a sign of awareness.
- **Once or twice a year**, run an Instagram or Telegram poll that lists 5–6 competitor names and asks which ones your audience knows.

How to catch people who remember you after offline advertising, using QR codes and promo codes, is covered in [our article on measuring offline advertising](/en/blog/measure-offline-advertising/).

### 4. Ad recall

Ad platforms have their own tools. Meta Ads Manager has an "Estimated ad recall lift" metric: Meta's model estimates how many extra people would remember your ad within two days of seeing it. It isn't a real survey, so treat the figure as approximate. Google Ads offers Brand Lift studies, which survey people who saw your ad and people who didn't and measure the difference ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12383701)). These tools have minimum budget requirements and other conditions, so check what's available in your account.

### 5. Mentions

Do people tag you on their own: Story tags, recommendations in Telegram groups, your name in reviews? This is hard to count precisely, but you can compare it month by month.

## A monthly measurement table

Put the metrics in one table and fill it in on the same day each month. The first month is your baseline.

| Metric | Source | October | November | December |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Branded searches (all spellings) | Search Console, Wordstat | | | |
| Direct visits to the website | Analytics | | | |
| Instagram profile visits | Instagram insights | | | |
| Searches by name on maps | Yandex/Google listing | | | |
| Share of "I already knew you" | CRM | | | |
| Tags and mentions | Counted by hand | | | |

Don't draw conclusions from a single month. Seasons, holidays and promotions all move the numbers. You need at least three months of trend and a comparison with the same month last year.

## How to grow brand awareness

### One clear idea

People can't remember lots of things at once. "Quality, cheap, fast, beautiful" is forgotten. "Durable furniture for families with kids" sticks. First write down, in one sentence, how you want to be remembered; this work is covered in detail in [our article on brand positioning](/en/blog/brand-positioning/).

### A consistent look

The same colours, typeface, logo placement and tone of voice across every channel. A brand that changes its style every month doesn't get recognised; people see a "new company" every time.

### Reach and repetition

People need to see your name several times before they remember it. That's why, for awareness, two things matter in advertising: the number of new people you reach (reach) and how many times each person sees the ad (frequency). The difference is explained in [our article on reach vs impressions](/en/blog/reach-vs-impressions/).

### Video and Reels

A short video makes people remember a name, a face and a voice together. Show the brand in the first seconds of every video, not at the end, because many people don't watch to the end.

### Offline touchpoints

Signage, the shop window, packaging, couriers' uniforms, a branded car: all of these give you free awareness every day. For a local business they often count for as much as online ads.

### Partnerships and events

A joint promotion with a business that shares your audience but isn't a competitor, a long-term collaboration with a blogger, an industry exhibition: all of these carry your name to new people.

## When to pay for brand awareness

Not every business needs a separate "awareness budget". Decide based on your situation.

| Situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| New business, few enquiries | Start with direct-response ads; build awareness only through local offline presence and content |
| Steady enquiries, but rising cost | Test a small share of budget on reach and video views |
| Many competitors, similar products | Awareness and positioning become the main task |
| New city or branch | Awareness ads before opening and in the first months |
| Expensive purchase with a long decision | Awareness matters: people need to see you several times before they buy |

> Illustrative example: a Tashkent furniture store with a steady flow of enquiries puts 10–20% of its budget into reach and video ads for three months and tracks how branded searches and cost per enquiry change over that period.

The rule is simple: judge money spent on awareness after three months, by branded searches, direct visits and cost per enquiry. Not by the click count of a single campaign.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating follower count as awareness.** Bought followers, or ones gathered through giveaways, won't remember your name.
- **Spending without measuring.** If you never recorded a baseline, you'll never know the result.
- **Expecting quick results.** Awareness grows over weeks and months, not in one campaign.
- **Switching off sales ads entirely.** Awareness doesn't replace direct-response advertising; it strengthens it.
- **A new style every month.** Without consistency, repetition doesn't work either.

## Questions and answers

### Can I measure brand awareness for free?

Mostly, yes. Search Console, Yandex Wordstat, Instagram insights, your maps listing statistics and a "how did you hear about us" question in the CRM are all free. Paid studies only make sense with larger budgets.

### Does Instagram reach show brand awareness?

Partly. Reach tells you how many people saw you, not how many remembered you. So look at reach together with branded searches and direct visits. If reach grows but searches don't change, people are seeing you but not remembering you.

### How long do awareness ads take to work?

There's no fixed timeline, but watch for at least three months. Small changes in branded searches and direct visits may show up in the first month, while the effect on cost per enquiry usually appears later.

### Does a small business need brand awareness?

Local businesses often build awareness without ads: signage, maps listings, reviews, Reels and word of mouth. A dedicated awareness budget makes sense once your enquiry flow is steady and its cost starts to rise.

## Conclusion

Brand awareness is people's ability to recall you at the right moment. Judge it by numbers, not feelings: branded searches, direct visits, surveys, ad recall and mentions. One clear idea, a consistent look and enough repetition grow awareness, and the spend is judged on a three-month trend.
