Where can you find people who look like your best customers? Meta's answer is **lookalike audiences**: you give the algorithm a sample, and it finds new people similar to it. Here is how lookalikes work, which source to build them from and which mistakes to avoid.

## How lookalikes work

You provide a **source audience** — for example, your list of buyers. Meta analyses what these people have in common — interests, behaviour, demographics — and gathers the people in your chosen country who resemble them most. You end up advertising to an audience close to your real customers without guessing interests by hand.

## Which source to use

| Source | Quality | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer list (phones, emails) | Highest | Export it from your CRM |
| Pixel: Purchase or Lead | High | If your site has tracking |
| People who messaged you in Direct | Medium–high | Good for message campaigns |
| People engaged with your Instagram profile | Medium | Large but "warmer" |
| People who watched 75% of a video | Medium | If your content is strong |

The rule is simple: the closer the source is to a sale, the better the lookalike works. The source needs at least 100 people from one country, but results are more stable with hundreds or thousands.

## Which percentage to choose

A lookalike covers 1% to 10% of a country's audience. 1% is the most similar but small; 10% is broad but less similar. Meta's audience in Uzbekistan is smaller than in large markets, so 1% is sometimes too narrow. A practical approach: start with 1–3%, then test 3–5% and broader separately.

## Step by step

1. In Audiences, create a source audience or upload a customer list.
2. Choose "Create lookalike audience" and set Uzbekistan as the country.
3. Pick the percentage — for example, 1%, 3% and 5% as three separate audiences.
4. Test each in its own ad set with the same creative.
5. Exclude existing customers — there's no need to advertise to them as if they were new.

## Common mistakes

- **A weak source.** Build from "buyers", not "all leads".
- **An outdated list.** Refresh the source every 1–2 months.
- **Overlapping audiences.** Several ad sets compete for the same people and push up costs.
- **Ignoring the creative.** Even the best audience won't save a weak creative.

Meta now often recommends Advantage+ audience, which gives the algorithm more freedom. In those campaigns you can add a lookalike as a "suggestion" the algorithm uses as a starting point. Only an [A/B test](/en/blog/ab-testing-ad-creatives/) will show which approach works better for you.

## How to prepare a customer list

- **Columns:** phone, email, name — each field in its own column.
- **Phone format:** with the country code, for example +998901234567.
- **File:** CSV or TXT.
- **Clean-up:** remove duplicates, test entries and employees' numbers.

Meta hashes uploaded data before matching, but you should still respect legal requirements and customer consent when using customer data. Upload only the fields you need.

## Value-based lookalikes

If you add each customer's purchase value to the list, Meta can build a value-based lookalike: the algorithm looks for people similar not just to buyers, but to those who spend more. This is especially useful for businesses where the average order value matters.

## Creative for a lookalike audience

A lookalike audience doesn't know you yet — it isn't retargeting. So the ad should open with a problem or benefit, show social proof and end with a simple offer. Retargeting creatives are different — see [How retargeting works](/en/blog/how-retargeting-works/).

## How to evaluate the results

Compare the lookalike with a broad audience or Advantage+ using the same creative and budget. Wait at least a week, then compare cost per lead and, if possible, cost per sale. Also check that the audiences don't overlap.

## When a lookalike doesn't fit

- **A very small area.** If a clinic or café serves only a radius of a few kilometres, a lookalike gets too narrow — a broad audience often works better there.
- **A new business.** With no buyers yet, the source will be weak.
- **A narrow B2B market.** If your customers are a few dozen companies, direct sales and LinkedIn are more effective.

In these cases, build your customer base first, then come back to lookalikes.

## Questions and answers

### What if the source is too small?

Combine several sources: buyers, leads and people who messaged you. Or start with a larger but slightly "colder" source, such as people who engaged with your profile.

### How often is a lookalike updated?

Meta updates lookalike audiences automatically, but the list you uploaded doesn't update itself. Re-upload it regularly so new buyers are included.

### Which is better — lookalike or interest targeting?

It depends on the business — only a test will tell. A lookalike built from a quality source often beats interests, but that isn't a rule.

### Is uploading customer data safe?

Meta matches the data in hashed form. Even so, upload only the fields you need, keep the file somewhere secure and follow data-handling rules.

### Can Pixel data be a source?

Yes, if the Pixel is set up correctly on your site: people who triggered a "Purchase" or "Lead" event make a quality source. Setup is covered in [Meta Pixel and Conversions API](/en/blog/meta-pixel-and-conversions-api/).

### Are there lookalikes on Google or Telegram?

Google Ads has replaced similar audiences with automatic expansion tools. Telegram Ads mainly targets by channels and topics. So lookalikes are primarily a Meta advertising tool.

## Conclusion

Lookalikes are a fast way to turn your existing customer base into a flow of new customers. A quality source, testing several percentages and excluding current customers are the three main rules. At Level Marketing we build audiences from the buyers in your CRM to bring cost per lead down.
