As a business grows, someone has to own marketing. There are three main routes: hire a freelancer, bring a marketer in-house or work with an agency. Each has advantages and risks, and the "best" option depends on your size, your goals and how much you can manage yourself. This article compares the three models on cost structure, skills and control.

![The three models in brief](/img/blog/illus/agentlik-frilanser-yoki-shtat-1.jpg 1200x675)

## The three models in brief

| | Freelancer | In-house marketer | Agency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who | One specialist working with several clients | Your employee | A team: strategy, paid ads, video, design, social |
| Cost structure | Monthly fee or per project | Salary + taxes + workspace + tools | Monthly service fee |
| Skills | Usually one area | One person — can't know everything | Several specialists |
| Management | You manage | You manage | Through a project manager |
| Risk | Disappears, falls ill, too busy | Leaves — the knowledge leaves too | Little attention for a small client (with the wrong agency) |

## Freelancer: when it's the right choice

**Pros:** flexible, usually cheaper, quick to start on a specific task.

**Cons:** one person knows one area well — a paid ads specialist won't shoot video, a designer won't set up ads. Working for several clients means they may be busy. If they fall ill or leave, work stops.

**Who it suits:** a small business with one clear task (say, paid ads only), where you handle content and strategy yourself.

What to check when choosing a freelancer — [how to choose a paid ads specialist](/en/blog/how-to-choose-a-paid-ads-specialist/).

## In-house marketer: when it's the right choice

**Pros:** full time on your business, knows the product deeply, close to internal processes.

**Cons:** one person can't be equally strong at strategy, paid ads, video, design and analytics — you'll still need outside specialists. The cost isn't just salary: taxes, workspace, tools and subscriptions, training. Good marketers are hard to find and keep; when they leave, the knowledge leaves with them.

**Who it suits:** companies with constant, large-scale marketing, especially those building a marketing department. An in-house marketer often manages an agency or freelancers.

What social media managers and marketers do — [what an SMM manager does](/en/blog/what-an-smm-manager-does/).

## Agency: when it's the right choice

**Pros:** one contract, a whole team — strategy, paid ads, video, design, social, analytics. If one person leaves or falls ill, work continues. Experience across industries. No running between different freelancers.

**Cons:** with the wrong agency — template work, little attention for smaller clients, opaque reporting. That's why the choice and the contract matter.

**Who it suits:** businesses that need full-cycle marketing — content, ads and analytics — but aren't ready to build their own marketing department.

## How to compare the real cost

Many people compare only the monthly fee and get it wrong. Look at the full picture:

| Cost item | Freelancer | In-house | Agency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base payment | Yes | Salary | Service fee |
| Taxes and workspace | No | Yes | No |
| Video and design | Separate specialists | Separate specialists | Often included |
| Tools and subscriptions | Sometimes | Yes | Often included |
| Your management time | A lot | A lot | Less |
| Risk of work stopping | High | Medium | Low |

Run the numbers for your own case: one model looks cheap until you add extra specialists and your own time. Budget structure — [planning a marketing budget](/en/blog/planning-a-marketing-budget/).

## The hybrid model

For many growing businesses the best option is a hybrid:

- **An internal owner** (a marketer or the boss) — goals, product knowledge, the link with sales;
- **An agency** — execution: content, ads, analytics, reporting.

Business knowledge stays in the company, while a full team does the execution.

## Which model to choose: 5 questions

1. **What do you need** — one task (paid ads) or the full cycle (strategy + content + ads)?
2. **Who will manage marketing** — do you have the time?
3. **How much marketing is there** — several channels and constant content, or one campaign?
4. **What do you lose if work stops** — a season, an opening, a big promotion?
5. **How will you measure results** — are reporting and KPIs agreed?

Which metrics to agree with an agency — [KPIs in an agency contract](/en/blog/kpis-in-an-agency-contract/).

## Must-haves with any model

- The ad account, page and pixel **in your name**, with specialists working as partners.
- A monthly report: spend, leads, cost per lead, sales, next steps — [what an ad report should include](/en/blog/what-an-ad-report-should-include/).
- A clear goal and point A — [marketing brief](/en/blog/how-to-fill-in-a-marketing-brief/).
- Feedback from sales on lead quality.

## Common mistakes

- Comparing only the monthly fee, not the full cost.
- Expecting one marketer to do everything: strategy, video, ads, design.
- Opening the ad account in the specialist's or agency's name.
- Working without agreeing how results are measured.
- Switching model and specialist every 2–3 months — results never get time to build.

## Questions and answers

### What's better for a small business?

If you only need ads set up, a freelancer is enough. If you need content, video and ads together, an agency is often more convenient — and cheaper in total cost.

### If I have an in-house marketer, do I still need an agency?

Often yes: the marketer handles strategy and oversight, the agency handles execution — video, design, ads. This hybrid works well in many companies.

### How can I vet an agency?

Case studies and numbers, a sample report, who will own the ad account, a first-month plan. A free audit is a good way to see how an agency thinks.

### When should I think about switching models?

After at least 2–3 months of data — if reports aren't transparent, tests aren't run, or metrics worsen without explanation.

## Conclusion

A freelancer suits one specific task, an in-house marketer suits a company building a marketing department, and an agency suits a business that needs full-cycle marketing.
