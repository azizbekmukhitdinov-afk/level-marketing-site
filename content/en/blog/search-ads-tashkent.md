Instagram ads show people something they weren't thinking about yet. Search ads do the opposite: they show your ad at the moment someone is actively searching — "air conditioner repair Tashkent", "driving school Yunusabad", "freight transport price". It's a channel that works where demand already exists. Here's when you need search ads, how Google and Yandex differ and how to start in Tashkent.

## What search ads are

Search (contextual) advertising is an ad matched to what someone types into a search engine. You choose keywords, and when people search for them, your ad appears among the results. You usually pay per **click**, not per impression.

How it differs from targeted ads — [what is targeted advertising](/en/blog/what-is-targeted-advertising/). Comparing platforms — [Google Ads vs Meta Ads](/en/blog/google-ads-vs-meta-ads/).

## Which businesses it suits best

- **Urgent needs**: repair technicians, tow trucks, plumbers, freight.
- **Services people actively search for**: driving schools, education centres, clinics, notaries, legal services.
- **B2B**: wholesale, manufacturing, corporate services — buyers search and compare. More in [B2B marketing](/en/blog/b2b-marketing-uzbekistan/).
- **Products with specific names**: things searched by model, brand or size.

Less suitable: impulse purchases and new products people don't know about yet — targeted ads and video work better there.

## Google or Yandex?

| | Google Ads | Yandex Direct |
|---|---|---|
| Who uses it more | Many users, especially via Android and Chrome | Part of the Russian-language searchers |
| Languages | Uzbek (Latin), Russian, English | Mostly Russian |
| Extras | Lead form assets, YouTube, Maps | Maps, Yandex services, options for ads in Telegram channels |

Each system's share varies by niche and audience. The most reliable approach is to start with one and compare results. More on Yandex — [Yandex Direct in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/yandex-direct-in-uzbekistan/).

## Keywords: two languages, three scripts

In Tashkent, the same service is searched in several ways: Uzbek in Latin, Uzbek in Cyrillic and Russian. Your keyword list should cover all of them. More in [Uzbek search](/en/blog/uzbek-search-latin-cyrillic/).

Group your keywords:

| Group | Example (illustrative: AC repair) | Intent |
|---|---|---|
| Service + city | "air conditioner repair Tashkent" | High |
| Problem | "air conditioner not cooling" | High |
| Price | "air conditioner cleaning price" | High |
| Generic | "air conditioner" | Low — often no buying intent |

**Negative keywords** matter too: "free", "DIY", "job", "download" — keep your ad from showing for those searches.

## The ad and the landing page

- the searched term and your main advantage in the headline;
- a price guide or a clear offer — [should you show the price in your ads](/en/blog/should-you-show-price-in-ads/);
- phone and address extensions;
- a landing page about that exact service, not a generic home page.

No website? A simple landing page is often enough — [does a business need a website](/en/blog/does-a-business-need-a-website/). Google Ads also offers a lead form asset inside the ad, so people can submit a request without visiting a site. How to build the form — [lead form questions](/en/blog/lead-form-questions/).

## Budget and getting started

- **A small test**: one system, one service, 2–4 weeks.
- **Area**: Tashkent city or the districts you serve.
- **Timing**: show ads during working hours — if you can reply to leads immediately.
- **Tracking**: leads land in the CRM with a source tag — [UTM tags](/en/blog/utm-tags/).

## Measuring results

Search ads are judged by sales too:

- cost per click and click-to-lead rate;
- cost per lead;
- lead-to-sale rate and cost per sale;
- calls — if they come via the phone number in the ad, count them as well.

More in [cost per lead](/en/blog/cost-per-lead-cpl-how-to-lower/) and [how to calculate ROMI](/en/blog/how-to-calculate-romi/).

## Common mistakes

- Only generic keywords — the budget goes to the merely curious.
- A campaign without negative keywords.
- The ad leads to the home page rather than the relevant service.
- Keywords in only one language.
- Slow replies to leads — the searcher clicks another ad.

## Questions and answers

### Are search ads more expensive than targeted ads?

Clicks are often pricier, but intent is higher too. Compare cost per lead and per sale.

### Can I run search ads without a website?

Some formats — call ads or lead forms — work without a site, but a landing page usually improves results.

### Should search and targeted ads run together?

For many businesses, yes: targeted ads create demand, search ads capture existing demand.

### When will I see results?

First leads can arrive quickly, but tuning keywords and negatives takes 2–4 weeks.

## Conclusion

Search ads work where demand already exists: people search, you answer. They're especially strong for urgent services, actively searched services and B2B. Keywords in two languages and three scripts, negative keywords, a precise landing page and fast replies are the foundation.
