A carousel is a single post with several photos or videos that people swipe through. Reels are good for reaching new people; carousels suit content that gets read carefully, saved and sent to a friend. This article covers carousel structure from the cover to the call to action, the main carousel types, design rules and how to judge the result.

## Why use a carousel

A carousel's strength is sequence. One photo makes one point; a carousel unfolds an idea step by step: problem, cause, solution, example, conclusion. The viewer swipes at their own pace and stops where they need to. That makes the carousel the best format for guides, checklists and comparisons.

According to Meta's developer documentation, a single carousel can hold up to 20 images, videos or a mix of the two ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/)). But 20 is a ceiling, not a target. The number of slides should match the number of ideas.

Adam Mosseri explained in 2023 how Feed ranking works: the posts you have liked, shared, saved or commented on help Instagram understand your interests ([Instagram](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)). The carousel is precisely the format that invites saving and sharing. More on ranking in [the Instagram algorithm in 2026](/en/blog/how-the-instagram-algorithm-works/).

## 8 types of carousel

| Type | Main purpose | Example topic |
|---|---|---|
| Step-by-step guide | Saves | "Cleaning a carpet at home: 6 steps" |
| Checklist | Saves | "10 things to check before a renovation" |
| Myth versus fact | Shares, comments | "5 myths about coffee" |
| Comparison | Helping people choose | "Laminate or parquet" |
| List of mistakes | Saves | "7 mistakes when furnishing a kids' room" |
| Story or case | Trust | "The problem a client came to us with" |
| Before–after | Showing results | Renovation, furniture, cleaning |
| Catalogue or collection | Choosing a product | "8 looks from the winter collection" |

Each type has its own job. Guides and checklists earn saves, myths and comparisons earn shares and discussion, cases and before–after posts build trust. We covered the before–after format [in a separate article](/en/blog/before-after-carousel/), and story structures in [brand storytelling](/en/blog/brand-storytelling/).

## Structure: from cover to call to action

A carousel has three parts: the cover (stops the scroll), the body (delivers on the promise) and the ending (gives the next step).

### The cover: slide one

The cover has one job: stop the viewer and move them to slide two. Formulas that work:

- **Number + result:** "7 habits that cut your electricity bill."
- **Problem as a question:** "Why did your new sofa sag within a year?"
- **Contrast:** "5 inexpensive materials that look expensive."
- **Mistake:** "This is how most parents choose kids' shoes wrong."

Six to ten words are enough on the cover. Make the text large enough to read on a phone in one second. The logic of Reels hooks applies to carousel covers too; ideas are in [10 Reels hooks](/en/blog/reels-hooks/).

### The body: one slide, one idea

The main rule: one idea per slide. Slide two confirms the promise ("These habits can cut the bill by 15–20%", an illustrative example) or explains why it is worth reading on. The following slides are the points. Each slide should make sense on its own, because someone may screenshot it and send it to a friend.

### The ending: summary and call to action

The last two slides are a short summary (the whole carousel on one screen) and the call to action. Make it specific: "Save this", "Send it to a friend who is starting a renovation" or "Request a free measurement via the link in our profile". Use a lead form or a link rather than "comment + below", so the enquiry reaches the sales team instead of getting lost.

## Worked example: an 8-slide carousel

Illustrative example. A carousel for a furniture workshop: "6 questions to ask before ordering a kitchen".

| Slide | Text | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Ask these 6 questions before ordering a kitchen" | Cover, stop the scroll |
| 2 | "They will save you from costly rework" | Promise |
| 3 | "What is the carcass made of?" + a short note | Idea 1 |
| 4 | "How many years of warranty on the hardware?" | Idea 2 |
| 5 | "Are measuring and installation included in the price?" | Ideas 3–4 |
| 6 | "What is the lead time, and will you show a drawing first?" | Ideas 5–6 |
| 7 | "In short: material, warranty, measuring, installation, lead time, drawing" | Summary |
| 8 | "Save this. Free measurement via the link in our profile" | Call to action |

Now, how to judge the result. Compare two posts with illustrative numbers:

| Metric | A — a nice photo | B — 8-slide carousel |
|---|---|---|
| Reach | 12,000 | 8,000 |
| Likes | 480 | 320 |
| Saves | 60 (0.5%) | 240 (3%) |
| Shares | 24 (0.2%) | 96 (1.2%) |
| Leads via profile link | 2 | 9 |

Post A wins on reach and likes. But relative to reach, post B gets six times more saves and six times more shares, and more than four times as many leads. So judge carousels by saves ÷ reach and shares ÷ reach, not by likes. How to calculate engagement is covered in [what is engagement rate](/en/blog/engagement-rate/).

## Design rules

- **The first slide sets the format.** According to Meta's documentation, all images in a carousel are cropped based on the first one ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/)). So make every slide the same size. Most brands choose the vertical 4:5 format (for example 1080 × 1350 pixels), which takes up more of the feed than a square.
- **Leave margins.** Do not push text to the edge; edges can be cropped in the profile grid and on different screens.
- **One template, one font family, one accent colour.** The carousel should look like one piece. The minimum on brand colours and fonts is in [visual identity for small business](/en/blog/visual-identity-for-small-business/).
- **Up to 30 words per slide.** A guideline, not a hard rule. Need more text? Add a slide.
- **A continuation cue.** A small arrow on the cover, a slide number (3/8) or a graphic element that runs over onto the next slide nudges people to swipe.
- **Contrast.** If text sits on a coloured background or a photo, add a dark overlay. Test readability on a phone in sunlight.

## Caption and production workflow

The caption should add to the carousel, not repeat it: why the topic matters, extra detail and the call to action. Put the strongest sentence on the first line, since that is what shows before "more". The basics of writing copy are in [what is copywriting](/en/blog/what-is-copywriting/).

The production order is simple: write the text of every slide in one document and proofread it, then move to design. Writing text inside the design tool doubles the time. With a ready template one carousel takes a few hours. It helps to build 3–4 templates up front for different types (guide, checklist, comparison and case), so the designer only swaps text and photos and the feed looks consistent.

## 7 common mistakes

1. **A logo or a generic photo on the cover.** No hook, no reason to swipe.
2. **A wall of text.** A whole article on one slide.
3. **The main point at the end.** If the most valuable tip is on slide 9, nobody gets there.
4. **Slides of different sizes.** Everything is cropped to the first slide and text disappears.
5. **Small fonts.** Fine on a computer, unreadable on a phone.
6. **No call to action.** The viewer could have saved the post or left a lead, but nobody asked.
7. **Judging by likes only.** The most useful formats get dropped as "not working".

## Questions and answers

### How many slides should a carousel have?

As many as you have ideas. Guides and checklists usually need 6–10: cover, promise, 4–6 ideas, summary and call to action. The ceiling is 20, but there is no need to fill it.

### Can I add video to a carousel?

Yes, a carousel can mix photos and videos. Video works well on slide two or three: the cover stops the scroll, the video shows the process.

### Which is better, a carousel or a Reel?

They do different jobs. Reels tend to reach new people, carousels get saved and read closely. You can make both from one topic: the Reel sparks interest, the carousel explains in detail. Alternate them in your [content plan](/en/blog/instagram-content-plan/).

### Can a carousel be used in ads?

Yes, Meta ads have a carousel format, but its requirements differ from an organic post. Before adapting a carousel that earned a lot of saves into an ad, check the current requirements in Ads Manager.

## Conclusion

A good carousel is a clear structure first and nice design second: a cover that stops the scroll, one idea per slide, a summary and a clear call to action. Measure it by saves, shares and leads rather than likes, and you will see which topics actually help your audience.
