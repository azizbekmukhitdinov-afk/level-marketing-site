Many business owners sign with a marketing agency and expect leads to start pouring in. A week passes, leads are few, and worry sets in: "money's going out, no results". In reality the first month is a period of preparation, testing and learning — and if it goes right, the following months' results depend on it. This article explains how the first 30 days with a marketing agency should go, week by week, what you'll be asked for and which signals to watch.

![Why the first month is different](/img/blog/illus/agentlik-bilan-birinchi-30-kun-1.jpg 1200x675)

## Why the first month is different

In month one, the agency doesn't know three things: who your real customer is, which message works and what a lead costs. Only testing can reveal them. So the first month's goal isn't maximum sales, but to:

- measure point A precisely — where you are now;
- set up tracking — to see where every lead comes from;
- test several hypotheses — audiences, creatives, offers;
- build a concrete plan for month two.

Level Marketing works in this order: brief and point A → strategy → content → launch → optimisation → report and point B.

## Week 1. Brief, point A and access

**What happens:**

1. **Brief** — an in-depth conversation about the business, product, prices, customers, competitors and goals. How to fill it in — [marketing brief](/en/blog/how-to-fill-in-a-marketing-brief/).
2. **Point A** — current numbers: monthly sales, leads, cost per lead (if you've advertised), profile condition.
3. **Access** — ad account, Business Manager, Instagram, website, pixel, CRM.
4. **Audit** — a review of current ads, profile, website and sales process. What an audit checks — [marketing audit checklist](/en/blog/marketing-audit-checklist/).

**What you need to provide:**

- 1–2 hours for the brief, with the main decision-maker present;
- sales figures for the last 3–6 months (even rough ones);
- access granted **as a partner** — not a password. The ad account and page should stay in your name — this matters.

## Week 2. Strategy and plan

**What happens:**

- **Strategy** — which platforms, what offer, which audience segments, what budget;
- **Monthly plan (plan/actual)** — expected leads, cost per lead, spend;
- **Creative plan** — which videos and images are needed, who will be on camera;
- **Tracking** — pixel, UTM tags, a source field in the CRM.

What a one-page strategy looks like — [one-page marketing strategy](/en/blog/one-page-marketing-strategy/); UTMs — [UTM tags](/en/blog/utm-tags/).

**What you need to do:** read the strategy, ask questions and approve it. "Why this platform?", "Where do these numbers come from?" — a good agency answers specifically.

## Week 3. Content and launch

**What happens:**

- **Shoot day** — the videographer and designer produce Reels, creatives and posts. At Level Marketing a finished Reel takes 48 hours;
- **Profile clean-up** — bio, highlights, prices, first posts;
- **Campaign launch** — testing several audiences and creatives;
- **Daily monitoring** — cost per lead and early signals.

How creatives are produced — [how ad creatives are produced](/en/blog/how-ad-creatives-are-produced/).

**What you need to provide:**

- time, a location and product for the shoot;
- **fast replies** to leads — that's your side. Even the best ads fail with slow replies. Why — [lead response time](/en/blog/lead-response-time/).

![Week 4. Optimisation and the first report](/img/blog/illus/agentlik-bilan-birinchi-30-kun-2.jpg 1200x675)

## Week 4. Optimisation and the first report

**What happens:**

- based on test results, budget moves to the audiences and creatives that work;
- what doesn't work is paused and replaced with new variants;
- **the first report** — spend, leads, cost per lead, what worked, what didn't, next month's plan.

At Level Marketing, the report is presented in person at the client's office every month on the project's date. What a report should include — [what an ad report should include](/en/blog/what-an-ad-report-should-include/).

**What you need to provide:** data from your sales team on **lead quality**: how many leads became real customers, how many sales. Without it, the agency optimises for lead count rather than sales.

## What to realistically expect in month one

| Metric | Realistic expectation in month one |
|---|---|
| Leads | Arrive from the first days |
| Cost per lead | Unstable, jumps during tests |
| Sales | First sales if the sales cycle is short |
| Stable results | Usually from month 2–3 |
| The most important result | Knowing what works, and a concrete plan |

What to expect over 90 days — [what to expect from marketing in 90 days](/en/blog/what-to-expect-from-marketing-in-90-days/).

## Warning signs

Be careful if, in the first month:

- you're refused access to the ad account, or it's opened in the agency's name;
- there's no strategy or plan — "let's launch and see";
- results are guaranteed: "500 leads a month, for sure";
- the report shows only reach and likes, no leads or costs;
- your questions don't get specific answers.

Questions to ask when choosing an agency or specialist — [how to choose a paid ads specialist](/en/blog/how-to-choose-a-paid-ads-specialist/); metrics for the contract — [KPIs in an agency contract](/en/blog/kpis-in-an-agency-contract/).

## Common mistakes (on the client's side)

- Demanding results in week one and stopping the test.
- Changing strategy daily — "this audience today, another tomorrow".
- Replying to leads late and concluding "the ads are bad".
- Not sharing sales data.
- Not making time for content — ads keep running on old creatives.

## Questions and answers

### When will the first leads arrive?

Usually in the first days after launch. A stable cost per lead comes after testing and optimisation.

### What budget is needed in month one?

It depends on the industry, city and goal. After the brief, the agency calculates a specific figure and the expected cost per lead. How to plan a budget — [planning a marketing budget](/en/blog/planning-a-marketing-budget/).

### If month one shows no results, should I end the partnership?

First understand why: is the problem in the ads, the offer or lead handling? If the report is transparent and the plan concrete, month two usually brings much more data.

### What should I monitor?

Weekly, short numbers: spend, leads, cost per lead and lead quality from sales. Monthly, a detailed report and next month's plan.

## Conclusion

The first 30 days with a marketing agency aren't a magic result but a foundation: point A, tracking, strategy, content and tests. If this month goes right, months two and three rely on data rather than luck.
