"If we put the price in the ad, competitors will see it and customers will get scared" — business owners say this a lot. On the other side are hundreds of "how much?" messages in DMs and customers who vanish after getting the answer. Showing the price in an ad is neither a good nor a bad decision — it's a **filter**. It reduces lead volume but improves lead quality. Here's when you need that filter and when it hurts.

## Price is a filter, and the filter has a cost

When an ad shows the price, two things happen:

1. People for whom the price doesn't work don't submit a lead. You get fewer leads, and cost per lead may rise.
2. Those who do submit already know the price. Conversations are shorter and more of them turn into sales.

Without a price, the opposite: lots of cheap leads, but many of them say "I'll think about it" once they hear the price. Your sales team's time goes into those conversations.

So the question isn't "should we show the price?" It's: **which bottleneck is more expensive for you — ad budget or sales time?**

## When showing the price pays off

- **Price is your advantage.** If you're cheaper than the market or have a clear package price, hiding it makes no sense.
- **Many leads, few sales.** When your team is drowning in "too expensive for me" conversations. More in [the cheap leads trap](/en/blog/cheap-leads-trap/).
- **Standard product.** A course, a membership, a product in a set size — people choose by comparing, and an ad without a price is inconvenient.
- **Impulse purchase.** For low-cost products, price is a big part of the decision: "149,000 soum, delivered today" (illustrative example).

## When hiding the price is right

- **Every order has a different price.** Renovation, custom furniture, construction — you can't quote without measurements and materials. A "free estimate" offer works better here.
- **Value needs explaining.** A premium service's price looks high without context. Show the result and the difference first, then the price. More in [premium segment marketing](/en/blog/premium-segment-marketing/).
- **A product new to the market.** People don't yet know what to compare it with.

## The middle ground: "from" prices and ranges

For many businesses the best option is neither the full price nor silence:

| Format | Example (illustrative) | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| "From" price | "Kitchens from 9 million soum" | Made-to-order production |
| Range | "Dental implant: 4–7 million soum" | Services, clinics |
| Package price | "3-month course — 1,200,000 soum" | Education, memberships |
| Monthly payment | "From 450,000 soum a month" | Expensive goods, instalments |

A "from" price must be honest: if nothing can actually be bought for 9 million, that's misleading advertising. Instalment prices have their own requirements — see [instalment advertising rules](/en/blog/installment-advertising-rules/).

## The law: prices in soum only

Article 16 of the Law "On Advertising" prohibits stating prices (tariffs) in foreign currency in advertising ([lex.uz](https://lex.uz/docs/6052631)). This is the most common mistake in real estate, car and electronics ads. If your price depends on the exchange rate, state it in soum or write "check the price with our manager", but don't put a dollar figure in the ad.

Another rule: key conditions can't be hidden in small print. A big "0% interest" with an invisible "3 months only" is also a violation. More in [advertising law in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/advertising-law-uzbekistan/).

## How to test the price

Testing is easier than arguing. A simple setup:

1. **One creative, two versions**: with and without the price. Everything else identical.
2. **Separate ads** in one ad set or through the A/B test tool — see [A/B testing ad creatives](/en/blog/ab-testing-ad-creatives/).
3. **7–14 days** and at least a few dozen leads per version.
4. **Measure sales**, not leads. For each version: cost per lead, lead-to-sale rate, cost per sale.

Illustrative result:

| Metric | No price | With price |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per lead | 40,000 soum | 70,000 soum |
| Lead-to-sale rate | 5% | 12% |
| Cost per sale | 800,000 soum | 583,000 soum |

In this example the lead got more expensive, but the sale got cheaper, and the team spent half as much time on empty conversations. Your result may differ — which is exactly why you test.

## Where to show the price: ad, profile, salesperson

Price doesn't live only in the ad. Customers meet it in three places:

- **In the ad** — as a filter or an advantage.
- **On the profile** — a "Prices" highlight. Even if the ad has no price, an interested person should find it on the profile.
- **In the salesperson's reply** — a template answer to "how much?". That's an art of its own, see [replying to "how much?"](/en/blog/replying-to-how-much/).

The worst option: no price anywhere, and the reply is "send us a DM". People don't ask twice.

## Questions and answers

### What if competitors see our price and undercut it?

A competitor can learn your price with one message — hiding it in ads won't stop them. If you compete on price alone, that's an offer problem, not an advertising one.

### Do leads get more expensive with a price?

Often, yes. But cost per sale may fall. So judge by sales.

### Can I write "price in DM"?

You can, but it puts many people off. If you don't want to show the price, offer a specific next step: "estimate in 5 minutes".

### What happens if I show the price in dollars?

The law prohibits foreign-currency prices in advertising, and improper advertising carries a fine. State prices in soum.

## Conclusion

A price in an ad is a filter: it lowers lead volume and raises quality. Standard product, a strong price or lots of empty conversations — show the price. Price depends on the order or value needs explaining — use a "from" price or a range. And always test by sales.
