"How much does SMM cost?" Ask two agencies or freelancers and you'll get two different prices — sometimes several times apart. Business owners compare the prices, but they're actually comparing different services. Here's what makes up the price of social media management, which pricing models exist and how to compare two offers properly.

## What makes up the price

"SMM" can cover very different work. The main components that affect price:

| Component | What's included | Effect on price |
|---|---|---|
| Strategy | Audience, competitors, content direction | Medium |
| Content plan and copy | A monthly plan, post and Reels scripts | Medium |
| Design | Posts, carousels, stories | Medium |
| Video production | Shooting, editing, Reels | High |
| Page management | Publishing, replying to comments and DMs | Medium |
| Paid social | Setup and optimisation | Medium (budget separate) |
| Reporting and analysis | Results, conclusions, next steps | Low–medium |

The biggest difference is usually **video**: simple phone-shot Reels and a shoot day with a crew are priced completely differently. What SMM is — [what is SMM](/en/blog/what-is-smm/).

## Pricing models

- **A monthly package** — a fixed volume, e.g. X posts, Y Reels, Z stories. The most common model.
- **Hourly or per project** — one-off work: rebuilding a profile, a content plan, a shoot day.
- **Fixed fee + performance bonus** — a base payment plus extra for agreed metrics. It's important to define those metrics precisely — [KPIs in an agency contract](/en/blog/kpis-in-an-agency-contract/).

**Important:** the ad budget is usually not included in the service fee — it's paid directly to the platform. Check this in any offer.

## How to compare two offers

Compare **volume and accountability for results**, not price. Ask each offer:

1. How many posts, Reels and stories a month — exact numbers?
2. Who films and edits the video? How many shoots?
3. Who replies to comments and DMs, and how fast?
4. Are paid ads included? Is the budget separate?
5. What reporting, and how often?
6. Which metrics will we measure results by?
7. What are the termination terms?

An illustrative example: offer A is cheaper but includes no video and no reporting; offer B costs more but includes 8 Reels a month and a monthly report meeting. Work out the price per Reel, and B may turn out cheaper.

## Where cheap SMM gets expensive

- Lots of content, but not connected to sales — [why social media doesn't sell](/en/blog/why-social-media-doesnt-sell/).
- Reports cover only likes and reach — leads and sales are invisible.
- Template design — your brand looks like everyone else's.
- Nobody replies — customers get lost in the DMs.
