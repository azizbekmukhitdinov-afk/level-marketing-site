Many founders spend a year building a product, launch it and discover nobody needs it. An **MVP** (minimum viable product) is the simplest version of an idea, built to test it on real customers with the least time and money. The term spread widely through Eric Ries's book *The Lean Startup* (2011).

## Why you need an MVP

- **To test demand** — will people actually pay?
- **To reduce risk** — before a big investment.
- **To learn fast** — to see what customers want.
- **To give investors and partners evidence** — in numbers.

An MVP isn't a "bad product". It's the smallest version that solves the main problem.

## 6 types of MVP

1. **A landing page** — a product description and an "order" button — [a landing page that converts](/en/blog/landing-page-that-converts/).
2. **A manual service** — it looks automated from outside, but a person does the work.
3. **Pre-orders** — collecting payments before the product is ready.
4. **A video or prototype** — showing how it works.
5. **A single-feature product** — only the most important capability.
6. **A pilot** — a trial with one customer or in one area.

## Small business examples (illustrative example)

- **Healthy meal delivery**: start with a Telegram channel and 3 menus, taking orders by hand — before building a kitchen.
- **An online course**: start with 1 live webinar and pre-payment — before recording videos.
- **A new furniture model**: start with a 3D render and ads — before production.

## Testing an MVP with ads

1. Prepare a simple page or Instagram profile.
2. Run ads on a small budget for 1–2 weeks.
3. Collect sign-ups or pre-payments through a lead form.
4. Call the people who signed up and ask them questions.

Be honest: if the product isn't ready yet, don't hide it — call it "early sign-up" or "first batch".

## Which numbers to watch

- **Cost per lead** — [cost per lead (CPL)](/en/blog/cost-per-lead-cpl-how-to-lower/).
- **Conversion rate** — from visit to sign-up — [what is conversion rate](/en/blog/what-is-conversion-rate/).
- **Willingness to pay** — a pre-payment or deposit is the strongest signal.
- **Return rate** — do your first customers buy again?

## Common mistakes

- Trying to make even the MVP "perfect".
- Relying only on friends' opinions — they're being polite.
- Mistaking "I like it" for demand — payment is what counts.
- Not setting a success threshold in advance: at what number do you continue?

## Questions and answers

### Are MVPs only for tech startups?

No. A restaurant, a course, a physical product — any idea can be tested this way.

### How long should an MVP take?

Usually a few weeks. If it takes months, it's already too big.

### What if the test fails?

That's a result too: you spent little money. Change the idea or try another segment.
