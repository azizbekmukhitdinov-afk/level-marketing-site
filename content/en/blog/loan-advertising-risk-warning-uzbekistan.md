On 10 November 2026 Uzbekistan amended its Law on Advertising: a loan advertising risk warning is now mandatory, and it must take up at least 10% of the ad's area or running time. This guide covers what the law says, who it applies to, what 10% means on a banner and in a video, and how to get your creatives ready before February.

> This article is not legal advice. We explain the news in plain language based on public sources. Before you launch ads, check the official text of the law on lex.uz or talk to a lawyer.

![Loan advertising risk warning: what the law says](/img/blog/illus/kredit-reklamasida-ogohlantirish-1.jpg 1200x675)

## Loan advertising risk warning: what the law says

The President signed Law No. ZRU-1187 on 10 November 2026. It adds to the Law on Advertising adopted in 2022 ([Spot.uz](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2026/11/10/loan-advertisement-warning)). The key change is in Article 43:

- **What is required.** An ad for a loan, including a microloan, must carry a warning that failing to meet debt obligations may lead to financial risks.
- **How much space.** The warning takes at least 10% of the ad's main area or time. For image ads that means area; for audio and video it means duration.
- **From when.** The law itself took effect on the day it was published, but the warning requirement starts three months later, on 11 February.

The Senate approved the amendments in August 2026. Central Bank figures were cited at the time: as of 1 January 2026, almost 2.7 million people had microloans from banks, up 37% in a year ([Gazeta.uz](https://www.gazeta.uz/ru/2026/08/13/credits/)). That is the point of the rule: people should see the consequences before they borrow.

These reports do not state a fine. Liability for breaking advertising rules is set under the general procedure; we gave the overall picture in our article on [advertising law in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/advertising-law-uzbekistan/).

## Who is covered and who is not

The law names the subject of the ad, not the type of advertiser: **advertising of a loan, including a microloan**. So the right question is not "am I a bank?" but "does my ad offer a loan?".

| Situation | Is the warning needed? | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| A bank or microfinance organisation advertises a loan or microloan | Yes | This is exactly what the law is about |
| A car dealer, electronics or furniture store says "loan from our partner bank" in its ad | Plan for yes | The ad promotes a loan. Agree the exact reading with the partner bank and a lawyer |
| A store advertises its own installment plan | Installments are not mentioned in this law | But separate rules apply: you must show the overpayment and the final price |
| The ad says nothing about a loan; only the salesperson mentions it | No | The ad contains no loan offer |

The third row is a topic of its own: the requirement to show the overpayment and the final price in installment ads came from a different document. We walked through it step by step in our article on [installment advertising rules](/en/blog/installment-advertising-rules/). If one ad mentions both an installment plan and a bank loan, you will have to meet both requirements.

Banks have one more source to follow: according to Spot.uz, the Central Bank separately required banks to include such a warning in their advertising. In a joint campaign with a bank, its internal rules may be stricter than the law, so show the bank your layout early.

## How to calculate the 10%

The rule is simple: a tenth of the area in an image, a tenth of the time in video and audio. The table shows the maths for the most common formats. It is arithmetic, not an official template.

| Format | Total | 10% minimum |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram post 1080×1080 | 1,166,400 pixels | A 1080×108 pixel strip |
| 4:5 post (1080×1350) | 1,458,000 pixels | A 1080×135 pixel strip |
| Stories and Reels cover 1080×1920 | 2,073,600 pixels | A 1080×192 pixel strip |
| Billboard 3×6 m | 18 m² | 1.8 m² |
| 15-second video | 15 seconds | 1.5 seconds |
| 30-second video | 30 seconds | 3 seconds |
| 20-second audio spot | 20 seconds | 2 seconds |

Three points need attention.

**First, the words "main area".** The reports do not explain in detail how it is measured. The safe route is to take the whole layout as the base and give the warning slightly more than 10%.

**Second, video.** Sources quote the rule differently: one says "area and time", another "area or time". So the cautious option for video is this: keep the warning on screen for at least 10% of the running time, and have it fill at least a tenth of the frame while it is shown. You can simplify once you have checked the official text.

**Third, readability.** The reports mention no separate font-size rule. But small text that blends into the background goes against the purpose of the rule. Use a contrasting strip and a font that reads easily on a phone.

## How to build the warning into your creative: 5 steps

### 1. List every ad that mentions a loan

Active ads on Meta and Telegram, website banners, outdoor, radio, influencer integrations, printed flyers. For each one, note the format, the size or duration, who made it and how long it will run.

### 2. Approve one warning text

Use the same text across all creatives. The meaning follows the law: failing to meet debt obligations may lead to financial risks. Agree the exact wording with a lawyer or the partner bank, and prepare Uzbek and Russian versions straight away, since creatives usually run [in two languages](/en/blog/ad-language-uzbek-or-russian/).

### 3. Add a permanent strip to the template

Ask your designer for a template per format: a full-width strip at the bottom or top with the warning inside. In Stories and Reels the lower part is covered by interface buttons, so place the strip above the safe zone. Once the strip lives in the template, it is hard to forget in a new creative.

### 4. Rebuild video and audio

In a 15-second spot, 1.5 seconds is a lot. Do not glue the warning onto the end. Write the script with that time in mind from the start: offer, terms, warning, call to action. If a short spot cannot fit it all, pick a longer format; we compared which length works when in our article on [video ad length](/en/blog/video-ad-length/). In audio, have the warning read at a normal pace, not rushed.

### 5. Put the check into your process

Add one line to your ad launch checklist: "is a loan mentioned → is the warning there → does it reach 10%". If you work with an agency or a freelancer, write this into the brief and the layout approval process.

**Illustrative example.** A car dealer in Tashkent runs a 1080×1350 banner and a 20-second Reel that say "loan from our partner bank". A dark 1080×140 pixel strip is added to the banner. In the Reel, the warning sits in the lower part of the frame for the last 2 seconds and is also read aloud. The template is made once; in later months only the car and the price change.

## Timeline: what to do before 11 February

Three months sounds like a lot, but December season and the New Year holidays fall inside it. Plan backwards.

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| End of November | List of ads that mention loans; agree the text with a lawyer or the bank |
| December | New templates; New Year campaigns go out in the new template straight away |
| January | Long-running media: billboards, print, website banners, radio |
| Early February | Switch off old creatives; check materials held by influencers and partners |

Online ads deserve separate attention. During the Senate discussion, a representative of the Competition Committee said a mechanism for monitoring online advertising with artificial intelligence was being developed, with plans to put it into practice from 1 January (Gazeta.uz). Counting on "nobody will notice it on Instagram" is risky.

Old materials are what people forget most often. A banner that has been up for a year, a flyer at the till, an influencer's archived post, an old page on your website: these are advertising too.

## 5 common mistakes

1. **Tiny grey text.** The strip takes 10%, but nobody can read it. The space is there on paper; the warning is not there in practice. A layout like that loses both in an inspection and in the customer's eyes.
2. **Warning only in the caption.** Putting it in the text under the post is not enough: the requirement applies to the ad itself, the image or the video.
3. **A half-second frame.** A line that flashes past at the end of a video does not add up to 10% of the time. Measure it with a stopwatch.
4. **Not asking the partner bank.** The store designs the layout itself, and the bank rejects it later. Show the layout before launch.
5. **Hiding the word "loan".** Trying to dodge the rule with phrases like "easy payment" or "through the bank" makes the ad misleading. Transparent ads sell better over time, as we also saw when looking at [showing the price in ads](/en/blog/should-you-show-price-in-ads/).

## Questions and answers

### Is the requirement already in force?

The law was signed on 10 November 2026 and took effect on the day it was published. The warning rule itself applies three months later, from 11 February. It is easier to release new creatives in the new template now, so you do not have to redraw them later.

### My store only offers installments, not loans. Does this apply to me?

According to the reports, installments are not mentioned in this law; it is about advertising loans and microloans. Installment ads have their own rules: show the overpayment and the final price. If the ad also offers a bank loan, plan for the warning.

### Is there an exact warning text?

The law sets the meaning: a warning that failing to meet debt obligations may lead to financial risks. The reports we have seen do not give one ready-made line. Approve the wording with a lawyer against the official document.

### Does this apply to influencer advertising?

If an influencer promotes a loan, that is advertising too. Write into the contract that the integration will carry the warning and an "advertising" label, and review the material before it goes out.

### Won't 10% spoil the creative?

Similar warnings have existed in medicine advertising for years and the market is used to them; we wrote about that in our article on [medicine and supplement advertising](/en/blog/medicine-supplement-advertising-uzbekistan/). When the strip is built into the template from the start, it becomes part of the design.

## Conclusion

A risk warning in loan advertising is now a legal requirement, not a design choice. The rule is short: if a loan or microloan is offered, at least 10% of the area or time goes to the warning, and the deadline is 11 February. The easiest way is to approve one text and a set of templates now, release December campaigns in them, and clear out old materials before February.
