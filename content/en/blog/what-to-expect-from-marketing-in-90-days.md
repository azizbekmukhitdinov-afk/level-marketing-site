Business owners starting marketing tend to make one of two mistakes: they expect sales to explode in week one, or they believe "marketing is a long game" and don't ask about results for a year. The truth sits in between. Ninety days is enough to build a system, run tests and double down on what works. This article walks through the three months step by step: what gets done, what you should see, and which signs should worry you.

![Why 90 days](/img/blog/illus/marketingdan-90-kunda-nima-kutish-1.jpg 1200x675)

## Why 90 days

- **Ad algorithms need to learn.** In the first weeks Meta or Google study the audience, and cost per lead is unstable.
- **Tests take time.** Judging one creative or offer requires enough data.
- **The sales cycle.** In many businesses, days or weeks pass between first message and payment.
- **One month is too short, six is too long.** In 90 days you can clearly see whether the direction is right.

## The big picture: three months in one table

| Month | Main task | What you should see | Red flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foundations: audit, strategy, tracking, first content and launch | First leads, data building up, a clear plan | No plan, no tracking set up |
| 2 | Tests: creatives, audiences, offers | Cost per lead starts to stabilise, winning creatives emerge | No tests, just "let's wait" |
| 3 | Scale: amplify what works | Comparison with point A, next-quarter plan | Results worsen with no explanation |

## Month 1. Foundations

The first month is often the least "visible" but the most important. It covers:

- **Brief and point A.** Current numbers: leads per month, cost per lead, sales, average order. Without them there's nothing to compare against — [marketing brief](/en/blog/how-to-fill-in-a-marketing-brief/).
- **Audit.** Profile, ad account, lead handling — [marketing audit](/en/blog/marketing-audit-checklist/).
- **Strategy.** Who for, what offer, which channels, what budget.
- **Tracking.** Pixel, UTMs, source in the CRM — [Meta Pixel and Conversions API](/en/blog/meta-pixel-and-conversions-api/).
- **Content.** First videos, creatives, a tidy profile.
- **Launch.** First campaigns.

**What to expect this month:** first leads and data. An unstable cost per lead is normal. More on the first month — [first 30 days with a marketing agency](/en/blog/first-30-days-with-a-marketing-agency/).

## Month 2. Tests and optimisation

Now there's data, so decisions can be made:

- **Creative tests.** 3–5 new videos or images, each with a different hook or offer — [A/B testing ad creatives](/en/blog/ab-testing-ad-creatives/).
- **Audiences.** Broad, lookalike, retargeting — which brings quality leads more cheaply.
- **Offers.** Free consultation, discount, gift, instalments — which drives more sales.
- **Feedback from sales.** Which leads became sales, which didn't, and why.
- **Simplifying the account structure.** If the budget is split across many ad sets — [Learning limited](/en/blog/meta-learning-limited/).

**What to expect this month:** cost per lead begins to stabilise; it becomes clear which creatives and audiences work, and the losers are switched off.

![Month 3. Scale](/img/blog/illus/marketingdan-90-kunda-nima-kutish-2.jpg 1200x675)

## Month 3. Scale

In month three it's time to amplify the winners:

- gradually increase budgets on working campaigns;
- make new variations in the winning creative format;
- test a new channel or audience (for example Telegram or Google);
- use your customer base for repeat sales — [repeat sales and LTV](/en/blog/repeat-sales-and-ltv/).

**What to expect this month:** a clear comparison with point A — leads, cost per lead, sales, cost per sale — and a plan for the next quarter.

## Speed of results depends on the business type

| Business type | Typical purchase decision | What's realistic in 90 days |
|---|---|---|
| Online store, everyday services | Fast: hours or days | Noticeable sales growth |
| Education, clinics, beauty | Medium: a few days | A steady lead flow, rising conversion to sale |
| Real estate, cars, B2B | Long: weeks or months | A base of quality leads, first deals |

For long-cycle businesses the main 90-day result is a base of quality leads and a working funnel, with most deals closing later — [what is a sales funnel](/en/blog/what-is-a-sales-funnel/).

## From our case studies: what three months can bring

Some projects show big results within three months. iTOPS reached 6 million organic views in 3 months — [iTOPS case](/en/blog/case-itops/) — and Chorsu Gold Center made $600,000 in sales in 3 months — [Chorsu Gold Center case](/en/blog/case-chorsu-gold-center/). But every business is different: these numbers aren't a promise, they show what the right system can do.

## What results depend on: your side

Marketing isn't only the agency's or marketer's job. The business side heavily shapes 90-day results:

- **Lead response speed** — [lead response time](/en/blog/lead-response-time/);
- **The sales team** — script, follow-ups, CRM;
- **Product and price** — are they competitive;
- **Approval speed** — are content and creatives signed off on time;
- **Sharing data** — feedback on sales.

![Red flags](/img/blog/illus/marketingdan-90-kunda-nima-kutish-3.jpg 1200x675)

## Red flags

- No clear plan or tracking by the end of month 1.
- The report shows only views and likes, no leads or sales — [what an ad report should include](/en/blog/what-an-ad-report-should-include/).
- No new creatives or tests in month 2.
- Metrics worsen and nobody explains why.
- The ad account isn't in your name.

## Questions and answers

### Is it bad if there are no sales in month one?

Not necessarily. Month one should bring leads and data. But if there are neither leads nor a plan, that's a red flag.

### What happens after 90 days?

What works gets scaled, new channels and offers get tested, and the plan is updated every month. Marketing doesn't stop — it becomes a system.

### How should results be compared?

Against point A: leads, cost per lead, sales and cost per sale at the start. That's why it matters to write the numbers down on day one.

### Can results come faster?

Yes — if leads get fast replies, content is approved quickly and sales gives feedback. Most often the biggest accelerator is speed on the business side.

## Conclusion

Ninety days is the right window to judge marketing: foundations in month 1, tests in month 2, scaling and a point-A comparison in month 3. Every month should bring a clear result and a clear plan. Level Marketing starts from point A, presents the report in your office every month and shows point B in concrete numbers.
