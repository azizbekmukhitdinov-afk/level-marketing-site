Many companies share the same picture: marketing says "we delivered plenty of leads", sales says "the leads are junk". Both are partly right, but the outcome is the same — money is lost and nobody owns it. When marketing and sales work apart, ads bring the wrong people and managers lose even the good leads. This article sets out 7 practical rules that get both teams working towards one result.

![Why they blame each other](/img/blog/illus/sotuv-va-marketing-hamkorligi-1.jpg 1200x675)

## Why they blame each other

| What marketing says | What sales says | The real cause |
|---|---|---|
| "Plenty of leads, sales isn't working them" | "The leads are poor" | No agreed definition of a "quality lead" |
| "We cut cost per lead" | "They don't pick up" | Only cost per lead is measured |
| "Sales gives us no feedback" | "Marketing doesn't listen" | No shared meeting or CRM |

## Rule 1. Define a "quality lead" together

Both teams need to agree on one definition. For example:

- **lead** — anyone who left their contact details;
- **quality lead** — in the target area, has a need, can be reached;
- **sales-ready** — budget and timing are clear, agrees to a meeting or visit.

Once the definition is written down, the "junk leads" argument becomes a matter of numbers.

## Rule 2. Agree on response times

Marketing brings the lead in, but sales has to "save" it. Agree:

- how many minutes until the first reply during working hours;
- who replies in the evenings and at weekends;
- if there's no answer, when and how often to follow up.

More — [lead response time](/en/blog/lead-response-time/).

## Rule 3. The same CRM stages

The CRM is the shared language of both teams. A simple set of stages:

| Stage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| New | Lead arrived, no reply yet |
| Contacted | First conversation happened |
| Qualified | Matches the definition |
| Meeting / visit | Next step booked |
| Sale | Payment received |
| Lost + reason | Why they didn't buy |

Every lead should also carry its source (UTM or campaign). Choosing a CRM — [CRM for small business](/en/blog/crm-for-small-business/).

## Rule 4. Record reasons for lost deals

Every lost customer is data for marketing. The most common reasons:

- too expensive;
- chose someone else;
- later;
- product or service unavailable;
- wrong area or not a fit.

If "too expensive" dominates, the creative needs to show value better or offer instalments. If "not a fit" dominates, change the audience or the form questions.

![Rule 5. A 30-minute weekly meeting](/img/blog/illus/sotuv-va-marketing-hamkorligi-2.jpg 1200x675)

## Rule 5. A 30-minute weekly meeting

Every week marketing and sales meet for 30 minutes:

1. **Numbers** — leads, quality leads, sales by source.
2. **Quality** — which campaigns brought good and bad leads.
3. **Customer questions** — new questions and objections the managers heard.
4. **Next week** — new creatives, promotions, scripts for sales.

## Rule 6. Build content from sales questions

Managers hear every day what customers ask. That's the best content source there is:

- frequent questions → Reels and Stories;
- objections → creatives and highlights;
- successful sales stories → case studies and reviews.

How to collect customer opinions — [collecting customer feedback](/en/blog/collecting-customer-feedback/).

## Rule 7. Shared KPIs

Don't measure marketing only by cost per lead and sales only by quota. Shared metrics:

- number of quality leads;
- lead-to-sale conversion;
- cost per sale;
- ROMI — [how to calculate ROMI](/en/blog/how-to-calculate-romi/).

The same applies when working with an agency — [KPIs in an agency contract](/en/blog/kpis-in-an-agency-contract/).

## Illustrative example: what changes after two months

| Metric | Before | After the rules |
|---|---|---|
| Average time to first reply | 2 hours | 15 minutes |
| Leads with a recorded loss reason | 10% | 80% |
| Lead to sale | 8% | 12% |

These numbers are only an example, but the direction is usually like this: even with the same ad budget, sales go up.

## Common mistakes

- Keeping leads in Excel or Telegram with no CRM.
- Labelling leads "poor" without recording why.
- Cutting marketing off completely from sales results.
- Keeping sales managers in the dark about content and promotions — a customer asks about the offer from the ad and the manager doesn't know it.
- Not preparing the sales team for holidays and promotions — [sales team during holidays](/en/blog/sales-team-during-holidays/).

## Questions and answers

### What if a small business has no separate teams?

The rules still work: the owner, one manager and an ads specialist or agency. A definition, a response time, CRM stages and a short weekly meeting are needed in a small team too.

### Who judges lead quality?

Sales sets the stage and reason in the CRM; marketing analyses that data by campaign. That way the judgement rests on numbers, not feelings.

### Should an agency work with the sales team?

Yes — at least at the feedback level: which leads became sales, and why the others didn't. Without that, an agency optimises only cost per lead, not sales.

### Which rule should I start with?

The "quality lead" definition and the response time. They bring the fastest results.

## Conclusion

When marketing and sales stop blaming each other, the same budget brings more sales. A quality-lead definition, response times, CRM stages, loss reasons, a weekly meeting, content from sales questions and shared KPIs — seven simple rules. In every monthly report Level Marketing looks not just at leads but at their link to sales, and sets up feedback with your sales team.
