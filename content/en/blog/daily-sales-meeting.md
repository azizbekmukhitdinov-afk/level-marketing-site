Many sales teams hold a morning meeting that achieves nothing: it runs for 40 minutes, everyone recounts what they did yesterday, and at the end the manager tells someone off. Reps treat it as a ritual to sit through. A good meeting looks different: 15 minutes, CRM numbers on the screen, and every rep says exactly what they will do today. This article covers the daily agenda, the numbers to look at and the weekly review.

## What the meeting is for

A daily sales meeting has one job: **to adjust today to the plan.** Reviewing yesterday in detail, training a new hire or changing strategy belong in other meetings.

The idea comes from software, not sales. Scrum defines the daily meeting as a 15-minute event whose purpose is to inspect progress towards the goal and adapt the plan for the day ([Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html)). The same principle works for a sales team: short, regular and based on numbers.

Signs that your meeting is not working:

- it lasts longer than 20 minutes;
- numbers are said out loud instead of shown on a screen;
- a rep says "I made lots of calls yesterday", but the CRM does not show it;
- nobody leaves with a specific task.

## The 15-minute agenda

Same time, same order, every day. For example 9:00 sharp, standing up or online.

| Time | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 min | Yesterday's numbers | The manager shows the CRM report on screen: new leads, sales, plan attainment |
| 3–9 min | Each rep | One minute each: which deals they close today, how many meetings, who to call back |
| 9–13 min | One blocker | One problem, for example "customers ask the price and go silent": who fixes it and by when |
| 13–15 min | Commitments | Who does what by when, written down in one place |

The key rule: **blockers are not solved in the meeting; they only get an owner.** If a discussion runs past 5 minutes, it moves to a separate meeting with only the people who need to be there.

## Which CRM numbers to look at

The meeting report should fit on one screen. An illustrative example:

| Number | Why it matters | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| New leads yesterday | Is marketing working | Sharply below normal: tell marketing |
| Leads not yet contacted | The money that disappears fastest | There should be none |
| Average first response time | Speed | Above your standard: find out why |
| Overdue tasks | Forgotten customers | Per rep |
| Meetings and visits today | Today's opportunity | Compare with yesterday |
| Deals at "invoice sent" | The nearest money | Next step for each |
| Plan attainment and pace | How we finish the month | Details below |

Why uncontacted leads matter so much is covered in [lead response time](/en/blog/lead-response-time/). For instance, Level Marketing's standard is to reply to leads within 30 minutes; set a similarly specific number for your own team. If many leads never pick up, the reasons are in [leads don't answer the phone](/en/blog/leads-dont-answer-the-phone/).

Collecting these numbers by hand takes half an hour a day and invites errors. Set them up as an automatic CRM report; on choosing a system, see [what a CRM is and which one suits a small business](/en/blog/crm-for-small-business/).

## Plan pace: how will we finish the month

The most useful number is "where do we end the month at the current speed". The formula is simple:

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|
| Current pace (per day) | Actual ÷ working days passed |
| Forecast | Actual + current pace × working days left |
| Required pace (per day) | (Plan − actual) ÷ working days left |

**Illustrative example.** The monthly plan is 300 million soums and the month has 22 working days. After 10 days, actual sales are 115 million soums.

- Current pace: 115 ÷ 10 = 11.5 million soums a day.
- Forecast: 115 + 11.5 × 12 = 253 million soums, about 84% of plan.
- Required pace: (300 − 115) ÷ 12 ≈ 15.4 million soums a day.

Now translate that into leads. Say the average order is 5 million soums and lead-to-sale conversion is 10%. The team currently makes about 2.3 sales a day, which takes about 23 leads. To reach the required pace you need about 3.1 sales a day, which at 10% conversion means 31 leads.

So there are two routes: either add 8 leads a day (a marketing job) or lift conversion to about 13% (a sales job). If you see this number in the meeting on the 10th, there is still time. If you see it on the 28th, all that is left is regret. How to turn a plan into a lead target: [Sales plan](/en/blog/sales-plan/).

## The weekly review: 45–60 minutes

The daily meeting manages the day; the weekly review manages the system. Do not mix them.

| | Daily meeting | Weekly review |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 15 minutes | 45–60 minutes |
| Question | What do we do today | What works and what does not |
| Numbers | Yesterday and today | The week, sources, reps |
| Attendees | Whole sales team | Sales team + someone from marketing |
| Output | Today's tasks | 1–2 process changes |

Weekly review agenda:

- **1.** Funnel conversions: lead → contact → meeting → sale, broken down by source.
- **2.** The rep table: whose conversion dropped at which stage. More in [sales rep conversion](/en/blog/sales-rep-conversion/).
- **3.** Loss reasons: the three most common and what you will do about them.
- **4.** Listen to one good and one bad call together.
- **5.** Feedback to marketing: which ads brought good leads and which did not. Why this matters: [making marketing and sales one team](/en/blog/marketing-and-sales-alignment/).

## Meeting rules

- **Same time, even without the manager.** On days the manager is away, a senior rep runs it.
- **If it is not in the CRM, it does not exist.** A deal the rep mentions but the CRM does not show is not counted. Within a week CRM hygiene improves.
- **Praise in public, criticise in private.** Public criticism teaches reps to hide numbers.
- **Every task has an owner and a deadline.** "We'll look into it" is not a task.
- **Record it in one place.** A CRM task or a short message in the team chat. The next meeting starts by checking yesterday's commitments.

The meeting is also part of motivation: naming a good result costs nothing and has a real effect. More on that in [sales team motivation](/en/blog/sales-team-motivation/).

## For remote and shift-based teams

If reps work in different places or shifts, a short video call or a standard message in a Telegram group also works. Every rep posts at the same time each day using one template:

- yesterday: how many contacts, meetings and sales;
- today: which deals I plan to close;
- blocker: what is getting in my way.

The manager checks the messages against the CRM report and only asks about discrepancies.

## The first two weeks: how to roll it out

Do not announce the new format as a "rule" on day one. A sequence that works:

| Days | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | The manager sets up the CRM report and fits it on one screen |
| 3 | The format is explained to the team: why 15 minutes, why numbers on screen |
| 4–10 | The meeting runs daily at the same time with a timer; the manager holds the time strictly |
| 11–14 | Reps are asked which number is useless and which one is missing |

After two weeks, remove the report rows nobody uses. The first version usually has too many numbers, and the team really relies on five or six of them.

## Common mistakes

- **Reading reports aloud.** The manager prepares the numbers in advance; reps do not recite them.
- **Solving every problem on the spot.** Fifteen minutes turn into an hour.
- **Looking only at sales value.** It shows the result but not the cause; check the contact and meeting stages too.
- **Cancelling on a bad day.** A bad day is exactly when the meeting is needed most.
- **Leaving marketing out.** If complaints about lead quality stay inside the sales team, the ads never change.

## Questions and answers

### Morning or evening?

Usually morning, at the start of the working day, so reps begin with a clear plan. Some teams add a short 10-minute wrap-up in the evening, which keeps the day's CRM entries up to date.

### Is a meeting needed with only two or three reps?

Yes, but shorter: 7–10 minutes. Even a small team needs numbers on a screen and each person's plan for the day, otherwise work slides into "everyone does their own thing".

### Can a rep run the meeting instead of the manager?

Yes, and it helps: a senior rep who runs it in rotation learns to manage. What matters is that the agenda and the numbers stay the same.

### What if we have no CRM?

You can start with a shared spreadsheet for now, but it quickly fills with inaccurate numbers. Once you get more than ten leads a day, setting up a CRM matters more than the meeting itself.

## Conclusion

A good sales meeting is 15 minutes, CRM numbers on screen, each rep's plan for today and an owner for one blocker. Check the plan pace every day: it tells you, in leads and conversion, what has to happen before the month ends. Leave system-level questions for the weekly review and invite marketing to it.
