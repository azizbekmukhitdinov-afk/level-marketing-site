"The algorithm cut our reach" is the most common complaint in SMM. But the Instagram algorithm is not one system: Feed, Stories, Reels and Explore each work by their own rules. Over the past two years Instagram has explained those rules far more openly and changed a few of them. Here is what affects reach as of November 2026 and what a business account should do in practice.

![What Instagram takes into account](/img/blog/illus/instagram-algoritmi-1.jpg 1200x675)

## What Instagram takes into account

Instagram head Adam Mosseri described how the system works on the [official blog](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained). Each surface ranks content for a user with several groups of signals:

| Signal | Example |
|---|---|
| The user's activity | What they like, save, send and watch to the end |
| Information about the post | How fast it gathers engagement, when it was posted, what it is about |
| Information about the author | How much people have interacted with the account in recent weeks |
| Interaction history | Whether the user has messaged you or commented |

The difference between surfaces is simple. Feed and Stories are shown mostly to followers, so relationship history weighs heavily there. Reels and Explore reach people who don't know you, so the post itself decides.

## The three main signals: watch time, likes and sends

In January 2025 Mosseri named the three signals that matter most for ranking: **watch time, likes and sends** (sharing a post with a friend in Direct). He advised watching three numbers in your insights:

| Metric | How to calculate | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Average watch time | Shown ready-made in Insights | Whether the video or carousel holds people |
| Likes per reach | Likes ÷ reach | Whether your followers like it |
| Sends per reach | Sends ÷ reach | Whether the content deserves to reach new people |

In his words, likes matter slightly more for content shown to followers, and sends slightly more for content shown to non-followers. So if you need a new audience, the question becomes: "Who would send this, and to whom?"

**Illustrative example.** A furniture store in Tashkent posts two Reels. The first is a review of a new sofa: reach 4,000, 12 sends, or 0.3%. The second is "3 mistakes when choosing a sofa for a small room": reach 4,000, 80 sends, or 2%. People forward the second video to friends who are renovating, so it has a better chance of reaching non-followers. The numbers are made up for clarity; yours will differ.

## What matters in Reels

Reels are mostly shown to people who don't follow you, so content quality decides here:

- **Watch time and completion** — the first seconds matter most; see the [hooks article](/en/blog/reels-hooks/).
- **Sends** — people forwarding the video to friends is a strong signal.
- **Saves, likes, comments.**
- **Originality** — covered in its own section below.

Instagram itself lists the Reels that get shown less: low-resolution video, video with another platform's watermark, muted video, video with a border, video that is mostly text, and video already posted on Instagram.

## What matters in Feed and Stories

Relationships decide here: followers who interact with you regularly see your posts higher. For Stories, Instagram names three signals: how often a person views your Stories, how often they reply or like them, and how close you are. That is why replying to comments and using interactive stickers in Stories also helps Feed reach. For formats, see [Selling with Stories](/en/blog/selling-with-stories/).

## What changed in 2025–2026

### Your Algorithm: users pick their own topics

In December 2025 Instagram launched Your Algorithm in Reels. It came to Explore in April 2026 and to the main feed on 10 June. According to [Instagram's announcement](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/reels-algorithm-control), it is now one unified system: the list of interests is shared across Feed, Reels and Explore.

Users see which topics Instagram thinks they are interested in and can add a topic, remove it or mark "more" or "less". For now it works only in the English-language interface.

The takeaway for a business: the account needs a clear topic. If the system can tie you to one topic, it is easier to reach the people who chose that topic. An account that posts furniture today, a meme tomorrow and a recipe the day after is hard to tie to anything.

### Hashtags: up to 5 per post

On 18 December 2025 Instagram announced it would gradually cut the number of hashtags allowed in a post or Reel caption to 5 (the limit used to be 30). Before that, Mosseri had said hashtags do not increase reach but help show what a post is about.

In practice, 3–5 precise hashtags are enough: niche, product and city. For example, `#furniture`, `#sofa`, `#tashkent`. Put your main keywords in the caption text itself: people search inside Instagram with words too.

### Originality: the original instead of the copy

Since April 2024 Instagram has been recommending the original instead of copies of the same video and labelling the copy with a link to the original creator. Accounts that repeatedly post other people's content unchanged can drop out of recommendations altogether. The same update said one more thing: a Reel is first shown to a small audience and, if it performs well, moves on to a wider one — even when the account is small.

For a store this means a ready-made clip from the manufacturer or a video taken from another page will not bring reach. A video shot in your own store on your own phone is worth more.

## Habits that increase reach

1. Post regularly — keep a rhythm.
2. Strong first seconds and short, dynamic editing.
3. Content worth sending to a friend: a list of mistakes, a comparison, a price guide, a before-and-after.
4. One clear topic: what the account is about should be visible from the first 9 posts.
5. Reply to comments in the first hours.
6. Collab posts — publishing together with another account.
7. Only your own videos, with sound and without watermarks.

## Myths

- **"The more hashtags, the bigger the reach."** You can no longer add more than 5 anyway. A hashtag signals the topic; it does not increase reach.
- **"There is one perfect minute to post."** The time your audience is active matters, but not more than content quality.
- **"Every drop in reach is a shadowban."** Instagram says it does not limit content without explanation and does not cut reach to sell ads. If there is a restriction, it shows in Account Status. More often the reason is simple: the content isn't interesting to the audience.
- **"Users can't control the algorithm."** With Your Algorithm, people have started choosing topics themselves.

## If reach drops: a checking routine

1. **Widen the period** — compare the last 1–3 months, not one week.
2. **Find which format dropped** — Reels, Feed or Stories.
3. **Find who dropped** — reach among followers or among non-followers. The first is about relationships, the second about content.
4. **Recall what changed** — topic, hook, video length, posting rhythm.
5. **Check your account status** — the Account Status section in settings shows whether your content is restricted from recommendations; you can appeal there if you disagree.
6. **Remove reposted and watermarked videos.**
7. **Test 2–3 new formats** over two weeks.

## What to look at in your insights

These numbers tell you more than the like count:

| Metric | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Reach among non-followers | Whether content gets into recommendations |
| Average watch time | How well the video holds attention |
| Sends per reach | Whether the content is worth forwarding |
| Likes per reach | Whether followers like it |
| Profile visits | Whether content sparks interest in the author |

Once a month, put your 5 best and 5 worst posts side by side on these metrics. The difference is usually in the topic and the first seconds.

## Common mistakes

- Deleting a low-reach post and reposting it.
- Buying followers and likes.
- "Engagement pods" — trading artificial likes and comments.
- Going silent for a month, then posting ten times in one day.
- Changing the topic every week — neither the audience nor the system knows what to expect from you.
- Still writing 20–30 hashtags under a post.
- Filling the feed with videos taken from other pages.

## Questions and answers

### Does deleting and reposting a post help?

Usually not. A video that was already posted on Instagram may be shown less when uploaded again. Put the effort into the next post: improve the hook, the first seconds and the topic.

### Do bought followers do harm?

Yes. They don't watch your content or engage, so likes and sends per reach fall and your insights get distorted. It is also against platform rules. For honest methods, see [How to grow Instagram followers](/en/blog/grow-instagram-followers/).

### How often should I post?

At a rhythm you can keep up. For example, 3–4 posts or Reels a week that come out steadily for months beat a month of activity followed by silence. On using trends properly, see the [trends](/en/blog/using-trends-for-business/) article.

### How many hashtags should I use?

Up to 5 — that is Instagram's limit. 3–5 precise words are enough: niche, product, city. Put the remaining keywords in the caption.

### Does Reels length affect reach?

Length itself doesn't; watch time and completion do. If the idea fits into 15–30 seconds, don't stretch the video; if the topic needs a longer one, say in the first seconds why it's worth watching to the end.

### Is there an honest way to increase reach fast?

Yes — ads and collaborations. Promote an organic post that performed well, or publish a Collab post with a suitable creator. Both show your content to new people. Services that promise to "trick the algorithm" only distort your insights and put the account at risk.

## Conclusion

Don't fight the Instagram algorithm — send it signals: content people watch to the end, like and send to friends, on one clear topic and shot by you.
