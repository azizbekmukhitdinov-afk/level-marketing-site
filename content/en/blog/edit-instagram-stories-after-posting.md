If a Story went out with the wrong price or an old address, there used to be only one option: delete it, post it again and lose the views it had collected. Now you can **edit Instagram Stories after posting**: for 15 minutes you can fix text, music and stickers. This article covers what a business can fix, what it can't, and what to check before posting so the mistake doesn't happen in the first place.

## What exactly changed

In mid-November Instagram announced that published Stories can now be edited, as reported by [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/instagram-now-lets-you-edit-stories-you-already-posted-2000841943) and [Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/11/instagram-finally-lets-you-edit-stories-after-posting-them.html). The key rules:

- you get **15 minutes** from the moment of posting;
- you can change **text, music, stickers and drawings**;
- views, likes and replies **are kept** — the Story doesn't start from zero;
- an edited Story shows an **"Edited"** label;
- you **cannot replace the photo or video itself**;
- after 15 minutes, the only option is to delete and repost.

The feature is rolling out gradually, not to everyone at once. If you don't see it yet, update the app and give it a few days.

## How to edit a Story

It takes three steps:

1. Open the Story you posted.
2. Tap the **⋯** (three dots) menu at the bottom.
3. Choose **"Edit story"**, fix what you need and save.

There are exceptions. Stories that are automatically cross-posted to Facebook and Stories with some interactive stickers may not be editable. So check an important Story with a poll, quiz or link sticker especially carefully before posting — you may not get a second chance.

## What a business can fix

The most common everyday mistakes are exactly the kind you can fix within those 15 minutes.

| Mistake | Fixable by editing? | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong price (extra zero, old price) | Yes, if the price is typed as text | Change the text |
| Wrong phone number or address | Yes, if it is text or a sticker | Replace the text or the location sticker |
| Music doesn't fit or is too loud | Yes | Pick another track |
| Link sticker goes to the wrong page | Possibly, depends on the sticker type | Check; if not, repost |
| Typo | Yes | Fix the text |
| Wrong product photo | No | Delete and repost |
| Price burned into the video | No | Delete and repost |

Look at the last row. Many businesses put the price straight onto the video in CapCut or Canva. That price still can't be edited, because it is part of the video, not a text layer. So anything that may change — price, date, promotion deadline — is better typed with Instagram's own text tool.

> Put fast-changing information on top of the video as text, not inside it.

## Should you worry about the "Edited" label?

Some social media managers fear the "Edited" label hurts trust. In practice, a Story left with a wrong price or a dead phone number does far more damage: the customer can't get through, or sees one price online and hears another at the till.

There is one subtlety, though. If many people have already seen the Story and your edit raises the price, it gets awkward. Even in the first 15 minutes, hundreds of people may see the old version. If the mistake favoured the customer (the price was lower than real), it's better to honour it for those who already messaged you, or explain openly. ## Pre-posting checklist: Stories that sell

Editing is insurance, not a plan. Fifteen minutes pass quickly, and if the manager is in a meeting or on the road, nobody notices the mistake. The best approach is a 30-second check before posting. For a furniture store or a beauty salon in Tashkent it looks like this:

- **Price** — the number is right, the currency (so‘m) is shown, a promotion has an end date.
- **Contact** — the phone number is complete, the link sticker works (tap it yourself).
- **One action** — one call to action per Story: "tap the link", "tap the button", "call us". Three calls in one Story means none of them works.
- **Readability** — text isn't pressed against the top or bottom edge, where Instagram's buttons cover it.
- **Music** — the Story makes sense without sound, since many people watch Stories muted.
- **Language** — spelling checked, no mix of Latin and Cyrillic.
- **Interactive sticker** — the poll or quiz question is clear, because you may not be able to change it later.

## Building edits into your team's routine

If several people post Stories — the owner, the social media manager, a salesperson — add a few simple rules around the new feature.

**Whoever posts, watches for 15 minutes.** Don't post a Story and put the phone away. In the first 15 minutes the author opens the Story, taps the link and reads the replies in DMs. Found a mistake — fixed it straight away.

**A second pair of eyes.** Show Stories with a price, a promotion or a new product to someone else before posting. An internal Telegram chat and a screenshot are enough.

**Account access.** To edit a Story you need access to the account that posted it. If the manager leaves or the password changes, nobody can fix it. How to hand out access properly is explained in [Business account security](/en/blog/business-account-security/).

## A Story template that's easy to edit

To get the most out of the feature, think of a Story in layers. The bottom layer is the photo or video — it can't be changed later. The top layer is text, stickers and music — those can be fixed within 15 minutes. If you know in advance what goes where, a mistake doesn't get expensive.

| Information | Which layer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Product, interior, process | Photo or video | Rarely changes |
| Logo, brand background | Photo or video (template) | Always the same |
| Price, discount percentage | Instagram text | Most mistakes happen here |
| Promotion deadline, date | Instagram text | Goes out of date fast |
| Phone, address, link | Sticker | Easy to check and swap |
| Music | Music sticker | Changes with the mood |

> **Illustrative example.** A beauty salon in Tashkent posts a "free slots" Story every week. The background is the same Canva template; the times are typed with Instagram text. If the administrator gets a time wrong, they fix it within 15 minutes without rebuilding the Story.

Make the template once in Canva or CapCut and keep it in a "Story backgrounds" album on the phone. Every day you only update the top layer — faster, and easy to correct.

## If the 15 minutes are up

If you spot the mistake late, you have three options:

1. **Minor mistake** (a typo, a small flaw) — leave it. The Story disappears in 24 hours anyway.
2. **Important mistake** (price, number, date) — delete the Story and post the correct one. You lose the views, but wrong information doesn't spread.
3. **Many people have already seen it** — post the correct version with a short note: "The previous Story had the wrong price, here's the right one." Being open builds trust.

If the Story was added to Highlights, check there too: the wrong version can sit there for months.

## Common mistakes

- **Posting without checking because "I can edit it".** Fifteen minutes is short; mistakes are usually found later.
- **Burning the price into the video.** You can't fix it afterwards.
- **Trying to swap the photo.** The feature doesn't do that — only the top layers are editable.
- **Forgetting about auto-sharing to Facebook.** Such Stories may not be editable.
- **Not checking Highlights** after an edit.
- **Not deleting out of fear of the algorithm.** A Story with a wrong price costs more than the views. What drives reach is covered in [The Instagram algorithm in 2026](/en/blog/how-the-instagram-algorithm-works/).

## Questions and answers

### How long can you edit an Instagram Story after posting?

15 minutes from the moment it goes live. After that the edit option disappears, and you can only delete the Story and post it again.

### Will editing reset my views?

No. According to the published information, views, likes and replies are kept. The Story simply gets an "Edited" label.

### Can I replace the video or photo?

No. You can change text, music, stickers and drawings, but the main photo or video stays. In that case, delete the Story and post a new one.

### I don't see the "Edit story" option. Why?

The feature is rolling out gradually. Update the app and wait a few days. Also, Stories cross-posted to Facebook and Stories with some interactive stickers may not be editable.

## Conclusion

Being able to edit Instagram Stories after posting gives a business small but useful insurance: within 15 minutes you can fix a wrong price, number, track or sticker without losing views. But the photo or video itself can't change, and some Stories can't be edited at all. So the real protection is a 30-second checklist before posting and the habit of putting changeable information in the text layer rather than inside the video.
