**Client:** TEH — a brand of power tools and construction equipment: drills, angle grinders, rotary hammers, welding machines, generators, pumps, compressors and garden equipment. Two stores in Tashkent, delivery across Uzbekistan.

**Partnership:** August 2024 to October 2025, 15 months.

**What we handled:** strategy and offer, a consistent brand style for the feed, Reels shooting and editing, ad creatives, paid campaigns on Instagram and Facebook.

## Point A: where the client started

- The company had been on the market for years but sold only wholesale. There were no online sales at all.
- Retail buyers — tradesmen, builders, homeowners — did not know they could buy TEH tools directly.
- The products are technical and not cheap: a welding machine costs several million soums, a hard sum for a tradesman to pay at once.
- The catalogue has hundreds of items, so it was unclear what exactly to advertise.

![What we did](/img/blog/illus/keys-teh-1.jpg 1200x675)

## What we did

1. **Found what was stopping the purchase.** A tradesman knows which machine he needs — he just doesn't have the full amount today. So the core of the offer became instalments with no down payment: the client connected several options, and we put them into every video and every product card.
2. **Built an offer that removes the fear of an unfamiliar brand:** a 6-month warranty, an in-house service centre, free delivery in Tashkent and delivery nationwide, and later “try it first, then buy”: the tool is brought to the tradesman's site and he tests it on a real job.
3. **Designed red-and-black product cards.** Each card shows the model, a photo and — in large type — not the price but the monthly payment, for example “72,000 soums × 12 months”. Next to it, two badges: “no down payment” and “6-month warranty”. The feed became a catalogue.
4. **Shot 217 Reels in 15 months.** We started with single-tool reviews, then added a “What is this tool for?” guessing series, life hacks for tradesmen, a “Try it first, then buy” series where tradesmen use the tool on site and give their opinion, tool comparisons and kits by profession.
5. **Ran ads for two jobs.** Lead campaigns — lead forms and Direct conversations — where we tested both cards and videos. And separate reach campaigns: every new Reel got a few dollars for views.
6. **Counted sales together with the client** instead of stopping at the number of leads, and reviewed with their sales team how enquiries were handled.

## Results

In February 2025 we counted sales together with the client: out of 1,063 leads, 97 became purchases with an average order of about 1 million soums. That month the ads paid back tenfold.

Before we started there were no online sales. By our estimate, by the end of the partnership they had reached 300–500 million soums a month.

## What worked best

- **A card with the payment instead of the price.** A static image — “Electric chainsaw: no down payment, 72,000 soums a month” — brought 895 enquiries at $0.24 each.
- **A 30-second video with a promise in the first sentence:** “Watch to the end and I'll show you how to buy this pump for 100,000 soums a month.” 820 enquiries at $0.27.
- **The product where it is actually used.** A video about a sheep-shearing machine, filmed right in a sheep pen, produced the cheapest lead of the whole project — $0.16.
- **A question instead of a review.** The Reel “What is this tool for?” got 584,000 views and 624 comments: viewers would rather show they know the answer than listen to specifications.
- **Instalments in Reels.** Two Reels — “no down payment, from 123,000 soums a month” — reached 2.1 and 1.6 million views.

What did not work: a plain review video with no specific offer. An enquiry from it cost $3.03 — six times the average.

In lead campaigns, images and videos cost almost the same: $0.46 and $0.44 per enquiry. So in product niches it pays to test both formats rather than pick one in advance.

## How to apply this approach

1. Find out what really stops people from buying. Often it is neither price nor trust, but the inability to pay the full amount at once.
2. Show the monthly payment on the product card, not just the price.
3. Build the offer from things that reduce risk: warranty, service, delivery, a chance to test the product.
4. Test a simple card and a short video with the same offer side by side.
5. Ask the viewer a question — such videos collect comments and reach.
6. Compare campaigns by sales, not by number of leads: count them with the sales team at least once a month.

## Questions and answers

### Do technical products need video, or are photos enough?

Both. In this project cards and videos produced the same cost per enquiry. A card helps people see the model and the payment quickly, while a video shows the tool in action and answers questions before they're asked.

### We only sell wholesale. Is Instagram worth it?

TEH had no online sales at all before we started. Retail buyers already use your product — they need to be shown where to buy it directly and on what terms.

### What ad budget is needed?

In this project, $650–900 a month on average. The offer mattered more than the amount: the same instalment plan worked in an image, in a video and in Reels.

### The buyers are male tradesmen. Are they on Instagram?

Yes. 87% of the ad budget went to a male audience, mostly aged 25–44, and Reels delivered the most impressions.

## Takeaway

A technical product is sold not by a polished review but by a clear offer: how much to pay per month, what the warranty is and where to buy. Once the offer is found, both a simple card and a short video amplify it — and online becomes a real sales channel even for a company that used to work wholesale only.
