If "what are we posting today?" comes up every day, the page becomes chaotic and doesn't sell. A content plan solves this: you know a month ahead what you'll post, when and why. Here is how to build a content plan, plus 30 ideas for any business.

## How much to post

A practical minimum for a small business: **3–4 posts or Reels a week** and a few **Stories** every day. Consistency beats volume: three posts every week is better than 30 posts in a month followed by a month of silence.

## Balancing content types

| Type | Share | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Useful | ~40% | Trust and saves |
| Proof | ~25% | Reviews, results, cases |
| Process and team | ~20% | Closeness to the brand |
| Offer | ~15% | Sales |

It isn't a strict rule, but a page made only of offers turns into a notice board.

## 30 ideas

**Useful**
1. How to choose the product — 5 tips.
2. Answers to 3 frequent customer questions.
3. Mistakes people make using the product.
4. "Cheap vs expensive option: what's the difference".
5. A seasonal tip.
6. A mini glossary: your industry's terms in plain words.
7. A "what to check before buying" checklist.
8. Myth vs fact.

**Proof**
9. A customer review (video or screenshot).
10. Before / after.
11. A project case with numbers.
12. A customer's question and your solution.
13. Awards, certificates, partners.
14. A year in numbers: customers, orders, projects.

**Process and team**
15. How the working day starts.
16. How an order is prepared.
17. Meet a team member.
18. Inside the office or production.
19. How we fixed a mistake — an honest story.
20. The founder's story: why they started the business.
21. How a new product is born.
22. The delivery process.

**Offer**
23. Launching a new product.
24. A time-limited promotion.
25. Instalments or special terms.
26. A bundle offer.
27. A "followers only" bonus.
28. A free consultation or measurement.
29. Gift ideas.
30. The month's results and next month's plans.

## How to lay out the plan

A simple table is enough (Google Sheets or Notion): date, content type, format (post, Reel, carousel), topic, call to action, status. Batch your shooting into one day — filming 8–10 videos in a day is far easier than one a day. For writing Reels scripts, see [Reels script templates](/en/blog/reels-script-templates/); for the first seconds, our [hooks article](/en/blog/reels-hooks/).

## A sample monthly plan (illustrative)

Three posts a week, for example for a furniture store:

| Week | Monday | Wednesday | Friday |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reel: 3 mistakes when choosing a sofa | Carousel: a customer review with photos | Offer: free measuring visit |
| 2 | Reel: the production process | Carousel: comparing material types | Reel: meet the designer |
| 3 | Reel: 5 ideas for a small room | Post: before/after | Offer: instalment terms |
| 4 | Reel: answering a customer question | Carousel: how delivery works | Reel: the month's results |

Stories every day — the process, polls and answers to questions.

## Prepare content in batches

So you're not asking "what do we post today?" every morning, split the work into stages:

1. **A planning day** — topics and scripts for the month.
2. **A shooting day** — 6–10 videos and photos in one day.
3. **An editing day** — all videos with one template.
4. **Publishing** — scheduled in advance with a planning tool.

That turns content creation into a clear few hours of work a week.

## Link the plan to results

At the end of each month, check your insights: which three posts did best for saves, shares and DMs? Which content series brought leads? Next month, give those series a bigger share, and change or drop the ones that didn't work.

## Common mistakes

- Planning only the feed and forgetting Stories.
- Copying someone else's plan.
- An unsustainable pace: 7 Reels a week, then silence.
- No clear call to action in offer posts.
- Making a plan but not tracking the results.

## Questions and answers

### How far ahead should you plan content?

A month ahead — but stay flexible: leave one open slot a week for trends and news. How to use trends is covered in [Using trends for business](/en/blog/using-trends-for-business/).

### Does every post need a caption?

Yes. The caption should have a hook, a benefit and a call to action. Add the keywords people search for, too — Instagram search takes captions into account.

### Do Stories belong in the plan?

Yes, but more simply: a theme for each day is enough — the process on Monday, a poll on Tuesday and so on. Selling through Stories is covered in [Selling with Stories](/en/blog/selling-with-stories/).

### What should the balance of Reels and carousels be?

Reels bring reach among new people, while carousels get saved more and carry more information. Mix both to start, then adjust the balance based on your own insights.

### Who approves the content?

One responsible person, with a clear deadline. If three people approve every post, the plan will always run late.

### What if you run out of ideas?

Customer questions, comments, questions under competitors' posts and new versions of your best posts — the source of ideas never runs dry.

### Which tool is best for keeping a content plan?

A simple spreadsheet is enough: date, format, topic, caption, status and result. For a larger team, a task board is more convenient. What matters isn't the tool but that the plan gets updated every week.

## Conclusion

A content plan turns a page of random posts into a systematic sales channel. Rotate content types, post consistently and review each month which posts brought leads. And don't forget your profile — see [an Instagram profile that sells](/en/blog/instagram-profile-that-sells/).
