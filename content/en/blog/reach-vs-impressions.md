Instagram insights and the ads manager show two similar numbers: **reach** and **impressions**. Many business owners mix them up and celebrate "50,000 people saw us" when 50,000 was actually impressions and the number of people may have been 15,000. Understanding the difference helps you judge your ad budget properly.

## Definitions

- **Reach** is the number of unique people who saw your content or ad **at least once**.
- **Impressions** is the **total number of times** it was shown, including repeat views by the same person.

## An example

An illustrative example: your ad reached 10,000 people and was shown 30,000 times. On average, each person saw it 3 times — that's **frequency** — [ad frequency](/en/blog/ad-frequency/).

**Frequency = impressions ÷ reach**

## Which matters when

| Situation | Key metric |
|---|---|
| Introducing a new brand | Reach — more new people |
| A complex product, a long decision | Frequency — people need to see it several times |
| A short promotion window | Reach and frequency together |
| Leads and sales | Neither — cost per lead and per sale |

Reach and impressions are tools, not goals. The result is leads and sales — [cost per lead](/en/blog/cost-per-lead-cpl-how-to-lower/).

## In Instagram insights

- **Reach** is the people your content got to, with the split between followers and non-followers.
- **Views**: Instagram now treats "views" as the main metric in many places — a concept close to impressions.

A high share of non-followers means your content is working in recommendations — [how the Instagram algorithm works](/en/blog/how-the-instagram-algorithm-works/).

## In the ads manager

- Add the reach and impressions columns.
- A frequency near 1 means each person saw the ad once — normal for a new audience.
- Very high frequency with falling CTR means the creative is fatigued — [creative fatigue](/en/blog/creative-fatigue/).
- The cost per 1,000 impressions — [what is CPM](/en/blog/what-is-cpm/).

## Common mistakes

- Calling impressions "the number of people".
- Chasing reach alone, which brings the wrong audience.
- Adding up reach across periods, which counts the same people twice.

## Questions and answers

### Can I add reach figures together?

No. Add Monday's and Tuesday's reach, and anyone who saw the ad on both days is counted twice. Look up the total reach for the whole period separately.

### Reach has dropped — what should I do?

Check your content format, hooks and posting consistency; for ads, check budget and audience.

### Lots of impressions, no results — why?

The ad may be shown too often to the same people, or the offer may not be interesting.

## Conclusion

Reach is how many people saw it, impressions is how many times it was shown, and frequency is the ratio between them. Read them correctly, but judge results by leads and sales. In a free audit, Level Marketing will analyse your statistics.
