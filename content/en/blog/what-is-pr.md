Ask business owners **what is PR** and many will say "an article in a newspaper" or "paying a blogger to praise us". In reality, PR is managing what people think about your company: who talks about it, where, in what words, and how the company responds when something goes wrong. This article explains PR in plain small-business terms: channels, a plan, expert comments, crises and measuring results.

## What is PR: a simple definition

PR stands for public relations. The Public Relations Society of America (PRSA) describes it as a strategic communication process that builds mutually beneficial relationships between organisations and their publics ([PRSA](https://www.prsa.org/about/all-about-pr)).

For a small business, it can be put even more simply:

- **advertising** — you say "we're good" yourself and pay for the space;
- **PR** — others say "they're good": a journalist, a channel author, a customer, an industry expert.

That is why people trust PR more, but it is harder to control. The journalist writes the text, the channel author adds an opinion, and your job is to supply a good reason and accurate facts.

PR and brand go hand in hand. A brand is the image in the customer's head; PR reinforces that image through other people's words.

## PR vs advertising: the difference in a table

| Criterion | Advertising | PR |
|---|---|---|
| Who speaks | The company itself | A third party: media, channel, expert, customer |
| Payment | For space and impressions | Mostly time and a newsworthy reason (paid placement is advertising) |
| Control | Full: text, timing, audience | Partial: someone else writes the text |
| Trust | Low to medium | Higher |
| Speed of results | Fast: switch it on today, leads today | Slow: builds up over months |
| Measurement | Precise: clicks, leads, cost | Harder: mentions, search, "where did you hear about us" |

One important point: a paid post in a Telegram channel is not PR, it is advertising. Under Article 18 of Uzbekistan's advertising law, advertising in any form must be recognisable as advertising — see [our article on the law](/en/blog/advertising-law-uzbekistan/). How to choose and measure paid placements is covered in [advertising in Telegram channels](/en/blog/buying-ads-in-telegram-channels/).

> PR does not replace advertising: ads bring today's leads, PR builds tomorrow's trust.

## PR channels in Uzbekistan

For a small business, PR is not limited to big media. These are the channels that work in practice.

### 1. Online media and trade publications

News sites, business outlets, industry portals. They want news: a new production line, exports, an interesting study, a social project. "We've opened" is usually not news — you need facts like "for the first time in Samarkand…" or "a survey of 300 cafés in Tashkent".

### 2. Telegram channels

Many people in Uzbekistan read their news on Telegram. Industry, city and expert channels are the main PR arena. To get coverage for free, give the channel author something useful: an exclusive figure, an interesting story, a specialist's comment.

### 3. Expert comments

Journalists look for specialists who can explain a topic: why prices went up, where the market is heading, how buyers have changed. A manager or specialist who can speak briefly and to the point is free PR for the company.

### 4. Events

Your own event (a workshop, an open day, a mini-conference) or speaking at someone else's. How to plan an event and turn it into clients is in [event marketing](/en/blog/event-marketing-for-business/).

### 5. Founder and team

The founder's personal page, podcast interviews, posts on LinkedIn or Telegram. People trust a person faster than a company — when the [founder is on camera](/en/blog/founder-on-camera/), PR gets stronger naturally.

### 6. Customers and reviews

Map reviews, customer stories, recommendations — this is PR too, at its most local level. How to collect reviews and respond to criticism is in [online reputation](/en/blog/online-reputation-and-reviews/).

## Where news comes from: 10 newsworthy reasons

A journalist or channel will write about you not because you are "a good company" but because there is an interesting reason. Keep a list of reasons in advance:

1. A new product or service, especially one the market does not have yet.
2. A new branch, production line or export market.
3. Figures from your own data: "the 5 things customers ask about most".
4. A small industry study or survey.
5. A social project: a free course, charity, a clean-up campaign.
6. A partnership with a well-known brand or organisation.
7. An award, a certificate, a competition win.
8. An interesting story about an employee or the founder.
9. Seasonal advice: for winter, a holiday, the school season.
10. A quick comment on market news.

For each reason, answer one question: "What will a reader learn or be surprised by?" If there is no answer, it is an ad post, not a PR reason.

## A PR plan for a small business: 6 steps

### 1. Write the goal in one sentence

"So people know us" is not a goal. Better: "By the end of the year, our Tashkent furniture store is known as the expert in office furniture." The goal says which audience and which image you need.

### 2. Define key messages

Three or four statements: who you are, how you differ, why people can trust you. These messages repeat in every interview, press release and post. Keep the tone and wording consistent everywhere.

### 3. Build a media list

A spreadsheet of 20–30 contacts: outlet or channel name, topic, author or editor, contact, date of last contact. Start with the 10 closest to your industry.

### 4. Prepare a spokesperson

Who speaks for the company? One or two people are enough. Give them the key messages, the numbers and a list of what not to say.

### 5. Put together a press kit

One folder: a short company description, logo, a photo of the founder, product photos, contacts. When a journalist asks, you send it in a minute.

### 6. Set a monthly rhythm

At least one newsworthy reason and one appearance a month — steady beats occasional.

## Press release and pitch to a journalist

A press release should be short. A simple structure:

- **headline** — the news in one sentence;
- **first paragraph** — who, what, when, where and why it matters;
- **details** — figures, facts, product information;
- **quote** — 1–2 sentences from the founder or an expert;
- **about the company** — 2–3 lines and contacts.

Make your email to a journalist or channel author personal: why this matters to their audience specifically. Sending the same text to 50 addresses does not work. If there is no reply, one short reminder is enough — do not write every day.

> **Illustrative example.** Before the school season, a dental clinic in Tashkent prepares 5 tips on children's teeth plus observations from its own appointments and offers the material to a parenting channel. The channel publishes it for free because it is useful to its audience. That is PR, not advertising.

## PR in a crisis: the first 24 hours

A crisis is when negative talk about the company spreads fast: a customer's complaint video goes viral, a product defect is found, an employee says something inappropriate. Speed and honesty decide the outcome.

| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| First 1–2 hours | Gather facts, appoint one person in charge, ask staff not to comment |
| 2–6 hours | A short first statement: we know about the problem, we are checking, we will update you by a set time |
| Within 24 hours | The outcome: what happened, what was done, how the affected person was helped |
| Afterwards | Lessons: what changed in the process, and say so openly |

What not to do in a crisis:

- stay silent — others will fill the gap;
- delete comments and block critics;
- publicly blame the customer;
- quote unchecked numbers or make promises.

Write the crisis plan before there is a problem: who drafts the statement, who approves it, where it is published. One page is enough.

## How to measure PR results

PR is hard to measure in clicks and leads, but not measuring it at all is a mistake. Simple indicators:

- **number of mentions** — how many outlets and channels mentioned you this month, and in what tone;
- **branded search** — is search for your company name growing in Google Search Console or Yandex Wordstat;
- **direct visits** — people who typed your site address into the browser;
- **"where did you hear about us?"** — sales asks every new customer and logs the answer in the CRM;
- **link clicks** — put a UTM tag on the link in a publication and you can count the visits.

Compare after 3–6 months, not after one. PR results accumulate.

## Common mistakes

- **Calling advertising PR.** A paid post or a paid article is advertising and should be labelled as such.
- **A press release without news.** "Our company turned 3" is usually interesting only to you.
- **Betting on one big article.** A single piece changes little; rhythm matters.
- **An unprepared spokesperson.** Answering a journalist with "I don't know, I'll get back to you" is a missed opportunity.
- **Deleting and staying silent in a crisis.** It only makes the problem bigger.

## Questions and answers

### Does a small business need PR, or is advertising enough?

At the start, advertising is the main channel because it brings leads quickly. But one or two PR actions a month — an expert comment, a useful piece for a channel, working with reviews — make your ads more credible. When someone sees an ad, searches for the company name and finds what others say about you, they decide faster.

### Should I hire a dedicated PR specialist?

In a small company, usually not. The founder or marketer needs a few hours a month: a list of reasons, a media list and a press kit. A PR specialist or agency is useful for a big event, a crisis or regular media work.

### Can I pay a journalist for an article?

Paid material is advertising and, by law, must be recognisable as advertising. A hidden paid article damages both trust and compliance. If you need a paid placement, run it with a clear "advertising" or "partner content" label.

### How soon will PR show results?

One appearance can bring traffic and calls within days, but the main result — awareness and trust — shows after 3–6 months of steady work. So compare results quarter by quarter, not month by month.

## Conclusion

PR means creating reasons and conditions for others to talk about you. Ads bring today's leads; PR builds tomorrow's trust through media and Telegram channels, expert comments, events, the founder and customers. Write the goal, list your newsworthy reasons, prepare a spokesperson and press kit, write a crisis plan in advance and measure results through mentions and branded search.
