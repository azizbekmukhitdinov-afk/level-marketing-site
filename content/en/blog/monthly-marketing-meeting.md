In many businesses, the monthly marketing "meeting" goes like this: the agency or marketer shows a 40-slide presentation, talks about reach and clicks, the owner nods — and no decisions are made. Next month, the same again. Yet the monthly meeting is the most powerful tool for managing marketing, if it's structured well. Here's a 60-minute format that ends with concrete decisions.

## The goal is decisions, not a report

You don't need a meeting to read numbers — the report can be sent in advance. The meeting should answer three questions:

1. **Was the plan met?** In numbers: plan vs actual.
2. **What worked, what didn't — and why?**
3. **What will we do differently next month?**

If 3–5 concrete decisions aren't written down by the end, the time was wasted.

## Preparation: 1–2 days before

- **The report goes out in advance** — the owner reviews the numbers before the meeting.
- **Sales data** — from the CRM: how many leads became sales. Without it, an ad report is half the picture.
- **A list of questions** — from the owner: what's unclear, what's worrying.

What numbers the report should contain — [what an ad report should include](/en/blog/what-an-ad-report-should-include/).

## The agenda: 60 minutes

| Time | Part | What's discussed |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 | Plan vs actual | Key numbers: spend, leads, sales, cost per sale |
| 10–25 | What worked | Best campaigns, creatives, channels — and why |
| 25–35 | What didn't | Weak spots, causes, what was changed |
| 35–45 | The sales team | Lead quality, response speed, lost reasons |
| 45–55 | Next month's plan | Budget, goals, tests, content |
| 55–60 | Decisions | Who, what, by when |

## Part 1: the plan-vs-actual table

The meeting opens with one table:

| Metric | Plan | Actual | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ad spend | 20m soum | 19.4m soum | −3% |
| Leads | 400 | 360 | −10% |
| Cost per lead | 50,000 soum | 53,900 soum | +8% |
| Sales | 40 | 43 | +8% |
| Cost per sale | 500,000 soum | ~451,000 soum | −10% |

The numbers are an illustrative example. The table immediately shows something important: fewer leads than planned but more sales — so leads were better quality or sales performed better. That's a topic for discussion.

How to agree goals and KPIs — [KPIs in an agency contract](/en/blog/kpis-in-an-agency-contract/).

## Parts 2–3: what worked and what didn't

Here you move from numbers to causes. Good questions:

- "Why did this creative beat the others — what's different about it?"
- "Did you stop this campaign? By which rule?" — more in [when to turn off an ad](/en/blog/when-to-turn-off-an-ad/)
- "Which tests were run, and with what result?"
- "What changed among competitors or in the market?"

A bad question: "Why are the results bad?" — it puts people on the defensive. Better: "What should we change to improve the results?"

## Part 4: the sales team

Many meetings skip this part — and miss the biggest losses. What to review:

- first response time to leads;
- lead-to-sale conversion by salesperson — [sales rep conversion](/en/blog/sales-rep-conversion/);
- lost reasons: "too expensive", "didn't answer", "low quality".

If sales says "the leads are bad" and marketing says "the leads are good", the meeting brings them together around one table. More in [marketing and sales alignment](/en/blog/marketing-and-sales-alignment/).

## Part 5: next month's plan

The plan should be specific:

- the budget and its split across channels;
- goals: leads, sales, cost per sale;
- one or two tests: what we're testing and how we'll judge it;
- content: how many videos, which topics, when to shoot;
- seasons and promotions — [marketing calendar 2027](/en/blog/marketing-calendar-2027-uzbekistan/).

## Part 6: the decision list

At the end of the meeting, a written list:

| Decision | Owner | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Reply to leads within 30 minutes, with a duty salesperson | Head of sales | The 1st |
| Test a creative showing the price | Agency | The 10th |
| Film a customer testimonial | Agency + owner | The 15th |

The next meeting starts by checking this list.

## Who attends

- **The owner or decision-maker** — without them, decisions stall.
- **The marketer or agency representative** — for numbers and causes.
- **The head of sales** — for what happened to the leads.

More people, fewer decisions. Three or four is enough.

## Common mistakes

- Seeing the report for the first time in the meeting.
- Only ad account metrics: reach, clicks, CTR — no sales.
- Ending without decisions.
- Not inviting sales.
- Turning the meeting into a blame session.
- A different report format every month, so nothing can be compared.

## Questions and answers

### Can the meeting be online?

It can, but an in-person meeting often leads to a deeper conversation: questions are asked more openly and decisions are made faster.

### Do I need a weekly meeting too?

A weekly one is short — 15–20 minutes — for technical control. The monthly one is for strategic decisions. One doesn't replace the other.

### What if my agency doesn't hold monthly meetings?

Ask. Ideally it's in the contract. How the first months with an agency go — [first 30 days with a marketing agency](/en/blog/first-30-days-with-a-marketing-agency/).

### What format should the report take?

The same every month: a plan-vs-actual table, results by channel, sales data and next month's plan. With a stable format, trends become visible.

## Conclusion

The monthly marketing meeting is about making decisions, not reading a report. Send the report ahead, start with plan vs actual, discuss causes, involve sales and finish with 3–5 written decisions.
