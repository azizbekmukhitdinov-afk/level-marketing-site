Today, 27 November, is Black Friday 2026, and one change stands out this season. **Black Friday AI traffic** is now a real thing: more shoppers start not with a Google search but by asking ChatGPT, Gemini or Google AI Mode what to buy. This article covers what changed, how to read the numbers without overreacting, and how to prepare your store for a customer who arrives on an AI assistant's recommendation.

## The numbers: what happened in the US

Adobe tracks US online retail every year. Its data for the 2026 holiday season ([Adobe Holiday Shopping Report](https://business.adobe.com/resources/holiday-shopping-report.html)) looks like this:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Pre-season forecast: AI-referred traffic to retail sites | +130% year over year |
| In-season data: visits referred by AI | +127% year over year |
| US online spend, 1–22 November 2026 | $82.5 billion (+9.1%) |
| AI traffic growth in the 2025 season | +693% (from a very small base) |

Keep three things in mind when you read this table:

- **these are US figures.** Shopping behaviour in Uzbekistan is different, and nobody has published a comparable measurement for our market yet;
- **this is a growth rate, not a share.** +127% does not mean more people come from AI than from other channels. Search, ads and direct visits still deliver most of the traffic;
- **the base is small.** That is why +693% in 2025 looks so dramatic: when a small number doubles, the percentage gets huge.

The takeaway is simple. Buying through AI is an emerging habit. It is not yet a large part of sales, but it is growing fast. Here it will show up first among younger audiences, in IT and among people who already follow international brands.

## What an AI-assisted purchase looks like

A typical shopper types "laptop price Tashkent" into Google and sees ten links. An AI-assisted shopper asks differently: "I need a laptop for a student, under 8 million sum, for programming. Which one is best and where can I buy it in Tashkent?"

The assistant returns three or four models, the differences between them and sometimes a list of stores. The shopper reaches your site or listing already prepared: they know what they want and only check price, stock and delivery.

That has two consequences:

1. **Where the AI gets its information matters.** If your site does not state the price, specs and terms, the assistant will not recommend you, or it will describe you incorrectly.
2. **The visitor decides fast.** If they don't find an answer on your page, they go back and pick another store from the same AI answer.

## The product page: 7 requirements for AI and for people

Both the assistant and the human are looking for the same thing: concrete facts. Check each product page against this list:

1. **Full name and model.** Not "great vacuum cleaner" but brand, model and the main spec: power, capacity, colour.
2. **Price as text.** An AI may not read a price inside an image. Price in sum; during a promotion, the old and new price and the end date.
3. **Stock status.** "In stock", "made to order in 3 days" or "sold out". Showing a sold-out item as available is the fastest way to lose an AI-referred shopper.
4. **Delivery and returns.** How long within the city, how it works for the regions, whether returns are possible.
5. **Reviews.** Real written reviews from customers. How to collect them is covered in our article on [online reputation and reviews](/en/blog/online-reputation-and-reviews/).
6. **A Q&A block.** The 4–6 questions people ask most: warranty, size, compatibility, instalment options.
7. **A way to get in touch.** Phone, Telegram or a lead form visible on every page.

> Remember the rule: an AI needs facts to recommend you, a person needs trust to buy from you.

### Illustrative example: a home appliance store in Tashkent

The store's site lists 400 products (illustrative example). Many listings have only a photo and "ask for the price". An AI cannot extract anything useful from that page. The store starts with its 30 best-selling items: it moves the price into the text, shows stock status, and adds four Q&As and the warranty terms to each. There is no need to fix all 400 at once — start with the products that bring most of the sales.

## The promotion page: don't let the AI get confused

A Black Friday promotion is the most confusing topic for AI assistants because prices change quickly. An assistant might pick up stale data and say "this store has 40% off" after the sale has ended. So:

- state the **start and end dates** clearly on the promotion page;
- don't delete the page after the sale — update it: "This sale ended on 1 December, current offers are here";
- keep the discount terms in one place: which products, how much, what limits;
- make sure prices match on Instagram, Telegram and the site. AI tools compare several sources, and contradictions reduce trust.

## How to see AI traffic in GA4

If you don't measure visits from AI tools, you will never know whether this channel exists for you. In Google Analytics 4 you can check it like this:

1. Open **Reports → Acquisition → Traffic acquisition**.
2. Switch the dimension to **Session source / medium**.
3. Type `chatgpt`, `gemini`, `perplexity` and `copilot` into the search box and look at the results.

ChatGPT often adds `utm_source=chatgpt.com` to its links, so it tends to appear as a separate source. Other AI services may show up as referral traffic or not show up at all. Visits from Google AI Mode usually stay inside ordinary Google organic traffic and are not broken out separately.

To make this easier, create a custom channel group: **Admin → Data display → Channel groups**, add a new "AI" channel with the condition that the source contains chatgpt, gemini, perplexity or copilot. You will then see the month-by-month trend in a single row.

If you supply a link to AI services yourself (for example in a catalogue file), add UTM parameters to it. How to build them is covered in our guide to [UTM tags](/en/blog/utm-tags/).

## Six lessons from Black Friday 2026 for 2027

When this season is over, write down the following for next year:

| Lesson | What to do | When |
|---|---|---|
| AI traffic wasn't measured | An "AI" channel group in GA4 | December |
| Listings had no prices | Complete the top 30 product listings | December–January |
| The promotion page was confusing | Dates, terms and an "ended" status | Every promotion |
| Too few reviews | A post-purchase review request routine | Ongoing |
| No FAQ on the site | An FAQ built from questions sales reps hear | January |
| Prices differ across channels | One source of prices, weekly check | Ongoing |

Several more sales peaks are ahead: New Year, then Ramadan and Eid. Product pages you clean up now will work in all of them, for AI and for ordinary search alike.

## What not to do

- **Don't write hidden text just for AI.** Text that only a bot can see and a person cannot may lead to search engine penalties. Write one clear text for everyone.
- **Don't post fake reviews.** Fake reviews get spotted quickly and undermine trust in the whole store.
- **Don't switch off ads to bet everything on AI.** For now AI traffic is an extra channel. Meta and Google ads, Telegram and repeat purchases still bring the core of sales.
- **Don't expect results within a week.** AI services refresh their data at different speeds. Listings you fix today will matter more next season.

## Questions and answers

### Do shoppers in Uzbekistan also ask ChatGPT before buying?

Some do, especially for electronics, courses, travel and expensive purchases. But there is no public research measuring that share. The most reliable approach is to check your own GA4 report and have your sales reps ask, "How did you find us?"

### Can I run ads specifically for AI traffic?

Ads in Google AI Mode run in a number of countries, but Uzbekistan is not on that list yet. Check your own account and official announcements for any options to advertise inside ChatGPT or Gemini answers. Right now the smartest investment is clear product pages.

### Does a store that sells through Instagram need this?

Partly. An AI may not read an Instagram profile as fully as a website. So you need at least one simple page or catalogue with prices, terms, address and contacts. The profile then carries the sale forward, and prices and terms there should match too.

### Black Friday is over — isn't it too late?

No. December and the New Year season are still ahead. If you tidy up your top product listings and your promotion page now, they will work in December and again next Black Friday.

## Conclusion

Black Friday 2026 showed fast growth in AI-referred traffic in the US, but that is a growth rate, not a share. In Uzbekistan the effect is smaller and unmeasured, so what you need is preparation, not panic: precise facts in listings, prices as text, stock status, reviews and an FAQ, dates on the promotion page and a separate channel for AI traffic in GA4.
