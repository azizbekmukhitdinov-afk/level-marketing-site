Your ads are running, your Instagram inbox is full, yet sales are flat. Often the reason is simple: nobody counts unanswered Instagram DMs. Some people got a late reply, some got none, and some said "I'll think about it" and were forgotten. This guide walks through an audit that takes 30–40 minutes a week and a simple reply standard for your team.

## Why DMs get "lost"

A missed phone call at least leaves a "missed call" notice. DMs give no such signal. Once a message has been opened, it slides down under newer ones and stops standing out.

The usual reasons:

- **No owner.** The SMM manager, a salesperson and sometimes the business owner all use the inbox. Each assumes someone else replied.
- **"Seen" is mistaken for "handled".** Opening a message doesn't process it, but it is no longer bold in the list.
- **The requests folder.** Messages from people who don't follow you can land in a separate "requests" section that few people check.
- **Evenings and weekends.** Ads run at night, but in the morning the manager only sees the latest messages.
- **Conversations stop halfway.** The price was sent, the customer went quiet, and nobody followed up the next day.

None of these is an advertising problem. It is a process problem, and you only see it if you check regularly.

## What counts as an unanswered conversation: 4 types

Agree on definitions before the audit, or you will count different things every week.

| Type | What it looks like | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| No reply | The customer wrote; the business sent nothing | Reply now, note the reason |
| Late reply | The reply came later than your standard allows | Check shifts and cover |
| Stopped halfway | The customer's question wasn't fully answered | Review the chat with the manager |
| No follow-up | Price or details sent, then silence from your side | One reminder, record the outcome |

Tip: count the last two types separately. These are often your warmest leads — they already know the price and just need a nudge.

## The weekly audit in 6 steps

Run the audit on the same day each week, for example Monday morning. It should be done by the owner or the head of sales, not by the person who answers the DMs.

### Step 1. Set the period

Take every conversation from the past 7 days. The shared Inbox in Meta Business Suite shows Instagram and Facebook messages in one place, which is far easier than scrolling through DMs on a phone.

### Step 2. Open requests and filtered messages

Check the requests and spam sections as well as the main list. If a real customer ended up there, reply and move the conversation to the main list.

### Step 3. Put each conversation into one of the 4 types

Use inbox labels: "no reply", "late", "stopped", "follow-up". Labelled conversations can then be found with a filter in seconds. Section names may differ slightly depending on your interface language and version.

### Step 4. Record the numbers in a sheet

A simple Google Sheet is enough:

| Metric | This week | Last week |
|---|---|---|
| New conversations | | |
| No reply | | |
| Reply later than standard | | |
| Stopped halfway | | |
| No follow-up | | |
| Became sales (per CRM) | | |

Judge the numbers as a weekly trend. One bad week can be chance; three in a row is a system.

### Step 5. Read 5–10 conversations in full

Numbers show "how many"; conversations show "why". Read 5–10 random chats from start to finish: did the manager answer the question, hide the price, suggest a next step? We covered price questions separately: [replying to "How much?"](/en/blog/replying-to-how-much/).

### Step 6. One action per problem

The output of the audit is a list of actions, not a report. For example: "nobody covers Saturdays — assign someone for Saturdays", "people who asked for the price get no reminder the next day — create a CRM task". At the next audit, check that the actions were done.

## A reply standard for your managers

You need a standard before the audit can check against it. Keep it to one page.

**1. First reply time.** During working hours — for example, within 15 minutes; outside working hours — an automated reply plus a personal answer in the first hour of the morning. Set the exact number for your business, but put it in writing. Why speed matters: [lead response time](/en/blog/lead-response-time/).

**2. Every conversation has an owner.** In the inbox you can assign a conversation to a specific team member. Rule: whoever replies first keeps the conversation to the end, or hands it over explicitly through the CRM.

**3. Every reply includes a next step.** Not just information but an offer: "We'll be expecting you at the showroom tomorrow at 11:00 — does that work?", "May I give you a call?".

**4. One reminder.** If the customer goes quiet, send one polite reminder within 24 hours. Don't push beyond that.

**5. Record the outcome.** When the conversation ends, add a label or CRM status: "bought", "thinking", "not a fit". Then it won't be counted again next week.

We have a separate guide on setting up automated replies: [automated responses in Meta Business Suite](/en/blog/meta-business-suite-automated-responses/). The key point: an automated reply doesn't replace a real answer, it only softens the wait.

## The 24-hour window: why delays also hurt technically

On Meta's platforms, the time a business has to respond to a user is limited. Under [Meta's messaging policy](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/messenger-platform/policy/policy-overview), the standard window lasts 24 hours for most user actions; for the first message after a click-to-Direct ad it can extend up to 7 days. This matters most for messages sent through a CRM, chatbot or other connected tool.

The practical takeaway: if you run your DMs through a CRM or bot, you may not be able to reply from there to a badly overdue conversation. One more reason not to leave replies until tomorrow. Check the exact limits in your tool's documentation.

## Illustrative example: a furniture store in Tashkent

*Illustrative example; the numbers are for explanation only.* A furniture store runs click-to-Direct ads and gets 120 new conversations a week. The first audit showed:

| Metric | Count |
|---|---|
| New conversations | 120 |
| No reply | 9 |
| Reply later than standard | 22 |
| Price sent, no follow-up | 31 |

The largest group was the 31 people who knew the price but never heard from the store again. Without adding any ad budget, the store introduced one rule — a reminder the next day — and a month later started comparing in the CRM how many sales this group produced. Since most late replies fell on Saturday and Sunday, a weekend duty manager was assigned.

The point of the example: an audit helps you keep the customers who already came to you before you pay to find new ones.

## Common mistakes

- **The manager audits themselves.** It's hard to spot your own mistakes. The reviewer should be someone else.
- **Measuring only speed.** A fast but empty reply ("Hi, we've messaged you") doesn't sell. Read the content too.
- **Auditing to punish.** The goal is to find gaps in the process. If managers are afraid, they start hiding conversations.
- **DMs and the CRM live apart.** If customers don't reach the CRM after a chat, you can't link sales to ads. More here: [Ads Manager leads don't match your CRM](/en/blog/ad-leads-vs-crm-mismatch/).
- **Doing it once.** The first audit reveals a lot, but the benefit comes from doing it every week.

## Questions and answers

**How long does the audit take?**
With 50–150 conversations a week, once the team is used to labels, about 30–40 minutes. The first one takes longer.

**Do I need special software?**
Not to start: the Meta Business Suite inbox, labels and a Google Sheet are enough. When volume grows, consider a CRM integration.

**What should I write to a customer who got a late reply?**
Apologise and give a useful answer straight away: "Sorry for the late reply. The sofa you asked about is in stock, the price is … . Could you visit the showroom today?" No long explanations needed.

**Do questions in comments count?**
Yes, if someone asks about price or location in a comment. We have a separate article on this: [handling comments and DMs](/en/blog/handling-comments-and-dms/).

## Conclusion

Unanswered Instagram DMs are money quietly leaking from your ad budget. You don't need a complex system to see it: a written reply standard, an owner for every conversation, 4 labels and one audit a week. When Level Marketing works with clients, it looks not only at the number of leads but at how they are handled — because the cheapest lead is the one who already wrote to you and never got an answer.
