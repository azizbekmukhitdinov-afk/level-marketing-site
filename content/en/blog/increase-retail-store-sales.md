"Sales are down — we need ads" is the most common reaction among owners of physical stores. But ads affect only one part of the formula: how many people walk in. Often the cheapest ways to grow sales are inside the store itself: what share of visitors buy, how much they spend and whether they come back. Here's a systematic approach to growing store sales, with differences for different kinds of stores.

## The sales formula

**Sales = visitors × conversion × average order value × repeat purchases**

| Metric | What it is | How to measure it |
|---|---|---|
| Visitors | People who enter the store | A manual count or a counter |
| Conversion | Share of visitors who buy | Number of receipts ÷ visitors |
| Average order | The amount per purchase | Revenue ÷ number of receipts |
| Repeat | Returning customers | Loyalty card, phone number |

Raising each metric by 10% lifts total sales by almost 46% (1.1 × 1.1 × 1.1 × 1.1 ≈ 1.46). That's why four small changes often beat one big one.

## 1. Visitors: getting people through the door

- **The shop window** — stops passers-by. More in [shop window display rules](/en/blog/shop-window-display-rules/).
- **A maps listing** — "store near me" searches — [ranking higher on Yandex Maps](/en/blog/yandex-maps-promotion/).
- **Local ads** — Instagram ads within a 2–5 km radius of the store.
- **Signage** — can it be read from a distance? — [storefront signs and outdoor advertising](/en/blog/storefront-sign-outdoor-advertising-uzbekistan/).

## 2. Conversion: making sure visitors buy

Conversion is often where the biggest losses happen. Someone walks in, looks around and leaves. Causes and fixes:

- **prices aren't visible** — a clear price tag on every item;
- **no staff, or indifferent staff** — a greeting and "can I help?" within 30 seconds;
- **the right size or item isn't there** — stock and an option to order;
- **queues** — if the till wait is long, people abandon items;
- **payment options** — card, QR, instalments.

How to count — a daily table: visitors and number of receipts. A week is enough to see the first picture.

## 3. Average order value

- **Related items side by side**: a belt next to trousers, sweets next to tea.
- **Small items by the till** — impulse buys.
- **Bundles**: "shirt + tie" at one price — not a discount, a convenience.
- **Staff suggestions**: one additional offer, without pressure.

Raising order value without discounts — [the discount trap](/en/blog/discount-trap/).

## 4. Repeat purchases

- **Collect phone numbers** (with consent) — messages about new collections and deliveries.
- **A Telegram channel** — for regular customers.
- **A loyalty programme** — bonuses or private offers.
- **Service**: size exchanges, warranties — a reason to come back.

Customer types and how to work with them — [types of customers](/en/blog/types-of-customers/).

## Differences by store type

| | Clothing store | Grocery store | Pharmacy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main lever | Conversion: fitting rooms, staff | Repeat: proximity, range | Trust and service |
| Order value | Bundles, accessories | Till items, seasonal goods | Advice (within the law) |
| Channel | Instagram, Reels, new collections | Maps, the neighbourhood, delivery | Maps, hours, availability |
| Risk | Missing sizes | Queues, stale stock | Advertising restrictions |

Pharmacies face specific legal advertising requirements — medicines and supplements, discounts and the limits of advice — [medicine and supplement advertising](/en/blog/medicine-supplement-advertising-uzbekistan/). ## A weekly table: illustrative example

| Day | Visitors | Receipts | Conversion | Revenue | Average order |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | 120 | 30 | 25% | 4,500,000 | 150,000 |
| Saturday | 260 | 52 | 20% | 9,360,000 | 180,000 |

The table immediately raises a question: why is Saturday conversion lower? Perhaps there aren't enough staff, or the queue is long. The numbers are illustrative.

## Common mistakes

- Trying to grow sales with ads alone.
- Not counting visitors, so conversion is unknown.
- Missing price tags.
- Constant discounts.
- Not getting buyers' contact details — they never come back.

## Questions and answers

### How do I count visitors?

At first by hand, for a week, hour by hour. Later, if needed, a door counter.

### Which metric should I start with?

Conversion: it's usually the cheapest to improve — price tags, staff, queues.

### Does online selling hurt the store?

No, often the opposite: people see products on Instagram and visit the store. Link the two — [marketing an online store](/en/blog/marketing-an-online-store/).

### How do I measure ad results in-store?

With a promo code, a QR code or a "how did you hear about us?" question — [measure offline advertising](/en/blog/measure-offline-advertising/).

## Conclusion

Store sales are the product of four metrics: visitors, conversion, average order and repeat purchases. Ads affect only the first; the other three happen inside the store.
