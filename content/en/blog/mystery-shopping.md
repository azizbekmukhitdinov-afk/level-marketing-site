Ads are bringing in leads but sales are flat. The sales team says the leads are poor; marketing says sales is not doing its job. The fastest way to find out who is right is to become a customer yourself. That is what **mystery shopping** is: calling, sending a DM or walking into the store as an ordinary customer, then scoring the experience against a sheet prepared in advance. This guide gives checklists for three channels, a scoring sheet, how often to check, and a way to use the results for the team rather than against it.

## What mystery shopping is and what it gives you

A mystery shopper is someone who plays the role of a customer following a prepared scenario. They do not have to buy anything: the goal is to record what a customer sees, hears and feels.

The method answers three questions:

- **Does the standard work in practice?** Written rules and real conversations often differ. How to write a standard is covered in [customer service standards](/en/blog/customer-service-standards/).
- **Where are leads getting lost?** A slow reply, a wrong price, or no next step offered.
- **Do channels differ?** Calls are handled well while DMs sit unanswered; this is a very common picture.

Mystery shopping does not replace your CRM or call recordings. Recordings show what happened; a mystery shopper also sees what is never recorded: the welcome in the store, the delay in DMs, the whole process through a customer's eyes.

## What to check: a checklist for three channels

### Phone calls

- How many rings before someone answered, and whether they gave the company name and their own name.
- Whether the rep asked questions or just quoted a price.
- Whether the price and terms were correct and clear.
- Whether a next step was offered: a meeting, a measurement visit, a trial lesson, delivery.
- If the call was missed, how long it took to call back.

Compare the call against the stages in our [sales script](/en/blog/sales-script/) guide.

### Instagram DMs and Telegram

- How long the first reply took (measured separately during and after working hours).
- Whether the reply answered the question directly or went round in circles.
- Whether the rep asked a clarifying question and asked for a phone number.
- Whether a reminder was sent if the customer went quiet.

How reply speed affects sales is explained in [lead response time](/en/blog/lead-response-time/).

### The store or office

- How many seconds before someone acknowledged the customer.
- Whether the salesperson found out what the customer needed or started praising products straight away.
- Price tags, cleanliness, window display, queue at the till.
- Whether a customer who did not buy was asked for contact details or left with an offer.

## The scoring sheet: a 100-point template

Every visit must be scored on the same sheet, or the results cannot be compared. Score each criterion yes/no or 0–2 points, never with impressions like "it was fine".

| Block | Criterion | Maximum points |
|---|---|---|
| Contact | Reply speed within the standard | 15 |
| Contact | Greeting, company name and own name | 5 |
| Needs | At least 2 clarifying questions | 15 |
| Offer | Product explained in terms of the customer's need | 15 |
| Offer | Price and terms stated correctly | 10 |
| Objection | Answered with substance, without pressure | 10 |
| Close | A specific next step and time | 20 |
| Close | Follow-up at the agreed time | 10 |
| Total | | 100 |

Give the "close" block the most weight. A polite rep who ends conversations without a next step is a common sight: pleasant to talk to, but not selling.

**Scale:** 85 or above means the standard works; 70–84 means one or two specific points need work; below 70 means review the process, not one person.

## Illustrative example: a month's results

Illustrative example. A furniture store in Tashkent has 4 sales reps. Over a month it runs 12 checks: 4 calls, 4 DMs and 4 store visits.

| Channel | Average score | Weakest block |
|---|---|---|
| Calls | 78 | Close: no next step in 3 of 4 calls |
| DMs | 61 | Contact: average reply time 2 hours 40 minutes |
| Store | 84 | Objection: no answer to "it's expensive" |

Conclusion: the problem is not "bad" reps. Nobody answers DMs in the evening, and the call script has no closing stage. The store makes two process changes: an evening rota for DMs and a "book a measurement visit" step in the script. If next month the DM score rises from 61 to 79 and calls from 78 to 86, the changes worked.

Read these results alongside rep conversion rates: mystery shopping answers "why", the CRM answers "how many". The calculation is in [sales rep conversion](/en/blog/sales-rep-conversion/).

## Who can be a mystery shopper and how to write the scenario

- **Someone you know whom the team does not.** A friend, a relative, a colleague from another branch. If the owner calls in person, the team may recognise the voice.
- **An outside contractor.** More objective, but the sheet needs to be explained in detail.
- **A scenario that resembles a real customer.** Take the 3–4 most common situations: asking the price, hesitating between two options, saying "I'll think about it", making a complaint.
- **One scenario per check.** A shopper who tries to "test everything at once" does not look natural.
- **Write it down immediately.** Time, date and key phrases go into the sheet right after the visit, not from memory.

## How often to check

| Situation | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Small business, 1–3 reps | 1–2 checks a month per channel |
| Sales team of 4+ reps | 1 check a month per rep, channels rotated |
| New script or standard introduced | 3–4 extra checks within 2 weeks |
| New employee | 2 in the first month, then the normal schedule |
| Several branches | At least 2 a month per branch |

Spread checks across days and times: morning, lunchtime, evening, weekends. Problems most often surface in the "awkward" hours.

## Using the results without punishing people

Once mystery shopping becomes a tool for fines, it stops working: the team starts hunting for the shopper while treating real customers the same as before. These rules prevent that:

1. **Tell the team in advance.** Everyone knows checks will happen and what the sheet looks like. Only the date and time are secret.
2. **Score the process, not the person.** Results are first discussed in general terms: "no evening replies in DMs", not "Jasur didn't reply in the evening".
3. **Fix the system first.** The script, the rota, saved replies. A template for saved replies is in [saved replies library](/en/blog/saved-replies-library/).
4. **Individual results only one to one.** Show a rep their own conversation in private, together, framed as "what went well, what we change".
5. **Praise in public.** Share the best conversation excerpt with the team as an example.
6. **Do not tie the score to pay straight away.** Use it purely for development for the first 2–3 months. If you do link it, keep the share small and combine it with other metrics; choosing KPIs is covered in [what is a KPI](/en/blog/what-is-kpi/).

If calls are recorded, mystery shopping can be combined with AI analysis: AI listens to every conversation, while the mystery shopper sees what never reaches a recording. More in [AI call analysis](/en/blog/ai-call-analysis/).

## Common mistakes

- **Checking without a sheet.** "They spoke nicely" measures nothing.
- **Only one channel.** Checking the store and forgetting DMs means missing the place where most leads arrive.
- **A one-off exercise.** One check may be chance. A trend shows after 3 months.
- **Reading results out in front of everyone.** It destroys trust in one go.
- **A report without decisions.** Each month of checks should end with 1–3 decisions about what changes.

## Questions and answers

### Do I need to hire a specialist company?

Not for a small business. A good sheet and 2–3 reliable people are enough. An outside contractor helps when there are many branches, a large team, or a strong need for objectivity.

### Should employees be told about the checks?

Yes, tell them the system exists, but not the dates and times. Open rules keep trust and encourage the team to work to the standard all the time.

### How long does one check take?

A call or DM takes 10–20 minutes, a store visit 30–40 minutes, plus 10 minutes to fill in the sheet. Twelve checks a month is roughly 5–6 hours of work.

### How do I connect the results with customer feedback?

Run both in parallel: mystery shopping checks the process, real customers rate the outcome. Ways to gather feedback are in [how to collect customer feedback](/en/blog/collecting-customer-feedback/).

## Conclusion

Mystery shopping shows your sales team through a customer's eyes. It covers three channels, is scored on one sheet and is repeated regularly. Most importantly, the results are used to fix the process, not to punish people. Then the team does not fear the checks; it learns from them.
