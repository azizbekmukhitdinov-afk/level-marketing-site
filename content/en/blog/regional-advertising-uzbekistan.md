Many Tashkent businesses eventually hit the same wall: ads in the capital have become expensive, competition has grown and cost per lead keeps rising month after month. The natural question follows — should you expand into the regions? Regional ads often bring cheaper leads, but those leads don't always turn into sales. Let's look at how to plan regional advertising, which province to start with and how to measure the result.

## First question: can you actually serve the region?

Before launching ads, check the operations side. A lead from the regions differs from a Tashkent lead: the customer can't visit your store, can't touch the product and doesn't know you.

Five questions to ask yourself:

1. **Delivery** — how many days, and who pays? Is it included in the price?
2. **Payment** — customers are afraid to prepay. Do you offer cash on delivery or a partial prepayment?
3. **Returns** — what happens if the product doesn't fit?
4. **Service** — how do installation, repairs and warranty work in the region?
5. **Answers** — can your salesperson immediately answer regional questions (delivery time, price by district)?

Without clear answers, regional leads will look "low quality", when the real problem is the offer, not the ads.

## Tashkent vs the regions: how the audiences differ

| Aspect | Tashkent | Regions |
|---|---|---|
| Ad competition | High, leads cost more | Often lower |
| Trust | Reviews, brand, portfolio | A familiar face, an address, "many people bought" |
| Language | Bilingual | Mostly Uzbek |
| Decision | Faster | More advice, decided with family |
| Main fear | Quality | Being cheated, delivery |

That's why regional creatives must be different. "Modern design" may work in Tashkent, while "Delivered to Samarkand in 2 days, pay on delivery" (illustrative example) is stronger in the regions. More on language in [ad language: Uzbek or Russian](/en/blog/ad-language-uzbek-or-russian/).

## Which region to start with

Going into every province at once spreads the budget too thin. Start with one or two. How to choose:

- **Existing data**: which provinces already send you customers organically, according to your CRM? That's your most ready market.
- **Logistics**: where is delivery fast and cheap?
- **Population and business activity**: larger cities (Samarkand, the Fergana Valley cities, Bukhara, Karshi and others) usually come first.
- **Competition**: is there a similar local offer?

The first 2–4 weeks are a test. Based on the result, you add the next region.

## How to structure the ad account

### Separate ad sets

Don't mix Tashkent and the regions in one ad set. The algorithm will pour the budget wherever leads are cheapest — which may be a region you can't serve. Separate ad sets make the budget and result for each region clear.

### The location setting

By default Meta targets "people living in or recently in this location". If you only want actual residents (for example, for renovation or a clinic), check the setting and add a city question to the form — see [lead form questions](/en/blog/lead-form-questions/).

### The region's name in the creative

Naming the city in the creative is a strong signal for regional audiences: "For Namangan residents", "Free delivery to Bukhara". People see the ad is meant for them.

## Building trust in the regions

Regional customers don't know you, and trust is the biggest barrier. What works:

- **Reviews from customers in the same region**: "Dilnoza from Andijan ordered" (with the customer's consent) beats a generic review.
- **Delivery videos**: how the product is packed and how it arrives.
- **Phone number and address**: clearly visible.
- **Pay on delivery**: if possible, the strongest argument.
- **A local partner or pickup point**: for big purchases, a showroom or dealer in the region.

## Measuring results by region

The main mistake is judging regional ads by cost per lead. Leads there may be cheaper, but conversion is lower, delivery costs more and returns are more frequent. The right measure is **profit per sale**.

Illustrative example: a furniture store.

| Metric | Tashkent | Samarkand |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per lead | 60,000 soum | 35,000 soum |
| Lead-to-sale rate | 10% | 6% |
| Ad cost per sale | 600,000 soum | 583,000 soum |
| Delivery cost | 100,000 soum | 400,000 soum |
| Total cost per sale | 700,000 soum | 983,000 soum |

The lead was almost half the price, but the sale cost 40% more. That doesn't mean you shouldn't go regional — it means delivery needs to be built into the price or a minimum order set. For the math, see [unit economics](/en/blog/unit-economics-for-small-business/).

Every lead in the CRM needs a "region" field, or you'll never be able to build this table.

## Common mistakes

- Putting all of Uzbekistan in one ad set and letting the algorithm "pick".
- Running Tashkent creatives in the regions unchanged.
- Not settling delivery terms in advance, so the salesperson keeps saying "I'll find out".
- Not tagging leads by region.
- Not replying to regional leads after Tashkent business hours — people in the regions often write in the evening.

## Questions and answers

### Is advertising in the regions cheaper?

Cost per lead is often lower because there's less competition. But total cost per sale depends on delivery and conversion, so measure by sales.

### Do I need a separate creative for every province?

Not for all of them. But a separate version with the city name and local terms for your top 2–3 regions noticeably improves results.

### Should I open a branch in the region or is delivery enough?

Test demand with delivery first. If one region brings steady sales, you can consider a pickup point or branch — see [marketing a second branch](/en/blog/marketing-a-second-branch/).

### Who should handle regional leads?

A salesperson who knows the regional terms and speaks Uzbek fluently, with a delivery price and timing table at hand.

## Conclusion

Regional advertising isn't about cheap leads — it's a new market. To make it work you need the right offer (delivery, payment, warranty), separate creatives and measurement through to sales.
