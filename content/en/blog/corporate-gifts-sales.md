In November and December, companies look for New Year gifts for employees, clients and partners. For sellers of sweets, flowers, books, textiles, accessories, cosmetics and souvenirs, it's one of the biggest B2B opportunities of the year. A single corporate order can equal dozens of retail purchases. But in this market, whoever makes a fast, well-organised offer wins. Here's how to sell corporate gifts.

## Who makes the decision

A corporate gift is usually chosen by a chain of people, not one person:

| Role | What matters |
|---|---|
| HR or office manager | Within budget, on time, no hassle |
| Marketing department | The brand logo, packaging, impression |
| Accounts | A contract, an invoice, bank payment |
| The director | Quality and the company's reputation |

Your offer should answer each of them: a price range, a branding sample, paperwork and a deadline.

## A catalogue with 3 price tiers

Companies start from a budget: "X soum per employee". So build the catalogue in price tiers:

- **Basic** — for companies with large headcounts;
- **Mid-range** — for department heads and regular clients;
- **Premium** — for key partners and VIP clients.

For each bundle: a photo, the contents, minimum order, branding options and lead time. How to present prices — [should you show the price in your ads](/en/blog/should-you-show-price-in-ads/).

## Branding is your main advantage

Packaging, a card or ribbon with the company's logo makes a gift personal rather than "bought in a shop". It also makes price comparison harder. Set out the mock-up approval process in advance: who approves, when and how many revisions.

## Deadlines are where things break

At the end of December everyone orders at once, and production, packing and delivery get overloaded. So:

- make an "early order" offer — for example, free branding on orders placed before 1 December (an illustrative example);
- set a clear final date for orders;
- plan a delivery schedule to offices.

## The B2B offer and sales process

1. **A list**: last year's clients, nearby companies, partners.
2. **A short first message**: "Our New Year gift catalogue — 3 budgets, with your logo. Shall I send it?"
3. **The catalogue and a proposal** — [commercial proposal template](/en/blog/commercial-proposal-template/).
4. **A sample**: for large orders, deliver one sample bundle to the office.
5. **Contract and payment**: an invoice, paid by bank transfer.

## A base for next year

Every corporate client is a repeat client for next year and for other holidays (8 March, Navruz, Ramadan). Keep in your CRM: the company, contact person, budget, what they bought and when. Then, at the end of October, get in touch first — [repeat sales and LTV](/en/blog/repeat-sales-and-ltv/).

## Questions and answers

### What should the minimum order be?

It depends on production and branding costs. Keep a small, unbranded option for smaller companies.

### Should I take a deposit?

For branded orders, yes — a partial prepayment is usual, since products with someone's logo can't be sold to anyone else.

### When should I start?

From early November. By mid-December, many companies have already chosen.
