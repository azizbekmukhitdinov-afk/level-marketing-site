Doctors, psychologists, lawyers, attorneys, dentists — clients usually reach specialists like these through referrals. Referrals are great, but you can't speed them up. That's why many experts want to win clients through Instagram, under their own name. Two things get in the way: "what do I talk about?" and "I feel awkward on camera". This article offers a practical answer to both.

![Why Reels work for experts](/img/blog/illus/ekspert-uchun-reels-1.jpg 1200x675)

## Why Reels work for experts

When people choose a specialist, they choose the person first. They wonder: does this doctor explain things clearly? Will this lawyer understand my situation? A short video answers that: face, voice, manner, knowledge — all visible in 30 seconds.

As a result:

- people arrive at the consultation already "knowing" you;
- they haggle less over price, because they choose on trust, not cost;
- referrals speed up: people send your videos to friends and family.

## Where to start: 4 content pillars

Split your content plan into four simple pillars:

| Pillar | What it covers | Example topics |
|---|---|---|
| Answers to questions | What clients ask most in consultations | "Does it hurt?", "How long does it take?", "When should I come in?" |
| Myths | What people wrongly believe | "It'll go away by itself", "You don't need to read the contract" |
| Process | What the work looks like from inside | How an appointment goes, how documents are prepared |
| Results and stories | The path from problem to solution | Only with the client's consent and without revealing identity |

The easiest start is the first pillar. For a week, write down the questions you get in consultations — that's a month of content topics.

## How to beat camera fear

- **Start with an answer, not a speech.** Not "talking to camera" but "answering a patient's question" — something you do every day.
- **Try the interview format.** Someone off-camera asks a question and you answer. It comes out more natural — [expert interview videos](/en/blog/expert-interview-videos/).
- **Write a short script.** Not a full text, just 3–4 key points. Ready-made structures — [Reels script templates](/en/blog/reels-script-templates/).
- **Shoot your first 10 videos without publishing.** Practise, then pick the best.
- **A phone is enough.** Light and sound matter more than the camera — [shooting Reels on a phone](/en/blog/shooting-reels-on-a-phone/).

## One shoot day, a month of content

Filming every day isn't realistic for an expert. A better approach is a "shoot day" once or twice a month:

1. Prepare 10–15 topics and short scripts in advance.
2. Bring 2–3 outfits and use 2–3 backdrops (office, corridor, desk) so the videos don't look the same.
3. Film each video 2–3 times with a different opening (hook) — [Reels hooks](/en/blog/reels-hooks/).
4. Hand the edit to someone else: subtitles, pace, formats.

One day yields several weeks of content, and you don't get pulled away from your main work.

## From profile to consultation: the funnel

A video brings someone in, but turning them into a client needs a path:

| Stage | What needs to be there |
|---|---|
| Reels | One question or myth, a clear and useful answer |
| Profile | Who you are, who you help, where you practise — [Instagram profile that sells](/en/blog/instagram-profile-that-sells/) |
| Highlights | Prices, how appointments work, results, reviews, address |
| DMs or form | A fast reply and booking a consultation |
| Consultation | A clear plan and price — [from consultation to sale](/en/blog/consultation-to-sale-conversion/) |

Often the videos are good, but the profile or the DM reply loses the client.

## Ethics and the law

In medicine and law, trust is fragile. A few rules:

- don't guarantee treatment or case outcomes;
- use client or patient stories only with written consent and without revealing identity;
- don't diagnose or give individual advice in videos — give general information and invite people to a consultation;
- follow advertising rules — [advertising law in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/advertising-law-uzbekistan/).

## What to measure

- which pillar gets saved and shared most — that's the one building trust;
- profile visits and follows;
- DM enquiries and consultation bookings;
- how many people at consultations "saw your videos" — ask at the appointment.

![Common mistakes](/img/blog/illus/ekspert-uchun-reels-3.jpg 1200x675)

## Common mistakes

- Only pretty photos and greetings, no useful content.
- Language that's too academic — clients don't follow.
- Trying for a month and stopping if there's no result.
- Investing in video without sorting out the profile and DMs.
- Using a client's story without consent.

## Questions and answers

### How often should I post?

Consistency matters more than volume. Start with 3–4 videos a week and keep a steady rhythm.

### When will I see results?

It usually takes a few months: trust builds over time. The first signs are saves, shares and questions in DMs.

### Do I need ads?

Organic content builds trust; ads carry it to more people. Promoting your best videos is an effective combination.

### Do I have to make the content myself?

You're the face and the knowledge. Scripts, filming, editing and publishing can be handled by a team or agency.

## Conclusion

Reels for experts aren't advertising — they build trust: answering clients' questions, busting myths, showing the process.
