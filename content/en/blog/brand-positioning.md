In your category, customers remember a handful of brands — usually two or three. "Cheap", "fast", "premium", "for kids" — each is linked in their mind to a single word. **Positioning** is deliberately choosing the word you'll be remembered by. A brand without positioning becomes "everything for everyone" and memorable to no one. Here's how to define your positioning. What a brand is — [what is a brand](/en/blog/what-is-a-brand/).

## The positioning formula

In one sentence:

> **For [audience], [brand] is the [category] that delivers [main benefit], because [proof].**

An illustrative example: "For young families moving into a new home, Zamon Mebel is the custom kitchen maker that delivers in 14 days, because we do our own manufacturing."

## Step 1: a competitor map

Pick two important dimensions (say, price and speed) and place competitors on a map:

| | Cheap | Expensive |
|---|---|---|
| **Fast** | An empty spot? | Premium express |
| **Slow** | Market imports | Traditional workshop |

An empty quadrant may be an opportunity. Studying competitors — [competitor analysis](/en/blog/competitor-analysis/).

## Step 2: choose your difference

Positioning can be built on one of:

- **audience**: "only for children", "B2B only";
- **benefit**: speed, guarantee, convenience;
- **price tier**: budget or premium — [premium segment marketing](/en/blog/premium-segment-marketing/);
- **method**: "home visits", "online";
- **expertise**: a narrow specialism.

The rule: the difference must be **important to customers**, **genuinely yours** and **absent from competitors**. More in [unique selling proposition](/en/blog/unique-selling-proposition/).

## Step 3: proof

Positioning lives on proof, not promises: figures, case studies, reviews, certificates, process videos — [social proof](/en/blog/social-proof-in-ads/).

## Step 4: apply it everywhere

- **Bio and website headline** — the positioning in one line.
- **Ads** — every creative reinforces it.
- **Content** — topics that prove it.
- **Sales script** — salespeople voice the same difference — [sales script](/en/blog/sales-script/).
- **Visual style** — a premium or budget look that matches — [visual identity for small business](/en/blog/visual-identity-for-small-business/).

## Common mistakes

- "Quality, reliability, professionalism" — everyone says that.
- Trying to please everyone.
- A new positioning every month, confusing customers.
- Promises without proof.
- Copying a competitor.

## When to change your positioning

When the market shifts, your audience changes or the old positioning limits growth. It's a big decision, often made alongside a rebrand — [when to rebrand](/en/blog/when-to-rebrand/).

## Questions and answers

### Does a small business need positioning?

Especially so: without a big budget, a clear difference is the only way to be remembered.

### How does positioning differ from a slogan?

Positioning is the internal strategy; a slogan is its short, memorable expression — [how to create a slogan](/en/blog/how-to-create-a-slogan/).

### Is positioning on price a good idea?

"The cheapest" is a dangerous position: someone will always be cheaper. Positioning on value is more durable.
