A product video is the fastest way to show what a buyer can't pick up and hold. Yet many shops have videos that do nothing: a pretty clip on the listing that explains nothing, and an ad that gets scrolled past in the first second. Here are 7 common product video mistakes for marketplaces and paid social, and how to fix each one.

![Why you need a product video](/img/blog/illus/mahsulot-videosi-1.jpg 1200x675)

## Why you need a product video

A photo shows what a product looks like; a video shows how it *works*. For a buyer that difference is huge: how does the sofa unfold, will a laptop fit in the bag, can the blender crush ice? Photos rarely answer that.

Video works in two places, and each has its own job:

| Where | Viewer's state | The video's job |
|---|---|---|
| Marketplace listing | Already searched for the product, comparing | Answer questions, remove doubts |
| Paid social (Reels, TikTok) | Not looking for it, scrolling the feed | Stop the scroll, spark interest, get the click |

Most mistakes come from mixing up these two jobs. Let's go through them.

## Mistake 1: one video for everything

The ad goes on the listing, or the listing video goes into the ad. The listing ends up full of music and effects but short on information, while the ad spends its first 5 seconds slowly spinning the product.

**How to fix it:** shoot for both jobs on the same day.

- **For the listing** — a calm, explanatory video: the product from every side, dimensions, how to use it, what's in the box.
- **For the ad** — a short, dynamic vertical video: a strong opening, one main benefit, the offer.

You can cut both from the same footage, but they need different scripts.

## Mistake 2: the product is small and blurry in the frame

The product gets lost: you see the interior, the model, the props, and the product itself is a tiny dot. On a phone screen it's even worse.

Yandex Market's seller help says the product should fill at least two-thirds of the frame in most shots ([Yandex Market help](https://yandex.com/support/marketplace/ru/assortment/create/main-fields/video)). That's a good benchmark for other platforms too.

**How to fix it:**

- Shoot the product close; use a wide shot once or twice at most.
- Show details in separate close-ups: fabric, stitching, buttons, ports.
- Keep the background simple: a plain wall, a clean table.

## Mistake 3: the product is spun, not used

A product rotating on a table is the most common and least useful video. It repeats what the photos already show.

**How to fix it:** show the product in action. Every category has its "proof shot":

| Product | What to show |
|---|---|
| Sofa, armchair | Unfolding, the storage box inside, how soft it is to sit on |
| Clothing | On a person in motion, fabric close up, inner seams |
| Home appliances | Switching on, modes, noise level, cleaning |
| Bag, backpack | What fits inside, pockets, fasteners |
| Cosmetics | Texture, applying it to the hand, opening the packaging |

For example, a 40-second listing video for a furniture store in Tashkent: the sofa folded → unfolding it → the bedding box → fabric close up → an on-screen line with the dimensions. Those five shots answer the buyer's main questions.

## Mistake 4: the video only makes sense with sound

Plenty of people watch with the sound off: on the bus, at work, late at night. If all the information is in the seller's voice, a muted viewer gets nothing.

Yandex Market asks sellers to add subtitles when there is speech and to make videos understandable without sound. Meta strongly recommends sound for Reels ads and also recommends captions ([Meta Ads Guide](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/instagram-reels)). So you need both: sound for those who turn it on, text for those who don't.

**How to fix it:**

- Repeat every key point as short on-screen text: "Unfolds in 3 seconds", "2 storage boxes inside".
- If you speak on camera, add subtitles. Editing apps generate them automatically, but always proofread. More on tools in [video editing apps](/en/blog/video-editing-apps/).
- Don't use licensed music in ads: Meta doesn't allow it; your own audio or free tracks from Meta's Sound Collection are fine.

## Mistake 5: the product shows up too late in the ad

The ad opens with a logo, an intro, or "Hi, today I'm going to tell you...". In the feed people give that 1–2 seconds and move on.

TikTok's creative guidance advises introducing your main proposition in the first 3 seconds ([TikTok Ads help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)). The same logic applies to Instagram Reels.

**How to fix it:** put the product or the result in the very first shot.

- Problem → solution: "Need a sofa for a small room?" — and instantly the sofa unfolds.
- Result first: the finished look, then how it's done.
- An unexpected action: a test, a comparison, a "stress test".

Ready-made opening ideas are in [10 Reels hooks](/en/blog/reels-hooks/), and the formats themselves in [7 types of video ads](/en/blog/types-of-video-ads/).

## Mistake 6: ignoring format and safe zones

A horizontal video looks tiny in a vertical feed. And in a vertical video, a price or headline at the bottom gets covered by Instagram's buttons and the ad text.

For Instagram Reels ads, Meta recommends a 9:16 ratio at 1440×2560 pixels. Keep text, logos and key elements out of the top 14%, the bottom 35% and the 6% on each side — that's where the profile icon and call-to-action button sit ([Meta Ads Guide](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/instagram-reels)).

**How to fix it:**

| Where | Format | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Reels, Stories, TikTok ads | Vertical 9:16 | Text in the centre, bottom third clear |
| Instagram feed | 4:5 | Product in the centre |
| Marketplace listing | Per platform rules | Check the requirements in the seller dashboard |

Each marketplace has its own rules, and they change. Yandex Market, for instance, allows up to 6 videos per listing, recommends about a minute or less, and bans other sites' watermarks, prices and external links in videos. Before uploading to Uzum Market or any other platform, read the current requirements in its seller dashboard.

## Mistake 7: shooting once and never measuring

One ad gets shot, launched, and judged: "video doesn't work." More often it's not the product that fails, but the opening or the first shot.

**How to fix it:**

- Shoot 3–4 different openings for the same video, keeping the rest identical.
- Run them as separate ads.
- Look beyond views to leads and sales: cost per click, number of leads, cost per lead. Which metrics matter is covered in [what an ad report should include](/en/blog/what-an-ad-report-should-include/).

*Illustrative example:* a store made 4 videos for one sofa, each with a different opening. After a week of testing, the version that opened with the sofa unfolding brought cheaper leads than the rest, so the budget moved to it. The point isn't the numbers but the method: change one thing at a time.

## Pre-shoot checklist

- Two scripts written: one for the listing, one for the ad.
- A "proof shot" defined for each product: how it works.
- Clean background, even light, the product fills most of the frame.
- The ad's first shot shows the product or the result.
- Key points repeated as on-screen text, subtitles proofread.
- A vertical 9:16 version exists, with text inside the safe zone.
- Marketplace requirements checked in the seller dashboard.
- 3–4 different openings shot for the ad.

For light, sound and framing on a phone, see [shooting Reels on a phone](/en/blog/shooting-reels-on-a-phone/).

## Questions and answers

**Do I need a professional camera for product videos?**
No. A modern phone, a tripod and good light are enough. What matters is that the product is clearly visible and the video says something useful.

**How long should a listing video be?**
Check the platform's rules. Short usually works best: answer the main questions — how it works, size, what's included — in 30–60 seconds.

**Can I use one video for both Instagram and TikTok ads?**
Yes, if it's vertical, the text sits inside the safe zone and the music is allowed on both platforms. But test it on each platform separately.

**Should I show the price in the video?**
In ads, yes, if price is your strong point. On marketplace listings, prices in videos are often banned because they change — check the platform's rules.

## Conclusion

A good product video starts with a clear job, not expensive gear. On a listing it answers the buyer's questions; in an ad it stops the scroll in the first seconds. Show the product close up and in use, make it work without sound, fit the format to the platform, and test several openings.
