The first branch works, customers are coming, the business is growing — the natural next step is a second branch. Many owners think: "the first one worked, so the second will too." But second branches often grow more slowly than expected: nobody in the new district knows you, the first branch's reputation doesn't transfer automatically, and the ads blur between two locations. Here's how to organise marketing for a second branch.

## Using marketing data to choose the location

The site for a second branch is often chosen by rent or "a friend's building". Marketing data is a much better adviser:

- **Addresses in the CRM**: which districts do your customers come from? An area with many customers travelling far is ready-made demand.
- **Ad statistics**: which areas send many leads in Meta but few sales? Perhaps you're too far for them.
- **Delivery addresses**: where do most online orders go?
- **Maps searches**: Google and Yandex listing statistics show where people request directions from.
- **Competition**: is there a similar business in the chosen area, and how does it perform?

## Before opening: 4–8 weeks

A second branch succeeds not by waiting for "we've opened, customers will come", but by marketing that starts before opening:

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| 8 weeks before | Create a maps listing (with the opening date), confirm the site |
| 6 weeks before | Local ads: "Coming soon to Yunusabad" + a waiting list |
| 4 weeks before | Renovation and preparation videos, local influencers |
| 2 weeks before | An opening offer for the waiting list |
| Opening | An opening-day event, local ads, start collecting reviews |

## Leveraging the first branch's reputation

The biggest advantage of a second branch is that it doesn't start from zero. The first has reviews, customers and content. Use them:

- **a message to existing customers**: the new branch may be closer for them;
- **"5 years at our Chilanzar branch"** — show your experience in ads;
- **one service standard**: customers expect the same quality at the second branch;
- **reviews**: but each branch's maps listing should collect its own.

## Maps listings: one per branch

The most common mistake is both branches sharing one listing — or the second having none at all. For each branch:

- a separate listing on Google and Yandex Maps;
- its own address, phone, hours and photos;
- its own reviews — ask the first customers after opening;
- listings that don't contradict each other.

More in [add your business to maps](/en/blog/add-your-business-to-maps/). Reviews — [online reputation and reviews](/en/blog/online-reputation-and-reviews/).

## Ads: one account, separate results

Both branches can sit in one ad account, but results must be visible separately:

- **separate campaigns** or ad sets per branch, targeting the area around each;
- **branch name and address in the creative**: "Yunusabad, ... street" — people instantly know where to go;
- **separate forms or UTMs**: the CRM shows which branch each lead is for — [UTM tags](/en/blog/utm-tags/);
- **lead routing**: each lead goes to the right branch's salesperson.

## Budget: more for the new branch

A typical mistake is splitting the budget equally. A new branch needs more awareness and customer flow in its first months. An illustrative approach:

- first 2–3 months: a larger share of the total budget to the new branch;
- after that: by results — cost per customer and capacity at each branch.

It's normal for the new branch's cost per customer to be higher in the first months. What matters is the trend: it should fall month by month.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing the site on rent alone.
- Starting ads on opening day — too late.
- One maps listing for two branches.
- Not saying which branch the ad is for.
- Not separating results by branch.
- Not controlling service standards at the new branch — its first negative reviews hit the whole brand.

## Questions and answers

### Does the second branch need its own Instagram?

Usually not: one brand page, with branches covered in highlights and posts. If the branches differ a lot or are in another city, a separate page may make sense.

### How long until a new branch pays off?

It depends on the niche and location. From a marketing standpoint, the first 2–3 months are an awareness period; after that, cost per customer should approach the first branch's.

### Won't first-branch customers move to the second and leave the first empty?

That's a risk if the branches are too close. When choosing the site, check customer addresses in the CRM so each branch serves a different audience.

### With a franchise, who's responsible for marketing?

That depends on the contract. Typically brand marketing is central and local ads are the branch's. Write the rules down in advance.

## Conclusion

A second branch is a new market, but not a start from zero. Choose the site using data, start marketing 4–8 weeks before opening, give each branch its own listing and ad set, and measure results per branch.
