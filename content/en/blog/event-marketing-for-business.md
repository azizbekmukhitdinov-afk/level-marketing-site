Many events go beautifully: a full room, great photos. A month later, someone asks: "So where are the results?" **Event marketing** means building trust and sales through meeting customers in person. The secret isn't the event itself — it's what you do before and after it.

## Types of events

- **Open classes or masterclasses** — showcase expertise (education, beauty, cooking).
- **Business breakfasts and seminars** — a small, high-quality B2B audience — [B2B marketing](/en/blog/b2b-marketing-uzbekistan/).
- **Openings and launches** — a new branch or product — [marketing before opening](/en/blog/marketing-before-opening/).
- **Trade shows** — contacts through a stand — [exhibiting at a trade show](/en/blog/exhibiting-at-a-trade-show/).
- **Private events for customers** — loyalty and repeat sales.
- **Online events** — webinars and live streams.

## Goals and planning

Before the event, write down one main goal:

- new leads (how many?);
- meetings or contracts;
- repeat purchases from existing customers;
- brand awareness (how will you measure it?).

Then decide who the audience is, what the offer is and what guests should do afterwards.

## Registration and reminders

- A simple registration form: name, phone and 1–2 questions (company, interest).
- Consent when collecting and storing data — [customer databases and personal data](/en/blog/customer-database-personal-data-uzbekistan/).
- Reminders 1 day and 2–3 hours before.
- Some people who register for a free event won't come — plan for it.

## At the event

- Collect contacts: a QR code, a short form, in exchange for a gift.
- Useful content, not a sales pitch: guests should leave having learned something.
- A clear next step: a free consultation, a special offer, a meeting.
- Film and photograph it — for Reels and posts later.

## After the event — the most important part

- **Within 24–48 hours**, message or call every guest.
- Add leads to your CRM and assign them to sales reps — [CRM for small business](/en/blog/crm-for-small-business/).
- Send the recording or materials to those who didn't come.
- Turn the event footage into content.

## Budget (illustrative example)

A business breakfast for 30 people:

- venue and food — 6,000,000 soums;
- ads and invitations — 3,000,000 soums;
- materials and gifts — 1,500,000 soums.

That's 10.5 million soums in total. If 5 meetings lead to 2 contracts, each client costs 5.25 million soums — compare that with a client's value — [customer acquisition cost (CAC)](/en/blog/customer-acquisition-cost-cac/).

## Measuring results

- registered → attended → leads → meetings → sales;
- cost per lead and per client;
- sales in the 30–60 days after the event.

## Common mistakes

- An event with no goal — held "because we should".
- No contacts collected, or nobody calls afterwards.
- A one-off event with no follow-up series.
- Pure self-promotion with no useful content.

## Questions and answers

### Does a small business need events?

Yes, if the product needs trust or explanation (education, B2B, healthcare, expensive services).

### Free or paid events?

Free events get more registrations but lower attendance. A small fee means fewer, but more interested, guests.

### How often should I hold them?

Regular events beat a single big one: monthly or quarterly, for example.
