Medicine and supplement advertising in Uzbekistan is among the most tightly regulated areas of marketing. A single Story saying "our client recovered" can already break the law. This guide puts the rules for pharmacies, clinics and supplement sellers into one table, swaps risky phrases for safer ones and sets out a pre-launch check.

> This article is not legal advice. We explain the text of the law in plain language. For a specific case, talk to a lawyer.

## Why medicine and supplement advertising is under closer watch

In August 2026 the Committee for the Development of Competition and Consumer Protection again reminded advertisers of the supplement rules: an ad must not suggest that a supplement has a therapeutic effect ([Podrobno.uz](https://podrobno.uz/cat/proisshestviya/ne-yavlyaetsya-lekarstvennym-sredstvom-v-uzbekistane-vnov-napomnili-o-pravilakh-reklamy-badov/)).

It goes beyond reminders. Two cases from the past year:

- In December 2025 a supplement maker was fined after videos claimed the product treated stomach and respiratory illnesses ([Spot.uz](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2025/12/15/ajr-fined/)).
- In March 2026 a report was filed against a blogger and the case went to court: an Instagram video had compared a supplement with a medicine ([Spot.uz](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2026/03/26/kahramon/)).

So liability does not stop at the manufacturer. We covered the general rules in our guide to [advertising law in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/advertising-law-uzbekistan/); here we look only at the health-related articles.

## Medicines, supplements and folk medicine: the rules in one table

The law (No. ZRU-776, [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/6052633)) has a separate article for each group. First work out which group your product belongs to: the registration document will say.

These medicines cannot be advertised at all (Article 34):

- prescription-only medicines;
- medicines containing narcotic, psychotropic or potent substances;
- medicines not approved for medical use in Uzbekistan;
- medicines not registered with the Ministry of Health.

For everything else, the rules differ:

| Rule | Medicines (Art. 34) | Supplements (Art. 35) | Folk medicine (Art. 45) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Citing specific cases of recovery | Banned | Banned | Banned |
| Thanks from individuals | Banned | Banned | Not addressed separately |
| Celebrities, doctors or people who look like doctors | Banned | Not addressed separately | Not addressed separately |
| Recommendations by scientists and doctors | Banned | May not use research to imply superiority over medicines | May not use research to imply superiority |
| Guaranteed results | Banned (over-the-counter) | May not be presented as a medicine | Banned |
| Implying no doctor is needed | Banned | Not addressed separately | Banned |
| Contests, games, prize promotions | Banned | Banned | Banned |
| Mandatory warning | Contraindications exist; read the leaflet or consult a specialist (over-the-counter) | Not a medicinal product | Contraindications exist; read the leaflet or consult a specialist |

"Not addressed separately" does not mean allowed. If a celebrity says a supplement cured them, Article 35 is still breached. The 2025 case above also featured celebrities.

Promotions are another trap. The law defines an incentive promotion as advertising in the form of an event, contest or game. So do not run an [Instagram giveaway](/en/blog/instagram-giveaway-rules-uzbekistan/) for a medicine, supplement or folk remedy. Whether an ordinary discount falls under that definition depends on the mechanics: check with a lawyer.

## Clinic advertising: what the law requires

The law has no separate article on advertising medical services. That does not mean a clinic can say anything. These rules still apply:

- **Licence.** Advertising a licensed activity without the document is banned (Art. 16), and you must show it on request (Art. 12).
- **No misleading claims.** "100% cure" or "no pain, no complications" without proof can count as improper advertising (Art. 5).
- **Naming a medicine.** If a doctor in a clinic video praises a specific drug, it becomes a medicine ad, and Article 34 bans doctors from appearing in those.
- **Folk medicine.** Advertising diagnosis and treatment by methods not permitted in this field is banned (Art. 45).
- **Patient consent.** A person's name or image can only be used with their consent (Art. 16). Health data is a sensitive matter of its own: see our guide to [customer data and personal data](/en/blog/customer-database-personal-data-uzbekistan/).

Our guide to [marketing a clinic](/en/blog/marketing-a-clinic/) covers how to earn trust. In short: talk about your doctors' experience, equipment, process and price, and do not promise an outcome.

## How to rewrite a risky ad

Most breaches come from ordinary sales language rather than bad intent. Here is a substitution table. All examples are illustrative.

| Do not write | Safer version | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Cures joint pain in 30 days" (supplement) | "Contains collagen. Not a medicinal product" | A supplement cannot claim to treat anything |
| "More effective and cheaper than medicines" | Ingredients and price, no comparison | A supplement cannot be shown as better than a medicine |
| "Our client Dilnoza recovered in 2 months" | Ingredients, how to take it, certificate details | Recovery stories and thanks are banned |
| An actor in a white coat recommends a drug | Packaging, name, where to buy | People who look like doctors are banned |
| "The safest medicine, no side effects" | "Contraindications exist. Read the leaflet or consult a specialist" | An OTC medicine cannot be called the safest |
| "Skip it and you'll get worse" | What the product is for, based on the leaflet | Suggesting health will decline without it is banned |
| "Guaranteed recovery at our clinic" | "At your first visit the doctor will explain the examination plan" | An unproven promise misleads |

Be careful with reviews too. [Online reviews](/en/blog/online-reputation-and-reviews/) help a clinic, but do not turn an "I got better" review of a medicine or supplement into an ad.

## Health ads on Meta: rules on top of the law

Even a legally compliant ad can be rejected by Meta. Its [Health and wellness standard](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness/) says:

- ads for weight loss, weight gain and health products and services must target people aged 18 or over;
- side-by-side weight-loss comparisons are not allowed (fitness class results are an exception);
- the same goes for wrinkle treatments such as Botox and fillers;
- before-and-after images for general cosmetic procedures are allowed as long as the ad does not make people feel bad about how they look;
- ads cannot claim to cure incurable illnesses such as cancer or diabetes.

These rules change; the latest revision was in July 2026. Check the page again before launch.

## 6 steps before launching an ad

1. **Identify the group.** Medicine, supplement, folk remedy or service? Look at the registration document. If it is prescription-only, do not advertise it.
2. **Prepare the paperwork.** Keep registration, certificate and licence in one folder so you can send them to an agency or blogger immediately.
3. **Check the copy against the table.** Search for "cures", "recovered", "guarantee", "best".
4. **Look at who is speaking.** A medicine ad must not feature a doctor, a white coat or a famous face.
5. **Make the warning readable.** In every ad: in the post text and, in video, as an on-screen caption that stays long enough to read.
6. **Brief the blogger.** Send the banned words and the mandatory warning in writing and review the post before it goes live. Our guide to [influencer advertising](/en/blog/influencer-advertising-uzbekistan/) covers the process.

**Illustrative example.** A pharmacy in Tashkent wants to post a Story about an over-the-counter vitamin. The draft says: "Restore your immunity in 7 days!". After the check, what remains is the product name, price in soums, nearest branches and the warning. If the vitamin is registered as a supplement, it reads "Not a medicinal product"; if as a medicine, the note about contraindications and the leaflet.

## What the fines are and who is liable

Under Article 47, for legal entities:

| Breach | Fine | In soums |
|---|---|---|
| Advertising a product whose advertising is banned or restricted | 100 BCA | 44 million soums |
| Improper advertising | 70 BCA | 30.8 million soums |
| Breaching the requirements for medicine and supplement ads | 30 BCA | 13.2 million soums |

Calculated at 1 BCA (base calculation amount) = 440,000 soums from 1 September 2026 ([Buxgalter.uz](https://buxgalter.uz/ru/publish/doc/text194589_uzbekistonda_bhm_mhekm_phbm_va_mih_miqdorlari)). Paying the fine does not remove the duty to comply with the Committee's order.

Article 49 splits liability: the advertiser answers for the content of the information, the producer for making the ad, and the distributor for its time, place and channel. Relying on a blogger or agency is no defence.

## Questions and answers

**Is the "Not a medicinal product" warning always required?**
Yes. Article 35 requires it in every case: post, video, banner or influencer integration.

**Can a doctor advertise a supplement?**
Article 35 has no separate ban on doctors. But if a doctor says the supplement treats something or recommends it instead of a medicine, the article is breached. The risk is high, so consult a lawyer first.

**Can a clinic publish before-and-after photos?**
The law has no separate ban for clinic services, but you need the patient's consent and the result must not look like a guarantee. On Meta, side-by-side comparisons are banned for weight loss and wrinkle treatments.

**Can a pharmacy advertise discounts?**
Telling people about a price is normal for a pharmacy, but contests and prize promotions for medicines and supplements are banned. If a discount works like a promotion, check with a lawyer. A prescription medicine cannot be advertised even with a discount.

## Conclusion

The main rule for medicine and supplement ads is simple: talk about the product and do not promise a cure. Identify the product group, check your copy against the table, add the warning and keep your documents ready.
