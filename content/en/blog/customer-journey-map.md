Business owners see their business from the inside: product, price, team. Customers see it from the other side: a video in an ad, a reply in DMs, the welcome in the store, a late delivery. A customer can drop off at any point on that path — and you never find out why. A customer journey map (CJM) shows the path through the customer's eyes: the steps they take, what they think, what they fear and where they meet you. This article covers how to build a map and how to use it in marketing and sales.

![Map vs funnel: the difference](/img/blog/illus/mijoz-yoli-xaritasi-1.jpg 1200x675)

## Map vs funnel: the difference

A sales funnel is numbers: how many people saw, wrote, bought — [what is a sales funnel](/en/blog/what-is-a-sales-funnel/). A customer journey map is reasons: **why** someone moved to the next step or didn't. The funnel shows where the losses are; the map explains why. They work together.

## The 6 stages of the customer journey

| Stage | Customer's state | Main question |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Need | A problem or desire appears | "What do I need?" |
| 2. Search | Looks for options | "Who offers this?" |
| 3. Comparison | Considers 2–4 options | "Who's better and more reliable?" |
| 4. Purchase | Decides and pays | "Am I making a mistake?" |
| 5. Use | Receives the product or service | "Did it meet my expectations?" |
| 6. Return and referral | Buys again or tells others | "Should I come back here?" |

## What to record at each stage

Each stage on the map has five rows:

- **actions** — what the customer does;
- **thoughts and questions** — what's on their mind;
- **fears and barriers** — what stops them;
- **touchpoints** — where they meet you (ads, profile, DMs, phone, store, delivery);
- **what we do** — a concrete action to remove the barrier.

## Illustrative example: a furniture store

| Stage | What the customer does and thinks | Barrier | What we do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Need | Moved into a new flat, needs a sofa | Doesn't know where to start | Reels with interior ideas and "how to choose for your room" |
| Search | Searches Instagram and maps | No prices on the profile | "From…" prices in highlights, a maps listing |
| Comparison | Messages, compares 3 stores | Reply comes after 3 hours | Reply within 15 minutes, videos by colour and size |
| Purchase | Visits the showroom, discusses price | Hard to pay in one go | Clear instalment terms |
| Use | Waits for delivery | Timing unclear | Delivery day and time sent by message |
| Return | Happy but leaves no review | Nobody asked | Ask for a review after 7 days, a bonus on the next purchase |

## What the map gives you

One map usually reveals several concrete tasks:

- **for marketing** — which questions need content and creatives, which objections to address in advance in ads;
- **for sales** — where replies are delayed, at which stage a script is needed — [handling comments and DMs](/en/blog/handling-comments-and-dms/);
- **for service** — delivery, warranty, after-sale contact;
- **for repeat sales** — when and how to reach the customer again.

## How to build a map in one day

1. **Pick one segment.** A single map for all customers doesn't work — [target audience and customer persona](/en/blog/target-audience-and-customer-persona/).
2. **Gather data.** DM threads and call recordings, loss reasons in the CRM, reviews.
3. **Talk to 5–7 customers.** "How did you find us? What made you hesitate? Why did you choose us?" — [collecting customer feedback](/en/blog/collecting-customer-feedback/).
4. **Become a customer yourself.** Click the ad, message, call, and write down every step.
5. **Fill in the table.** 6 stages × 5 rows.
6. **Choose the 3 main barriers** and assign an owner and deadline to each.

## Look at competitors' journeys too

Customers talk to several companies at once. Submit a lead to competitors as well and compare how they respond: speed, information, next step. This is often where the easiest advantage is found — [competitor analysis](/en/blog/competitor-analysis/).

## Common mistakes

- Building the map "by guesswork" in the office without talking to customers.
- Looking only at the pre-purchase stages and forgetting what comes after.
- Squeezing too many segments into one map.
- Building the map without assigning tasks and owners.
- Never updating the map.

## Questions and answers

### Does a small business need a map?

Yes — especially a small one, where every lost customer is felt more. A simple table and a day's work are enough.

### How often should the map be updated?

Once or twice a year, or when something big changes: a new channel, a new product, a new sales process.

### How does the map relate to the content plan?

Every question and fear on the map is a ready content topic. "Where to start", "how to choose", "what's the warranty", "how long is delivery" — each can become a Reel or a carousel.

### Can an agency build the map?

Yes, but the business must take part: without conversations with sales managers and customers, the map won't reflect reality.

## Conclusion

A customer journey map shows your business through the customer's eyes: six stages from need to referral, each with questions, fears and touchpoints. It gives a concrete task list for marketing, sales and service. During the strategy stage, Level Marketing analyses the customer journey and points content, ads and the sales process at removing the barriers on exactly that path.
