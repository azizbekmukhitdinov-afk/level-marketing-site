When someone complains that "ads don't work", the cause is often not the ads but another stage on the way to a sale. A **sales funnel** is the customer's path from first contact with you to purchase. Split it into stages and measure them, and you can see exactly where money is leaking.

## Funnel stages

| Stage | What happens | Metric |
|---|---|---|
| Reach | People saw an ad or post | Impressions, reach |
| Interest | They clicked or visited the profile | CTR, visits |
| Lead | They messaged or filled in a form | Number of leads, CPL |
| Qualified lead | Genuinely ready to buy | Share of qualified leads |
| Sale | They paid | Conversion, average order |
| Repeat purchase | They came back | LTV, share of repeat purchases |

## An example (illustrative numbers)

A furniture store showed ads to 50,000 people in a month:

- 1% clicked → 500 visits;
- 10% of them messaged → 50 leads;
- 20% of leads bought → 10 sales.

Now improving any stage changes the result: if lead-to-sale conversion rises from 20% to 30%, the same budget brings 15 sales.

## How to find the leaks

| Where you lose people | Typical cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Reach to interest | A weak creative or the wrong audience | New hooks, [A/B tests](/en/blog/ab-testing-ad-creatives/) |
| Interest to lead | The profile doesn't build trust, a vague offer | [A profile that sells](/en/blog/instagram-profile-that-sells/), a clear offer |
| Lead to sale | Slow replies, no script | [Response time](/en/blog/lead-response-time/), [sales script](/en/blog/sales-script/) |
| Sale to repeat | No contact with the customer | [Repeat sales and LTV](/en/blog/repeat-sales-and-ltv/) |

## How to track the funnel

A simple spreadsheet is enough for a small business: reach, visits, leads and sales every week. When lead volume grows, move to a [CRM](/en/blog/crm-for-small-business/). For calculating cost per lead, see our [CPL guide](/en/blog/cost-per-lead-cpl-how-to-lower/).

## Conclusion

A funnel lets you see advertising and sales as one chain. Measure each stage, find where you lose the most and fix that first — it's often cheaper than increasing the ad budget.
