An optical store sells two things at once: clear vision and a face people see every day. The first purchase is driven by need and trust, the second by style and self-image, and most stores advertise only one of them. Below is a system that covers both: an eye test as the entry offer, content built around frames, reminders that bring people back, and a maps profile that catches people searching nearby.

## Why an optical store is different

Customers do not buy glasses every month. A pair of spectacles is a rare purchase, and when it happens the customer has to trust you twice: that the lenses are right for their eyes and that the frame suits their face. Contact lenses work the other way round: they are a consumable, and every user comes back on a schedule.

That gives an optical store three sources of revenue, and each needs its own marketing:

| Segment | What they want | What decides the purchase | Main channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| First glasses (often a child, the parents decide) | Certainty that the prescription is right | Specialist, calm explanation, durable frame | Maps, recommendations, Instagram |
| Adults 40+ and progressive lenses | Comfort at different distances | Clear explanation of lens types, price range | Targeted ads, maps |
| Contact lens users | Same lenses again, fast | Stock, delivery, reminder | Telegram bot, SMS, repeat orders |
| Sunglasses and fashion frames | Style | Try-on videos, choice, price | Reels, carousels, Stories |

If you mix all four in one generic ad («Optics — wide choice, low prices»), nobody recognises themselves. Pick one segment per campaign.

## The eye test as an entry offer

People rarely walk into an optical store «just to look». They come when they already feel they see worse. An eye test is the natural first step, and it is a good [entry offer](/en/blog/entry-offer/): one clear service with a clear price and duration, which leads to the main purchase.

How to package it:

- **Name the result, not the procedure.** «In 20 minutes you will know whether you need glasses and which lenses fit your tasks» works better than «computer vision diagnostics».
- **Give a price or say it is free, and say who does it.** An optometrist or an ophthalmologist, with experience and photo.
- **Offer booking slots.** «Today 18:00 or tomorrow 11:00» converts better than «come any time».
- **Use a lead form or a booking link**, not «write to us in Direct». Which works better for your store depends on the audience, see [lead form or Direct messages](/en/blog/lead-form-or-messages/).

A legal point. If a doctor examines the eyes, it may count as medical activity. Uzbekistan's advertising law bans advertising licensed activities without the required licence and bans misleading claims; see [advertising law in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/advertising-law-uzbekistan/) and the law text on [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/en/docs/6052631). In practice: show the licence where it applies, do not promise «perfect vision» and do not advertise the test as a way to «detect eye diseases» unless a doctor actually does that under licence.

### Illustrative example: does a free test pay off?

All numbers below are an illustrative example, not data from a real store.

| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| Ad budget per month | 6,000,000 soums |
| Bookings for a free test | 150 (cost per booking 40,000 soums) |
| Came to the test (60%) | 90 people |
| Bought glasses (45% of those who came) | 40 sales |
| Average check | 1,200,000 soums |
| Revenue | 48,000,000 soums |
| Gross margin at 50% | 24,000,000 soums |

The ad cost per sale here is 150,000 soums, about 12.5% of the check. The decision rule: watch two numbers every week, the show-up rate and the purchase rate after the test. If people book but do not come, the problem is reminders and phone handling, not ads. If they come but do not buy, look at the consultation and the price range on the shelves.

## Frames are the visual product

Lenses are hard to show; frames are easy. Frames are what people share, save and send to friends asking «does this suit me?». Content ideas that sell rather than just decorate the feed:

- **One face, three frames.** A short Reels where a person tries three frames, with prices in soums on screen.
- **Face shape guide.** Round, square, oval: which frames balance which shape. Show real staff or customers (with written consent), not stock photos.
- **«What you get for 500,000 / 1,000,000 / 2,000,000 soums».** Price-ladder content removes the fear of the price.
- **Lens explainer.** Thin lenses, anti-glare coating, progressive lenses: what changes in daily life. Avoid medical promises such as «protects your eyes from damage» unless the manufacturer documents it.
- **Behind the scenes.** How lenses are fitted into the frame, how long it takes, how glasses are adjusted to the face.
- **Children's frames.** Durability tests (bending, dropping) speak to parents more than design.

A weekly plan that a small team can keep up:

| Day | Format | Topic |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Reels | One face, three frames |
| Wednesday | Carousel | Frames for a round face, with prices |
| Friday | Reels | How glasses are made in our workshop |
| Saturday | Stories | New arrivals, free test slots for next week |

## Reminders that bring customers back

This is where most optical stores lose money. A customer bought contact lenses or glasses and disappeared, and the store never reminded them.

Contact lenses have a set replacement schedule: daily lenses are thrown away every day, others are replaced on a planned cycle, and extended-wear lenses are worn for a prescribed period, for example 7 to 30 days, and then discarded ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/contact-lenses/types-contact-lenses)). The box tells you the schedule, so you know almost to the day when a customer will run out.

| Event | When to remind | Channel | Message idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lens pack running out | 7 days before the estimated end | Telegram bot or SMS | «Your lenses run out around the 15th. Reorder with delivery?» |
| Glasses bought | 2 weeks after purchase | Call or message | «Are the glasses comfortable? Free adjustment any time» |
| Vision check | On the date agreed with the customer or the doctor | Telegram bot | «It has been a year since your test. Book a slot?» |
| Child's glasses | On the re-check date the doctor set | Call to the parent | «Time for the planned check» |
| Warranty ending | 1 month before | Message | «Your warranty ends soon, drop in for a free check» |

Two rules. First, ask permission to send reminders and promotions, and keep promotional messages separate from service ones; see [Telegram bot broadcast rules](/en/blog/telegram-bot-broadcast-rules/) and, for SMS, [the new SMS advertising rules](/en/blog/sms-advertising-rules-uzbekistan/). Second, store the replacement date in the CRM at the moment of sale; nobody will remember it later.

## Maps: catching people who search nearby

When someone needs glasses, they often search «optics near me». Your profile in Google and Yandex Maps works as a shop window: address, hours, photos of the storefront and inside, price range, the eye-test service and booking link.

What to do:

- Choose the correct category and list the eye test as a service.
- Upload photos of the frames wall, the test room and the entrance, so people recognise you from the street.
- Ask every satisfied customer for a review, but do not offer discounts or gifts for it: Google's policy forbids incentives for reviews and selective requests for positive reviews only ([Google](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)).
- Reply to every review, including negative ones, calmly and without discussing a person's health details. More in [online reputation and reviews](/en/blog/online-reputation-and-reviews/).

## Targeted ads: what to launch

- **Radius around the store**, 3 to 5 km in the city, wider for a mall location.
- **Separate campaigns by segment:** eye test for adults 40+, children's frames for parents, sunglasses in spring.
- **Retargeting** of people who watched the frame videos or opened the booking page.
- **Seasonality:** sunglasses before summer, children's checks before the school year.

## Common mistakes

- Advertising «discounts on frames» without a reason to come now.
- No prices at all: people assume it is expensive and leave.
- Consultants who sell the most expensive lens instead of explaining options.
- No record of when the customer's lenses run out.
- A maps profile with old photos and no hours.

## Questions and answers

### Should the eye test be free?

Not necessarily. A free test brings more bookings but more no-shows. A symbolic price that is credited towards the purchase often filters out people who are not serious. Test both for a month and compare the cost per sale, not the cost per booking.

### Is Instagram enough for an optical store?

For frames and sunglasses, Instagram is the main showcase. But many first-time buyers search on maps, and contact lens users are best served by a Telegram bot with repeat orders. Use Instagram for attention, maps for nearby searches, and the bot for repeat sales.

### How do I show prices if the final price depends on the lenses?

Show the frame price and a «from» price for lenses, and explain what changes the total: lens thickness, coatings, progressive design. Customers trust a store that explains the range more than one that hides prices.

### How often should I remind a customer?

Only when there is a reason: lenses running out, a check date, a warranty. Three to five well-timed messages a year are better than a weekly promotion that gets the bot blocked.

## Conclusion

An optical store grows when the eye test brings people in, frame content gives them a reason to choose you, reminders bring them back on time, and the maps profile catches people searching nearby.
