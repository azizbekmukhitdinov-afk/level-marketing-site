As a business grows, its owner asks, "How do I build a marketing department?" The first step is often hiring one marketer "who can do everything". The result: one person trying to handle strategy, design, ads and Reels. A **marketing department** is a system of roles that serves the business's goals. Here's how to build one step by step.

## The roles you need

- **Head of marketing / strategist** — responsible for goals, the plan, the budget and results — [what does a marketer do](/en/blog/what-does-a-marketer-do/).
- **Ads specialist** — setting up and optimising campaigns.
- **SMM manager** — the content plan and social pages — [what an SMM manager does](/en/blog/what-an-smm-manager-does/).
- **Content production** — video, mobile videographer, designer.
- **Analyst** — numbers, reports, CRM data.

In a small business, one person covers several roles — but not all of them.

## 3 models by business size

1. **Starting out**: the owner plus an agency or freelancers. Strategy and ads are outsourced; the owner makes decisions.
2. **Growing**: an in-house marketing manager plus an agency. The manager knows the business deeply; the agency brings execution and expertise.
3. **Large**: a full in-house department (head, ads, SMM, content, analyst) plus contractors for narrow tasks.

A comparison — [agency, freelancer or in-house](/en/blog/agency-freelancer-or-in-house/).

## Who to hire first

The first in-house hire is usually a **marketing coordinator**: someone who knows the business, works with the agency, monitors results and liaises with sales. Be wary of candidates who say "I'll do it all myself".

## Tasks and KPIs

The department works for business results, not likes:

- number and cost of leads — [cost per lead (CPL)](/en/blog/cost-per-lead-cpl-how-to-lower/);
- share of qualified leads — [qualified leads](/en/blog/qualified-lead/);
- customer acquisition cost and ROMI — [how to calculate ROMI](/en/blog/how-to-calculate-romi/);
- a monthly plan-versus-actual report.

A KPI system — [what is a KPI](/en/blog/what-is-kpi/).

## Linking with sales

Marketing and sales work in one funnel: lead quality, response speed and sales results should all be visible in the CRM — [marketing and sales alignment](/en/blog/marketing-and-sales-alignment/).

## Working alongside an agency

- An in-house employee brings business knowledge and oversight; an agency brings a team of specialists and experience.
- Set goals, KPIs and the report format in the contract — [KPIs in an agency contract](/en/blog/kpis-in-an-agency-contract/).
- Level Marketing has 25+ specialists; we present the report in the client's office every month, and our specialists' bonus depends on the contract being renewed.

## Common mistakes

- Expecting one "all-rounder" to do everything.
- A department with no goals or budget.
- Measuring results only by followers.
- Marketing and sales blaming each other, with no shared numbers.

## Questions and answers

### When do I need an in-house department?

When the budget and workload justify full-time staff. Before that, an agency plus a coordinator is often cheaper.

### What test task should I give a marketer?

A real one: a 1-month plan and KPIs for your business. Judge the logic, not how pretty the slides are.

### Who should the head of marketing report to?

The owner or CEO — on the same level as the head of sales.

## Conclusion

A marketing department is a system of roles, KPIs and links with sales. Small businesses usually start with "owner plus agency", then hire a marketing coordinator, and expand the in-house team as they grow. In a free audit, Level Marketing can help you choose the right model.
