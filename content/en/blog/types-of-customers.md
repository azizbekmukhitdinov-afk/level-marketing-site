For many businesses, "customer" is a single word: whoever pays is a customer. As a result, everyone gets the same message — the first-time buyer and the person who has bought from you for five years. Yet customers differ by relationship stage, and each needs different words, a different offer and a different channel. Here are the customer types and how to work with each.

## 5 customer types by relationship stage

| Type | Who | Their main question | Marketing job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prospect | Interested but hasn't bought | "Can I trust them?" | Trust and a first purchase |
| New | Bought once | "Did I choose right?" | A good experience, a second purchase |
| Repeat | Bought 2+ times | "What else do you have?" | Higher spend, making it a habit |
| Loyal | Regular and recommends you | "Do they value me?" | Gratitude, a referral programme |
| Lapsed | Used to buy, doesn't any more | "Why should I come back?" | Find out why, win them back |

## Prospects: trust

They don't know or trust you yet. They need:

- a clear offer and a price guide — [should you show the price in your ads](/en/blog/should-you-show-price-in-ads/);
- social proof: reviews, customer videos — [social proof](/en/blog/social-proof-in-ads/);
- a low-risk first step: a free consultation, measurement or trial — [entry offer](/en/blog/entry-offer/);
- a fast reply.

Channels: ads, retargeting, maps listing, Instagram profile.

## New customers: the first experience

The 30 days after the first purchase matter most. A good experience brings them back; a bad one gets shared with others.

- after purchase, a thank-you and a usage tip;
- after 3–7 days: "is everything OK?" — fix any problem immediately;
- ask for a review — [online reputation and reviews](/en/blog/online-reputation-and-reviews/);
- a reason for a second purchase: a matching accessory, a service, a reminder.

## Repeat customers: habit

They know and trust you. The job now is to make buying a habit and raise the spend:

- personal recommendations based on past purchases;
- reminders: service intervals, seasons, refills;
- news via a Telegram channel or bot — [Telegram or Instagram](/en/blog/telegram-or-instagram-for-business/).

The economics of repeat sales — [repeat sales and LTV](/en/blog/repeat-sales-and-ltv/).

## Loyal customers: gratitude and referrals

A loyal customer is the cheapest marketing channel there is: they bring others. With them:

- attention, not discounts: private offers, first access to new products, a dedicated manager;
- a referral programme: a bonus for bringing a friend;
- a video testimonial — with consent — [customer testimonial video](/en/blog/customer-testimonial-video/).

Constant discounts turn even loyal customers into bargain hunters — [the discount trap](/en/blog/discount-trap/).

## Lapsed customers: find out why

Customers who used to buy but haven't in a long time are often forgotten gold. Winning back an old customer is often cheaper than acquiring a new one. Steps:

1. Pull a "haven't bought in a long time" list from the CRM (the period depends on your business cycle).
2. A short message: "We miss you. What changed?" — ask for the reason.
3. An offer that fits the reason: a new product, improved service.
4. No reply — don't pester them.

Consent rules — promotional messages may need separate consent: [customer database and personal data](/en/blog/customer-database-personal-data-uzbekistan/) and [SMS advertising rules](/en/blog/sms-advertising-rules-uzbekistan/).

## CRM segmentation: a simple method

You don't need a complex system. Three CRM fields are enough:

- first purchase date;
- last purchase date;
- number of purchases and total spend.

These three fields let you sort customers into the five groups automatically. Choosing a CRM — [CRM for small business](/en/blog/crm-for-small-business/).

## What to measure for each type

| Type | Key metric |
|---|---|
| Prospect | Lead-to-first-purchase rate |
| New | Share moving to a second purchase |
| Repeat | Purchase frequency, average order value |
| Loyal | Number of referrals |
| Lapsed | Share who come back |

## Common mistakes

- The same message for every customer.
- Ads aimed only at new customers — existing ones forgotten.
- Stopping contact after the first purchase.
- Rewarding loyal customers only with discounts.
- Promotional messages without consent.

## Questions and answers

### Which customer type matters most?

They all matter, but many businesses give new customers too much attention and existing ones too little. Check your balance.

### Does a small business need segmentation?

Yes, in a simple form: even three columns in a spreadsheet are enough.

### When does a customer count as "lapsed"?

It depends on your business cycle: a month for a café, several years for furniture. Set your own period.

### Should existing customers be excluded from ads?

In new-customer acquisition campaigns, often yes — it saves budget. Give them separate messages and offers.

## Conclusion

Customers aren't one group: prospects, new, repeat, loyal and lapsed customers need different words, offers and channels. Start with simple CRM segmentation and one metric per type. Level Marketing measures ad results not only by new leads but by sales — and shows where existing customers can bring extra revenue.
