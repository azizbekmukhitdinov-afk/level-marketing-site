SWOT analysis is one of the best-known strategy tools: it shows up in business plans, investor decks and student projects. But most of the time it stays as four lists and leads to no decision at all. Here's how to run a SWOT properly and turn it into concrete actions, using a small-business example.

## What SWOT is

| | Helpful | Harmful |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** (you control it) | **S** — Strengths | **W** — Weaknesses |
| **External** (market, competition) | **O** — Opportunities | **T** — Threats |

The key rule: S and W are things **inside your business**; O and T are **external** factors. A common mistake: "lots of competitors" isn't a W, it's a T.

## How to do it in 5 steps

1. **Define the question**: "Should we open a second branch in 2027?" — a SWOT should serve a specific decision.
2. **Gather data**: sales figures, customer feedback, competitors — [competitor analysis](/en/blog/competitor-analysis/), [marketing research](/en/blog/marketing-research/).
3. **3–5 points per quadrant**, specific and verifiable.
4. **Rank them by importance**.
5. **TOWS — actions**: connect the quadrants to each other.

## Example: a furniture store (illustrative example)

**S — Strengths**
- In-house production — ready in 14 days.
- High ratings in customer reviews.

**W — Weaknesses**
- The Instagram page isn't run consistently.
- Only one salesperson.

**O — Opportunities**
- New housing complexes nearby.
- Demand for instalment payments.

**T — Threats**
- Cheap imported furniture.
- Rising ad costs.

## TOWS: from analysis to strategy

| | S (strengths) | W (weaknesses) |
|---|---|---|
| **O** | **SO**: advertise fast production to new homeowners | **WO**: set up Instagram content for people moving into new homes |
| **T** | **ST**: show quality and guarantees as the difference from imports | **WT**: hire a second salesperson so leads aren't lost |

Each strategy becomes an action, with an owner, a deadline and a metric.

## Common mistakes

- Generic words: "good quality", "great team" — with no evidence.
- Mixing up internal and external factors.
- 15 points per quadrant, so the important ones get lost.
- The analysis ends with no decision.
- Only the founder's opinion — customers and staff aren't asked.

## Questions and answers

### How often should we do a SWOT?

Before big decisions, and once a year — [year-end marketing review](/en/blog/year-end-marketing-review/).

### Can I do a SWOT on a competitor?

Yes — it reveals their weaknesses and your opportunities.

### How does SWOT differ from PEST?

PEST looks only at the external environment (political, economic, social, technological); SWOT combines internal and external factors.

## Conclusion

A SWOT analysis needs a specific question, facts, 3–5 points per quadrant and, above all, actions through TOWS. Produce an analysis that leads to a decision, not just a list.
