"AI voice-over for ads" is a fast-growing search for video advertising. Write the script, pick a voice — the voice-over is done, no studio or recording needed. In Russian and English, AI voices are sometimes indistinguishable from humans now. In Uzbek, it's a different story. Here's when you can use AI voices in ads, what goes wrong in Uzbek and how to test.

## Where AI voices help

- **Quick drafts**: hearing an edit with sound before a human records it.
- **Many variations**: voicing 5 hook versions of one video becomes cheap.
- **Translation and dubbing**: from Russian to Uzbek or vice versa — as a draft.
- **Internal videos**: staff instructions, presentations.
- **Text-to-speech**: short explainer videos.

General rules for sound in ads — [sound in video ads](/en/blog/sound-in-video-ads/).

## Problems in Uzbek

| Problem | How it sounds | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Word stress | Stress falls on the wrong syllable | Listen to every word; change the spelling if needed |
| o‘ and g‘ | Read as plain "o" and "g" | Use the correct characters, then listen |
| Russian pronunciation | Uzbek words with a Russian accent | Choose another voice or tool |
| Intonation | Flat, "robotic" | Short sentences, rhythm through punctuation |
| Numbers and prices | "1 200 000" read incorrectly | Write numbers as words |
| Brand names | The name is mispronounced | Phonetic spelling |

Even when a tool supports Uzbek, quality varies widely by tool and voice. So every recording must be checked by a native speaker.

## When a human voice is better

- **Main ad videos**: videos carrying a big budget — use a human voice.
- **Trust-sensitive niches**: clinics, education, finance — a synthetic voice may lower trust.
- **Emotional stories**: customer stories, brand videos.
- **Someone speaking on camera**: lip movement and voice must match — that needs live speech.

Who should speak in your ad — [who should star in your ad](/en/blog/who-should-star-in-your-ad/).

## Voice cloning: extra caution

Some tools can copy a real person's voice. It's powerful but risky:

- **only with written consent**: the voice's owner must agree where and for how long it's used;
- **no famous voices**: it misleads people and carries reputational and legal risk;
- **when an employee leaves**: don't keep running ads with their cloned voice without an agreement.

The law prohibits reproducing voices and music from someone else's ad without permission (Article 16, [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/docs/6052631)), as well as misleading advertising (Article 5). More on the AI law — [AI and the law in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/ai-law-uzbekistan-business/).

## A test: AI voice vs human voice

One video, two voice versions:

1. Same script, same edit, same music.
2. Two ads in one ad set with equal budget.
3. 7–10 days.
4. Compare: 3-second views, average watch time, cost per lead, conversion to sales.

If the difference is small, the AI voice makes production cheaper. If the human voice is clearly better, keep it for your main videos. Testing technique — [A/B testing ad creatives](/en/blog/ab-testing-ad-creatives/).

## Preparing a script for an AI voice

- short sentences — up to 10–12 words;
- numbers as words: "one million two hundred thousand soum";
- tricky names spelled phonetically: write how the brand name is pronounced;
- punctuation creates pauses: a comma is short, a full stop longer;
- listen to every recording to the end, not just the start.

## Common mistakes

- Putting an Uzbek AI voice-over into ads without listening to it.
- "Dubbing" an AI voice onto someone speaking on camera.
- Choosing a voice that sounds like a celebrity.
- Cloning a voice without permission.
- Using the same AI voice in every video — the ads start to sound mass-produced.

## Questions and answers

### Is there a natural-sounding AI voice in Uzbek?

Some tools give good results, but quality varies. Try several and have a native speaker listen.

### Do I need a licence for an AI voice?

Read the tool's commercial-use terms — a paid plan is often required for advertising.

### Can I dub a Russian ad into Uzbek with AI?

As a draft, yes. But first adapt the script into natural Uzbek rather than translating word for word. On language — [ad language: Uzbek or Russian](/en/blog/ad-language-uzbek-or-russian/).

### Do I need to label the video as using an AI voice?

Law ZRU-1115 contains no mandatory labelling requirement. Check the platforms' rules.

## Conclusion

AI voices speed up ad production: drafts, variations, dubbing. In Uzbek, though, stress, pronunciation and intonation are problems — so listen to every recording and test your main videos against a human voice.
