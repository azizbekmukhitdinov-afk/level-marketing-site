Beauty salon marketing comes down to one question: does the client trust this stylist? A haircut, manicure or skin treatment can't be "tried on" in advance, so people decide by the stylist's work, the reviews and how easy it is to book. This article gives a practical plan for a salon in Tashkent to win new clients and turn them into regulars.

![What makes the salon business different](/img/blog/illus/gozallik-saloni-marketing-1.jpg 1200x675)

## What makes the salon business different

| Feature | What it means for marketing |
|---|---|
| Results are visible | The portfolio is your strongest ad |
| Clients attach to a stylist | The stylist's personal brand matters as much as the salon's |
| The service repeats | A visit every 3–6 weeks is the main income |
| A local business | Most clients live nearby |

The takeaway: the money in a salon comes not from ads but from clients who come back. Ads only start the flow.

## Step 1. A clear offer for each service line

"All services in one place" is a weak offer. Clients come with a specific need: thinning hair, a wedding to prepare for, brittle nails. Write a one-sentence offer for each key service line:

- "A complete bridal look: a trial updo and a home visit on the wedding day".
- "A manicure in 60 minutes — we show you the sterilisation process".
- "Hair colouring: consultation and a strand test first".

How to strengthen an offer — see [unique selling proposition](/en/blog/unique-selling-proposition/).

## Step 2. The portfolio is the core of Instagram

In the first 5 seconds on a salon profile, people look for one answer: "do they do good work here?". So:

- **Before / after** — same light and angle. Post with the client's consent.
- **Process videos** — 10–20 second Reels: colouring, styling, manicure layers. Process builds trust.
- **Stylist pages** — a highlight for each stylist: name, speciality, work, booking button.
- **Hygiene** — the steriliser, single-use materials. For many clients this is decisive.
- **Prices** — at least as "from…". A profile without prices floods your DMs with "how much?".

More on profiles that sell — [Instagram profile that sells](/en/blog/instagram-profile-that-sells/).

## Step 3. The stylist's personal brand

Clients often choose the stylist, not the salon. That is both a risk (a stylist leaves, clients follow) and an opportunity. The right approach is to feature stylists on the salon's profile and publish their Reels under the salon's name: "Our stylist Madina will show you today…". Trust then goes to both.

Short scripts help stylists talk to camera — [Reels script templates](/en/blog/reels-script-templates/). Personal brand basics — [personal brand: where to start](/en/blog/personal-brand-where-to-start/).

## Step 4. Maps and reviews

"Manicure near me" and "salon Yunusabad" are map searches. Your listing needs the right category, hours, 20+ photos (the room, workstations, results), a list of services and prices. Ask every client for a review after the treatment — the receptionist showing a QR code is enough. More in [adding your business to maps](/en/blog/add-your-business-to-maps/) and [online reputation and reviews](/en/blog/online-reputation-and-reviews/).

## Step 5. Make booking easy

Many salons lose requests because booking is hard: back-and-forth in DMs, "I'll let you know tonight", unanswered calls. A system that works:

1. **One main channel** — a Telegram bot, an online booking service or the phone. Use the same one on the profile, maps and ads.
2. **Visible free slots** — if clients pick a time themselves, they don't need to chat with the receptionist.
3. **Reminders** — a message the day before and 2 hours before. Fewer no-shows.
4. **Reply speed** — answer DMs and Telegram within 10–15 minutes during working hours. Why it matters — [lead response time](/en/blog/lead-response-time/).

## Step 6. Paid ads

Key settings for a salon:

- **Radius:** 3–5 km around the salon. Wider for skin treatments and complex services.
- **Audience:** don't narrow it too much — the creative does the filtering.
- **Creative:** a before/after video, the stylist's face and voice, a clear price or offer.
- **Objective:** messages or a click to the booking bot.

A first-visit offer works for new clients — not a discount but added value: "A hair care mask as a gift on your first visit". Big discounts attract only bargain hunters. Setting up ads — [Instagram ads](/en/blog/how-to-set-up-instagram-ads/).

## Step 7. Repeat visits are the main income

The most important number for a salon is how often clients return. Ways to raise it:

| Method | How it works |
|---|---|
| Book the next visit on the spot | After the treatment, the receptionist offers a time 4 weeks later |
| Reminder | Colouring after 6 weeks, manicure after 3 |
| Membership | Pay for 5 visits upfront, get the 6th free |
| Birthday | A personal greeting and a small free service |
| Referral | A bonus for the client who brings a friend, and for the friend |

A follow-up calendar and LTV maths — [repeat sales and LTV](/en/blog/repeat-sales-and-ltv/).

## The receptionist is the salon's salesperson

Ads and the profile bring clients to the door; the receptionist does the rest. In a day they answer dozens of calls and messages, confirm bookings and offer the next visit. Write a simple standard for them:

1. **Greet and use the name** — "Hello Nilufar, welcome to the salon".
2. **Find the need** — which service, which stylist, which day suits.
3. **A concrete offer** — two free slots: "Thursday 15:00 or Friday 11:00 — which works better?".
4. **Suggest an add-on** — paraffin therapy with a manicure, a treatment with colouring.
5. **After the visit** — ask for a review and book the next time.

Check the standard monthly by listening to a couple of conversations. A full structure for calls and chats — see [sales script](/en/blog/sales-script/).

## Holidays and seasons

A salon knows its busiest periods in advance: wedding season, 8 March, Navruz, New Year and graduation parties. Prepare 3–4 weeks ahead: open advance booking, offer a "holiday look" package and move ad budget to those weeks. A small offer to sell empty hours on busy days — for example, morning slots — also helps.

## What to measure

- new clients and the channel each came from;
- share of clients who return after the first visit (for example, within 60 days);
- percentage of booked hours per stylist;
- average bill and add-on services;
- cost of one booking from ads.

If you get many new clients but few return, the problem is not the ads but the service.

## Common mistakes

- No prices and "we'll tell you in DMs" for every question.
- Only the interior, no stylists' faces or work.
- Ads with deep discounts — clients come once and leave.
- Three booking channels and a phone nobody answers.
- Not offering the next visit.

## Questions and answers

### Which ads work best for a salon?

Video ads showing the stylist and the result, within a radius of the salon. Combined with maps and reviews, cost per request is usually lower.

### If a stylist leaves, will clients leave too?

Some will. To reduce the risk, keep the client base and booking system owned by the salon, and let the salon's brand and service show in the content too.

### Should I offer discounts?

To attract new clients, a small gift or extra service beats a big discount. For regulars, memberships and bonuses work better.

### If I publish prices, competitors will see them.

They will, but clients leave profiles without prices for another salon. "From…" pricing is the best balance.

Beauty salon marketing is a portfolio, trust in the stylists, easy booking and a system of repeat visits. Ads bring a new client, but you earn when that client comes back. Level Marketing shoots Reels featuring salon stylists, runs the ads and reports monthly on new clients and bookings.
