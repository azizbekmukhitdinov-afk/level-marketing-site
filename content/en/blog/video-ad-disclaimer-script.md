A disclaimer in a video ad is usually remembered on the last day. The video is shot, the edit is done, the lawyer asks "where is the warning?" and the text gets squeezed into a corner in tiny type. From 11 February 2027 that will no longer work for loan advertising in Uzbekistan: the warning becomes a visible part of the video. This article shows how to plan it at the script stage: the timing maths, three placements and ready plans for 15, 20 and 30 seconds.

> This article is not legal advice. We explain reports from open sources in plain language. Before your video goes live, check the official text on lex.uz or ask a lawyer.

## Disclaimer in a video ad: where it is mandatory

Three cases come up most often in video.

- **Loans and microloans.** Law No. ZRU-1187, signed on 10 November 2026, amended the Law on Advertising. Advertising for a loan, including a microloan, must carry a warning that failing to meet debt obligations may lead to financial risks. It must take up at least 10% of the ad's main area and time ([UzA, in Uzbek](https://www.uza.uz/uz/posts/endi-kredit-reklamalarida-moliyaviy-xatarlar-haqida-ogohlantirish-bolishi-shart_935476)). The requirement starts to apply three months later, from 11 February. Details are in our article on [the loan advertising risk warning](/en/blog/loan-advertising-risk-warning-uzbekistan/).
- **Medicines and supplements.** The warning text is different here, but the job in video is the same: people must have time to read it. The wording and the bans are in [the rules for medicine and supplement advertising](/en/blog/medicine-supplement-advertising-uzbekistan/).
- **Installments.** This is not a warning but a mandatory condition: the overpayment and the final price must be shown. In a video it also takes space and time. See [installment advertising rules](/en/blog/installment-advertising-rules/).

If one video mentions both "installments" and "bank loan", both requirements apply. So before you write the script, find out exactly what the offer includes.

## What 10% means in seconds

The maths is simple: divide the length of the video by ten. The figures in the table are arithmetic, not an official template.

| Video length | 10%, the minimum | Plan with a margin |
|---|---|---|
| 6 seconds | 0.6 seconds | 1 second |
| 15 seconds | 1.5 seconds | 2 seconds |
| 20 seconds | 2 seconds | 2.5–3 seconds |
| 30 seconds | 3 seconds | 3.5–4 seconds |
| 60 seconds | 6 seconds | 6.5–7 seconds |

Three things to note.

**First, "area and time".** The UzA report joins the two words with "and". The safe reading is this: the warning stays on screen for at least a tenth of the running time and during that time covers at least a tenth of the frame. If an official clarification appears, you can simplify.

**Second, 10% is a minimum, not a target.** If people cannot read the text in that time, give it more. The test is easy: read the warning aloud with a stopwatch, without rushing. However many seconds that takes is the least it should stay on screen.

**Third, the 6-second video.** Nobody reads anything in 0.6 seconds. This format does not suit a loan offer, so start from 15 seconds. We covered how to choose the length in [the article on video ad length](/en/blog/video-ad-length/).

## Four parts of the video: offer, terms, warning, call to action

A video with a warning has four required blocks. Each has its own job, so do not mix them.

1. **Offer.** What the person gets: a car, furniture, a phone. In the first seconds the frame shows the product, not the loan.
2. **Terms.** How to get it: the bank's name, the term, the rate or the monthly payment. The numbers are agreed with the bank.
3. **Warning.** The text the law requires. It is not changed, shortened or turned into a joke.
4. **Call to action.** The next step: leave a request, visit the showroom, call.

A common mistake is to put the terms and the warning in one small line. The terms sell and the warning protects, so give each its own place.

## Where to place the warning: three options

| Placement | What it looks like | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Permanent band | A high-contrast strip stays at the edge of the frame for the whole video | The time requirement is met with room to spare, and people who leave early still see it | Part of the frame is taken, so the design adapts to it |
| Separate frame | Full-screen text in the middle or at the end | Easy to read, simple to edit | Takes a noticeable share of a short video |
| Band plus voice | The text is on screen and the voice-over reads it | Clear with sound and without it | The voice-over gets longer, leaving less time for the offer |

The choice depends on the format.

- **Reels and Stories.** Most people do not watch to the end, so a permanent band is safer. But do not put it at the very bottom of the frame. For Reels ads, Meta recommends keeping text out of the top 14%, the bottom 35% and 6% on each side of the frame, because the interface covers those areas ([Meta Ads Guide](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/instagram-reels)). Place the band above that zone.
- **TV and LED screens.** Both a separate frame and a bottom strip work. The screen is watched from a distance, so make the letters large.
- **Watching without sound.** On social media, videos are often watched muted. If the warning exists only in the voice-over, the viewer does not get it. On-screen text is always needed, and the voice is an addition.

## Ready scripts: 15, 20 and 30 seconds

The three plans below are an illustrative example: a car showroom in Tashkent offers a car on a loan from a partner bank. Replace the square brackets with your own data. Agree the warning text with the bank and a lawyer.

### 15 seconds: with a permanent band

A short video has no time for a separate frame. So the band stays from the first second to the last.

| Time | Shot | Text and voice |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 s | The car drives out of the showroom, close-up | "[Model] is in the showroom" |
| 3–9 s | Interior, steering wheel, boot: 3 short shots | "Partner bank loan: [term], [rate]" |
| 9–15 s | The car and the showroom address | "Leave a request: [phone]" |
| 0–15 s | High-contrast band at the edge of the frame | Full warning text |

### 20 seconds: with a separate frame

| Time | Shot | Text and voice |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 s | The car in motion | "[Model] is now in Tashkent" |
| 3–9 s | Interior and exterior | Voice-over: the car's 2 main advantages |
| 9–14 s | A manager hands over the keys | "Loan: [bank], [term], [rate]" |
| 14–17 s | Calm full-screen background | Warning text, also read by the voice-over |
| 17–20 s | Logo and address | "Book a test drive: [phone]" |

Here the warning gets 3 seconds, which is 15% of 20 seconds. The call to action comes after the warning, so the video ends with a clear step rather than a dry notice.

### 30 seconds: band plus voice

| Time | Shot | Text and voice |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 s | A family by the car, loading luggage | Question: "Choosing a car?" |
| 4–12 s | The car in the city, the interior | Voice-over: 3 advantages |
| 12–20 s | A talk with the manager, documents | "Loan: [bank], [term], [rate], down payment [amount]" |
| 20–24 s | The shot slows down, the band grows | The voice-over reads the warning calmly |
| 24–30 s | Showroom, address, phone | "Leave a request today" |
| 12–30 s | High-contrast band at the edge of the frame | Full warning text |

The band stays from the moment the loan terms are mentioned until the end, which is 18 seconds. In the voice-over the warning takes 4 seconds.

For another niche the order is the same: product, terms, warning, call to action. To strengthen the first shot, take ideas from our list of [Reels hooks](/en/blog/reels-hooks/).

## Radio and audio ads

Audio has no "area", only time. In a 20-second spot the warning takes at least 2 seconds, in a 30-second spot at least 3.

- The voice-over reads the warning at the same pace as the rest of the text. Rushing through it goes against the point of the requirement.
- Read the text with a stopwatch first. If it does not fit, shorten the offer, not the warning.
- Turn the music down during the warning. For choosing a voice and sound, see [sound in video ads](/en/blog/sound-in-video-ads/).

## 6 mistakes

1. **Adding the warning on the last day.** It ends up small and unnoticeable. Give it its own row in the script table.
2. **Putting it only in the last frame.** On social media few people reach the last frame. In a short video a band is safer.
3. **Placing it under the interface.** In Reels the button and the caption cover the bottom of the frame. The text is there, but nobody sees it.
4. **Text that blends into the background.** White letters on a light background cannot be read. Use a high-contrast band and a large font.
5. **Rewriting the text to sound nicer.** The warning text is not a creative element. The bank and the lawyer approve it.
6. **One video for every placement.** In 9:16, 1:1 and 16:9 the band sits differently. Check each format separately.

## Checklist before the video goes live

- You have checked whether the video mentions a loan or a microloan.
- The warning text is agreed with the partner bank and a lawyer.
- The warning stays on screen for at least 10% of the running time.
- During that time it covers at least 10% of the frame.
- The text can be read on a phone with the sound off.
- The band is not hidden under the Reels and Stories interface.
- Each format (9:16, 1:1, 16:9) has been reviewed separately.
- If installments are offered, the overpayment and the final price are shown.
- The approved script and the final file are saved.

## Questions and answers

### Can the warning be voice-only?

Do not rely on that in video. The reports mention both area and time, and videos are often watched muted. Keep the text on screen and let the voice support it.

### Do old videos need to be reshot?

Usually not. Adding a band in the edit or attaching a frame at the end is enough. But if the band covers important text or the product, the shot has to be rebuilt.

### Does an influencer's video need the warning?

If the influencer advertises a loan, it is advertising too. Put the warning text and its on-screen time in the brief, and watch the video before it is published.

### Will the warning kill the video?

If it is stuck on at the last minute, yes. If it has its own place in the script, no. When viewers see honest terms, trust grows and there are fewer questions.

### Can we wait until 11 February?

A video shot in December will still be running in February. Make it with the warning now and you will not have to re-edit it later.

## Conclusion

A disclaimer in a video ad is not the last fix in the edit. It is the fourth block of the script. First work out the time: at least a tenth of the running time, with a margin. Then choose the placement: a permanent band for a short video, a separate frame or a band plus voice for a longer one. And always check on a phone with the sound off to see whether the text can be read.

At Level Marketing we write video scripts as a table and give mandatory texts their own row from the start, so no surprise fixes appear at the end of the edit.
