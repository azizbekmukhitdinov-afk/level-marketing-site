Almost every business owner has heard about artificial intelligence by now: "AI writes posts, draws pictures, sets up ads — you don't need a marketer any more." Reality is more complicated. AI speeds up many tasks, but in some it still makes confident mistakes — especially in Uzbek and on local market questions. Here's what a small business can hand over to AI in marketing, what it can't, and the risks involved.

## What AI is good at

| Task | How AI helps | What a human checks |
|---|---|---|
| Ideas | Lists of ideas for posts, Reels and ads | Does it fit the business, do competitors already do it |
| Draft copy | Posts, descriptions, reply templates | Language, facts, brand voice |
| Translation and adaptation | A Russian → Uzbek draft | Naturalness, terminology |
| Analysis | Grouping reviews and questions | Are the conclusions right |
| Script structure | Hook — problem — solution — call to action | Realism, the customer's language |
| Editing | Subtitles, cutting, formatting | Errors, especially in Uzbek |

The general rule: AI is for **drafts and speed**; final decisions and checks belong to a human.

## Where AI is weak

### 1. The Uzbek language

Many AI tools work less well in Uzbek than in English or Russian: grammar mistakes, a translated tone, wrong words, confusion with the letters o‘ and g‘. Auto-subtitles and AI voices often get it wrong too. So Uzbek copy should always be proofread by a native speaker. On sound — [sound in video ads](/en/blog/sound-in-video-ads/).

### 2. The local market and facts

AI can give confident-sounding but outdated or wrong information about Uzbekistan's laws, prices or platform rules. Check every fact against the primary source: laws on lex.uz, platform rules in official help centres.

### 3. Strategy

AI will write "a marketing strategy for a restaurant" — but it doesn't know your numbers, customers or competitors. The result is a generic plan that fits everyone. Strategy starts with your business data — [one-page marketing strategy](/en/blog/one-page-marketing-strategy/).

### 4. Trust and authenticity

AI images and videos look good, but customers want to see the real product, the real team and real results. Trust comes from real people — [social proof](/en/blog/social-proof-in-ads/) and [team content](/en/blog/team-content-employees/).

## AI inside ad platforms

Ad platforms already have AI built in: Meta's Advantage+ tools automatically choose and adjust audiences, placements and creative variations. That's useful, but:

- review automatically modified creatives before launch — text and images can change badly;
- the algorithm learns from the data you give it: the wrong objective or poor tracking means the wrong optimisation. On tracking — [Meta Pixel and Conversions API](/en/blog/meta-pixel-and-conversions-api/);
- still judge results by sales, not by ad account metrics.

## Risks: data and the law

### Customer data

Uploading your customer base, phone numbers or chats to public AI services is risky. Processing personal data is regulated by law — more in [customer database and personal data](/en/blog/customer-database-personal-data-uzbekistan/). Anonymise data for analysis: no names or numbers.

### Misleading creatives

If an AI-generated product image differs from the real product, it misleads customers. The Law "On Advertising" prohibits unfair (misleading) advertising (Article 5, [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/docs/6052631)). AI for backgrounds or decor is fine, but the product itself must be real.

### Copyright

The terms for using AI-generated images, music and text depend on the service. Read the tool's terms before commercial use. Reproducing text, images and music from someone else's ad is prohibited by law (Article 16).

## A practical workflow for a small business

1. **Ideas**: ask AI for 30 post ideas for the month and pick 10.
2. **Drafts**: draft copy for the chosen ideas.
3. **Human editing**: language, facts, brand voice, customer objections — [content from customer objections](/en/blog/content-from-customer-objections/).
4. **Real content**: videos and photos featuring the actual product and team.
5. **AI in editing**: subtitle drafts, cutting — then manual checks.
6. **Results**: which posts and ads drove sales — analysed by a human.

In this workflow AI saves time, while quality and trust stay in human hands.

## Common mistakes

- Publishing AI copy without checking — errors in Uzbek destroy trust.
- Trusting laws and numbers that AI produced.
- Uploading the customer base to an AI service.
- Replacing the real product with an AI image.
- Deciding "we have AI, so we don't need a marketer": the tool exists, but strategy, testing and analysis are still human work.

## Questions and answers

### Will AI replace marketers?

Not yet. AI provides drafts and speed, but strategy, understanding the local market, creative ideas and analysing results by sales are human work.

### Which AI is best for posts in Uzbek?

Tools change fast. Try several, but in every case a native speaker should edit the text.

### Can I create ad images with AI?

For backgrounds, decor and concepts, yes. The product itself must be real, otherwise it's misleading.

### How much should I spend on AI tools?

Many tools have free or cheap versions. Try one on a single task first and measure how much time it saved.

## Conclusion

Artificial intelligence is a strong assistant, not a manager. Hand it ideas, drafts and technical tasks, but keep strategy, facts, the quality of Uzbek copy and customer data under human control. The Level Marketing team uses AI tools to work faster, but every piece of copy, creative and decision is checked by a team of more than 25 specialists.
