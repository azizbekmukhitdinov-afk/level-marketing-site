When people talk about video ads, they think about framing, lighting and editing. Sound gets decided at the last minute: "let's add a trending track" or "the sales manager will read it". Yet sound is half the video. It stops people or makes them scroll, builds trust or instantly gives the ad away. Here are four sound options for video ads, their strengths and when to choose each.

## Two audiences: sound on and sound off

Video ads are watched by two kinds of people: with sound and without. Meta recommends designing for both: sound is an important driver of engagement, but for people watching without it, on-screen text and captions make the message clear ([Meta for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/video-ad-format)).

The practical takeaway: your video must work **both with and without sound**. Without sound, people get the main point from on-screen text; with sound, they get extra emotion and trust.

## 4 options compared

| Option | Strength | Weakness | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Live speech (a person talks to camera) | Most trust, a "human" voice | Needs someone who speaks well and a good mic | Services, experts, clinics, education |
| Voice-over (off camera) | Clear, structured, easy to edit | Risk of an "advert voice" | Products, explainer videos |
| Music only | Mood, quick editing | The offer may stay unclear | Visual products, clothing, interiors |
| Natural sound (ASMR, process sounds) | Authenticity, grabs attention | Works only for some products | Food, manufacturing, packaging |

The best results often come from combinations: live speech with quiet background music, or natural sound with on-screen text.

## Live speech: the strongest for trust

When someone talks to camera in their own words, it doesn't feel like an ad. That's why live speech often works best for service businesses: a doctor, a craftsman, a teacher, the owner.

Rules:

- **Don't memorise a script.** Know the main point and say it in your own words. Memorised text is obvious.
- **The first line is the hook.** Don't open with "Hello, my name is...". Lead with a problem or a result. Hooks — [Reels hooks](/en/blog/reels-hooks/).
- **A lavalier mic.** A phone's built-in mic records room echo. Even a cheap lav mic improves quality sharply. More in [shooting Reels on a phone](/en/blog/shooting-reels-on-a-phone/).
- **A quiet place.** Street traffic, air conditioning, a kitchen — viewers hear it better than you do.

Who should speak on camera — [who should star in your ad](/en/blog/who-should-star-in-your-ad/).

## Voice-over: for structure

A voice-over is text read off camera. It suits product videos, "how it works" explainers and information-heavy creatives.

The main risk is a "TV commercial" tone. A loud, rushed "Today only! Hurry!" voice is instantly recognised as an ad on social media and scrolled past. Better:

- a conversational tone, as if explaining to a friend;
- short sentences;
- copy that complements the picture instead of repeating it;
- for Uzbek creatives, a native-speaker voice — more in [ad language: Uzbek or Russian](/en/blog/ad-language-uzbek-or-russian/).

AI voices exist too, but they often still sound robotic, especially in Uzbek. If you use one, test it against a human voice. More in [AI in marketing for small business](/en/blog/ai-in-marketing-for-small-business/).

## Music: mood, not message

A music-only video can look beautiful, but music sells nothing — it only sets the mood. So in a music-only video, **the offer must be clear in on-screen text**.

When choosing music:

- **Copyright.** A popular song in an ad is a legal risk, and the platform may mute the audio. Use Meta's library of music cleared for ads or licensed music. Reproducing music and sound from someone else's ad without permission is also prohibited by law (Article 16, [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/docs/6052631)).
- **Tempo matches the cuts.** Fast music for fast editing, slow music for a premium feel.
- **Lower than speech.** If someone speaks, the music should be noticeably quieter.

## Natural sound: authenticity that grabs attention

Sizzling meat, fabric being cut, a box closing — these sounds can grab more attention than music because they're real. A good option for restaurants, manufacturing, packaging and unboxing videos. More in [unboxing videos](/en/blog/unboxing-video-online-store/).

To record natural sound, keep the mic close to the source and reduce background noise.

## Subtitles: not optional

Whatever you choose:

- **Speech means subtitles.** For people watching without sound.
- **Large, readable font** on a contrasting background.
- **Safe zone.** In Reels, the top and bottom are covered by the interface — keep text closer to the centre.
- **Spelling.** In Uzbek subtitles, write o‘ and g‘ correctly — errors reduce trust.

## How to test

One video, two sound versions: for example, live speech vs voice-over, or music vs natural sound. Everything else stays the same. Compare for at least a week, by leads and sales — [A/B testing ad creatives](/en/blog/ab-testing-ad-creatives/).

## Common mistakes

- Recording with the phone's built-in mic in a noisy room.
- Using a trending song in an ad without a licence.
- Music drowning out speech.
- A music-only video with no offer on screen.
- Speech without subtitles.
- A "TV announcer" tone.

## Questions and answers

### What sound works best for Reels ads?

There's no universal answer. Live speech often works for services; music and natural sound for visual products. Test.

### Can I use trending music?

In organic Reels, music from the platform library works within what's permitted. In ads, use licensed music or music cleared for advertising.

### Where can I find a voice-over artist?

Professional voice artists, actors or an employee with a good voice. The key is a natural tone. Recording needs a quiet room and a good mic.

### Can subtitles be automatic?

Yes, many editing apps generate subtitles automatically, but they make many mistakes in Uzbek. Always check by hand. Apps — [video editing apps](/en/blog/video-editing-apps/).

## Conclusion

Sound is half the video. Live speech for services, voice-over or natural sound for products, music for mood — and always subtitles for sound-off viewers.
