Many business pages have products, a logo and promotions — but no people. Customers don't know who they're buying from, who prepares their order or who answers the phone. Yet in services and big-ticket purchases, people buy not from companies but **from people**. Team content — showing your employees on your page — is one of the cheapest ways to build trust. Here's how to make it systematic.

## Why team content works

- **It removes the fear of the unknown.** If customers have seen the doctor before visiting the clinic, or the mechanic before handing over their car, they arrive to meet someone "familiar".
- **It makes the company feel alive.** Stock photos and design templates look the same everywhere. Your team exists only at your company.
- **It shows professionalism.** A craftsman demonstrating how they work convinces more than the line "we do quality work".
- **Ideas never run out.** Every employee is a source of new topics.

This matters most for service businesses: clinics, salons, car services, education centres, renovation. How trust is built — [social proof in ads](/en/blog/social-proof-in-ads/).

## 6 team content formats

| Format | What it shows | Example (illustrative) |
|---|---|---|
| "Who will serve you" | An employee and their role, 20–30 seconds | "I'm Jasur, I'll come to measure" |
| The work process | Hands, tools, details | A craftsman assembling a sofa frame |
| Expert tip | One tip from their field | A dentist: "When to replace your toothbrush" |
| Behind the scenes | Start of the day, preparation | Morning prep in the kitchen |
| With a customer | Handover, result, thanks | Returning a car to its owner |
| Team life | Celebrations, training, achievements | A staff training session |

The last format is the weakest — it interests the team, not the customer. Use it sparingly. The strongest are process and expert tips: they deliver both trust and value.

## Who goes on camera

Not every employee wants to be filmed, and that's fine. How to choose:

- **Customer-facing staff first**: salespeople, technicians, doctors, teachers — customers will meet them anyway.
- **Voluntary only**: someone forced looks forced on camera.
- **2–3 "faces"**: not the whole team, just a few recurring heroes. Your audience will start to recognise them.

Whether the owner should appear on camera is a separate question — see [founder on camera](/en/blog/founder-on-camera/).

## Working with camera-shy employees

Many say "I can't speak on camera". But they know their job very well. What helps:

1. **Show instead of tell.** First videos are just hands and process, no voice or with music.
2. **Interview format.** The employee answers the person behind the camera, not the lens. Simple questions: "What matters most in this job?" More in [expert interview videos](/en/blog/expert-interview-videos/).
3. **One idea, not a script.** No memorising — let them say one idea in their own words.
4. **Many takes in one day.** Film 5–10 short videos in a single shoot day so staff aren't stressed every week.
5. **Show the result.** After the video goes live, show the employee the good comments — next time they'll volunteer.

Technical tips for phone filming — [shooting Reels on a phone](/en/blog/shooting-reels-on-a-phone/).

## Consent, and when an employee leaves

Two important issues:

**Consent.** The Law "On Advertising" prohibits using an individual's name or image in advertising without their consent (Article 16, [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/docs/6052631)). If an employee appears in your content, get written consent stating which channels and for how long the material will be used. On handling personal data — [customer database and personal data](/en/blog/customer-database-personal-data-uzbekistan/).

**If the employee leaves.** It happens often. Agree in advance how long their videos stay on the page after they leave and whether they're used in ads. Don't tie all content to one person — with 2–3 faces, one departure won't leave the page empty.

## Using team content in ads

A team video that performs well organically often does well in ads too: it doesn't look like an ad. For example, a video of a craftsman showing "how we prepare your order", paired with an offer, may build more trust than a designed banner.

But test it: same offer, one creative with the team, another with the product. Compare by sales — [A/B testing ad creatives](/en/blog/ab-testing-ad-creatives/).

## Common mistakes

- Only "team life" — birthdays and parties. Customers don't care.
- Forcing employees — it shows on camera.
- Not getting consent.
- Tying all content to a single "star" employee.
- No subtitles on videos where staff speak — many people watch without sound.

## Questions and answers

### Should employees be paid extra for appearing in content?

That depends on your agreement. In many companies it's part of the job; in others, a small bonus. The key is agreeing in advance and in writing.

### Won't competitors poach my employees?

That risk exists with or without content. Good employees stay because of working conditions, not because they're hidden.

### How much team content per week?

One or two pieces a week is a good start. Consistency matters most, so the audience starts recognising your heroes.

### What if an employee says something wrong?

Videos are reviewed before publishing. In medicine, finance and law, check every fact a specialist states.

## Conclusion

People buy from people. Showing your employees removes the fear of the unknown, demonstrates professionalism and keeps content ideas flowing. Two or three willing heroes, process and tip formats, written consent — start there.
