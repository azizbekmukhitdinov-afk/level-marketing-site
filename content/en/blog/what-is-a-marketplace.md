A few years ago, selling online meant having a website or an Instagram page. Today many business owners take their first step through a marketplace. This guide explains in plain terms **what a marketplace is**, how it makes money and whether it suits your product — using Uzbekistan as the example.

## What is a marketplace: a simple definition

A marketplace is an online market where many sellers sell products on one platform. The platform brings the buyer, takes the payment and often runs delivery or pickup points itself. The seller is responsible for the product, the price and the listing.

It helps to separate it from other online channels:

| | Marketplace | Your own online store | Classifieds site |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where buyers come from | The platform's own traffic | You bring them | The platform, but fewer |
| Payment and delivery | Usually the platform | You | Seller and buyer between themselves |
| Price competition | Very strong, side by side | Weaker | Moderate |
| Who owns the customer base | Mostly the platform | You | Nobody |
| Costs | Commission and services | Website, ads, logistics | Low or free |

Put simply: on a marketplace you rent a stall in a ready-made market; with your own store you build the market yourself.

## How a marketplace works

Every order has four parties: the seller, the platform, the buyer and logistics. The flow looks roughly like this:

1. The seller creates a listing in the seller account: photos, description, price, specifications.
2. Stock goes to the platform's warehouse or stays in the seller's own warehouse.
3. The buyer finds it in the app, pays and chooses a pickup point or courier.
4. The platform delivers; the buyer accepts or returns the item.
5. The platform keeps its share and pays the rest to the seller on a set schedule.

There are usually two fulfilment models:

- **from the platform's warehouse** (often called FBO) — you send stock to the warehouse in advance and the platform handles storage, packing and delivery;
- **from the seller's warehouse** (often called FBS) — stock stays with you; when an order arrives you pack it and hand it to the platform.

Names and terms differ between platforms, so check them against the official rules in your seller account.

## Costs: what there is besides commission

New sellers often count only the commission. In practice the list is longer:

- **commission** — the platform's share of each sale;
- **logistics** — getting stock to the warehouse, platform delivery, sometimes the return trip;
- **storage** — there may be a separate fee if stock sits in the warehouse for long;
- **returns** — especially frequent in clothing and footwear;
- **internal ads** — to push a listing up in search;
- **promotions** — discounts for joining the platform's sales events;
- **content** — photos, video, descriptions.

Illustrative example for one product:

| Line | Amount, UZS |
|---|---|
| Selling price | 200,000 |
| Cost of goods | −100,000 |
| Commission (illustrative 15%) | −30,000 |
| Logistics and packaging | −15,000 |
| Returns and internal ads | −15,000 |
| **What's left for you** | **40,000** |

The figures are illustrative. The point: out of a 200,000-som sale you may keep 40,000, and taxes, salaries and rent still come out of that. Do this calculation before listing — the method is in our article on [unit economics](/en/blog/unit-economics-for-small-business/).

## Marketplace vs your own store: pros and cons

**Pros:**

- ready-made traffic — people are already searching for products in the app;
- trust — buyers trust the platform even if they don't know you;
- logistics and payments in place — no need to build a courier service or checkout;
- fast start — a listing can go live within days;
- nationwide delivery.

**Cons:**

- price competition — buyers compare you side by side with similar products;
- the customer isn't yours — their contact details and history stay with the platform;
- rules change — commissions, tariffs and algorithms update without asking you;
- your brand is invisible — buyers say "I got it on Uzum", not your store's name;
- dependence on ratings and reviews.

That's why, for many businesses, the best path isn't either-or: the marketplace brings volume, while your own channels — Instagram, Telegram, a website — build the brand and repeat customers.

## Who a marketplace suits, and who should be careful

| Good fit | Be careful |
|---|---|
| Mass-demand goods: homeware, cosmetics, accessories, kids' products | Large, heavy goods: furniture, building materials — logistics are expensive |
| Businesses with their own production and low unit cost | Resellers with very thin margins |
| New brands without an audience yet | Products that need advice or measuring |
| Sellers who want to reach the regions | Services — marketplaces are built for products |

For a furniture store in Tashkent, a marketplace won't be the main channel, but it can work as an extra one for small items — lamps, cushions, shelves.

## How to start selling on a marketplace: 7 steps

1. **Run the numbers.** Including commission, logistics and returns, check that every product makes a profit.
2. **Pick a platform.** Start with one — for a local audience that's usually Uzum Market.
3. **Prepare documents.** A sole proprietorship or company, a bank account, certificates if needed. Confirm the requirements with the platform.
4. **Build the listing.** Quality photos, a clear title, specifications and sizes. The listing is your salesperson on the marketplace. How to prepare it with AI and which mistakes to avoid is in [Uzum Market product listings with AI](/en/blog/ai-product-listing-uzum/); for video, see [product video mistakes](/en/blog/product-video-mistakes/).
5. **Start with a small batch.** Don't send your whole range at once — test 5–10 items.
6. **Collect your first reviews.** Neat packaging, fast dispatch and answers to questions are what ratings are built on.
7. **Review weekly.** Views, add-to-carts, orders, returns and net profit per product.

Detailed first steps on Uzum Market are in [how to start selling on Uzum Market](/en/blog/selling-on-uzum-market/).

## How to keep your brand on a marketplace

If you compete on price alone, someone cheaper will always turn up. A brand softens that: buyers search for your name and compare prices less.

Practical steps: put your brand and Instagram or Telegram handle on the packaging, keep listing photos in one style, and in Instagram ads show the product and link to the listing. The path from reseller to brand is covered in [building a brand on Uzum Market](/en/blog/building-a-brand-on-uzum-market/).

## 5 mistakes new sellers make

- **Starting without the maths.** The price is set below a competitor's, and after commission and logistics there's no profit left.
- **A weak listing.** A dark phone photo and a one-line description — the buyer moves on to the next listing.
- **Sending the whole range at once.** Unsold stock sits in the warehouse and eats storage fees.
- **Ignoring reviews.** An unanswered negative review is the first thing the next buyers read.
- **Relying on the marketplace alone.** If the rules change or the platform closes, there's no other channel.

## Questions and answers

### Do I need to register a business to sell on a marketplace?

Usually yes — platforms ask for sole-proprietor or company status. Exact requirements and document lists differ, so check them on the seller registration page.

### Marketplace or Instagram — which is better?

They do different jobs. A marketplace gives you ready buyers and logistics; Instagram gives you brand, trust and direct contact with customers. Many businesses use both.

### Can I sell on several marketplaces at once?

You can, but we don't recommend it at the start: each platform has its own rules, warehouses and reports. Get the process working on one, then add a second.

### Can my marketplace price differ from my own store's price?

Technically yes, but a big gap confuses customers. Set the price with the commission in mind and keep any difference between channels explainable.

## Conclusion

A marketplace is a ready-made market: traffic, payments and logistics are in place, and in return you get commission, tough price competition and dependence on the platform's rules. Before listing, calculate net profit per product, start with one platform and a small batch, and grow the marketplace alongside your own brand and channels.
