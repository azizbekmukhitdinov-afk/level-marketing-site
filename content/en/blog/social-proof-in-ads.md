Before buying from an unfamiliar business for the first time, people look for an answer to one question: "Can I trust them?" However beautiful the ad, if there's no answer, there's no purchase. Social proof is the evidence that other people have already chosen you and were satisfied. Many people reduce it to reviews, but reviews are just one of eight or nine tools. Here are the different forms of social proof and how to use them in ads and on your profile.

## Why social proof works

When uncertain, people look at others. If I don't know a company, but people like me have bought from it and are happy, the risk feels lower. This matters especially for:

- **expensive purchases** — mistakes are costly;
- **services** — you can't see the result in advance;
- **new brands** — there's no reputation yet;
- **online purchases** — you can't touch the product.

## 8 trust signals

| Signal | What it is | Example (illustrative) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Reviews | Customers' written opinions | Reviews on Google Maps |
| 2. Video testimonial | A customer speaking on camera | "Our kitchen was ready in 2 weeks" |
| 3. Real-life use | Customers using the product | Customer photos, Stories reposts |
| 4. Numbers | Measurable experience | "12 years in business", "over 3,000 installations" |
| 5. Well-known clients | Companies that chose you | Partner logos (with permission) |
| 6. Experts | Expert endorsement, certificates | Certificate, licence, diploma |
| 7. Process transparency | Showing how you work | A production video |
| 8. Activity signals | People are buying right now | "40 orders delivered this week" |

Not every business needs all of them. But every business should have at least three or four.

## 1–3: the customer's voice

**Reviews** are the most common but weaker form: they work in volume, while a single review barely registers. A system for collecting them — [online reputation and reviews](/en/blog/online-reputation-and-reviews/).

**Video testimonials** are the strongest form: face, voice and emotion. How to film one — [customer testimonial video](/en/blog/customer-testimonial-video/).

**Real-life use** is content customers post themselves with your product. It looks least like an ad. More in [UGC content](/en/blog/ugc-content/).

## 4–6: numbers and authority

**Numbers** build trust, but only if they're real. The Law "On Advertising" requires advertisers to provide, on request, documents confirming the accuracy of their claims (Article 12), and misleading advertising is prohibited (Article 5, [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/docs/6052631)). If you write "10,000 happy customers", be ready to prove it. More in [advertising law in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/advertising-law-uzbekistan/).

**Well-known clients** are a strong argument in B2B. But get permission before using another company's logo — many work under NDAs.

**Experts and certificates** matter for clinics, education and construction. Don't just post a certificate as an image — explain what it guarantees.

## 7–8: transparency and activity

**Process transparency** is underrated proof. Showing your production, kitchen or workshop says "we have nothing to hide". An example — [construction progress videos](/en/blog/construction-progress-videos/).

**Activity signals** show the business is alive: new orders, deliveries, a full room. Only real ones: fake "15 people are viewing this now" signals get exposed quickly and kill trust.

## Where to show social proof

Proof is needed at every stage of the customer journey:

| Stage | Which proof | Where |
|---|---|---|
| First impression | A number, a customer video | The ad creative |
| Visiting the profile | Reviews, results, process | Highlights, the first 9 posts |
| Before submitting a lead | A selected review, a guarantee | Lead form, landing page |
| Talking to sales | A similar customer's story | A link the salesperson sends |
| After purchase | A request for a review | A message, a QR code |

More on the profile — [an Instagram profile that sells](/en/blog/instagram-profile-that-sells/).

## Social proof in ads: 3 formats

1. **A review in the creative**: a screenshot or quote with a product photo.
2. **A customer video**: a 15–30 second testimonial, especially in retargeting.
3. **A number as a hook**: "2,000 families chose this kitchen" (only with a real number).

Test each one — you can't know in advance which proof will move your audience most. [A/B testing ad creatives](/en/blog/ab-testing-ad-creatives/).

## Common mistakes

- Writing or buying fake reviews — once exposed, reputation takes a very long time to recover.
- Numbers you can't prove.
- Using a customer's name and photo without consent.
- Reviews on the page but none in the ads.
- All reviews in the same style — they look fake.
- Deleting negative reviews — a good reply builds more trust.

## Questions and answers

### A new business has no social proof — what now?

Start with process transparency, the owner and team, certificates, and asking your first customers for reviews. Getting reviews from your first 10–20 customers is an important task.

### Does a negative review damage social proof?

No, if it gets a calm, constructive reply. Only positive reviews can sometimes seem suspicious.

### Can I round numbers?

Rounding down is safe: with 1,230, say "over 1,200". Rounding up is misleading.

### Is an influencer recommendation social proof?

Yes, but it must be labelled as advertising. More in [influencer advertising](/en/blog/influencer-advertising-uzbekistan/).

## Conclusion

Social proof is much broader than reviews: video testimonials, customer content, real numbers, process transparency and more. Place it at every stage of the customer journey and use only genuine proof.
