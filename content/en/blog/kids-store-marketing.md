In a kids store, the person who uses the product and the person who pays for it are different people. The toy is for the child, but a mother, a grandmother or a birthday party guest chooses it. So marketing children's goods speaks to adults, not to children: it saves them time, protects them from a bad choice and gives them confidence about safety. Below is a practical plan for a store selling children's clothing, toys and kids' goods.

## Who buys: four buyers

| Buyer | Their job | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| Mother (sometimes father) | Clothes as the child grows, everyday items, educational toys | Choice by age and size, delivery, easy repeat purchases |
| Grandparents | A gift for a grandchild, often a large one | A salesperson's advice, ordering by phone, beautiful wrapping |
| Birthday party guest | A quick gift that won't be duplicated, a clear budget | Ready-made "age + budget" selections, gift wrapping |
| A family with a newborn and their relatives | First items, going home from the maternity ward, a gift for the beshik toy (cradle celebration) | Ready-made sets and lists |

The most overlooked segment is gift-buying guests. They don't know the child; they only know the age and their own budget. They need a ready answer: "a gift for a 3-year-old boy under 200,000 soums". How purchase decisions are split within a family is covered in [family purchase decisions](/en/blog/family-purchase-decision/).

## The advertising law: talk to the adult, not the child

Article 17 of Uzbekistan's Law "On Advertising" protects minors in advertising. Among other things, it bans urging minors to buy a product or to ask third parties to buy it for them, showing minors in dangerous situations, using real or toy weapons, and discrediting parents and educators ([lex.uz, Law No. 776](https://lex.uz/en/docs/-6052631)).

In practice this means:

- no calls aimed at the child, like "Tell your mum to buy it today!";
- no scenes of children without a car seat, on a balcony or near water unsupervised;
- in ads for toy weapons, don't show a child holding the weapon;
- the copy and the call to action are addressed to the parent: benefit, age, safety, price.

Meta follows a similar logic: the company announced that advertisers can reach users under 18 only by age and location ([Meta, 2023](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/01/age-appropriate-ads-for-teens/)). For a kids store this is no problem, because your audience is parents anyway. General restrictions: [advertising law in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/advertising-law-uzbekistan/).

## Age-based content: answer the parent's question

Parents don't search for "toys"; they ask "what toy should I get a 2-year-old?". Build the content plan around ages, not categories:

| Age | The parent's question | Content example |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | What's safe, what's needed, what's unnecessary | "Hospital bag: a list of 15 items" |
| 1–3 | Development, the risk of small parts | "5 toys for an 18-month-old, and why" |
| 3–6 | Kindergarten, creativity, role play | "Kindergarten clothes: which fabric and size" |
| 6–10 | School, sport, building sets | "The complete first-grader list" |

Repeat one format in each rubric: a salesperson or a mum-expert explains three products in 30 seconds. An "age" filter on the website and in the catalogue is a must, since it is the fastest route for a gift buyer.

Clothing has its own problem: size. Children grow fast and parents don't know the size. Give a height-based size chart in every post and in your quick replies in Direct, and state the exchange terms up front.

If you photograph children, get the parents' written consent; it is both a legal requirement and a matter of trust. Details: [photo and video consent for advertising](/en/blog/photo-video-consent-for-advertising/).

## Birthdays: the most predictable date of the year

A birthday is the one purchase occasion you know about in advance. Use it from two sides.

**1. The child's birthday in your database.** In your loyalty card or bot, ask the parent for the child's name and date of birth (with consent). A week before, send a personal message: congratulations and an invitation to choose a gift. How to build the loyalty system: [loyalty programmes](/en/blog/loyalty-program/).

**2. A wish list.** The parent builds the child's wish list in the store or in the bot, and guests pick from it via a link, so the same gift never arrives twice. This mechanic brings not one buyer into the store but the whole group of guests.

Illustrative example: your database has 1,200 children, about 100 birthdays a month. 25% respond to the message and spend 250,000 soums on average: 6.25 million soums of revenue a month with no ad spend. If one in five of them creates a wish list and guests buy 3 gifts from each list on average, that's 15 more purchases. The figures are made up, but the calculation applies directly to your own database.

## Seasons: school and holidays

Children's retail has a few clear peaks. In Uzbekistan, school classes start on 2 September, and the 2026/2027 school year began that day ([Kun.uz](https://kun.uz/news/2026/09/02/yangi-oquv-yili-boshlandi-maktabning-birinchi-kunidan-reportaj)). So back-to-school demand heats up in August, which means ads and stock should be ready in July.

| Period | What sells | Preparation |
|---|---|---|
| July–August | Backpacks, uniforms, stationery, shoes | A priced "first-grader set", a checklist |
| October–November | Winter clothing | Size reminders, messages to past buyers |
| December | New Year gifts | Selections by budget, wrapping, delivery deadlines |
| 1 June and summer holidays | Outdoor games, bikes, swimming | Summer collection, family content |

Sets like the "first-grader set" raise the average order and spare parents dozens of small decisions. On sets and add-on sales: [upselling and cross-selling](/en/blog/upsell-and-cross-sell/). Dates for the year: [marketing calendar 2027](/en/blog/marketing-calendar-2027-uzbekistan/).

## Trust in safety: evidence, not claims

With children's goods, parents look at safety first. "100% safe" convinces nobody; evidence does.

Uzbekistan has a general technical regulation on the safety of children's toys (Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 451 of 23 July 2020). Under it, toys are certified before being placed on the market, and the marking must include, among other things, the minimum age or an age pictogram, in the state language ([lex.uz, Resolution No. 451](https://lex.uz/uz/docs/-4909963)). Check the current version of the document yourself.

How to use this in marketing:

- the product page shows that a certificate exists and displays the age marking;
- a rubric on "how to choose a safe toy": reading the label, small parts, smell, sturdiness;
- staff always warn about small parts when a product is for a child under 3;
- never sell uncertified goods as "the cheap option"; one bad incident costs the store its reputation.

## Repeat purchases: the child grows, the customer returns

A kids store has a natural advantage: customers come back every season, because the child grows. Turn that into a system:

- record the child's age and size with every purchase;
- when a new collection arrives, message parents of children that age;
- keep phone orders and delivery for grandparents, who won't write in Direct;
- reuse customer videos (with consent): real families earn more trust than ad actors.

## Common mistakes

- An ad where a child cries "buy it!": against the law and off-putting to parents.
- A catalogue organised by category rather than age.
- No size chart and vague exchange terms.
- No gift wrapping and no "gift by budget" section.
- Starting back-to-school preparation at the end of August.

## Questions and answers

### Which channel works best for a kids store?

Instagram and Telegram for content and repeat contact, maps for parents nearby, targeted ads during seasonal peaks. The core audience is mothers, so show the parent's problem in the creative.

### Can I show a child in an ad?

Yes, with the parents' written consent and a scene that meets the law: no dangerous situations, and the child doesn't urge anyone to buy.

### How do I set up a wish list technically?

The simple option is a list page in a Telegram bot or on your website: the parent marks products, a guest opens the link and reserves a gift. To start, a Google Sheet and a salesperson are enough.

### Do I need an online store, or is offline enough?

You need both: parents are short on time and many order from home, but before the first big purchase they come in to look. At minimum, have a catalogue and delivery.

## Conclusion

Kids store marketing talks to adults: an age-based answer for the mother, advice and wrapping for the grandmother, a ready gift selection for the guest.
