Restaurant and cafe marketing differs from other businesses in one way: guests decide in a few minutes. They answer "where shall we go tonight?" on Instagram, on Yandex or Google Maps and from friends' recommendations. This article lays out a step-by-step plan for a restaurant or cafe in Tashkent to fill the room and grow delivery orders.

![Where guests find you](/img/blog/illus/restoran-kafe-marketing-1.jpg 1200x675)

## Where guests find you

Restaurant marketing channels fall into three groups:

| Channel | Guest's state | Main job |
|---|---|---|
| Google and Yandex Maps | Searching "cafe near me" | Rank first, rating and photos |
| Instagram and TikTok | Scrolling, not decided yet | Trigger appetite, be remembered |
| Friends and reviews | Asking for advice | Trust: "the food there really is good" |

Many restaurants only work on Instagram and forget maps. Yet someone searching "breakfast near me" or "family restaurant Chilanzar" is already a ready guest. We covered how to fill in a business profile in [adding your business to maps](/en/blog/add-your-business-to-maps/).

## Step 1. Concept and one main reason

Why should a guest come to you? "Tasty and cosy" is not a reason — everyone says it. A strong reason is specific:

- **Food:** not "the thinnest lagman in Tashkent" but "lagman noodles are pulled by hand in front of you every day".
- **Occasion:** a 40-minute business lunch, family dinner with a kids' room, a coffee shop open late.
- **Experience:** open kitchen, tandoor, live music, terrace.

Repeat that reason everywhere: profile bio, maps description, ads and menu. How to phrase an offer — see [unique selling proposition](/en/blog/unique-selling-proposition/).

## Step 2. Maps — the cheapest source of guests

Your Google and Yandex Maps listing is the restaurant's second sign. It should have:

1. **The right category** ("Restaurant", "Coffee shop", "Uzbek cuisine") and opening hours, including holidays.
2. **20–30 good photos:** the room, dishes, entrance, parking. Dark flash photos scare guests away.
3. **Menu and prices** — guests want to know upfront whether it is expensive.
4. **A reply to every review.** Answer negative ones calmly and specifically — how, in [online reputation and reviews](/en/blog/online-reputation-and-reviews/).

Waiters can collect reviews: a small QR card with the bill — "Rate us". Never write or buy fake reviews; platforms detect them.

## Step 3. Instagram — appetite and mood

A restaurant's Instagram is a mood, not a catalogue. Rubrics that work:

| Rubric | Example |
|---|---|
| Food close-up | Steam from samsa, shashlik being sliced, sauce poured over a dish |
| Behind the kitchen | The chef rolls dough, bread goes into the tandoor |
| People | Guests (with consent), the team, a short chat with the chef |
| News | Seasonal menu, a new dessert, breakfast hours |
| Offers | Business lunch, holiday set, family box |

The most watched format is a 7–15 second Reel: the dish in the first second, sizzling in the sound. How to shoot — [shooting Reels on a phone](/en/blog/shooting-reels-on-a-phone/); first-second ideas — [Reels hooks](/en/blog/reels-hooks/).

Your profile must have: address and map link, the menu (highlights or link) and a way to book — phone or Telegram.

## Step 4. Paid ads — radius and timing

The main rule of restaurant ads is geography. Nobody drives 40 minutes for lunch. So:

- **Radius:** 1–3 km for lunch, wider for dinners and holidays.
- **Timing:** show ads when guests decide — 10:30–12:30 before lunch, 16:00–19:00 before dinner.
- **Objective:** "messages" (bookings and questions) or profile visits; for delivery, a Telegram bot or website.
- **Creative:** the dish and the price in one frame. "Business lunch — illustrative example: 45,000 UZS, 3 courses, in 40 minutes".

How to set up Instagram ads — see the [step-by-step guide](/en/blog/how-to-set-up-instagram-ads/). Plan budgets ahead for holiday months (December, Navruz, Eid) — see [December ad budget](/en/blog/december-ad-budget/).

## Step 5. Delivery and returning guests

Delivery aggregators bring orders, but the customer base stays with them. Build your own channel too:

- **A Telegram bot or channel** — menu, ordering and members-only offers. How to set it up — [Telegram bot for leads](/en/blog/telegram-bot-for-leads/).
- **A regulars' programme** — every 6th coffee free, dessert on your birthday. The rule should fit in one sentence.
- **Banquets and corporate events** — a separate offer and a separate manager. One corporate order can equal a week of normal takings.

Why a returning guest matters in numbers — [repeat sales and LTV](/en/blog/repeat-sales-and-ltv/).

## The menu sells too

Marketing doesn't stop once a guest walks in — now the menu sets the average bill. A few simple rules:

- **Where the eye lands first** — the top right corner and the start of each section. Put your most profitable dishes there.
- **Photos only for the best 5–7 dishes.** With a photo for every item the menu becomes a catalogue and choosing gets harder.
- **Short descriptions:** "Tandoor lamb, slow-cooked for 6 hours, with bread" sells better than "Meat dish".
- **Pairings:** plov + salad + tea for one price. Have waiters suggest the set too.
- **QR menu** — the online menu should follow the same order and photos; add it to your maps listing as well.

Give waiters a short script: "Today our chef recommends…". That is marketing too — the cheapest and fastest kind.

## What to measure

Likes are not a result for a restaurant. Track:

- bookings and calls (ask guests which channel brought them);
- direction requests and calls from maps (listing statistics);
- average bill and table turnover;
- for delivery — order count and share of repeat orders;
- cost of one booking from ads.

To know where a booking came from, use a separate promo code or "dessert for guests from the ad".

## Common mistakes

- Pretty photos but no address, prices or booking method.
- Ads across the whole city — guests won't travel far.
- Ignoring reviews or arguing with unhappy guests.
- A new "concept" every month — guests can't remember you.
- Waiters and hosts don't know about the promotion in the ad.

## Questions and answers

### Which channel comes first for a restaurant?

Maps. They bring the readiest guests almost for free. Then the Instagram profile, and only then paid ads.

### Where should a new cafe start?

Two or three weeks before opening: a maps listing, a profile and "opening day" ads within 1–2 km. Local bloggers work well too — how to choose them, in [influencer advertising](/en/blog/influencer-advertising-uzbekistan/).

### Should ads show the price?

Yes, especially for lunches and sets. Price is the quickest answer to "is this for me?". Write it in UZS — that is a legal requirement.

### With aggregators, do I still need my own delivery channel?

Yes, in parallel. Aggregators bring new customers; your channel turns them into regulars and cuts commission.

## Conclusion

Restaurant marketing comes down to three steps: be found on maps, trigger appetite on Instagram, and aim ads at the right radius and hours.
