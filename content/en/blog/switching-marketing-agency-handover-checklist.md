Switching marketing agencies or parting ways with an SMM manager is a normal part of business. The trouble starts afterwards: the Instagram password is tied to a former employee's phone, nobody has the logo source file, and the website domain is registered to someone else. What your brand built over years — followers, content, ad history — can slip away in a day. Below is a checklist that splits the handover into five groups and walks you through it in order.

![Switching marketing agencies: first find out what is really yours](/img/blog/illus/agentlikni-almashtirish-1.jpg 1200x675)

## Switching marketing agencies: first find out what is really yours

Many owners assume "it's all mine, I paid for it". In practice an asset is in one of three states.

| State | Example | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Yours, the contractor only has access | Instagram is registered to your email, the agency is connected as a partner | Removing access is enough |
| The contractor's, but transferable | The SMM manager created the Telegram channel from their own number; the domain is in the developer's name | Transfer ownership formally — it takes time |
| The contractor's and not transferable | The ad account was created inside the agency's business portfolio | Export the data and open a new one of your own |

So the first job is a list. Open a spreadsheet and note three things next to each asset: who owns it, who has access, and which phone and email are set for recovery. Make this list **before** you announce the split: passwords and files are easier to get while everyone is calm.

## What to take over: 5 asset groups

### 1. Accounts and pages

- **Instagram and Facebook.** The phone and email on the account should be yours. The Page should sit in your business portfolio, and you should have full control.
- **Telegram channel and bot.** A channel has one owner (the creator). Only that person can transfer ownership to another admin. Telegram asks for two-step verification to be on, and if it was enabled recently the app itself shows how long you need to wait. So this cannot be left for the last day. A bot is a separate matter: it lives in the account of whoever created it.
- **Google and Yandex Maps listing.** In Google Business Profile only the primary owner can transfer primary ownership, and it cannot be transferred to a new owner during their first 7 days — that is in [Google's help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3415281). So add yourself to the profile at least a week ahead.
- **TikTok, YouTube, your marketplace store** — the same questions: whose email, whose phone.

More on protecting accounts in our piece on [business account security](/en/blog/business-account-security/).

### 2. Advertising assets

The most painful case is an ad account created on the agency's side. In Meta an ad account belongs to one business portfolio, and you cannot "move" another business's account into yours. In that case campaign history and saved audiences stay with the agency. What you can do is export the reports and open a new ad account in your own portfolio.

Check and take over:

- the ad account, Pixel and dataset, product catalogue — whose portfolio they sit in;
- whose card is on file and where the latest invoices are;
- an export of results for at least 12 months: campaign, spend, results, cost per result;
- an archive of creatives and copy, with a note on which performed best;
- leads from lead forms: did they reach the CRM or only stay in Ads Manager.

How to grant and remove access properly is covered step by step in [giving an agency access to your ad account](/en/blog/give-agency-access-meta-ad-account/). Meta also has its own [help page](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1717412048538897) on working with partners.

### 3. Content and source files

A finished post is a result. A brand also needs the sources: without them you cannot even change a price on a banner tomorrow.

- logo and identity in vector formats (AI, SVG, PDF), fonts and their licences;
- design files: Figma or Canva files moved into **your** account, not just shared by link;
- raw video footage and finished videos without captions and music;
- full photo shoot archives;
- the content plan, list of content pillars, stats for the best posts.

Having the file does not mean you have the right to use it. Under Uzbekistan's [Law on Copyright and Related Rights](https://lex.uz/acts/-1022944), usage rights pass by contract. What to write down is in our article on [content copyright](/en/blog/content-copyright-uzbekistan/). If there is no [brand book](/en/blog/what-is-a-brand-book/), at least get the colour codes and font names in writing.

### 4. Website, domain and analytics

- **Domain.** Check in the registrar's dashboard or via WHOIS who it is registered to. If it is the developer or the agency, ask the registrar how re-registration works — it is not a one-day job.
- **Hosting and site admin.** The admin login is yours and the bill is paid from your card.
- **Analytics.** In Google Analytics, Google Search Console, Yandex Metrica and Webmaster you should be the owner or an administrator. If the counter was created in the agency's account, years of statistics stay there too.
- **Links.** The link-in-bio service, short links, QR codes — whose account were they created in? If the QR code on your printed ads points to a former contractor's service, one day it will stop working.

### 5. Customers and conversations

- the CRM database and access rights to it;
- open chats in Direct and Telegram: who has not been answered, who is waiting to pay;
- saved replies, sales scripts, the list of frequent questions;
- arrangements with influencers and subcontractors: who has been paid for what, and which obligations remain.

## The order: four stages

| Stage | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Preparation | Before you say anything | Asset list, add yourself as owner everywhere, read the notice period in the contract |
| Notice | During the contract notice period | Written notice, list and date of the handover, agreement on campaigns still running |
| Acceptance | Last working day | Open and check the files, transfer ownership, sign the handover record, final payment |
| Close-out | The next 30 days | Remove access, change passwords, review connected apps |

Two things matter here.

**First, the sequence.** You accept first and close access second. Do it the other way round and the contractor cannot export files — and cannot transfer the Telegram channel at all.

**Second, live ads.** Do not switch campaigns off in one day. Let them run until the new team is ready, and agree in writing who watches them.

> Illustrative example: a furniture store in Tashkent wants to switch agencies from 1 December. The contract requires 30 days' notice. On 25 October the owner adds themselves as an owner of the Google listing, on 1 November sends written notice, on 28 November accepts the files and signs the handover record, and on 1 December closes access.

## The handover record

A spoken "I've sent you everything" is not enough. Write a one-page record:

1. The accounts handed over and who now owns each one.
2. The list of files and the folder they are in (on your cloud drive).
3. What was not handed over and why — for example, "the ad account stays in the agency's portfolio; reports exported".
4. Open obligations: unpaid invoices, an influencer ad that has not gone out yet.
5. Confidentiality: the contractor will not use the customer database or internal figures.
6. Date and both signatures.

Even if the contract has no such clause, propose the record — a fair contractor will not refuse. And write the handover procedure into the next contract in advance: when [choosing an agency](/en/blog/choosing-an-agency-red-flags/), ask about it at the first meeting.

## 6 common mistakes

1. **Closing access first and asking for files later.** The relationship is damaged, and technically much is no longer possible.
2. **Leaving with a row.** You will still need the former contractor: a forgotten password, a source file, one quick answer. Calm written communication costs less.
3. **Taking only the finished posts.** Without sources, the new designer redraws everything from scratch.
4. **Forgetting connected apps.** If a scheduling tool, a chatbot or a CRM integration is connected through the former contractor's account, access may survive a password change — or everything may simply stop.
5. **Not checking the phone number.** The account is on your email, but the confirmation code goes to a former employee's number.
6. **Handing nothing to the new team.** Without reports, winning creatives and a list of past mistakes, the new team spends its first month repeating old lessons. More in [the first 30 days with an agency](/en/blog/first-30-days-with-a-marketing-agency/).

## Questions and answers

**The agency won't hand over the files. What now?**
Reread the contract first: does it say what is handed over? Then send a written request with a specific list and date. Tying the final payment to the handover record is usually enough. If the dispute grows, talk to a lawyer.

**The ad account was created on the agency's side. Can it be moved to me?**
As a rule, no: an ad account stays in the business portfolio where it was created. Export the reports, collect the creatives and open a new ad account in your own portfolio. If the Pixel and the Page are yours, they will work with the new account too.

**Our SMM manager was an employee. Do we still need a record?**
Yes, a shorter one. Accounts, passwords, the files folder and the list of open chats should be handed over before their last day.

**When should passwords be changed?**
On the day everything has been accepted. Along with the password, end active sessions and check that two-step verification is tied to your number.

**Will the new agency start from zero?**
Not if the assets are yours. Audiences, Pixel history and the content archive remain, and the new team builds on top of them.

## Conclusion

Switching agencies is not the risk; a messy split is. List your assets in advance, add yourself as owner everywhere, accept the handover first and only then close access. Better still, do this at the start of the relationship rather than on the day it ends: if the accounts and the ad account are in your name from day one, the handover becomes a one-day formality.
