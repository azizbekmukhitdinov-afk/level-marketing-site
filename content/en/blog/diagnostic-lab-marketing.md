A diagnostic centre does not sell a pleasant experience. People come because a doctor sent them, because something worries them, or because they finally decided to get checked. That changes how marketing works: fear-based ads backfire, prices must be clear, and the moment results are delivered matters as much as the ad that brought the patient. Below is a working system for a diagnostic centre or medical lab, with a careful eye on what medical advertising may and may not say.

## Three flows of patients

Before planning ads, split your patients by where they come from. Each flow needs a different message and a different metric.

| Flow | What the person wants | Where they come from | Key metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| Referred by a doctor | Do the prescribed tests quickly and correctly | Doctor's referral, nearby clinics | Share of visits with a referral, by doctor |
| Self-directed | Check their health, understand a symptom, get a package | Search, maps, Instagram, recommendations | Cost per visit, average check |
| Corporate | Check-ups for employees, pre-employment exams | Direct sales to companies | Contracts, people per contract |

Most centres spend their whole budget on the second flow and forget the first one, although referred patients usually arrive with a ready decision. The third flow is slow to build but gives volume on weekdays when the centre is half empty.

## Check-up packages: what to offer

A list of 300 test names in a price list does not help a person who just wants to «check everything». A package does. It works as an [entry offer](/en/blog/entry-offer/): one clear product with a price, a duration and a list of what is included.

Rules for building packages:

- **Compose them with your doctors**, not with the marketing team. The content of a medical package is a medical decision.
- **Name them by life situation, not by disease.** «Annual check-up», «Women's check-up», «Check-up 40+», «Before the sports season» sound calmer than packages named after illnesses.
- **Show what is inside and how long it takes:** number of tests, whether a doctor's consultation is included, when results are ready.
- **Explain preparation** in one short message: fasting, time of day, what not to do before the visit.
- **Give a reason to do it now** that is not fear: a season, a birthday month, a family package.

### Illustrative example: package economics

The numbers below are an illustrative example, not data from a real centre.

| Indicator | Value |
|---|---|
| Package price | 850,000 soums |
| Direct costs (reagents, materials, doctor's time) | 380,000 soums |
| Margin per package | 470,000 soums |
| Ad spend per month | 9,000,000 soums |
| Visits from ads | 60 |
| Ad cost per visit | 150,000 soums |
| Extra services after the package (20% of patients, average 400,000 soums) | 4,800,000 soums revenue |

Margin from packages is 28,200,000 soums against 9,000,000 soums of ads. The real value is what comes next: patients who liked the service return next year and bring family members. Count [repeat visits and LTV](/en/blog/repeat-sales-and-ltv/), not just the first check.

## Working with referring doctors

Doctors send patients where results are reliable, fast and easy to read. Marketing to doctors is therefore mostly about service, not advertising.

- **Speed and clarity.** Results that reach the doctor on time, in a clear format, with reference ranges.
- **Easy referral.** A simple referral form, printed or online, with a code for each doctor or clinic, so you can see in the CRM where patients come from.
- **Communication with patient consent.** Sending results to the referring doctor is possible only with the patient's consent. Health data is a special category of personal data under the Law «On Personal Data» ([lex.uz](https://lex.uz/docs/4396419)); see [customer database and personal data](/en/blog/customer-database-personal-data-uzbekistan/).
- **Useful contact, not gifts.** A short note about new tests, a visit to explain how samples are taken, a direct phone line for questions.

Avoid schemes that pay doctors per referral. They damage patient trust, and if they come to light, they hurt the whole brand. Any partnership with clinics should be discussed with a lawyer before it starts.

## Results delivery is a marketing moment

The patient waits for results anxiously. How you deliver them decides whether they come back and recommend you.

| Step | Good practice |
|---|---|
| Ready notification | A short message in Telegram or SMS: «Your results are ready», with no medical details in the text |
| Access | A personal account or a link with verification, not a PDF in an open chat |
| Readability | Reference ranges and a clear note: «Discuss the results with your doctor» |
| Next step | An offer to book a consultation, not a diagnosis by the operator |
| Feedback | A short survey a day later, not on the same message as the results |

Two rules. Do not mix service messages and advertising: results go in one message, promotions only to those who agreed to receive them, see [the SMS advertising rules](/en/blog/sms-advertising-rules-uzbekistan/). And train the call centre never to interpret results over the phone: that is the doctor's job.

## Trust signals that work

A person chooses a lab they cannot check. So show what can be checked:

- licence and accreditation documents, visible on the site and in the profile;
- equipment and the brand of analysers, with a short explanation of why it matters;
- how quality control is organised, in plain words;
- doctors and nurses by name and photo;
- prices on the site, including packages;
- realistic turnaround times, and keeping to them.

Reviews also matter, especially on maps where people search «tests near me». Ask for reviews, reply to every one, and never discuss a patient's health in a public reply. More in [online reputation and reviews](/en/blog/online-reputation-and-reviews/).

## What medical ads may not say

Uzbekistan's advertising law has no separate article for medical services, but general rules apply: advertising a licensed activity without a licence is banned, and misleading advertising is improper advertising. Details are in [advertising law in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/advertising-law-uzbekistan/); the law text is on [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/en/docs/6052631).

Platforms add their own limits. Meta does not allow ads that assert or imply knowledge of a person's health condition ([Meta Advertising Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes/)). So «Do you have diabetes?» is a risk, while «Blood sugar test: results in 24 hours» describes the service.

| Risky | Safer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| «Do you have thyroid problems? Get tested!» | «Thyroid panel: what is included and how to prepare» | Implies knowledge of the viewer's health |
| «Find out if you have cancer» | «Check-up 40+: list of tests, price, time» | Fear and an implied diagnostic promise |
| «100% accurate results» | «Modern analysers and daily quality control» | An absolute claim that cannot be proven |
| «Our patient was cured thanks to early diagnosis» | Description of the service, with no patient stories | Invented or unconsented patient stories |

## Channels: where patients look

- **Maps.** Many search «laboratory near me». Your profile must have hours, address, photos, the list of popular tests and prices.
- **Search ads.** People search for test names. This is the highest intent, but check every ad text against the rules above.
- **Instagram and Telegram.** Short videos by a doctor explaining what a test shows and how to prepare. Educate, do not frighten.
- **Corporate sales.** A one-page offer for HR managers: package, price per employee, on-site sampling if you offer it.

## Numbers to review every week

A diagnostic centre has many small leaks between the ad and the visit. Review these numbers once a week with the administrator and the marketer:

| Metric | How to count | What a drop usually means |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per visit | Ad spend divided by patients who actually came | Ads attract curious people, or booking fails |
| Booking to visit rate | Visits divided by bookings | No reminder, inconvenient slots, unclear preparation |
| Share of referred patients | Visits with a referral code divided by all visits | Doctors stopped referring: check speed and quality of results |
| Results on time | Results delivered by the promised time divided by all results | A trust problem that ads cannot fix |
| Return within 12 months | Patients with a second visit divided by all patients | No reminder, or the first experience was weak |

If the cost per visit grows while the booking to visit rate falls, do not raise the budget. Fix the phone handling and reminders first.

## Questions and answers

### Should a diagnostic centre run discounts?

Discounts on medical services are not banned in general, but constant promotions make the price look unreal. A seasonal package or a family offer works better than «minus 50% on all tests».

### Can we show patient reviews in ads?

Only with the patient's written consent, and without health details that identify them. A safer route is reviews about service: speed, politeness, cleanliness.

### Which metric matters most?

Cost per visit, not cost per lead. A lead who never comes does not matter. Then the share of returning patients over 12 months.

### Do we need a doctor in the content?

Yes, if possible. A doctor explaining preparation or what a test shows builds trust faster than any designed banner. Make sure the doctor does not promote specific medicines: that falls under separate rules.

## Conclusion

A diagnostic centre grows on three things: packages that are easy to understand, doctors who refer because the service is reliable, and results delivered calmly and privately. Advertising helps only when it describes the service rather than frightening people.
