Buyers think they choose logically. In reality, attention, emotion and quick mental shortcuts weigh heavily on decisions. **Neuromarketing** studies how buyers' brains and behaviour respond to ads, prices and packaging. A small business doesn't need a lab — but it does need the findings.

## How neuromarketing does its research

- **Eye-tracking** — where people look on a page or package.
- **EEG** — changes in brain activity, attention and reaction.
- **fMRI** — which brain areas activate (expensive, mostly used in academic research).
- **Skin response and facial expressions** — emotional reactions.
- **Behavioural experiments** — A/B tests and real choices.

For a small business, the cheapest "neuro lab" is an A/B test — [A/B testing ad creatives](/en/blog/ab-testing-ad-creatives/).

## 7 principles a business can apply

1. **Attention is the scarcest resource.** What's visible in the first seconds decides everything — [Reels hooks](/en/blog/reels-hooks/).
2. **Faces and gaze.** People look at faces; if the person in a photo looks at the product, viewers' eyes follow.
3. **Anchoring.** The first number people see shapes how they judge the next price.
4. **Loss aversion.** As Kahneman and Tversky showed, people feel a loss more strongly than an equal gain: try "don't overpay" as well as "save".
5. **Choice overload.** Too many options slow decisions — 3 plans often beat 10.
6. **The decoy option.** A third, less attractive option makes the plan you want to sell look better.
7. **Social proof.** Other people's choices build confidence — [social proof in ads](/en/blog/social-proof-in-ads/).

## A pricing example (illustrative example)

Three plans for a course:

- Basic — 900,000 soums.
- Standard — 1,500,000 soums ("most popular").
- Premium — 3,500,000 soums.

Even if Premium rarely sells, it acts as an anchor and makes Standard feel cheaper. More — [how to set prices](/en/blog/how-to-set-prices/).

## Examples in advertising

- One main message — the brain doesn't absorb several messages at once.
- A precise number ("in 15 minutes") beats a vague word ("fast").
- Short copy plus a strong visual — [how to write ad copy](/en/blog/how-to-write-ad-copy/).
- A familiar context — local language, places, situations.

## Ethical limits

Neuromarketing is not a licence to manipulate:

- Fake scarcity ("today only" when it runs every day) destroys trust and can count as misleading advertising — [advertising law in Uzbekistan](/en/blog/advertising-law-uzbekistan/).
- Fake reviews and counters are prohibited and risky.
- Advertising aimed at children calls for extra care.

## Common mistakes

- Using the word "neuro" to sell promises with no scientific basis.
- Applying a principle without testing — it may work differently with your audience.
- No psychological trick will rescue a poor product.

## Questions and answers

### Does a small business need neuromarketing?

A lab, no. The principles and A/B tests, yes.

### Is it true that "95% of decisions are unconscious"?

The figure is repeated a lot, but its scientific basis is disputed. What matters is that emotion and attention strongly influence decisions.

### Where should I start?

With your pricing page and ad creatives: pick one principle and test it.
