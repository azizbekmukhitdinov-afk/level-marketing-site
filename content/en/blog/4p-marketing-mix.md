For many business owners, "marketing" means advertising. But advertising is only one part of marketing. The classic **4Ps** model (the marketing mix) shows four parts: **Product**, **Price**, **Place** (distribution) and **Promotion**. If one of them is weak, even the best advertising won't save sales. Here are the 4Ps, illustrated with a small business. What marketing is overall — [what is marketing](/en/blog/what-is-marketing/).

## 1. Product

Questions:

- Which customer problem does it solve?
- How does it differ from competitors — [unique selling proposition](/en/blog/unique-selling-proposition/)?
- Are packaging, guarantees and service part of the product?
- The range: what's surplus, what's missing?

## 2. Price

Questions:

- Does the price fit cost, competition and value to the customer — [how to set prices](/en/blog/how-to-set-prices/)?
- Are there packages and options?
- Is payment convenient: cards, instalments?
- Are discounts eating the margin — [the discount trap](/en/blog/discount-trap/)?

## 3. Place (distribution)

Questions:

- Where do customers find and buy the product: a shop, website, Instagram, marketplace?
- How fast and convenient is delivery?
- Can people find you on maps — [add your business to maps](/en/blog/add-your-business-to-maps/)?
- Do your hours and location suit customers?

## 4. Promotion

Questions:

- Which channels: paid social, search, social media, influencers, outdoor — [types of advertising](/en/blog/types-of-advertising/)?
- What's the main message?
- How are results measured — leads, sales, ROMI?

## Example: a café (illustrative example)

| P | Situation | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Product | A big menu but no signature dish | Single out 2–3 signature dishes |
| Price | Slightly pricier than competitors | A business-lunch package that shows value |
| Place | Near offices, but no delivery | Delivery to nearby offices |
| Promotion | Only Instagram posts | Area-targeted ads and a maps profile |

Clearly, decisions on product, price and place were needed before raising the ad budget.

## The 7Ps for services

Service businesses add three more Ps:

- **People**: staff determine service quality — [customer service standards](/en/blog/customer-service-standards/);
- **Process**: the stages of enquiry, meeting and delivering the service;
- **Physical evidence**: the office, website, reviews, certificates.

## Common mistakes

- Spending only on Promotion while the other three are weak.
- Copying a competitor's price blindly.
- Not being present where customers actually are.
- Writing down the 4Ps once and never revisiting them.

## Questions and answers

### Aren't the 4Ps outdated?

The core questions haven't changed. In the digital world, only the Place and Promotion channels have changed.

### When should I use the 4Ps?

When launching a new product, when sales fall and in annual planning — [one-page marketing strategy](/en/blog/one-page-marketing-strategy/).

### How do SWOT and the 4Ps connect?

SWOT shows the situation; the 4Ps show what to change — [SWOT analysis](/en/blog/swot-analysis/).

## Conclusion

The 4Ps look at marketing more broadly than advertising: sales grow only when product, price, place and promotion work together. For services, add people, process and physical evidence. In a free audit, Level Marketing will show you which part of your marketing is weakest.
