Someone buying an off-plan apartment is really buying a promise: the building doesn't exist yet, there is only a render and a contract. Construction progress videos make that promise visible — every month the buyer sees with their own eyes that the building is growing. In this article we cover 6 types of construction videos for developers, a monthly progress video in 6 steps, safety and drone questions, and how to use the videos in ads and sales.

![Why construction progress videos sell better than renders](/img/blog/illus/qurilish-jarayoni-video-1.jpg 1200x675)

## Why construction progress videos sell better than renders

A render looks good, but it only shows the end result. The buyer's main question is different: "Will the building be finished on time, and is my money safe?" A render can't answer that. A video from the site can.

The fear is not unfounded. According to Spot.uz, in 2025 nearly 3,000 people in Uzbekistan lost a combined 668 billion soums because of troubled construction projects; the same year, the volume of housing built with buyers' money passed 11 million square meters ([Spot.uz](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2026/07/27/escrow/)). A bill on shared-equity construction is still under discussion. So today buyers look for reassurance not only in documents but in the developer's own content.

A construction progress video does three jobs:

- **It proves movement.** Every month a new floor, new walls, new utilities — the project hasn't stalled.
- **It shows people.** The chief engineer, the site manager, the project lead — the buyer knows who they are dealing with.
- **It gives the sales team evidence.** Instead of saying "construction is going well", a manager sends the client last month's video.

One of the main mistakes in developer advertising is promoting only the render and a discount. We covered this in [real estate developer ads](/en/blog/real-estate-developer-ads/); this article is the practical follow-up to that advice.

## 6 types of construction progress videos

Not every video has to be the same. Each type has its own job:

| Video type | What it shows | Length | Where it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly progress video | What was done this month: floors, facade, utilities | 45–90 seconds | Reels, Telegram channel, sales team |
| "Same spot" video | Shots from one angle, filmed month after month | 15–30 seconds | Reels, ads |
| Engineer interview | A specialist answers one technical question | 40–90 seconds | Reels, retargeting |
| Materials and technology | Walls, windows, insulation, elevators — close-up | 20–40 seconds | Reels, website, project page |
| Buyer question answered | Questions like "When can I see the apartment?" | 30–60 seconds | Stories, Direct, Telegram |
| Completed buildings | Past projects, key handovers, a lived-in courtyard | 30–60 seconds | Ads, sales presentation |

Two are enough to start: the **monthly progress video** and the **"same spot" video**. The rest are built around them.

How to prepare an engineer interview is covered in detail in [expert interview videos](/en/blog/expert-interview-videos/). For construction, one rule matters most: one question per video, in plain words.

## Monthly progress video: 6 steps

The monthly progress video is the backbone of construction content. Release it on the same day every month and in the same order: buyers start expecting it.

### 1. Fix your filming spots

Choose 4–6 spots on site: a wide view from the access road, the facade from a neighboring building or a high point, the courtyard, one typical apartment inside, the entrance and elevator area. Photograph each spot and write down a marker ("next to the column, phone at eye level"). Film from exactly the same place and height every month — that's what makes the difference between months visible.

### 2. Write down what changed this month

Before filming, get a list of 3–5 points from the site manager or technical director: "9th-floor frame finished", "facade started on block 1", "heating pipes installed". Every shot in the video should show one fact from that list.

### 3. Show the date and place

The first shot should carry the date and the project name: "November 2026 · Block 2". A video without a date doesn't build trust — it could have been filmed in any month.

### 4. Keep the script short

A simple template for a 60-second report:

1. **0–3 seconds:** the most visible change plus on-screen text ("+3 floors this month" — only if that's true).
2. **3–40 seconds:** 3–5 facts from the list, each a 5–8 second shot with a short caption.
3. **40–55 seconds:** what will be done next month.
4. **55–60 seconds:** a call to action: "Book a site visit".

A voiceover helps, but subtitles are a must: many people watch with the sound off. Ready-made templates are in [Reels script templates](/en/blog/reels-script-templates/).

### 5. Film on a phone, but keep it steady

You don't need a professional camera. You need a stabilizer or at least slow two-handed movement, daylight, a clean lens (construction sites are dusty), and vertical format. More on phone filming in [shooting Reels on a phone](/en/blog/shooting-reels-on-a-phone/).

### 6. Edit and publish within a day

A late progress video loses value. Edit on the day of filming or the next day, and publish on the same date each month. Phone apps are enough for simple editing — see the list in [video editing apps](/en/blog/video-editing-apps/).

## Filming on a construction site: safety and drones

A construction site is a dangerous place. Never break safety rules for the sake of a video.

- **Permission and an escort.** Agree on filming times with the site manager. The camera operator shouldn't walk the site alone — a responsible staff member goes with them.
- **Protective gear.** Hard hat, vest, closed shoes — for the operator and for everyone in the shot. A worker without a hard hat on camera raises questions, not trust.
- **Workers' consent.** If you show someone in close-up or in an interview, get their consent first.
- **Don't interrupt the work.** Don't stop for a shot under a crane or in areas where people work at height, and don't ask workers to "pose".

**About drones.** An aerial shot is very effective, but in Uzbekistan flying a drone requires several permits: registering the drone and getting permission to use it from the Uzaviation agency, then permission to use the airspace ([Civil Aviation Agency](https://gov.uz/oz/uzcaa/pages/dronlar-uua)). Work with an operator who has the permits, or skip the drone and film with a phone from a neighboring building or an upper floor of the site (from a safe spot).

## What not to show

Construction videos exist to build trust. These things make them work the other way:

- **Deadlines you can't meet.** Before saying "keys this summer", check it with the technical director. A broken promise stays in the video forever.
- **Only the pretty angles.** If the video is always filmed from one tidy corner, buyers will ask "what about the rest?" Show different areas.
- **Old footage.** Don't reuse shots from last month's report. If someone notices once, trust in every video is gone.
- **Too many effects.** Fast music, lots of transitions and filters turn a documentary video into an ad. Here, simplicity is the strength.
- **Safety violations.** A person without a hard hat, an open pit, a messy site — cut such shots in editing and fix the problem on site.

## Using the videos in ads and sales

Making the video is half the job. The other half is getting it to the right people.

1. **Instagram profile.** Post progress videos as Reels and create a Highlight called "Construction" so the months appear in order.
2. **Telegram channel.** One post a month: the video, 3–5 lines of facts, next month's plan. Channel subscribers are the most interested audience.
3. **Retargeting.** Show the monthly report as an ad to people who visited the website, didn't finish the form, or watched the video to the end. Cold audiences get the render and the offer; warm audiences get the construction video. Setup is covered in [how retargeting works](/en/blog/how-retargeting-works/).
4. **Sales team.** After a call, managers send the client last month's video: "Here's how the site looked last week." It's stronger than a plain text message.
5. **Past buyers.** Send it to people who have already bought: they feel reassured and show it to friends.

**Illustrative example.** A developer building a 3-block residential complex in Tashkent releases a progress video on the 5th of every month, an interview with the chief engineer once a quarter, and answers one buyer question every week. The progress video goes into retargeting, and managers send it to every client who leaves a request. This example shows only the content structure, not a promised result.

## A one-month content plan

| Week | Video | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Monthly progress video (60 seconds) | Reels, Telegram, retargeting, sales team |
| Week 2 | Materials or technology (30 seconds) | Reels, Stories |
| Week 3 | Engineer or project lead answers a question (60 seconds) | Reels, retargeting |
| Week 4 | Buyer question or a completed building | Stories, Telegram |
| Once a quarter | "Same spot" video: 3 months in a row | Reels, ads |

One filming day can produce material for several videos: report spots, close-ups of materials, and an interview. How to plan that kind of work is covered in [how ad creatives are produced](/en/blog/how-ad-creatives-are-produced/).

### Pre-shoot checklist

- 4–6 fixed spots chosen and photographed.
- List of this month's facts received from the site manager.
- Filming time agreed with the site manager; an escort is assigned.
- The operator has a hard hat and vest.
- Lens clean, phone charged, storage free.
- Date and project name ready for the first shot.
- One question chosen for the interview.
- Editing and publishing dates set.

## Questions and answers

### How often should a developer post construction videos?

The monthly report at least once a month, always on the same date. Together with shorter videos of other types, one video a week is enough and realistic for most developers.

### What can you show if construction hasn't started yet?

Site preparation, a short explanation of the permit documents, an introduction to the project lead, and buildings the developer has already completed. Trust is built before the building appears.

### Do you need a professional operator, or is a phone enough?

A phone is enough for monthly reports and short videos — what matters is a steady shot, good light and fixed spots. For ad videos and important interviews, an operator and a good microphone help.

### If construction is delayed, should you stop the videos?

No. When the videos stop, buyers assume the worst. Say openly what is happening and what the new date is — that keeps more trust than silence.

## Conclusion

Construction progress videos give a developer what it needs most — trust. Pick fixed spots, publish a dated report on the same day every month, show the people, don't break safety rules, and hand the videos to retargeting and the sales team.
