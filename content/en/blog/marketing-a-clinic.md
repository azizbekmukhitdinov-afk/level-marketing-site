When it comes to health, people choose a doctor, not a building. Marketing a clinic or dental practice relies on trust more than almost any other field — and follows stricter rules. Here is how to approach the doctor's personal brand, reviews, maps and advertising.

## What's different about healthcare marketing

- Patients arrive with fear and worry: pain, price, outcome.
- Decisions are often based on recommendations and reviews.
- Many people search for a clinic "near me".
- Medical advertising is restricted by law and by platform policies.

## The doctor's personal brand

Videos in which a doctor answers common questions, explains procedures in plain language and debunks myths turn them into a familiar face. Our [personal brand cases](/en/blog/case-personal-brand/) include a chiropractor and a gastroenterologist-nutritionist. For where to start, see [personal brand: where to start](/en/blog/personal-brand-where-to-start/).

## Content that builds trust

- Introducing the doctors: experience, qualifications, specialisation.
- A clinic tour: equipment, the sterilisation process, treatment rooms.
- Answers to common questions: price ranges, pain, treatment time.
- Patient stories — only with written consent.
- Before/after photos — only with the patient's consent and where platform rules allow: Meta may restrict such images in healthcare ads.

## Reviews and maps

Your Google and Yandex Maps listing is the clinic's second reception desk: address, hours, phone, photos and reviews. Ask satisfied patients for reviews and reply to negative ones calmly and professionally, without disclosing any patient's medical details. More in our articles on [online reputation](/en/blog/online-reputation-and-reviews/) and [adding your business to maps](/en/blog/add-your-business-to-maps/).

## Stay within advertising rules

- Don't guarantee outcomes: no "100% cure" or "guaranteed painless".
- Don't refer to the reader's health directly: instead of "Do you have gastritis?", try "Gastritis: when to see a doctor".
- Check the legal requirements for licences, warnings and ad copy with a lawyer before launching.
- To keep your ad account safe, follow the rules in [ad account blocked](/en/blog/ad-account-blocked/).

## Paid ads

- The area around the clinic — especially for dentistry.
- Offers: a free consultation or diagnostics, check-up packages, "prices from…".
- Lead form or Direct messages — [which suits you](/en/blog/lead-form-or-messages/).
- [Retargeting](/en/blog/how-retargeting-works/) website visitors and video viewers.

## From lead to appointment

1. The receptionist replies quickly and books the appointment — a [sales script](/en/blog/sales-script/) is needed here too.
2. A reminder the day before the visit — fewer no-shows.
3. A call after treatment and a request for [feedback](/en/blog/collecting-customer-feedback/).

## A sample one-week content plan

| Day | Content |
|---|---|
| Monday | A doctor answers a frequently asked question |
| Wednesday | Inside the clinic: sterilisation or new equipment |
| Friday | A patient's story — only with written consent |
| Stories, every day | Free slots, reminders, team life |

One day of filming with a doctor can be enough for a month of videos.

## How to show prices

Price is what scares patients most. Don't hide it: list "from" prices and what they include. Explain why the final cost is set after diagnosis — then the patient sees a professional approach, not a trick.

## The administrator carries marketing forward

Ads bring in the call, but the administrator books the appointment. Their script should cover: a greeting, understanding the patient's problem, two specific time options, the necessary information and a reminder. Track the no-show rate every week.

## Common mistakes

- Guaranteeing results.
- Posting before/after photos without consent.
- Revealing patient details in a reply to a review.
- Not replying to reviews at all.
- Showing ads in areas far from the clinic.

## The patient journey: where patients are lost

| Stage | What to check |
|---|---|
| Ads and maps | Can people find the clinic; is the listing complete? |
| Call or DM | How quickly do you reply? |
| Booking | Are convenient times offered? |
| Reminder | Does the patient actually come? |
| Appointment | How do the doctor and staff treat the patient? |
| After the visit | A review, a return visit, a recommendation |

Measure how many patients drop out at each stage: often the problem isn't the ads but the call or reminder stage.

## A Telegram channel for a clinic

A Telegram channel keeps you in touch with existing patients: doctors' tips, schedules, reminders about preventive check-ups and answers to questions. Add a booking bot and patients can book without waiting for the administrator's working hours. Share the channel link at reception and in the message after each visit.

## Questions and answers

### What if a doctor doesn't want to be on video?

Don't force it. Start with text posts, voice answers or videos showing only hands and the procedure. Often one doctor takes the lead and others join once they see the results.

### How should you ask patients for reviews?

After successful treatment, by message, with a direct link to the review page. Don't offer discounts or gifts for reviews — that's against platform rules.

### Which services are best to advertise?

Entry-level ones: consultations, diagnostics, hygiene. They're easy to decide on, and once patients know the clinic, they come back for more complex treatment.

### How should you measure ad results?

Not by cost per lead, but by the cost of a patient who booked and actually came. Track the no-show rate separately — it reflects how well the administrator and reminders work.

### Which words should you avoid in medical ads?

Avoid promises like "the best", "100%", "guaranteed" and "painless", as well as questions that point directly at the reader's health. Use neutral, informative wording: what the service is, who it suits and what stages it involves.

## Conclusion

The strongest marketing tool a clinic has is its doctors: their knowledge, openness and care for patients. Add reviews, maps and rule-compliant paid ads. Level Marketing helps build doctors' personal brands and create content for clinics.
